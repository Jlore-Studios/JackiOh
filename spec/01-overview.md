## 1. Overview

JackiOh is a 1v1 collectible card game: a Hearthstone-style mana curve, combat math and keyword vocabulary, played on Yu-Gi-Oh-style lanes with a hidden backrow of traps. This document is the complete specification: the rules, all 634 cards of the seven sets (100 Core, 90 Classic, 78 Classic+, 102 Meditative, 26 Meditative+, 178 Stinkyscape, 60 Training Grounds) and their 140 tokens described by what they do to game state, and the architecture and engine they run on. Patch v0.2.0 (issue #40, [[R380]] on) added Classic and Classic+, the Meditative set (issue #496) shipped with issue #553 ([[R1420]]), the Meditative+ set (issue #1173) with issue #1187, in patch v0.3.15 ([[R1178000]]), the Stinkyscape set (issue #1836) with issue #1884 ([[R1857000]]), and the Training Grounds set (tracker #2193) with issue #2204, a patch of the v0.5 line ([[R2194000]], [[R2204000]]).

Design pillars:

- Short, sharp games: 25-card decks with no duplicates, 4 max mana, 30 hero health, a 60-turn cap that is a backstop, fatigue being the usual end of a long game ([[§2.5]]).
- Two combat axes: Attack/Defense position (Defense = Taunt + Armor) layered over Hearthstone-style free-target attacking.
- Radiant: every card has an upgraded form, and upgrading cards mid-game is a core resource loop (the Glowy Jelly Bean family, Radiant Saintess, Gifted Program, Selective Breeding).
- Lanes matter: 5 shared lanes give adjacency (Cleave, Hit Job, Collateral Damage), rotation (Silly Silas) and per-lane traps (Zoomerbin Oomen).
- Chaos is a feature: coin flips, random keywords, Call to Chaos, Pocket Chaos, Transmogulate. All of it must run on a seeded RNG so any match replays exactly.

How to read this spec: sections 2 to 7 are the rules; 8 is the card catalog (mechanics, not flavor text), with each Core card's rarity assigned by mechanical complexity and each Classic and Classic+ card's rarity the designer's; 9 and 10 are the architecture and the engine design; 11 collects every ruling this spec makes where the source was silent or ambiguous, with the ones still open marked decide; 12 is unused (it held custom cards, removed in issue #2956); 13 is Duos, the co-op mode that plays two such games side by side ([[§13]]); 14 is the collection economy, how an account comes to own cards and cosmetics ([[§14]]).

Source material: the original JackiOh design notes (a mechanics sheet, the Core card list and a CCG architecture document), and for Classic and Classic+ the designer's card list of 2026-09-30 (`JackiOh_Classic_Cards.md`, whose reading card by card is the design brief `docs/classic-sets.md`), and for Meditative the designer's card list of 2026-10-08 (`JackiOh_Meditative_Cards.md`, whose reading card by card is the design brief `docs/meditative-set.md`), and for Meditative+ the designer's card list of 2026-10-18 (`JackiOh_MeditativePlus_Cards.md`, whose reading card by card is the design brief `docs/meditative-plus-set.md`), and for the collection economy ([[§14]]) the designer's model in issue #1294, referred to below as "the source". Wherever this spec goes beyond them it is marked **Ruling:** in place, and the same item appears in section 11 so it can be confirmed or overturned in one place. Card references read "#N" for Core, "C #N" for Classic and "C+ #N" for Classic+, "M #N" for Meditative ([[§8.8]]), "M+ #N" for Meditative+ ([[§8.9]]), "S #N" for Stinkyscape ([[§8.10]]), and "T-AI-N" for Classic+'s AI generated cards ([[§7]]); Stinkyscape's own ten reuse the indices T-AI-1 to T-AI-10 under its prefix, "S T-AI-N", as its shared tokens are "S T-…", so a bare "T-AI-N" is always Classic+'s.

### 1.1 The rules at a glance

The game on one page, for a first read and for finding the section a question belongs to. It decides nothing: each line's link is the rule, and where a line and its section differ, the section wins and this summary is the bug.

- **The game.** Two players, 30-health heroes, decks of exactly 25 cards with no duplicates and no tokens, any sets mixed ([[§2]], [[§2.6]]). Players see the library as the Deck and Sacrifice as Tribute, and Nerf and Buff are the engine's Degrade and Upgrade ([[§6]]).
- **Setup.** Both decks are shuffled with the match seed; Player 1 draws 3 and Player 2 draws 4; both mulligan at once, replacements drawn before the returned cards go back; Player 1 goes first and draws on turn 1 ([[§2.1]]).
- **A turn.** Start: refresh mana, start-of-turn effects and triggers, then draw 1. Main phase, one action at a time: play a card, attack, switch a unit's position, Activate, Trade, offer or answer a draw, concede, end the turn. End: end-of-turn triggers, the end-of-turn trap window, delayed effects, cleanup ([[§2.2]]).
- **Mana.** Max mana is the number of turns you have started, at most 4 before modifiers, refilled at the start of your turn; temporary mana may go past 4. An X cost is chosen at play, at least 1 ([[§2.3]]).
- **Cards and fatigue.** The Nth draw from an empty library deals N damage to your hero. A hand holds 10, and a card that would go past that burns; a library holds 60 ([[§2.4]]).
- **Winning.** A hero at 0 or less at a state check loses, both at once is a draw, and the 60th player-turn ends the game in a draw; conceding, disconnecting, an agreed draw and the cards that win or lose a game are in [[§2.5]].
- **The board.** Each side has 5 unit zones and 5 backrow zones; lane N faces lane N, and adjacent means N−1 and N+1 in the same row. Attacks are not lane-restricted, bar the few cards that say so ([[§3]], [[§3.1]]). A play needs an empty, unlocked zone the player picks; a summon with no named zone takes the leftmost empty, unlocked one ([[§3.2]]).
- **Hidden information.** A hand is its owner's; nobody knows a library's order; a face-down Trap is its controller's, the opponent seeing only a face-down card and its cost ([[§3]]). The client is shown only what its player may see ([[§10.8]]).
- **Card types.** Unit, Spell, Field Spell, Trap and Field Trap. A Trap is placed face-down and fires by itself when its condition is met, on either turn, then goes to the graveyard; a Field Trap stays and can fire again ([[§5]], [[§5.1]]). Every card has a Radiant face, about twice as strong at the same cost, and Radiant is a flag on the instance ([[§5.2]]). Tokens are made only when a card or a rule names them ([[§7]]); [[§8]] is every card.
- **Positions.** Units enter in Attack Position; Defense Position gives Taunt and Armor +1. Each unit has one exertion a turn, an attack or a position switch, and only an Attack-Position unit attacks. A unit cannot attack the turn it enters, unless Rush (units only) or Charge (units and hero) lifts that ([[§4.1]]).
- **An attack.** Choose a target, an enemy unit or hero, Taunt permitting; the trap window opens; then First Strike hits first and the rest at once, a hero striking back with nothing ([[§4.2]], [[§4.3]]).
- **Damage.** Every hit runs one pipeline, whose steps include, in order: Spell Damage, Divine Shield, Armor (Pierce skips it), hit caps, Indestructible, then the damage, then Poisonous, Lifesteal, Trample and Cleave ([[§4.4]]).
- **The state check** runs after every action, every fully resolved effect and every combat, never between the hits of one effect: the dead are collected, a hero at 0 ends the game, Death triggers fire, Reborn returns, and it repeats until nothing changes ([[§4.5]]).
- **Keywords** each map to one engine primitive, and a card's script only composes them ([[§6]]): unit keywords ([[§6.1]]), triggers and timing ([[§6.2]]), actions and verbs ([[§6.3]]).
- **The engine** is pure and seeded, so any match replays exactly: every random draw comes from the state's seed, and every choice a player makes is a prompt held in state ([[§9.3]], [[§10]], [[§10.6]], [[§10.7]]).
- **A rule the spec does not make** follows Hearthstone and becomes a ruling ([[§11]]).
- **Beyond the duel.** Accounts, matchmaking, the practice AI, the tutorial and the ladder ([[§9]]); Duos ([[§13]]); the collection economy ([[§14]]); dungeon runs ([[§15]]).
