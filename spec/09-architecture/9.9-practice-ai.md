### 9.9 Practice against the AI

Practice is a single-player game against a computer opponent, on the client's `/practice` route. It needs no account and no server. The engine, the AI and the practice session itself (`jackioh-shared`'s `host/practice`, a mode of the host machine, which the server steps over a claimed game to check it, [[R2560000]]) run in the player's browser, in a Web Worker, and the page receives only `viewFor(state, human)`, the human's `legalActions` and whether the AI owes an action. The worker stands where [[§9.1]] puts the server. A practice game records no result, no rating and no collection change, and it replays exactly from `(seed, decks, handicaps, log)` ([[R187]]); an active account's free game on a server seed earns as [[§14.8]] says ([[R1303000]]). A finished free game is kept on the device, the last `PRACTICE_REPLAYS_KEPT` of them, with its setup, and its result dialog and the recent games replay it in the worker; an Infinite Assault fight is kept with them ([[R5125000]]), and a lesson, a dungeon run's fight and a game Glitch voided are not ([[R768]], [[R767000]], [[R768000]], [[R2706000]]). Its result dialog, like a lesson's, ends its answers with Main menu, which leaves for the Play hub's Solo group, where Practice and the Tutorial are, and so does the board's Menu ([[R4742000]]).

The human always plays with this spec's resources, but for a lesson's smaller deck ([[§9.10]], [[R1705005]]). The AI seat gets a handicap ([[R180]]) by difficulty, held as `AI_DIFFICULTY` in `config.ts`:

| | Easy | Medium | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deck size | 25 | 30 | 35 |
| Max mana | min(turns, 4), as a human | min(turns + 1, 5) | min(turns + 1, 7) |
| Opening hand | as a human | +1 card | +1 card |
| Draws per turn | 1 | 1 | 2 |

"+1" means one more crystal than a human has on every turn, up to the tier's cap, with persistent and temporary mana modifiers on top exactly as for a human ([[R181]]). Hard includes Medium's extra opening card ([[R182]]), and its second draw is a separate draw ([[R183]]). An AI deck holds its tier's number of distinct, token-free cards of every set ([[R184]], [[R380]]): Medium's `MEDIUM_EXTRA_DECK_CARDS` (5) and Hard's `HARD_EXTRA_DECK_CARDS` (10) more than `DECK_SIZE` ([[R1705000]]). The Coin is not a resource of a tier: whichever seat goes second, the AI's or the human's, is dealt it after the mulligan at every tier ([[R244]]). The tiers change resources only: the AI's search, evaluation and budget are the same at every tier. Below Easy sits one more handicap, the tutorial's (`AI_TUTORIAL`, [[§9.10]], [[R290]]), which lowers the AI seat's resources instead of raising them; no player picks it, the tutorial deals it. In Duos the same AI plays both seats of the AI team, each with its tier's handicap, and the tier also sets that team's pool and pass cost ([[§13.7]]).

The AI (`crates/ai`) is pure and seeded like the engine. It also emotes: at match creation every AI opponent is dealt one of four personas — Balanced, Polite, BM or Silent — that rolls cosmetic emotes off an RNG injected apart from the engine's and the search's, under the players' own rate limit and caps ([[R645]]), choosing only within the hand of emotes the AI seat was dealt ([[R1344]]). The practice page and the ladder bots roll the same module, `jackioh-shared`'s, the page through the WebAssembly module on a stream of its own ([[R5269000]]). The tutorial's persona is always Silent. The same AI plays the ladder bots on the server, at strengths of their own and on this spec's resources, and the same personas emote for them, through a Rust port of their tables and rolls; each bot keeps the persona it was dealt when it was made, by weights of its own, and waits out its emotes in windows refitted from people's ([[§9.12]], [[R1452]], [[R645]], [[R639000]]).

- **Information.** It decides from what its seat may know and nothing else ([[R185]]). Before it simulates, it determinizes: the opponent's hand, the backrow it cannot read and the opponent's library are resampled from the non-token cards of every set the opponent has not shown ([[R185]]), or, when the game's setup names the sets the opponent's deck is dealt from (an Infinite Assault enemy's, public in the view), from those sets' alone ([[R4595000]]), its own library is shuffled, and the seed is its own. Its own library holds what its owner's list shows ([[R310]]): a card it was never shown is resampled from the cards its own side has not shown, since a deck holds one copy of each, and one that came from the opponent's deck from those the opponent has not shown ([[R312]], [[R73]], [[R2826000]]), and every other card is the definition and face it went in with, its Created mark kept and nothing a change made inside the library wrote on it (a Radiant roll of #28 or #42, a Nerf, a discount), unless its own open prompt offers the card, which the prompt shows in full ([[R2691000]]). A face-down card shows its cost ([[R351]]), so each one the seat cannot read is resampled from the Traps and Field Traps of that cost while one the opponent has not shown is left ([[R762]]).
- **Search.** Each action is chosen by a turn-level beam search over `legalActions` sequences played through `reduce` on several determinizations. The search starts with a lethal solver, a bounded search for a line that wins this turn on every determinization, answers the AI's prompts the same way, and re-plans after every action. A line the solver proves is played trimmed to the actions it needs: its shortest end that still wins on every determinization, less any other action it still wins without. Only its first action is played, and a free play that changes nothing, such as a (0) Spell played again from the graveyard, could otherwise stand in front of the same lethal at every decision ([[R1965000]]). An action every determinization allowed can still be refused when it is sent, by a cost the sampled worlds could not see; the AI then sends in its place the first action of the next of its finalists (the best first actions, scored on every determinization) that is accepted, best mean first, and only when none is does it fall back to ending its turn, an answer or any other legal action. A finalist whose line wins on the first determinization alone, the one the beam searched, is ranked by its mean on the others it was scored on, since the beam found that line because it wins there; a line that wins on two is still played at once ([[R4608000]]). A finalist's replay that meets a step its world does not offer, such as the play of a card the line drew that this world's shuffle did not deal, plays that world's turn on greedily before it ends, so a line that draws and then plays what it drew is ranked by what the turn gives on each world ([[R4447000]], [[R4697000]]). A line that waits on one of the AI's own prompts, a Discover's for one, is ranked by the position its answers lead to, the AI answering ahead as it would, so a card whose payoff comes after its prompts is not dropped before the payoff lands. Every prompt the AI answers ahead, its own or the opponent's, in a simulated step or the opponent's reply, tries at most `AI_SEARCH.max_auto_answer_options` of its answers, drawn from all of them on the world's own stream rather than taken from the top of the list, so neither the order of a prompt's answers nor a late place among them keeps a strong or harmful answer from being weighed. The Coin is one more card to the search: the AI plays it when a line that spends its mana scores better than one that keeps it. The mulligan returns cards costing more than 3 plus the seat's mana bonus, within its mana cap, so Medium's and Hard's four-drops stay ([[R2977000]]). There a card that asks for X units to sacrifice (Tribute X, [[§6.3]]) costs the larger of its mana cost and X plus one, so a Tribute 3 three-drop goes back ([[R3936000]]). It keeps no card dearer than that, and where its curve must choose among the cheap cards (at most two one-drops, only the cheapest of a hand with no early play), it keeps those its evaluation prices highest as a card in hand, ties in hand order ([[R4110000]]). The AI answers the mulligan as soon as the mulligans open, without waiting for the human's, so the human sees the AI ready ([[R265]], [[R266]]). A question of a sequence (Meditative+ #20) is answered at once with no search, by the option its statement works out to, never Stop, so thirty questions stay inside the AI's budget ([[R1184002]]), and so is Classic+ #42 KY's Test's Easy problem. Ahead, a simulated step answers such a question of the AI's own as it would, judged right on its world, not by drawing from its answers: a sequence's questions until the streak reaches what the AI's accuracy earns on average, then Stop, so the search expects the Kenny the AI will summon ([[R1563005]]). Two positions that differ only in the choice a Mind Games keeps in secret are two positions to the search, so the three choices of one cast are three lines it weighs, not the first listed ([[R3772000]]). Two placements of one play that differ only in where its cards stand, a backrow card's lane or which side of a standing Unit a new one goes among them, take one line of the beam between them, and up to `AI_SEARCH.zone_variants` of them still end their turn beside it ([[R4132000]]). Positions that differ in the last Spell played while a card that copies it (Classic #57 Echo) is in the AI's hand or graveyard, or in which damage chain a Churnwalker put a Unit in (Stinkyscape+ #7), are two positions too, and a Dormant card's lane and an animated card's home zone count as where its cards stand ([[R4808000]]). So are positions that differ in which card of the AI's hand a buff or a granted keyword went on (Stinkyscape+ #100 Plesiosize, Stinkyscape+ #23 GIGA-ify), which the card keeps onto the field, to the beam, the lethal solver and the concession check alike ([[R4793000]]). The lethal solver keeps two placements apart when they differ in a Unit's lane, so a lethal that stands only in the second lane a play is tried in, across a slash or outside The Gauntlet, is found and the lethal hint lights for it ([[R4806000]]). While its count of what the ready attacks deal stands short of lethal, the solver's best-first search takes first, of two equally far positions, the one that has kept more of its attackers ready, so an attacker spent on a Taunt comes after the moves that keep it, and once it has tried 75 moves below one move without coming closer it turns to that move's alternatives first ([[R5119000]]). Two lanes are one placement of a play when the zones beside the lane and across it read alike; a zone a GIGA covers reads as that GIGA, and each half of a slashed board is apart ([[R4799001]]). While a Feng Shui acts (Meditative #40), each step tries a play its card would be rewarded for before the other plays and one it would be punished for after them, so the plays that chain the elements are not cut, and two orders of the same plays that end on different elements are two positions ([[R3951100]]). Its own Feng Shui's Activate, a free Auspicious Rock, comes before every play ([[R4154403]]). While its own Feng Shui acts, the Rock the next turn's Activate adds counts among the cards its next play may be, and a play that leaves an element generating another card of its hand is tried before the other plays no Feng Shui judges ([[R4783041]]). A Trade of a Tradable card no coming crystals pay for comes before the plays too, so a line can play what it draws ([[R4154400]]), and a Plague Counter's placement under a Living Bomb tries the enemy's permanents first ([[R4154402]]). The beam keeps, past its width at each depth, the best line one of whose steps played a listed discount card (`AI_SEARCH.discount_def_ids`) and made the seat's other hand cards cheaper, so a "your cards cost less this turn" play has the plays it pays for searched ([[R4595002]]); a beam that spent nodes and closed no line takes the candidate whose one unanswered step evaluates best, a search of one ply rather than a fallback ([[R4595001]]). It keeps Classic #37 Last Hurrah and Stinkyscape+ #79 A Glimpse into the Endless Sea for a last resort: a play of either is a move it considers only while its life is low or the enemy's board threatens it with lethal, or while it has played no card this turn and has no other card to play, and A Glimpse also while a card of its own would shuffle cards back into its deck ([[R4783040]]).
- **The opponent's reply.** The best lines are scored one turn deeper. When none of them leaves an effect of the AI's own waiting that it did not hold before (a countdown, a next-turn reward), the best line that does is scored after the reply too, last, on the nodes left past those kept for scoring the finalists on the other determinizations, and not once a host's clock has passed its wrap-up, so a counter or a clock that runs short drops it first ([[R3696000]]). On each determinization the opponent answers the line by a fixed rule and ends its turn, and the line is scored at the start of the AI's next turn. Its turn never ends by itself there ([[R82]]): every sampled world holds it open, so the AI's End turn stops where that turn begins with the crystals the line left unspent still counted, even against an opponent with nothing to do, and the reply ends it ([[R4797000]]). The opponent's turn there takes at most `AI_REPLY.max_steps` (12) steps, of which the cards and Activates below take at most `AI_REPLY.play_steps` (6), so its attacks keep the rest; a turn whose steps run out with the opponent still on turn ends there, in up to `AI_REPLY.close_steps` (3) more steps for its End turn and the prompts that turn's end opens ([[R4810000]]). A line found on one determinization is played again on each of the others; where one of the AI's own prompts there does not offer the line's answer (each world draws its own Discover), the AI answers that prompt as it would ahead and plays on, so every finalist is scored after the reply on every world. The opponent plays a card the line itself put in its hand (a Pocket Chaos handed back, units a Flood bounced), when that improves its position, because the AI watched that card arrive. While it holds such a card that its own turn's end would discard (a Fortify Mind), it compares each play, and standing still, after its turn ends; a guess at an unrevealed secret of the AI's it tries once, drawn on a stream of its own before anything is weighed, so the guess never depends on the secret ([[R3696001]]). It uses an Activate of a card the AI can see face-up on the field (The Power to Punish's damage, a Heroic Power's rolled Ping) the same way, trying at most `AI_REPLY.visible_activations` (4) uses: those aimed at the AI's hero first, then one use of each such card and ability in turn at the AI's most valuable card, then each one's next, so a Unit in the fifth lane or a second gun is never out of the reply's reach ([[R4515000]]). Otherwise it swings with its board by static trade value: lethal first, then a kill it survives, a trade up, or the face. While a Mayor acts and the engine draws every attack's target ([[R1200]]), an attack is worth the mean of a hit on each target it may draw, in the reply and in the order the AI's own search tries its attacks, and the lethal solver counts it on the hero only when the hero is its only target ([[R4792000]]). Each blow meets Armor, the caps, a conversion and the damage pools in [[§4.4]]'s order, and a converted one counts as the Burn or Toxic it adds ([[R4064001]]). An attacker a First Strike defender destroys before it strikes lands nothing there, so the attack is worth no damage dealt and no Divine Shield broken, only the attacker lost ([[R4171000]]). A hit from a source that exiles the Unit it damages (Meditative #72 The Banisher, [[R1124]]) reads as a Poisonous one there and in the count of the damage past the AI's Taunts, and what it exiles is priced whole, with no Death and no Reborn body left ([[R4065001]]). A Cleave splash there meets a GIGA beside the target in both its lanes, and beside a Unit standing in the backrow only the Units standing there, as the engine deals it ([[R3546001]], [[R1081000]], [[R4257000]]). Lethal counts every attack it has left: when those would together reach the AI's hero past its Taunts, every attack on the hero counts as the win, so the face goes first and no attacker is spent on a kill ([[R5027000]]). A unit of its with Deft in Defense Position switches to Attack first when that unit's attack is then the best of its attacks ([[R49]]). When no attack is left worth making, it switches a unit of its from Attack to Defense Position, or one without Deft from Defense back to Attack, if its own evaluation, the AI swinging next, prefers that: a unit that attacked has spent its switch unless it has Deft, and one that switches back has spent its attack for the turn, so it threatens the AI's face from its next turn ([[§4.1]], [[R49]], [[R4433000]]). While the AI's board could deal the opponent's hero its life next turn, the opponent also weighs, by the same evaluation, the switches to Defense Position that lower that damage against its best attack, and makes one when it beats both that attack and standing still, so it blocks a lethal swing rather than chip the AI's face. It walls nothing while its own attacks left would together reach the AI's hero past its Taunts, and a Deft unit that may still attack switches only after its attack ([[R4187000]]). It plays none of the cards it held unseen, because those are samples, and never uses the Reveal of a face-down card. Every step is a real `reduce`, so traps, First Strike, Taunt and end-of-turn effects happen as they would in the game, with one exception: a turn the autopilot owes (Cognitive Offloading, [[R1183002]]) is left owed, not played (`reduce_unpiloted`), so a line that hands a turn to it is scored where that turn begins, as every other line that hands a turn over is.
- **Evaluation.** Hero health (concave, so the last points weigh most), hero armor, board stats and keywords through [[§10.4]]'s layers, cards in hand (a Unit the AI holds also by part of what the face it will enter with, Radiant or buffed, is worth over its printed one), library, unspent mana at the end of the turn (less each crystal of it a Float took toward a "Float reduces cost" card in the AI's own hand that still cost more than its crystals, [[R3695000]]), the enemy's face damage next turn against the AI's health and the reverse (each side's attackers taking the better of two orders at the Taunts in their way, [[R3937000]], or of three where a Cleave splash meets a GIGA, a Cleave attacker first, [[R4257000]], each swing taking the Taunt's strike back, so an attacker it destroys swings no more and a Frenzy attacker that destroys the Taunt it attacked and survives swings again, [[R4402000]], each Cleave splash landing on every unit it strikes and a Trample attacker's sending what it deals beyond each to the hero, [[R4805000]], each hit on a hero meeting the Armor the walk's earlier hits have worn off it, [[R5228000]], every hit read through [[§4.4]] steps 3a and 3b: one that Burns or is Toxic instead deals nothing, and each side's damage pools soak what they have left, once across its hits, [[R4064000]], [[R4064001]]), and the enemy's face-down cards. Burn and Toxic ([[§6.3]]) count by the ticks still to come over their controller's next few turn starts, each met as the tick is met ([[R3540001]]), the side's refilled damage pools included ([[R4064000]]): a unit is worth less by the damage they will deal it, and one they will kill only a share of itself, as a Brittle one is; a hero's side loses a share of the life they will take, never below 1 ([[R3822000]]). While a Feng Shui acts, the AI's own hand counts a share of the best verdict its last element gives one of its cards, since that verdict judges its next play: a reward at what the card would gain in hand as Radiant, a punishment at the hit's cost on the hero curve, a neutral verdict nothing ([[R981]], [[R3951100]]). A Tradable card in its own hand that neither this turn's crystals nor its next refresh's pay for counts at a discount, which a Trade turns into a card drawn ([[R4154400]], [[R4812000]]); S #89 Spice counts at half a hand card's price, Credits waiting to be cashed, so the AI sells it more often, and its Credits once its play or discard gains them ([[R4410001]], [[R4440002]]); and a permanent a Living Bomb will destroy at its controller's next turn start counts as a Brittle 1 one ([[R400]], [[R4154402]]). Neither side's face damage counts a Unit its own Brittle count destroys at the start of its controller's next turn, before it can attack, unless it is Indestructible ([[R3793000]]). Nor does it count one the first tick of its own Burn and Toxic destroys there, met as [[R3540001]] meets it, the side's refilled damage pools included ([[R4064000]]), unless that side is on turn with its attacks still to come; an unspent Reborn does not keep it counted, its body coming back summoning sick ([[R83]]), unless that body has Charge, or Rush where only a Unit is the target ([[R4065000]]). Read at a side's own turn start, with its attacks still to come (the AI's after the opponent's reply, the enemy's in a line the AI has passed), the face damage counts no attack from a Frozen unit, which attacks nothing until its cleanup thaws it, and none on the hero from a unit that entered the field this turn without Charge ([[R1174000]], [[R4170000]]). A Dormant card on the board ([[§6.1]]) counts as the unit it wakes as, a share of it for each turn left on its countdown or a share of its own when it waits for an effect, though it threatens no face until it wakes ([[R2380000]]). What its "When this awakens" text does counts too: once a decision, on the same copy, the AI wakes each such card on either field and each such Unit in its own hand, placed first, beside the same card with its text switched off and its printed keywords kept, and the card counts the difference in its controller's evaluation at the same share as its body, so an enemy Imprisoned Cosmality counts against the AI for the damage it will deal ([[R3064000]]). An unevolved card with an Evolve text ([[§6.3]]) counts a share of what its evolution gives. Once a decision, on the same copy, the AI evolves each such card on either field and each such Unit in its own hand, placed first. The card counts the change in its controller's evaluation, once for each time its text would run ([[R3536003]]), times how close it stands. That is the share of its side's unit zones filled, of the (0) Cost cards the AI's own hand holds of those it needs, or of its health lost, or a fixed share when it waits on an event or on a hand the AI cannot see. A card that its own turn or attack text evolves counts through that text's price ([[R4648000]]). A card beneath one of the AI's own Stack piles ([[§3.2]]), such as the Drone Battleship's lower layers, counts half the unit it is, since it acts again once the cards above it leave; one beneath the enemy's, which the AI sees only as a count ([[R447]]), counts nothing ([[R3695001]]). A Unit standing in the backrow ([[§3.2]], [[R1081000]]) counts once, as the Unit it is, never again as a backrow card ([[R2490000]]). A unit's Death counts by what it leaves behind: once a decision, on a copy of a sampled world, the AI compares the sandbox's destroy of each unit whose Death it can read, its own hand's Units placed first, with its remove ([[R923003]]); the unit counts a share of the difference for its controller and a kill of it the whole difference less, so a Trigor counts toward the 6/6 it returns as ([[R2424000]]). A kill of a unit whose Reborn is unspent removes it less the body it comes back as, read as the engine makes that body: its keeper's card, on the side it died on, and a unit token at the X/X it was summoned as ([[R4798000]]); the frozen greedy baseline reads it as before. A Starship Piece counts the same way by the copy its death fuses into its owner's Starship ([[§6.1]]), probed after every unit with a Death so it never takes their share of the budget ([[R2566000]]). A backrow card's Death counts the same way, for each card the AI can read in either backrow (never a face-down enemy card) and its own hand's Field Spells, set first, probed after the units within the same budget, the share counted whatever its Brittle, since a crumble is a destroy: a Bauble Bubble counts toward the Stockpiles it leaves ([[R2687000]]). A card with a start-of-turn or end-of-turn effect counts by what one firing of it gives its controller: once a decision, on the same copy, the AI fires each such effect once, for the Units on either field, the cards it can read in either backrow (never a face-down enemy card) and its own hand's Field Spells and Units, set or placed first, and the card counts a share of the change in its controller's evaluation, each crystal of mana gained at a next-turn crystal's worth, so a Mana Well counts toward the mana it gives and an enemy Gatling Pea toward the hits it deals, and a kill of such a Unit is worth its price ([[R2477000]], [[R2565000]]). A start-of-the-opponent's-turn effect, Living Bomb's, is fired and counted the same way; a start-of-turn or end-of-turn effect counts once for each time its controller's turn runs it, twice beside that player's Joint Filing, while the start of the opponent's turn, which no multiplier repeats ([[R821]]), counts once ([[R2689000]]). A unit's Activate counts by what one use of it gives: once a decision, on the same copy, the AI ends its own turn and then the enemy's, tries each player's units' Activates it can see at the start of that player's next turn, as the reply does, and counts a share of the best use's change in its controller's evaluation, so a Cannon v3 on either side counts toward the Unit it can destroy each turn ([[R2564000]]). A card acting face-up in either backrow, a Field Spell such as The Power to Punish, counts its Activate the same way, and a face-down card never does ([[R2603000]]). A pick that gives a card its power for the game, Heroic Power's Discover ([[R3134002]]), counts each offered power the same way: once a decision, at the AI's own prompt, each answer is played on a copy and the power it leaves is used at the start of the AI's next turn, and a card counts the price of the power it holds, never another's ([[R4197000]]). A unit's "when this attacks" and "after this attacks" text counts by what its next attack gives: once a decision, on the same copy and the same two turns, the AI plays each visible unit's attack at the start of its controller's next turn, on the enemy hero where it may and else on the first target the rules allow, beside the same attack by a twin whose text is switched off and which keeps every keyword the unit holds, those its side lends it among them ([[R4811000]]), and the unit counts a share of the difference in its controller's evaluation while it may attack, so an enemy Propagation's Wasp counts toward the copy it makes and an own Badger toward the Resource it gains ([[R2688000]]). Frenzy is no such text, the twin holding it too; what it gives is read where a Frenzy attacker swings again past the Taunts ([[R4799002]]). A card in either graveyard that its owner may still play from there, by Flashback, Sanctum's lent one or a permission such as Second Wind's, counts a share of what it would count in a hand, the enemy's too since a graveyard is public ([[§6.3]], [[R454]], [[R783012]], [[R1869003]]). A unit in Defense Position counts only part of its attack, since it cannot attack until it switches back (a unit with Deft can switch and still attack, so it keeps its whole attack and its threat to the face, [[R49]]), and the position's Taunt and Armor count only through the damage they stop ([[§4.1]]). Every point of damage on the enemy hero also counts at a flat rate, and more as the turn cap nears, because a game that reaches the cap is a draw ([[§2.5]]). A position scored after the opponent's reply counts the enemy's threat at a discount, because the AI moves first and can still answer it. A countdown on the board ([[R1181000]]) counts beyond the reply too, a payoff for its owner and a threat for the other side: a line that leaves one waiting is scored again with it resolved at the start of the AI's next turn, the two scores blended by a share per turn it would still wait (`AI_REPLY.countdown_share`). An unconditional price hike on the coming turn (IA #123 Cost Injection, IA #144 Total Compromise, IA #225 Root Kit, C+ T-AI-7 Alignment Tax) counts in coming refreshes as harm against that player's next-turn crystals, at next-turn crystals' worth times the plays it taxes ([[R5122000]]). A Nerf written on a card in the opponent's hand counts against that side a step each, never a secret Nerf, and a misfire waiting on a side's next Spell counts against it until a Spell spends it (IA #137 Patch Tuesday Exploit, IA #130 Prompt Injection), each at a weight of its own ([[R5246000]]). The search scores every line and every finalist with this evaluation alone; the learned network that corrected it and the policy prior are retired ([[R4159000]]).
- **Budgets.** Budgets count `reduce` calls, so the same state and AI seed give the same decision. The practice AI's, `AI_BUDGET`, is 1,200 nodes, of which the lethal solver may spend 300, twice the 600 and 150 it had; the ladder bots' two middle strengths and the Duos AI team keep those ([[R2575000]]). A step whose `reduce` does far more work than most is charged extra nodes for it, so a budget bounds time as well as count: a play that ranks every shipped card (Zephrys, Zephrys Zealotism, or a fused card holding one), an attack My Pawn would answer, one its own projection calls lethal ([[R4809001]]), and a play the other side's Pareto Optimality judges (per card held). A step that casts one of those ranking cards without playing it, from a graveyard (Spell Tyrant) or at random (Call to Chaos, Jogg's Box), pays the same once it has run, since nothing in its action says so before. Where the budget cannot pay a step's surcharge, the beam passes that step over and tries the next, but the opponent's reply stops there unscored, as when the budget is spent, since a reply that skipped the step would read as an opponent who held back that play or passed its turn ([[R4809000]]). The lethal solver searches depth-first on a small slice of its nodes, then best-first on the rest; a step that slice cannot pay for leaves the lethal to the second search instead of ending the solver's search, whether the step is on the world it searches or on another world it proves the line on ([[R4807000]]). A search's step never plays the autopilot's turn (above): one that did, an End turn whose next draw cast Cognitive Offloading, cost a decision what hundreds of ordinary steps do. The browser adds a wall-clock cap, `PRACTICE_AI_CLOCK_MS` (3 s), to every practice game, one on a server seed included, since the server folds a practice claim without re-running its AI moves ([[R2560000]]). Every host with a clock (the browser's practice AI and dungeon fights, the ladder bots' `BOT_DECISION_DEADLINE_MS` and the Duos AI team's) also gives the search a wrap-up at `AI_WRAP_UP_SHARE` (60%) of its limit: past it the beam explores no further once it has tried every move at its root ([[R4801000]]) and its open lines end their turn, while the finalists' replies and their scores on the other sampled worlds run on to the cap, so a slow device's decision still weighs the reply and every world; a decision that still left a finalist unscored on a sampled world reports it (`SearchStats.replied`, `worldsScored`), and with no clock nothing changes ([[R3498000]]). The beam simulates its root's End turn before the root's other moves and files that line in its own place, so a root move that draws every node the decision has left still leaves it a line ([[R5044000]]). A beam its budget cut short closes only the lines it had not finished expanding, ranks a line whose turn it could not end as a passed one, less its unspent crystals, and ends the turn of each such line it shortlists on the decision's own nodes before the reply, so no line the cut left mid-turn is scored unreplied beside the replied ones; a beam its budget never cut decides as before ([[R4791000]]). The quality gates and the shadow-ban sweep play at the browser's budget, and one decision at that budget takes under 1.5 s on the development machine. The timing gate reads the clock against a yardstick, a fixed piece of engine work timed just before each decision, and converts the ratio to the development machine's milliseconds, so a busy or slower machine slows both and fails nothing. It also times three pinned positions, decisions found slow (two by the improve screen of 2026-10-16, one whose search ended the turn into the other side's Cognitive Offloading), each under the tighter `AI_GATE.pinned_decision_ms`. A fat state's allowance is scaled from at most 600 nodes, so it costs what it did ([[R2575000]]). A simulated step also pays a node for every hundred events it emitted past a thousand ([[R4595003]]), and a face-down expensive card's reveal pays the expensive surcharge up front, as its Play does ([[R4595004]]). Its floors, 150 nodes and 60 of them the lethal solver's, hold up only a fat state's scaled allowance and never raise it past the budget's own figures, so a budget that gives the solver fewer nodes keeps to them on every board ([[R4802000]]). There the lethal solver's walk runs on past its scaled share, up to the budget's own, only while it stands at a position whose ready attacks already reach lethal or proves a line that won on the first sampled world, and what it spends that way is added to the decision, which never passes the budget's own nodes ([[R4788000]]).

The greedy baseline simulates each candidate once, on nodes of its own that pay what a costly step is charged, a Zephrys play's among them ([[R4799000]]). `cargo jackioh gate` plays the first 20 games of each. Only wins count; a draw at the turn cap is reported beside them. The brief asked for 95%, 70% and 80% wins; Hard against Easy's floor is 75% instead, which the user set when a change to the search ([[R4132000]]) left that smoke run one game short of the brief's 16 of 20 ([[R4132001]]). Against greedy that now clears the brief's 70% ([[R4513000]]). **Proposed, pending the user's acceptance:** a run of n games needs the brief's share of them, or the count that an AI as strong as measured reaches in 95% of runs of n games, whichever is lower (`gateNeeded`). That asks for 95 of 100 and 19 of 20 wins against random, 35 of 50 and 14 of 20 against greedy, and the floor's 38 of 50 and 15 of 20 for Hard against Easy, where the brief's 80% asked for 40 and 16 ([[R4132001]]). An AI at the measured rates fails any one of these runs by chance at most once in twenty, and one of the three about once in thirteen (once in eighteen in the smoke run). - **Decks.** An AI deck is a curve- and tag-aware random draw of distinct non-token cards of every set, minus the shadow ban ([[R186]]). The training lanes' arena and promotion gate deal both seats by that one draw, each minus its own AI's shadow ban at Easy's resources, and weight neither by the unban lane's dealt-quality table, so a promotion's decks are drawn as practice's and the quality gates' are ([[R976000]]). A lane's promotion gate is a sequential test on its paired hold-out deals, H0 an edge of 0 against H1 +0.06 with a 5% chance of either error, which stops as soon as its evidence is clear, between 100 and 1,000 deals ([[R1679000]]). The draw's units' floor (`AI_DECK.minUnitShare`) and a theme's (`AI_DECK.themeMinShare`) are hard floors, kept together whenever the cards left can meet both, the theme's first when they cannot ([[R4784000]]). The draw may lean on a set: at least half the deck from it (`AI_DECK.leanMinShare`, a hard floor beside the units' and the theme's, all three kept together), the rest dealt as without it ([[R1370]]). Every deck the draw deals holds at most two Quickdraw cards (`AI_DECK.maxQuickdraw`), the cards it is forced to include counted, and Quickdraw is never its theme ([[R817000]]). "More cards from" leans a player's random deck, All Random's or practice's, on the set they pick: the newest set that ships ([[R1371]]) or any set that ships ([[R924000]]); the AI's own decks do not lean. The AI's random decks hold no card of a set behind its soft gate (`AI_DECK_GATED_SETS`), whatever ships; a player's random deck is dealt from every set, and a lean on a gated set leans on the newest set that ships and is not gated ([[R1390]]). The gate held the Meditative set until the AI was trained on its cards (generation 9, issue #732), held the Stinkyscape set from its release (issue #1884) until issue #1885's sweep measured the AI on it and emptied the list, and held the Stinkyscape+ set from its release (issue #3573) until fullsend 2's sweep of record measured the AI on it and emptied the list again ([[R3574000]]). The shadow ban holds Meditative, Meditative+, Stinkyscape, Training Grounds and Stinkyscape+ cards only, at most each set's share of its non-token cards (its row of the set table, [[R3114001]]): half of each Meditative set and of Training Grounds and a fifth of Stinkyscape's and of Stinkyscape+'s: no card of an earlier set is banned again ([[R732000]], [[R1186000]], [[R1885000]], [[R2203000]], [[R3535000]]). A sweep of record runs again after every push to `main` that moves the AI's generation, over the bans and the watched cards of the sets that may hold them, and after every one that ships a set, over that set's cards ([[R1678000]]). A change that retires or replaces part of what the generation names (its evaluation or its search) moves that generation by hand, under an issue and a ruling of its own, as the retirement of the learned evaluation did (generation 14, lane `hand`, [[R4921000]]); an ordinary fix to the AI keeps the generation. The shadow-ban sweep judges every card at the Easy and the Hard handicap, because the ban holds at every tier and a tier's mana decides what the AI can do with a card. Since patch v0.2.0 it runs in two passes and leans toward the cards at risk of a ban ([[R390]]): pass 1 forces each non-token card of every set that ships into AI decks as before; a card is at risk when a pass's numbers meet a flag's condition at half strength, or it is banned or on the watch list (`SHADOW_WATCH`, the cards that were at risk and cleared, which the next sweep counts as at risk from the start); pass 2 sweeps only the at-risk cards, with more games each (`seedsPerCardAtRisk`, 24), and in every pass-2 game the filler draw multiplies at-risk cards' weights (`atRiskBoost`, 4) and lifts the ban for those banned as `neverPlayed`, `selfHarm` or `selfKill`, never for `error` or `timeout`. A ban for `neverPlayed`, `selfHarm` or `selfKill` needs pass-2 evidence: 6 playable turns with no play (turns on which the rules let the AI play the card, not merely ones it had the mana for, [[R2425000]]), 8 plays, or 4 plays that lost the AI the game, at that tier, a card's numbers adding up over every game it was dealt in, forced or as filler; `error` and `timeout` ban as before, only the game's forced card, and are listed against at-risk filler of that game as suspects. A `timeout` is a decision slower than `AI_SWEEP.decisionMs` (2 s) or a game past 600 actions; a decision the sweep's clock finds slow is timed again as the timing gate times one, against the yardstick, and counts only if it is still over `AI_SWEEP.decisionMs` × `retimeMargin` (2.5 s), and the sweep reports the clock's count beside it ([[R977000]], [[R5248000]]); a game past 600 actions is counted apart (`cappedGames`) and named as such in the reason, and a pass-2 row shows only the flags its own tier raised ([[R5248000]]). A play that ends the game is left out of a card's mean evaluation change, which `evaluate`'s score for a finished game would decide alone, and counted as a win or a loss instead ([[R390]]). Every card is swept on both its faces, since live games deal and turn cards Radiant and a face the sweep never played could throw games unseen: each pass plays the card's ordinary face as before, then its Radiant face, the forced card dealt Radiant through its seat's deck mark ([[R2702000]]) on seeds of its own (`sweep:<tier>:<id>:radiant:<n>`, `sweep2:<tier>:<id>:radiant:<n>`) with as many games as the ordinary face, so a sweep plays twice the games (splitting the seeds would change the ordinary face's numbers). The faces are judged apart, every flag over that face's own games, a filler card's numbers counting for its ordinary face since only the forced card is dealt Radiant; either face's numbers put a card at risk, pass 2 then sweeps both, and a flag on either face bans the card, its reason naming the face when it is the Radiant one (`error: easy (Radiant): …`). Pass 1's at-risk list is a pure function of pass 1's results and pass 2's seeds are named, so a sweep of record replays. The sweep never rewards the search for playing a card, which would measure another AI than the one that plays, and the ban's scope is unchanged: AI deck-building only.

- **The lethal hint.** In every game against the AI, at any tier, in a sandbox game and in a tutorial lesson ([[§9.10]]), the board rings the enemy hero and End turn and says "You have lethal" while the human has a sure win this turn, never how ([[R2102000]], [[R2291000]]). The check is the AI's lethal solver run for the human's seat on the human's own redaction ([[R185]]), so it never reads the AI's hand, library or face-down cards; a sampled trap that stops the line keeps it dark. It never shows online (a Duos game against the AI team included, which the server plays), in a hotseat game or on a replay, and the Lethal hint setting turns it off.

The human's deck is one of their saved decks (an active account's, complete ones only), a fresh random deck (with "More cards from" beside it, [[R1373]], [[R924000]]), or one of three named practice decks: Human Vanguard, Blitz and Fortress, each hand-built from Core cards outside the AI's shadow ban. A saved deck is offered only when it is complete and [[§9.4]]'s validator finds no fault in it under L2, L3 and L6, against the catalog the game is created with and with nothing banned; what the player owns (L5) never holds one back. Any other saved deck is listed disabled, with the validator's reason and a link to Decks, and the worker's `createGame` still judges every deck it is sent ([[§2.6]]). Before the game starts the setup shows the chosen deck's cards and mana curve, read from the catalog the worker sends. The AI's turns are paced for the player to follow: each step waits while the board still animates the last one, while the page marks the card the AI just played as held up (`data-showcase`) and while it marks a voice line as playing (`data-speaking`), each capped so a mark nobody clears cannot stop the game; a dungeon fight's AI is paced the same way ([[§15]]). A practice game in progress survives a reload or a closed tab, as a live match survives a server restart by folding `(seed, log)`: after every answer the worker keeps the game on the device (its setup, its log and the AI's place in its own random stream), the page remembers only the setup it chose, and the next visit to `/practice` asks the worker to fold the log back and goes on from the same state, the AI included; a game that will not fold the same way, on another catalog version for one, is dropped and the setup shows. The save stays with the worker, because its log names the AI's hidden cards. Leaving a game through New game or the menu asks whether to Save and leave or Leave without saving: a game saved that way is not picked up by the next visit, which shows the setup with a banner at its top that resumes it, and starting another game from there ends it ([[R765]]). A finished game is not kept, and a link that starts a game starts it in place of the saved one. A tutorial lesson is not kept, since its coach follows the game from the deal ([[§9.10]]), so while a lesson is in progress the page asks before a reload or a closed tab ends it ([[R668]]).

**The sandbox.** The setup's Sandbox box starts a practice game with dev tools beside the board, for trying a card or a line on a board built by hand ([[R923000]]). A tool is one engine action, `sandbox`, sent as the human's intent, logged and folded like any other, and accepted only by a game created as a sandbox: it spawns any card or token of a set that ships, for either seat, into the hand, a free zone of the field its type stands in, the graveyard or the top of the library, placed by the engine's own verbs and never played ([[R923001]]); sets a hero's health and Armor or a player's mana and draws, within the bounds of the engine's config ([[R923002]]); and destroys a card on the field, its Death run, or removes any card the human can see to exile ([[R923003]]). The tools work on either turn, for either seat, but wait on the mulligan and an open prompt; `legalActions` never lists one, so the AI never takes one, and the panel can hold the AI's next step while the board is arranged. A sandbox game counts for nothing: no statistics, no last board brought or left, and a server match refuses the action.

**Today's deal.** The setup's Play today's deal starts a free game on the deal of the UTC day it is clicked in, which changes at midnight UTC ([[R2882000]]): the seed `daily-` and the day (`daily-2026-11-03`), the human in p1 on an even UTC day counted from 1970-01-01 and in p2 on an odd one, so the seat alternates every day, month ends included, and a fresh random deck leaning on the setup's "More cards from" pick ([[R1373]], [[R924000]]) at the setup's difficulty, whatever deck the picker shows; the Sandbox box does not apply.
