### 9.12 Ranked ladder

Every player has a hidden skill rating to climb with and a visible Grape tier to show for it, and the top 100 compete on a numbered global leaderboard ([[R603]]–[[R612]]). A season holds two ladders, each with all of these of its own: **Constructed**, which the Best of 1 and Conquest queues play for, and **All Random** ([[R2778000]]).

- **The hidden rating.** Each player carries a hidden Glicko-2 rating — rating, deviation and volatility — starting at a new player's values. One rated game is one rating period for both sides, each rated against the other's rating from before the game, so the order of computation changes nothing. Draws, offered or by the turn cap, score 0.5 for both; concedes and disconnects are losses like any other ending. A `fled` game is a win for the fleer and a draw for the other side, each side scored by its own result (1 and 0.5) against the other's rating from before the game, its pips moved by its own result and its record counting a win and a draw; a voted-out game is the removed player's loss ([[R1843004]]). The update is deterministic: the same inputs give the same outputs on every machine ([[R603]]).
- **What rates.** A ranked game moves only its own mode's ladder: Best of 1 and Conquest the Constructed one, All Random its own; each ladder rates from its own hidden rating and ranks against its own season rows, placements, floors, peaks, streaks and Jlorious, and a player new to one ladder starts it as a new player whatever they hold on the other ([[R2778000]]). Only the ranked match type moves a rating or a rank: a match the queue paired, or a rematch of a ranked match ([[R672]]). A room challenge is unranked and records both ratings unchanged. A game of a Conquest series is recorded but not rated; a ranked series moves the rating once, as one game between its two sides, when it ends ([[R604]]).
- **The visible tiers.** A player who has not finished the season's placement games is shown as Raisin, with how many placements they have played. Past them, the ladder is one integer per player — pips above the bottom of Rotten Grape III — read as Rotten Grape, Normal Grape, Large Grape, Golden Grape and Mythic Grape, each of three divisions (III at the bottom, I at the top) of pips per division. The tiers below Jlorious target the Grape drop rates as percentiles of the season's placed people, with no ladder bot among them ([[R640000]]) — Rotten 12%, Normal 60%, Large 20%, Golden 7%, Mythic 1% — and the boundaries are config values, tuned without code changes ([[R605]]).
- **How pips move.** A win gives pips and a loss takes pips; a draw moves nothing. The move leans gently toward the hidden rating: a game played a division or more away from the rank the rating calls for leans one pip extra toward it when the game's own move already heads that way — a win below the called-for rank or a loss above it — so the visible rank converges on true skill without big point swings. Wins in a row earn bonus pips below Mythic Grape ([[R606]]).
- **Floors and peaks.** A player cannot drop below the Grape tier they have reached this season, though divisions inside a tier can still drop. Each season's best — the highest ladder reached, or the best Jlorious position held — is kept and shown as a badge on the profile ([[R607]]).
- **Jlorious.** The final tier is not a ladder position but the top 100 placed Mythic Grape players by hidden rating, shown as a numbered leaderboard position (#1–#100) instead of divisions. When fewer than 100 Mythic players qualify, every one of them is Jlorious. A player who falls out of the top 100 is Mythic Grape again, where the tier floor holds them ([[R608]]). Jlorious is people's alone: a ladder bot whose rating would qualify stays Mythic Grape ([[R640000]]). On a strict server a dev account's hand-set rating is out of the percentiles, Jlorious and the public leaderboard until it is reset ([[R3074001]]). A banned account's standing is left off the leaderboard and out of the percentiles and Jlorious on every server, as a ladder bot's is out of the last two, until the ban is lifted ([[R3290000]]).
- **Seasons.** A new season opens on each minor version bump (v0.2 → v0.3), named by the minor version. Opening one soft-resets every rated player's hidden rating on each ladder, a rated game on either making a rated player of both ([[R2778000]]): pulled partway toward that ladder's players' mean, deviation widened and capped at a new player's, volatility kept. Everyone starts the season back in Raisin placements; the first season resets nothing ([[R609]]).
- **Bots.** Each AI bot keeps its own internally maintained Glicko-2 rating on each ladder, rated like a player's when a rated game seats one ([[R610]], [[R2778000]]), and after an AI promotion its deviation is widened before its next rated game, so its rating re-settles on the new AI ([[R1560000]]). A ranked match seats one only through the empty-queue fallback, a rematch of a match it played or, while the top bots grind, the empty-queue kickoff of a match between two bots, and a ranked Conquest series only through the fallback ([[§9.5]], [[R1453]], [[R639000]], [[R641000]], [[R1555000]]): a ladder bot, a profile of the server-only kind `bot` that the server plays itself. At each door [[R1989000]]'s coin seats it, so it starts from the first seat as often as the second. It plays on this spec's resources with no handicap, through the practice AI's search at one of five strengths, each with a skill, the share of its decisions that follow the search at all, the rest being a beginner's move drawn from a one-ply reading of what it sees ([[R2214000]], [[R4216000]]), with a measured Elo for the skill ([[R747000]]), the strengths measured against the strongest by an offline calibration, run again after each AI promotion, and a player rated above every bot is given none but a grinder ([[R748000]], [[R1555000]]), at a person's pace inside its clock, with an emote persona of its own; it has no socket and so no disconnect grace, and its match replays from `(seed, log)` like any other ([[R1452]]). It concedes only facing a lethal showing on the board that it cannot stop ([[R2660000]], [[R4796000]]), and may offer a draw while it trails clearly, at a hand-set chance ([[R2660001]]). It answers a draw offer, lets an occasional turn run out, shows its aim before a targeted move and, once a ranked game ends, a series game aside, stays a while and may offer or meet a rematch, each at the rates and the pace fitted from people's play ([[R639000]]). The pool holds as many active bots as the people who finished a match in the last week, between 40 and 400, each on 12 to 18 hours a day, so a player grinding the ladder always finds one ([[R2778001]]), retires and reinstates bots as that number moves, and gives each a name, an account age, a schedule and two or three Best-of-1 decks ([[R1454]]), and a Conquest trio, three decks sharing no card, from which it picks a deck before each game of a series, by the matchup and at a person's pace ([[R641000]]). It wears cosmetics drawn as a person's: a wardrobe from its seed, kept off the ledger, of the starters and what enough people have owned long enough, at the rates people of its account age own each, and picks drawn from it at the rates people wear them, fitted from people's matches ([[R1834005]], [[R1834006]]). While the season's third-highest placed person is more than `BOT_GRIND_GAP` above every bot, the top three bots grind: they are on 20 hours a day, may meet a player above every bot, play each other while the queue is empty, and step up a strength every ten rated wins, their ratings still their games' alone ([[R1555000]]). A bot holds a season row that its ranked games move as a person's games move theirs, and nothing a client reads labels a bot or tells its seat from a person's: the leaderboard and the match screen show that row as a player's, never in Jlorious ([[R610]], [[R1452]], [[R640000]]). The Terms say so, and say what a bot looks like and does ([[R2614000]]). Practice stays in the browser and unrated ([[R187]]).
- **Rated-game records.** Every rated game is recorded with the ladder it moved ([[R2778000]]), the patch and build version, the pilot per player, the result, and the rating before and after for both sides, with each side's visible rank before and after alongside ([[R611]]).
- **Reads.** Each read names its ladder: its own rank and the leaderboard on the ladder asked for, Constructed when none is, and the match screen on the match's own; the profile shows both ladders, the Play online lobby the ladder of the mode picked, and the leaderboard switches between them ([[R2778000]]). The client reads its own rank and win streak, the leaderboard, and both seats' ranks and win streaks on the match screen, with each seat's rank before and after the game once a ranked match's rated record is written (a ranked series' on its last game), which the result panel shows as the seat's move and the match bar as its new rank ([[R2388000]]); the profile shows its tier, division and pips with its season badges and, from the season's first ranked game, its win–loss–draw record for the season, labeled apart from the all-time record ([[R2255000]]), and the Play online lobby shows the same rank with a link to the leaderboard ([[R661]]). While a player's ranked win streak is `RANK_STREAK_FLAME_LENGTH` (3) or more, a flame with its count shows beside their rank on the profile and on their seat of a ranked match's line, and nowhere else ([[R2101000]]). No read carries the hidden rating, not even to its owner ([[R612]]), but the dev dashboard's, a dev account's alone, which reads its own and the whole ladder with each row marked person or bot ([[R3074002]]). Each player a read shows, its own caller included, comes with their `profileId` and `username`; the old `tag`, a hash of the profile id shown as a name, is gone ([[R1436]]).
