### 9.5 Matchmaking and lifecycle

- Direct challenge by room code (6 characters from the invite-code alphabet) ships before the ranked queue and is the primary mode while the player base is small. Enqueue asserts the account is active and not in a match, a series or a live co-op match ([[R1065000]]), validates the chosen deck or trio ([[§9.4]]), freezes it into the ticket and returns the ticket id; a ticket that stops waiting (left, unheard from, or cleared when its player enters a match another way) is marked cancelled, and the hourly retention purge deletes it once it was enqueued more than `QUEUE_TICKET_CANCELLED_KEEP_SECONDS` (1 hour) ago, a claimed ticket staying since its match reads its mode from it ([[R4446000]]). Pairing runs on enqueue plus a sweeper every few seconds; the window widens ±50 rating every 10 s from ±100 and stops at `MAX_FAIR_GAP` (±400, its width at 60 s), so two people further apart than that never pair, however long they wait ([[R640001]]); a ticket carries its player's rating on the ladder its mode plays for, which the window and the bot fallback compare ([[R2778000]]); both tickets are claimed in one atomic statement. A queue pair's seats come from [[R1989000]]'s coin on the match seed, so the same seed always seats the same way. A match seat is claimed the same way: the first writer wins and every other starter backs out through its own failed-start cleanup, so no queue pair, room join, rematch or series game seats a player twice ([[R890000]]). Since [[R1523000]] the guard is one seat ledger in the server, which every door into a 1v1 match, a series or a co-op match reserves all its seats through in one step, the flag staying the record and a second guard; a claim a newer server's epoch has fenced during a deploy writes nothing and answers 503. The client shows the queue's readout instead of an endless spinner: each mode's expected wait and the games played in the last hour, every number one the server counted ([[R640002]]), kept a few seconds so every waiting client's poll shares one count ([[R1996000]]); the lobby reads it when it opens and then every `QUEUE_POPULATION_POLL_SECONDS` (30 s), while its account read keeps the ticket alive every `SERIES_POLL_SECONDS` ([[R3207000]]). A queued player may leave the lobby while they wait: a pairing takes them to the game from wherever they are in the client, and while their match or series is live the main menu and the practice menu show a banner with the way back to it ([[R765]]), read again every `LIVE_GAME_POLL_SECONDS` (10 s) ([[R3207001]]). A ticket whose player has gone quiet is canceled before it can pair: every signed-in request refreshes the caller's presence, and a sweep cancels a ticket unheard from for longer than `QUEUE_TICKET_STALE_MS`, so an absent player is never seated and never charged ([[R897000]]). A ticket that waited through a card patch ends the same way: the sweep cancels each open ticket stamped with another catalog version than the running one, in any mode and before the listening window has passed, and the player queues again on today's cards ([[R5159000]]).
- Empty-queue fallback. While the server's `LADDER_BOTS` is `on`, a ticket of any mode that has waited past its own fallback time, 15–40 s drawn from its id and a key no client holds, pairs with a ladder bot, an opponent the server plays itself ([[§9.12]], [[R1452]]). Each sweep pairs every two people it can first, so a person inside the window always comes before a bot, and a bot before a person further away than `MAX_FAIR_GAP` ([[R640001]]). The terms of service's "AI opponents" section says that ranked may match a player with an AI opponent when no one near their rank is available, and that nothing in the game marks which opponents are AI, and names no seat ([[R2614000]], [[R3110000]]). The bot is the free one on its schedule closest to the player's rating, never one of the player's last five opponents, and a player rated above every bot gets none but a grinder within `MAX_FAIR_GAP` of them ([[R1555000]]). The ticket and the bot are claimed together, as a pair of tickets is, and [[R1989000]]'s coin on the match seed seats them; whoever takes Player 1 takes the first turn, and the match is ranked ([[R1453]]). A Conquest ticket's bot holds a trio the pool dealt it, and the two play a ranked series, [[R1989000]]'s coin winner as series Player 1: the bot makes each sealed pick a person's delay after the pick phase opens, by a simple rule from the matchup, the series is rated once when it ends, and until it does nothing else seats the bot ([[R641000]]). While the top bots grind, a sweep that finds no ticket queued in any mode kicks off one ranked Best-of-1 match between the lowest-rated free grinder and the free bot closest in rating to it, at most one live and five minutes after the last ended, which the two play out alone, rated as any bot's game is; whoever queues meanwhile is paired as before ([[R1555000]]).
- The turn clock (75 s) ends a stalled turn. Each prompt held by the non-active player has its own 30 s clock, the next one for the same player included ([[R4848000]]). The two mulligans share one 45 s clock, which a player's answer neither stops nor extends for the other ([[R268]]). Disconnect grace (60 s) and concede end the match as a loss, and the hard wall-clock ceiling (120 minutes since patch v0.2.0, [[R389]]) ends it as a draw through the `ceilingReached` action. In the client every way off a live 1v1 board asks first and names the grace in seconds, and once the match has a result it goes at once ([[R2479000]]). Every ending records a result and clears both players' in-match state, in one write: one the store fails is tried again, and one that never lands leaves the match live for the next socket's rebuild to record ([[R1437]]). The write clears a seat's in-match flag only while the flag still names the ending match, so a late result never takes a player out of a newer one ([[R1896000]]). A clock's action the store fails to write is tried again a second later until it lands, and an actor its log has moved past lets go for the next socket to rebuild it from the log ([[R776000]]). A live action whose `reduce` panics changes nothing and is answered `internal` and filed for the night bot, and a clock's action that does ends the match at once as the reaper's ceiling draw ([[R1253000]]). A reaper resolves anything past the ceiling, recording the result a match's log already holds and drawing only one that does not end ([[R833000]], [[R112]]), and deletes a room or a reserved match id still open five minutes past its expiry or its claim, which frees its code and both players ([[R1450]]). Ranked games move the hidden Glicko-2 rating ([[§9.12]], [[R603]]). The clocks, the ceiling, the grace window and the room codes are server config values ([[R79]]).
- Failing sweeps. Each background sweep (the matchmaker, the reaper, its open-room reap and its seat reconcile, the series, co-op and challenge sweepers, and the retention purge's steps) logs a failed pass and tries again on the next, and a step that has failed on every pass for at least `LOOP_FAILING_ALERT_MS` and at least `LOOP_FAILING_ALERT_PASSES` passes in a row raises one alert named for the step, `matchmaker.failing`, `matches.reaper.failing` and so on, and logs `<step>.recovered` on its next success. A reaper pass in which any match failed to reap counts as failed, and a co-op sweep in which a match would not end likewise. Operational, not a rule: it changes nothing a player can see.
- Failing routes. A route, or one of the four socket upgrades, that answers an internal error to at least `ROUTE_FAILING_ALERT_COUNT` requests in a row over at least `ROUTE_FAILING_ALERT_MS`, with no other answer between (a 4xx refusal is an answer), raises one alert, `api.route.failing`, naming the route's method and pattern, never a path with ids, and logs `api.route.recovered` on its next answer. Operational, not a rule: it changes nothing a player can see.
- Reconnect gets a fresh full view, never a log replay. Repeated `hello` frames while one is still queued collapse into it ([[R835001]]), and one of the same socket sooner than `WATCH_HELLO_INTERVAL_MS` after its last answered one is dropped, while the first hello of a new socket is always answered at once ([[R2686000]]). The clock keeps running while a player is disconnected, and the grace countdown is stored on the match so both clients show it. A crashed actor rebuilds its state by folding `(seed, log)`, from its checkpoint when one passes ([[§9.3]]). A seat whose socket has not attached when the first socket reaches its match's actor (after a restart, or a player who never opens the match) is disconnected from then, and keeps a grace deadline the restart left stored ([[R744]]). A match socket that goes quiet is found rather than waited for: the server pings it every 15 s and drops it after 45 s with no frame of any kind, which is a disconnect and starts the grace, and closes one whose outgoing queue overflows; a page that wakes asks a socket it has not heard from for a ping interval for a full view and replaces it if none comes ([[R1441]]). A match socket refused because its sign-in did not verify (4401) opens once more when the page holds a different token, a renewed one; the 4401 stays final for the token it refused, as 4403 and 4404 are final for the match, so nothing loops ([[R1115000]]). A second socket of one account for a seat it already holds (another tab, or another device) takes the seat, and the one it replaced is closed with 4412 and does not reconnect: its page says the game is open elsewhere and offers Play here, which takes the seat back ([[R3767000]]); a watcher's replacement is unchanged.
- Modes. The queue and the room challenge offer three modes, and a ticket pairs only with a ticket of the same mode ([[R257]]): **Best of 1**, one saved deck; **Conquest**, a trio (below; its queue name is still `bo3`, [[R330]]); **All Random**, both players dealt a deck by the weighted random deck-builder [[§9.9]] names, with no saved deck needed ([[R258]]); each player may pick "More cards from" a set for their own deck, the newest set that ships or any set that ships, at least half of it from that set, sent as intent with the queue, room or rematch request and dealt by the server ([[R1372]], [[R924000]]), and offered beside every random deck a player can start a game on, a friend's All Random challenge, sent or accepted, among them ([[R2031000]]). A game with a friend starts in a private lobby (below); a friend's challenge opens a room, which its friend joins at once in its host's mode ([[R264]], [[R1831000]]), and a room an older client made, or an old link names, still joins so ([[R3730006]]). The host and the joiner are seated by [[R1989000]]'s coin on the match or series seed, which the join's answer names. Its host may cancel a room nobody has joined, which frees its code at once ([[R110]]) and never touches a room someone joined; a room a friend's challenge opened is canceled with its challenge ([[R1075000]]).
- Private lobbies. A friend game, 1v1 in a room mode, co-op against the AI team or a 2v2 between two teams of two people ([[§13.9]]), waits in a private lobby at `/lobby/:code` until its host starts it ([[R3730000]], [[R3731000]]). It has a code as a room does, two seats (a co-op lobby's other two the AI team's; a 2v2's four, in two teams, each member picking a team), each showing its player, presence, the host's crown and its deck state, and an empty seat invites a friend by a live toast ([[R3730005]]). A friend's row on Play together offers Lobby, which makes a Best of 1 lobby, invites the friend into it and opens it, one press at a time; a refused invite still opens the lobby, whose screen says the refusal once ([[R3874000]]). Each member picks their own deck, trio or random deck, checked and frozen as the queue's, and presses Ready ([[R3730001]]); the host's settings, the 1v1 mode or the co-op difficulty, are everyone's to see, and a change takes every Ready off ([[R3730003]]). The host's Start needs every seat filled and ready, checks the catalog and both seats again, and starts the game as a room's join does, unranked; an unstarted lobby whose catalog is older moves onto the running catalog, picks and Readies off, when first read after the patch ([[R4072000]]); the clients count `LOBBY_START_COUNTDOWN_SECONDS` (3) down to the board ([[R3730002]]). A member holds no seat until then ([[R1523000]]). A guest leaves or is removed, the host leaving closes it, an idle lobby closes after its format's room TTL from its last change, a host holds `MAX_OPEN_ROOMS_PER_HOST` of each format, and a reload finds it again ([[R3730004]]). Every change reaches its members over `/ws/social` ([[R3214000]]). Before any lobby has been shown, a non-404 read failure takes its place with the failure card; after one has been shown, a non-404 re-read failure keeps it mounted and schedules one more read after `LOBBY_POLL_MS`, even while the social socket is live, while a 404 still closes it ([[R3792000]], [[R4021000]]). A room's link, a co-op room's, chat's Join and a friend's "Open in lobby" open it ([[R767]], [[R2094000]], [[R3730006]]).
- The Play hub and the inbox. `/play` holds every way to play as a card in one of four groups, Online (Best of 1, Conquest and All random, each showing its ladder's rank), Daily, With friends (the private lobby and a challenge) and Solo (practice, the dungeon run and the tutorial), each saying what it is, how long a game takes and what it needs; an Online card opens one panel with the deck or trio and Find a match, on the last mode and deck used ([[R3729000]]). The queue search is the shell's banner on every menu screen, `/play` included, with the mode, a clock, the expected wait and Cancel, and a match found names the opponent and their rank ([[R3729001]]). A bell in the top bar, with a badge, opens one inbox of what waits on the player: live challenges with their time left, lobby invites, daily games on the player's move, daily challenges, friend requests and replays a friend shared, each answerable inline, built from what the client already reads; a challenge's toast can be sent away with Later and the challenge stays in the inbox ([[R3729002]]). Below what waits, the inbox's Recent section lists the account's notification feed ([[§9.4]]), newest first with how long ago and a link to its screen, unread rows marked, an older page at a time; opening the inbox marks what it shows read, and the bell gets a dot, never a count, for unread rows while nothing waits. A row that arrives while the app is open is also one toast at a time, none for a kind with its own toast and none over a board until it closes; while the tab is hidden its title starts with the bell's count, the boards that title themselves excepted; and a player who turns them on in the notification settings gets the browser's own notifications for a daily move, a daily challenge or its acceptance, a lobby invite and a friend request while a tab of the site is open and hidden, which needs no push service ([[R4039001]]).
- Portraits and emotes. A match's two seats are shown as hero portraits ([[R641]]), each fixed when the seat is fixed — a queued deck's own, an All Random seat's dealt at random, a Conquest game's the picked deck's — and sent to both clients on join and on reconnect in a `portraits` message ([[R642]]). Each seat's loadout of cosmetics, frozen as the match starts, rides the same way in a `loadouts` message, to a watcher too, and a card's own picks in a `cardLoadouts` message once a view has shown that card, none of it in `PlayerView` ([[R1834002]], [[R1834003]]). The emotes of [[§10.10]] are a pool of twenty-four — five voice lines in each portrait's own voice and nineteen shared emoji ([[R1340]]) — of which each seat is dealt a hand of eight each game, three lines and five emoji, from the match seed on a stream of its own (`{seed}:emotes:{seat}`), so the same eight come back on a reconnect, a restart and a replay, and no game state sees them ([[R1341]]). The `portraits` message carries the receiving account's own hand, never the opponent's and never `PlayerView` ([[R1342]]). In a match a player may send an emote of their hand through a top-level `emote` message that never enters `ActionBody`, the action log, the replay hash or a game record; the actor relays it to the opponent alone, refuses one outside the sender's hand silently and before the limit ([[R1342]]), drops it silently past the shared rate limit, and answers an unknown id as `malformed` ([[R643]]); between two players either of whom blocks the other it relays nothing, after both checks ([[R1351000]]).
- Rematch. After a match that is not a game of a Conquest series ends, either seat may offer a rematch: a normal one, or double-or-nothing ([[R672]]). An offer is taken only through `REMATCH_OFFER_TTL_MS` after the finished match's end, a later one lapsing silently and recording nothing ([[R2685000]]), and a new match is created only when both seats have offered with equal stakes within that window; mismatched stakes create nothing. The rematch swaps the finished match's seats — the previous Player 2 taking Player 1 — and replays its frozen decks under a fresh seed (an All Random rematch deals fresh decks from that seed, each leaning on the set its seat's offer picked, if any, [[R258]], [[R1372]], [[R924000]]), and it is ranked exactly when the finished match was. A match played on a catalog version other than the server's, a card patch having gone live since it began, offers no rematch: every offer on it, a ladder bot's too, is refused with `update_required`, telling the players the cards changed and to start a new game ([[R1638000]]). Either seat sees the offer buttons only while the opponent is still present — presence is the opponent's match socket being open, so the buttons disappear when they leave or log out. The result screen says "Your opponent has left." in their place, unless its own reason is the opponent's disconnect, which already says so ([[R3791000]]). A seat may reopen its finished match's socket only within `REMATCH_OFFER_TTL_MS` of the match's end, and never once the retention purge has taken its log; after that the game is watched from the replays list ([[R1285000]]). Once that window has passed, the server closes the finished match's sockets still open, its seats' and its watchers', with a close no client reconnects on, and lets the match go from memory; the client keeps the result with no offer buttons and reads the rematch status once more, polling it no longer ([[R3694000]]). A ladder bot has no socket: after a ranked game it reads as present for a linger it draws, as a person still on the death screen does, and may offer a rematch or meet one in that time ([[R639000]]). A game of a series offers no rematch: the Conquest continue flow owns what comes next. Every screen that shows a match's rematch status reads it through one poll for that match in the tab ([[R3207002]]), and while the finished match's socket is open the server pushes each seat its own status over it instead ([[R3213000]]).
- The opponent's aim. While a player aims a play, an Activate or an attack, by drag or by click-select, the opponent sees Hearthstone's targeting arrow from its source to the target it is over, gone when the aim ends. It travels in a top-level `aim` message that is never an `ActionBody` or part of `PlayerView` and decides no rule; its ends are public handles only — a hero, a zone of the field, a hand card's position — so a hand card's arrow starts at its back and a face-down card is only ever its zone. The actor shape-checks it, relays it to the opponent alone, coalesces it to one relay per `AIM_RELAY_INTERVAL_MS`, and drops one that names anything the opponent may not see ([[R738]]). A ladder bot's targeted moves show its aim the same way, through the same relay, a beat before the move ([[R639000]]).
- The opponent's choice. While a player answers a card picker whose count the other seat is shown ([[R2739000]]), the opponent sees its options as card backs and the one under the chooser's pointer or keyboard focus outlined in the chooser's color. The hover travels in a top-level `choiceHover` message, an option's place or `null`, sent only when it changes, which is never an `ActionBody` or part of `PlayerView` and decides no rule, and which says only which back the pointer is over, never what is under it. The actor shape-checks it, relays it to the opponent alone, coalesces it to one relay per `AIM_RELAY_INTERVAL_MS`, drops one while no prompt of the sender's is open, while the opponent is shown no count or past the count, and relays a `null` when the prompt closes, the sender's socket closes or the game ends ([[R2739001]]).
- Conquest. A player takes the series by winning one game with each of the three decks of the trio frozen when they queued: `SERIES_WINS_NEEDED` (3) game wins, one per deck, because a deck that wins is locked for the rest of the series, while a deck that lost or drew may be picked again ([[R330]]). Before each game both players pick at once, from their decks that have not won, on the series screen: a pick is sealed, final once made and hidden from the other player, who sees only that it is in, and the game starts as soon as both are in ([[R331]], [[R338]]). A player with one deck left has it picked for them, and when both do the game starts at once ([[R332]]). Picking has a clock, which gives a late player their first deck that has not won ([[R333]]). A drawn game counts for neither side and locks nothing, and a series plays at most `SERIES_MAX_GAMES` (7) games, after which more wins takes it and equal wins is a series draw; a concede or a disconnect loses the game, not the series; between games a player may forfeit the series ([[R334]]). Series seat p1, [[R1989000]]'s coin winner on the series seed, goes first in odd games ([[R335]]). Each side sees which of both sides' decks have won, and nothing else of the other side's trio ([[R336]]). A series moves the rating once, when it ends ([[R262]]). It lives in the database, picks included, and survives a server restart ([[R263]]); one begun as a Best of 3 goes on as a Conquest ([[R337]]). While a game's match socket is open, the server pushes each seat its own view of the series as it moves on ([[R3213000]]).
- Replays. Either seat of a finished match may watch it again, from its result panel or from the recent games (`/replays`), until the retention purge takes its action log (`MATCH_ACTION_RETENTION_DAYS`). `GET /api/replays` lists the caller's finished matches whose log is kept, newest first, a page at a time, and `GET /api/replays/:matchId` answers a page of steps of the caller's own seat; an account that held no seat gets what an unknown id gets, so a match id's existence never leaks ([[R768]], [[R767000]]). Opening a replay the server does not hold in memory folds its whole game, so those folds run one at a time for the whole server, 1v1 and co-op together, a second request for the match being folded waits for that fold, and one that waits too long is a 429 that states its wait ([[R3500000]], [[§9.8]]). Nothing but each step's `view_for`, with the seats' portraits ([[R3976000]]), their frozen loadouts and the picks of the cards those steps show ([[R1834003]]), leaves: no seed, log, state or deck. The player draws each hero as its seat's portrait, an online match's from its pages, a practice game's from its summary and a downloaded file's from the file, and `vanilla` where its source holds none; a GIF made from it draws the same, and a replay's hero opens no emote menu ([[R3976000]]). "Watch the replay" opens a replay at its first step and "Share the last turn" at its last, its turn's GIF already being made ([[R768000]], [[R513000]]). A row of the recent games that can be watched can be downloaded as one JSON file for a bug report: its steps' views, the row's own facts and the build's catalog version, and no account's name or id ([[R1141000]]), each step after the first kept as its change from the one before and the whole gzipped, where the browser can, into `.json.gz` ([[R5124000]]). A downloaded file opens again from the recent games, packed or plain ([[R5124000]]): the page parses only the documented `jackioh-replay` v1 shape, or a co-op replay's `jackioh-duos-replay` v1 ([[R1242000]]), requires its own catalog version, holds the file to byte and step caps, and mounts the verified steps in an in-memory source, or a co-op file's on the co-op replay player, refusing anything else where it stands; the file is never uploaded or kept ([[R1141000]]).
- Play together. One page, Friends (`/together`), holds what a player does with friends, behind the sign-in and the active account Play online asks for, in five tabs by `?section=` ([[R3732002]]): Friends, the add form by `name#tag`, which shows the server's refusal in its own words, and the friends grouped as Requests (accept, decline), In a game, Online and Offline (collapsed), with counts and a search, each friend's row saying what they are doing and carrying one button by their state, Watch (a game the player may watch, below), Join (a lobby they have open, [[R3730000]]), Challenge (live for an online friend in no match, [[§9.13]]) or Daily (a daily challenge, any friend's), and a ⋯ menu holding Chat, Daily challenge, Invite to lobby, Watch, Remove and Block; Requests, in and out, with the challenges sent; Recently played, the opponents of the player's recent room, lobby, challenge and daily games, each with Add friend, and never one the queue paired ([[R3732000]]); Blocked, with unblock; and the replays friends have shared. Co-op lobbies ([[§13.9]], [[R3730000]]) are made and joined from `/play`. The landing's Play door opens the `/play` hub ([[R1578000]]), which holds every way to play on one screen, online first, and links this page; Friends lives at `/together` behind the gate. Every signed-in screen but sign-in and a password reset has a Friends button at the top right (on a board, in its controls), badged with the incoming requests (as last read), challenges and unread messages; its panel, a drawer from the menus' shell ([[R3728001]]), holds the player's `name#tag`, the add form, a search and the same groups with the same buttons, and a link to the page.
- Challenging a friend. A player challenges an online friend who is in no match: the challenge makes a room exactly as a room challenge does, in the challenger's mode with the same choices ([[R264]]), and an open challenge naming its code. A second open challenge to the same friend, and one to a friend who is in a match or offline, are refused. The friend sees the challenge wherever they are in the signed-in client; accepting it is joining the room by its code, which marks the challenge accepted, from the toast with the deck or trio picked there, a refused join offering the lobby with the code filled in ([[R767]], [[R1831000]]). The challenge dialog opens on the last mode and pick; a player in a game is offered none. Only the friend may join a challenge's room, and only while the two are friends neither of whom blocks the other: anyone else, and the friend once that has ended, gets the answer an unknown code gets, and a challenge whose friendship ends while it is being made is not opened ([[R1689000]]). Declining it, the challenger canceling it, and its expiry `CHALLENGE_TTL_MS` (2 minutes) after it was made each free the room. A challenge is kept no longer than that: once its `CHALLENGE_TTL_MS` has passed the sweeper deletes it, whatever became of it. A player sends at most `CHALLENGES_PER_HOUR` (30) challenges an hour, daily ones among them. A friend's challenge is a room challenge, so unranked ([[§9.12]]). A daily challenge ([[§9.13]]) goes to any friend, online or not and in a game or not, and makes no room.
- Watching a friend. A player may watch a friend's live 1v1 match, ranked or unranked, or co-op game, while fewer than `SPECTATORS_PER_MATCH` (8) watch it, and no setting turns that off: the friendship is the way out ([[R2615000]]). The watcher sees the friend's seat's `view_for`, each view `SPECTATE_DELAY_MS` (5 s) after the state it shows and with no legal action, so a watcher cannot tell the opponent what the friend holds while it still matters. A full match, a player who is not a friend and a blocked pair are refused, and a watcher already in is let go the same way (`forbidden`, then closed) the moment the two stop being friends or one blocks the other. A watcher's frames never reach the reducer, and a watcher leaving changes nothing in the match. A watcher throws emotes from the sidelines: emoji from a hand of its own ([[R2990001]]) under a limit stricter than a seat's ([[R2990002]]), each relayed at once to both players (a co-op game's two people) and the other watchers, never to its sender, never into the game or to a bot's persona, and never to anyone a block stands between it and ([[R2990000]], [[R2990003]]). Both players (a co-op game's two people) are told how many watch whenever that count changes. A watch ends with the match: once a watcher has been sent the view that shows the result, its socket is closed and both players are told the new count ([[R1107000]]). A co-op game is watched from the friend's human seat, its view that seat's ([[§13.6]]) and never a ping, the cap counts every watcher of the game, and the friend's own teammate is refused; as that view shows the teammate's duel, a block between the watcher and the friend's teammate refuses the watch and ends one running as a block with the friend does ([[R2881000]]). A watch socket that goes quiet is checked when its tab wakes: a page that wakes probes a socket quiet past the ping interval with hello, which the server answers with the view it last sent that watcher and nothing newer ([[R1103000]]), and replaces it if nothing answers within the probe deadline, up to the reconnect attempt bound ([[R1441]]). A watcher's socket keeps a seat's limits: it is answered at most one later hello per `WATCH_HELLO_INTERVAL_MS` (1 s), and its frames that are neither a hello nor an emote of the pool, unanswered but for an emote frame naming no pool id, which is answered `malformed` ([[R2990000]]), close it with 1008 past `MALFORMED_FRAMES_PER_WINDOW` ([[R2609000]]).
- Shared replays. A player who held a seat in a finished match may share its replay with a friend: the friend's replay route then answers the sharer's seat's steps, as it answers the sharer ([[R768]]), until the sharer withdraws it (only the sharer can), the two stop being friends or one blocks the other, which deletes every share between them for good, or the retention purge takes the log, after which the purge's next step deletes the share ([[R4390001]]). Anyone else still gets what an unknown id gets. The recent games and the replay player offer "Share with a friend", which shows the friends the replay is already shared with, read from the server as it opens, so the sharer withdraws a share there on any later visit ([[R1435000]]), and the Friends page lists the replays shared with the player, each opening its replay. An account may share at most `REPLAY_SHARES_PER_HOUR` (30) times an hour, every attempt counted, and sharing a replay already shared with that friend tells the friend nothing again ([[R4391000]]).
- Chat. Friends may write to each other, and nobody else: a message is 1 to `CHAT_MAX_CHARS` (500) characters once trimmed, a word the usernames' profanity filter catches ([[R1433]]) is masked with `*`, and a sender writes at most `CHAT_MESSAGES_PER_MINUTE` (20) a minute. A conversation is read a page at a time, `CHAT_PAGE` (50) messages, newest last, messages sent in the same instant in the order of their ids, each earlier page asked for by the cursor the page after it answered ([[R1384000]]), and reading a page marks it read; the client asks again every `CHAT_POLL_MS` (4 s) while a conversation is open and the tab's live socket ([[§9.4]]) is down, and while it is up reads the newest page whenever the server says a message between the two was sent ([[R3214000]]), and the friends panel opens a conversation on any screen. A message the player sends shows at once at the end of the conversation, marked as sending, the sends going to the server one at a time in the order sent, and the server's copy replaces it when it answers; a refused or failed one is marked failed in the server's words, with Retry and Discard ([[R2120000]]). A message holding a room link of this site draws a Join button that opens the link in the app, while the message itself stays text ([[R2094000]]). A message may be reported, once per reporter, and its sender blocked ([[§9.4]]). A message is readable by its two players only, and the retention loop deletes those older than `CHAT_RETENTION_DAYS` (30).
- Co-op. Two friends play Duos against an AI team from a co-op lobby on the `/play` hub ([[§13]], [[§13.9]]), or four play a 2v2, two teams of people, from a 2v2 lobby there ([[R3731000]]); a friend is invited into an empty seat, or by its link, shared in chat or by hand ([[R3730005]], [[R2094000]]).
