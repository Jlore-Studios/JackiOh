Presentation only (CLAUDE.md rule 7): a picture reads a card's id, name, tags and type, never anything hidden.
The issue (part of #3330) asked for the foundation every per-set picture pass builds on: a written look per
set family, a way to compose a picture as a scene of several parts, and a measure that makes a rarer card
more detailed ([[R3331001]]), redrawing no card beyond a few proofs.

**The art bible.** `apps/web/src/cards/art/ART.md` gives each set family (Core; Classic with Classic+;
Meditative with Meditative+; Stinkyscape; Training Grounds) its home, from Set showcase's five
([[R2943001]]): Core's glass orb, Classic's temple stone, Meditative's ink wash, Stinkyscape's deep space and
Training Grounds' carved wood; its palette in palette.css's names only; its light; its line; its camera; and
what each rarity adds, from a Common's one clear subject in a simple setting to a Mythic's set piece.

**Scenes.** A scene recipe is data per card (`sceneRecipes.ts`): a sky of two palette colours, a light,
where the card's emblem stands, an optional figure, and seven layers back to front (sky, far, mid, subject,
near, light, particles), each a list of placements of kit parts with a count, a spread and a repaint. The
scene kit (`sceneKit.ts`) holds the scenery, six kinds of at least six parts each (terrain, architecture,
plants, creatures, objects and tools, weather and light); the figure kit (`figureKit.ts`) replaces the
procedural painter's one silhouette with poses, heads, hair and headgear, outfits by tag and held items,
put together by a look per tag (Terminus, CN, KY, Felinor, Pancake, Fruit, Jlockeed, Human, the first a
card holds winning, a hooded traveller for none). The two kits hold at least 60 parts; every colour is a
palette.css name and none of a base face's is in Radiant's gold window ([[R2725004]]). One stream seeded by
the card's id draws every part, so a picture's own seed (Alt art's and the repaints', [[R2940001]],
[[R3017002]]) grows the same parts apart, and the Radiant face draws the same shapes, each mixed halfway
toward its layer's gold, under the painter's rays. The figure stands in front of its subject layer's other
parts. A family's line outlines its inked shapes (Classic's and Meditative's slate ink, Stinkyscape's fine
black, Training Grounds' dark wood; Core draws none).

**One painter for scenes.** The procedural painter's spec takes the scene (`withScene`), keeping the card's
theme, layout, backdrop, sky scheme, emblem glyph, motif and rays and replacing its shapes, sky, glow and
emblem's place, so a scene prints, moves on its layers' beats ([[R2715001]]: its light and particles move as
the light and motes do), splits for Out of frame (its subject layer and its emblem are the subject,
[[R2943001]]) and restyles as every procedural picture does. The Stinkyscape and Training Grounds painters
hand a recipe card to it. A Legacy card keeps its Core card's subject as it froze it ([[R2724000]]), never the
Core card's scene.

**Five proofs.** Corpse Plantation (Classic #74, Epic: a moonlit graveyard farm, rows of grave-mounds to the
horizon, hands and sprouts pushing up from the furrows, a bone scarecrow over it, plague mist in the rows, a
lantern on a fence post), Speciesist (Core #2, Common), Heroic Power (Core #98, Mythic), Ceaseless Void (Core
#100, Mythic) and Arg Arg Defender (Stinkyscape #15, the Stinkyscape Common) draw from recipes on both faces
and clear their floors. Every other card draws its picture byte for byte, which the golden fixtures prove
([[R3331002]]).

Rejected: a scene printer for each painter, which would print and split three ways what one spec prints
once; gradients for glows, which a restyle filter and the replay's raster would each have to follow; marking
layers and parts in the markup with `data-*` attributes, which adds bytes to every picture; and drawing the
figure before the subject layer's parts, which hid Ceaseless Void's Terminus behind its own void.
