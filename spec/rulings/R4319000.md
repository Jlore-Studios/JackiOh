A screen on the improve lane's dev set (`cargo jackioh arena --set improve-dev`) reads its paired tally's new counts ([[R850000]], [[R850001]]). A deal is **decided** when its edge is not 0: both games won (`wonBoth`) or both lost (`lostBoth`). A draw or a game without a result is nobody's win, so a deal holding one still counts as decided when the other game gives it a nonzero edge. A deal is **diverged** when its two games end on different hashes, so the two agents played differently at least once.

A screen with fewer than `SCREEN_MIN_DECIDED` (20) decided deals is **inconclusive**: it prints `inconclusive: true` and says nothing. It neither keeps nor drops the change; the knob is settled by its scene tests, and its config comment records the screen as inconclusive with its decided count. Twenty is the point where the keep rule can keep a change that wins three deals in four (15 of 20 clears an `edgeLow` above 0), so no smaller floor lets a rarely-acting change through on its own screen.

`--extend` is the one way to play past 400 games, decided in advance by the flag: the screen plays on in 100-deal batches (`SCREEN_EXTEND_DEALS`) until `SCREEN_MIN_DECIDED` deals are decided or `SCREEN_MAX_GAMES` (2,000, the promotion gate's own maximum) games are played, and the edge is read only at the end. The stop looks only at how many deals were decided, never which way they went; under a neutral change each decided deal is a fair coin however many are played, so it is not the second read of the same deals, nor the best of several arms, that `training/improve.md` forbids.

Hearthstone has no counterpart: there is no public paired screen to tune an AI on.
