Nothing deleted a daily invite. §9.13's sweep marked an open one `expired` once its `expires_at` had passed, and every read already skips a closed or lapsed one (the active count, the lists, the seek's claim, and `open_challenge`, which answers 404 to anything not open and unlapsed), yet a challenge or a seek accepted, declined, cancelled, expired or paired stayed in the table for good, each with the sender's frozen deck in its `choice` and who challenged whom. §9.5 keeps a live challenge no longer than its `CHALLENGE_TTL_MS`, deleted whatever became of it; the daily kind, built for the same job, never got that half.

**What changes.** The sweep, in the transaction that expires the lapsed open invites, deletes every invite, challenge or seek, whose `expires_at <= now`, in any state: open, accepted, declined, cancelled, expired or paired. An invite not yet lapsed stays, closed or not, so a read in its week finds it as before. The count goes into the sweep's answer (`purged`) and its log. The game an accepted challenge or a paired seek started lives in `daily_games` and `matches`, and nothing reads `daily_invites.match_id`, so deleting the invite leaves the game to open, be played and end. The rows just expired are still told to their players, since the sweep holds them before it deletes them. The `dailyChallenge` and `dailyAnswered` rows of the notification feed carry only the invite's id and are never joined to it, so they stay as [[R4233000]] has them, read or not, until the feed's own retention ([[R4039000]]) takes them. The cap on a player's open sends and running games ([[R3727004]]) reads only open, unlapsed invites and is unchanged.

**Reach.** After the first pass the table holds at most about a week of invites, and the sweep reads it once an hour, so the delete needs no index and no migration.

Hearthstone semantics: a friend challenge nobody answered in time is gone, as §9.5's live one is, and an answered one is no more kept than a lapsed one.

Rejected: deleting an invite when it closes, which needs every closing path to delete and still leaves the lapsed open rows to the sweep; and keeping a closed invite as history, which nothing reads and which keeps who challenged whom and the sender's deck for ever.
