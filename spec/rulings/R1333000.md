The owner's clarification in #1333, as the built economy carries it:

- **Two scales, two types.** A card's rarity is the catalog's `PrintedRarity` ([[§5]]); a
  cosmetic's is `CosmeticRarity`, Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary or Mythic, never tied to the card it
  goes on ([[§14.2]]). Nothing converts one into the other: a duplicate's cosmetic roll
  ([[R1301002]]), a cosmetic pack's slots ([[R1306000]], [[R1306001]]), a cosmetic token's tier
  ([[R1305003]]), the Chase Shard's purchase ([[R1306002]]) and a cosmetic token's inventory key all
  carry `CosmeticRarity`, and the client's `CosmeticRarity` type is generated from the registry's
  rarities. Both scales still spell their rarities with the same five words, so every answer, every
  opening's log ([[R1301003]]) and every stored key reads as before.
- **The chase tier** is Legendary and Mythic. The registry keeps its `chase` mark, and its check
  refuses an entry whose mark disagrees with its rarity: a chase is exactly a Legendary or Mythic
  cosmetic. The two Legendary starters, Cracked ice and Gilded edge, are marked; every account owns
  them already, so no chase can be earned yet.
- **Chases drop.** A duplicate's roll that lands on Legendary or Mythic drops an unowned chase of
  that tier for the card, and so does a cosmetic pack slot, as any other tier does; this amends
  [[R1306000]], which kept chases out of cosmetic packs. A full Chase Shard bar still buys one of
  the player's choice ([[R1306002]]), and no token reaches one ([[R1305003]]). `CHASE_DROP_RATE`
  is gone: the chase odds are the Legendary and Mythic weights of `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS`, the one
  table a duplicate's cosmetic and every cosmetic pack slot roll since #1339 ([[R1339000]]).
- **No cosmetic roll touches pity.** A card pack's counters come from the slots' tier draws, the
  opening's first, and pity on top of them, before any cosmetic is rolled ([[R1339002]]), and a
  cosmetic pack writes no pity counter ([[§14.4]]).
- **The full arts** stay Epic whatever their card's rarity ([[R1313000]]): an Epic token buys one,
  a Rare token does not.

Hearthstone semantics: a golden or signature card sits apart from its card's rarity, and its
rarest finishes are the ones chased; the spec's cosmetics follow that, with the owner's tiers.

Rejected: dropping the `chase` field, since the registry refuses unknown fields and every logged
opening's `cosmetics` carries it ([[R1301003]]), so an older log would no longer read back; keeping
chases out of cosmetic packs, against the owner's "a cosmetic pack slot landing on Legendary or
Mythic"; and re-rating the two Legendary starters to Epic, which the owner did not ask for.
