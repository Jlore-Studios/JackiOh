S #25 KY's Tricknology reads "Choose a zone. Its Jacobian stretches a unit square. A Starship Piece lands on each zone it covers. F(x, y) = (x² − y, xy)". The designer left the coordinates to the build (ST9 Q14).

- **The coordinates.** A zone is (x, y): x its lane, 1 to 5; y its row from the caster's seat, 1 their unit row, 2 their backrow, −1 the enemy's unit row, −2 the enemy's backrow. A zone's point is its centre, (x, y) itself. The caster chooses any of the 20 zones, empty or not (`choose_cell`, B5 E18).
- **The cover.** The unit square is mapped through the Jacobian of F at the chosen point, J, its rows (2x, −1) and (y, x), with its corner at that point: the parallelogram of the points (x, y) + s·(2x, y) + t·(−1, x) for s and t from 0 to 1, its edges included. A zone is covered when its point lies in it, so the chosen zone always covers itself. det J = 2x² + y is positive at every zone but (1, −2), where J is singular and the cover is the segment through it along (1, −1), which holds no other zone.
- **What lands.** Each covered unit zone that is open and not Locked gets one random Starship Piece Unit (`query::starship_pieces()`, [[R1878000]]) summoned for the player whose zone it is: a summon, so no Cry ([[R1]]), never the Tricknology's own definition ([[R387]]), one draw of the match rng per zone. The caster's zones go first, then the enemy's, each lane upward. An occupied unit zone gets none, and so does a Locked one, though a named summon could enter it ([[R688]]): the card says "lands on", and a Locked zone takes no landing. The Radiant face reaches the caster's covered zones alone, each piece Radiant.
- **No pieces in the backrow.** The brief proposed that a covered backrow zone takes the piece as a Unit standing there, as [[R1081000]] lets a Flying Unit stand. The build decided otherwise: a summon places a card in its type's row ([[§3.2]]), no summon verb puts a Unit in a backrow zone, and not every piece flies. So a covered backrow zone gets none (ST-S18-2 left this choice to the build).

Rejected: reading the zones as unit squares and covering a zone by any overlap with the parallelogram, which makes nearly every choice cover half the board.
