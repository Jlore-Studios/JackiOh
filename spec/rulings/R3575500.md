An own hand card's price in the AI's evaluation is `hand_card + hand_per_cost × min(cost, hand_cost_cap)` off its printed cost, less `hand_cost_delta` (0.5) for each crystal its `costMod` moves it by (B3.4, [[R65]]): an Upgrade's cheaper card gains it, a Degrade's dearer one loses it. A `costOverride` laid on a card in a hand moved nothing. S+ #5 Dingleberry Droat fuses Fruits until their printed costs reach 5 and adds the fusion "costing (2)" ([[R3558004]]), but the fusion is printed at min(sum, 4) ([[R77]]), so the AI counted a (4) card where its hand holds a (2) one. NA Tech's set price ([[R1183001]]) and every effect that hands a card back at a price ([[R766]]) read the same. No game rule changes: the evaluation changes no game.

**What it counts.** `cost_shift` reads the card's own cost (`own_cost`: its `costOverride`, or the Embiggened version's own price, else its printed cost, plus its `costMod`) less its printed cost (`printed_cost`), each floored at 0 as a price is, so a set price below the printed one gains `hand_cost_delta` a crystal and one above it loses as much, the same number a `costMod` of that size gives. With no override the shift is the `costMod`'s, as before; an X-cost card has none. The frozen `GREEDY_EVAL` keeps `hand_cost_delta` 0, so it reads none. Reading the override as the card's whole cost basis, in place of the printed cost, was rejected: it would price Droat's fusion as a (2) card, below the (4) card it is printed as, and a set price would then take more off a card than the discount of the same size.
