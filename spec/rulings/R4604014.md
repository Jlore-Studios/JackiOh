`decide` gives its beam a slice of the decision's nodes and keeps the rest back for the finalists' replies ([[R3498000]]). Under [[R4801000]] a host's wrap-up is polled only once the beam has tried every move at its root, so the beam always has a line. The slice's own limit, though, bound from the first node. In the run-sim daily sweep's seed 0, a decision on turn 31 had two moves, both answers to the seat's own prompt. Each cost 206 nodes in one simulation, with the opponent's prompts it set off auto-answered. The lethal search had spent 128 of the decision's 514 nodes, and the beam's slice was 193. The first answer drained the slice and its simulation came back unfinished, the beam stopped with no line, and the decision fell back, which every gate and the daily sweep count as a fault.

The beam's slice (`create_beam_counter`) now lets the moves at its root draw on the decision's counter however many nodes they cost, as its wrap-up already did; the slice's limit binds once the root is tried. The decision's own limit and its clock still stop it. Here that is 334 of 514 nodes after the first answer, so the beam closes that answer's line and the second is cut. Any other slice binds at once, as before. A decision whose root moves fit the slice, nearly every one, spends exactly as before.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.

Rejected: running the beam again on the reserve when it ends empty. The second beam starts over and gets only what the first left, here 193 nodes, short of one answer again (round 5 of the repro). Rejected too: refusing a root move whose simulation overruns the slice, as an unpaid surcharge is refused. The next root move then finds the slice spent, so the beam still ends empty.
