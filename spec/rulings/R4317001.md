[[R1303001]] had the account's next seed request delete the account's seeds issued over `PRACTICE_SEED_TTL_SECONDS` ago, and nothing else deleted one, so an account always kept its newest hour of seeds and an account that never asked again kept them for good. With a seed holding a whole setup, that was unbounded storage per account.

**What changes.** Every pass of the retention loop, after it settles the claims left unsettled ([[R2422000]]), deletes every account's seeds issued more than `PRACTICE_SEED_TTL_SECONDS` ago that are unclaimed or settled, paid or refused, and one claimed before the claim was kept, which holds none: the rows the account's own prune deletes, for every account. A claim not yet settled stays, as it does for the account's prune, since only the sweep settles it ([[R2422000]]); an hour later, once settled, it goes. A seed issued within the lifetime is never deleted, so a seed that could still be claimed stays. The step runs apart from the others under its own failing streak (`practice.prune`), so one failure holds no other step and is alerted on its own (`FailingStreak`, issue #3770), and it runs whether the economy is on or off, since deleting a lapsed row pays nothing. The account's prune at its next seed request stays. A purged seed's claim answers `unknown_seed` where a lapsed one answered `expired` ([[R1303001]]): both pay nothing, and the lifetime is the whole difference between them.

**Reach.** With a seed request refusing a deck its claim could not deal ([[R4317000]]), every row holds a setup of about a kilobyte and none outlives its hour by more than a pass of the loop, so the table holds about an hour of seeds. The delete reads by issue time, where the table's index leads with the account; it scans a table of that size once an hour, so it needs no index and no migration. The count of open seeds ([[R1483000]], [[R3500001]]) is unchanged.

Hearthstone semantics: a reward ticket nobody redeemed expires, and is not kept for ever.

Rejected: also deleting a claim not yet settled, which would drop a claim whose pay failed before the sweep could settle it ([[R2422000]]); running the step only while the economy is on, as the sweep does, which gains nothing since the delete pays nothing and the economy's switch says nothing of storage; and a database job outside the server, which the hosts' settings could not hold in the repository.
