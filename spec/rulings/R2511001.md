A Legacy card ([[R2511000]]) and the Core card it copies are two cards in every rule (issue #2511). Nothing treats them as one:

- **Decks.** [[§2.6]] L3 counts each by its own id, so a deck may hold both. `create_game`'s check (`validate_deck_with`) and the shared validator's L3 (`validate_loadout`, the server's too) drop the pair check, and the deck builder no longer shows the other card of a pair as unavailable. [[R2195000]] is superseded.
- **Random decks.** `build_ai_deck` ([[R258]]) and its floors ([[R1370]]) count each card apart and deal both, as do the fuzz tool's deal, the golden tool's forcing and the duos fuzz. The Legacy tag is never a random deck's theme, as Quickdraw is not ([[R817000]]): it marks copies of Core cards, not a family that plays together, so it moves no deal beyond what dealing both cards moves.
- **Discover.** A Discover from the catalog ([[§6.3]]) may show both, Core #97 Zephrys' top three ([[R29]]) included: [[R2204001]]'s "never both of a pair" ends for every game played on this catalog. Random pools keep both, as they did.

The build's catalog sets `snapshotOf` on no card, and `catalog check` refuses the field. The catalogs shipped from v0.5.5 to v0.5.7 set it on #41–#60, so a replay of a game played on one of them, which folds under its carried catalog ([[R767000]]), would offer other cards if the offer stopped reading it. The two Discovers therefore keep passing over a card whose pair key (`catalog::pair_key`: the `snapshotOf` of the definition the fold runs under, else the card's id) an earlier option holds. That is the version gate: the carried definitions decide, so no game recorded before this change replays differently, and every key on this catalog is the card's own id, so its offers are the shuffle's and the ranking's first three as they stand. Nothing else reads the field. Decks are never judged again by the old rule: a deck a replay was dealt was legal under it, and the validator now accepts more.

Hearthstone semantics: two cards with different ids are different cards for deckbuilding and for Discover, even when one reprints the other.

The classifier ([[R1365000]]) replays the head's games with a build from before this change, which refuses a deck holding a Core card beside its Legacy card. It reads that refusal as [[R1705004]] reads a refused deck size: the change lifted a deck rule ([[§2.6]] L3), a rule, so its fragment claims `--rule`.
