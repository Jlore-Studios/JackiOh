"Trigger a Cry" ([[R467]]) reached only a Unit: on top of its unit pile, standing in a backrow zone, or in a graveyard. A face-up Field Spell on the field with a Cry is now reached too, so a summon that names a Field Spell fires its Cry as [[R411]] has a summon fire a Unit's (Radiant S #6 Tylannt's The Greatest Heist, [[R1873000]]). It runs as a Unit's on the field does: the Field Spell is the Cry's "this", the triggering card's controller runs it and makes its choices ([[R70]]), its declared choices are asked as prompts, it is not a play, and a Field Spell that leaves before its choices are made triggers nothing ([[R174]]).

- **Where.** On the field only. A Field Spell in a graveyard has no Cry to trigger, unlike a Unit there, and a face-down one reads as the Trap it was set as, so it has none either. A set Spell revealing from the backrow is still [[R1044]]'s resolution, not a triggered Cry.
- **Who reaches it.** Only an effect that names the card, as a summon's follow-up does. Classic #54 Rewind declares "one of your Units on the field or in your graveyard", whose candidates are Units, so it reaches no Field Spell; nor does Stinkyscape #71 A'Zoth, whose pool is Units. The card kit's `has_triggerable_cry` reads the same answer.
- **Why.** Hearthstone fires no Battlecry on a summon, but this spec's summon that names its card does ([[§6.1]] Cry row, [[R411]]), and a Field Spell's Cry otherwise runs only when it is played. Widening the reach to the field was the smallest change that lets the named summon keep its promise for every card type that keeps a Cry on the field.
