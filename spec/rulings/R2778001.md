The owner asked in #2778 for more bots on the ladder, so people can grind it ([[§9.12]]).

**Why the old pool ran out.** The pool held a quarter of the people who finished a match in the last week,
clamped to 8–200, and each bot was on 8 to 16 hours a day ([[R1454]]). A player is never paired with one of
their last five opponents ([[R1453]]). So with a small player base, a handful of bots were on at any hour,
and a grinder used them up within a few games.

**The new numbers.**

| Constant | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `BOT_POOL_MIN` | 8 | 40 |
| `BOT_POOL_MAX` | 200 | 400 |
| `BOT_POOL_PER_WEEKLY_ACTIVE` | 0.25 | 1.0 (one active bot per weekly active person) |
| Hours a day a new bot is on (`BOT_ACTIVE_HOURS_MIN` to `BOT_ACTIVE_HOURS_MAX`) | 8 to 16 | 12 to 18 |

At the floor, with every bot on its fewest hours, the bots on at any hour average 20. That is more than twice
the five recent opponents plus the last one, so a grinder always finds a bot the last few games have not
used up.

The most hours a day stays under a grinder's `BOT_GRIND_ACTIVE_HOURS` (20), so a grind still gives its bots
more of the day than their own schedules do ([[R1555000]]).

The pool's next refresh reinstates retired bots and draws new ones up to the new size, as any refresh does.
A bot drawn before keeps the schedule it was drawn with.

Hearthstone semantics do not reach this: Hearthstone's ladder has no bots. This follows the ladder bots' own
reason for being: a person in the queue should always find an opponent near them ([[R1453]]).

Rejected:
- Raising the active hours to 20, which would leave the grind no more of the day than a bot's own schedule
  ([[R1555000]]'s constant check).
- Lifting the five-recent-opponents rule, which would hand a grinder the same bot over and over.
