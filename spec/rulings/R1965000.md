A card that lets its player play cards from their graveyard ([[R454]]: C #90 In Too Deep's reward L puts no
price on it) makes a (0) Spell there free to play again and again, since it lands back in the graveyard each
time. With its controller's deck empty, C #37 Last Hurrah ("Draw your deck") and M #62 Small Time Recruits
("Draw (1) Cost Units from your deck") then do nothing. Each replay still reads as a new position to the AI's
search, because the turn's record of plays is part of it (`search_signature`, kept there for the cards that
count plays). The lethal solver's best-first walk breaks a tie in its distance from lethal (`ready_gap`)
toward the longer line, so it followed such replays down to its depth limit and returned a lethal that
opened with one. `decide` plays only a line's first action and then plans again ([[§9.9]]), so it replayed
the Spells one at a time and the turn never ended.

The AI decides this: neither walk of the lethal solver goes on from a **replay for nothing**, a play of a
card the AI has already played this turn (an instance in its turn's `playedIds`) that leaves its mana and
`ready_gap` as they were. Such a move is a dead end, as a position the walk has already reached is. A
replay that costs mana, gains it, or moves `ready_gap` (damage to the enemy hero, a Taunt removed, an
attacker grown or readied, a prompt opened) is searched as before, and so is the first play of a card in a
turn, from hand or graveyard. The beam needs no such rule: the evaluation reads none of the play record, so
a line of replays that change nothing scores the same as the End Turn without them, and the tie goes to the
End Turn, which the beam closes first.

The rule reads only the AI's own turn log and mana and the public board that `ready_gap` reads, so no hidden
card changes it ([[R185]]). It adds no rule of the game: the replays stay legal, and In Too Deep, Last
Hurrah and Small Time Recruits play as before.
