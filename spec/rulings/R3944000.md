A version's tier follows the release cadence, not the size of its change. Three tiers, each with one trigger:

- a **major**, `v0.(X+1).0`, when a set ships ([[R1420]]; a set the owner wraps keeps its line, [[R2204000]]), named by `patches ship` at once;
- a **micro**, the newest version with the next letters ([[R3631000]]), for every other fragment `patches ship` promotes, whatever its size: a fragment that reprints `PATCH_MIN_REPRINTS` families ([[R2499000]]), plays `PATCH_MIN_CARDS` or claims a rule every game meets ([[R2455000]]) is a micro, and so is a system the owner names ([[R2529000]], whose claim no longer makes a patch);
- a **patch**, `v0.X.(Y+1)`, only from `patches cut`: the day's patch.

`patches cut [--production <ref>]` rolls every micro after the newest major or patch into the next patch: `tier: patch`; `includes`, those micros' versions, oldest first; `plays`, theirs united and sorted; the newest micro's snapshot (and card lists) byte for byte, so its own `changes` are none; and a player note ([[R724001]]) merged from theirs, its title the title of the micro whose plays hold the most families (the oldest of a tie) followed by ", and N more changes", and under each heading their lines in order, a line two of them share once. It is dated by, and recorded in `shipped.json` at, the commit it was cut from; it carries no `commits`, since no pull request of its own is to be retitled; and it bumps the catalog version everywhere it lives, as a ship does. It writes nothing when no micro followed the newest major or patch, and with `--production <ref>` it writes nothing while that ref's `patches.json` does not hold the newest major or patch, so each daily patch is one production release. It refuses while fragments are pending: the ship runs first, and the cut rolls up what it shipped. A major `patches ship` names rolls up the micros before it the same way, in its own `includes`.

`patches check` fails on an `includes` on a micro, or on a major or patch whose `includes` names anything but the micros between it and the major or patch before it, in order. A major or patch with none, every version shipped before this ruling among them, is whole: no version is renamed.

`patches-ship.yml` runs the cut once a day after the ship, early enough that its pull request lands on main before the commit the daily production run tests, with `--production origin/_production`; run by hand with `cut` ticked it cuts at once, without the wait (the fast-forward). A pull request's tier label is the tier it ships as (`classify::shipped_tier`): `tier:major` or `tier:micro`, never `tier:patch`. The classifier's own reading ([[R1365000]]) is unchanged, and its tier still says `patch` past its bars.

Card statistics are unchanged ([[R1367000]]): a major or a patch, the day's patch among them, opens a window for every card, and a micro one for the families it plays. Readers read `tier` and `includes`, never a version string ([[R105]], [[R388]]).

This supersedes the clauses of [[R1366000]] that name a patch from a fragment's tier (the patch bullet) and of [[R2529000]] that ship a named system as a patch. Their other clauses stand.
