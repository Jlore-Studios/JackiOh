`PUT /api/admin/rating` with `{ ladder, rating }` sets the caller's own hidden rating on one ladder ([[R2778000]]) to `rating`, a number from `DEV_RATING_MIN` to `DEV_RATING_MAX`; its deviation and volatility are kept, and `ladder` must name a ladder exactly. The first hand-set rating of a ladder keeps the rating, deviation and volatility its games had earned (`hand_set_ratings`), and a later one keeps that same earned rating. `POST /api/admin/rating/reset` with `{ ladder }` puts the earned triple back and ends the hand-set rating; on a ladder that is not hand-set it is 409 and writes nothing. `GET /api/admin/rating` answers the caller's triple on both ladders, Constructed first, whether each is hand-set and the earned rating while it is. Every set and reset writes a row of the rating ledger (`rating_grants`, reason `dev`, marked `dev`, with the triple before and after, [[§14.11]]) beside its audit row ([[R3074000]]).

A rating is not the dev account's alone: it pairs the account against people, ranks it on the public ladder and moves the percentiles and who reaches Jlorious ([[R605]], [[R608]]). So on a strict server (production) a hand-set ladder takes the account's season row off the public leaderboard, out of the percentiles every other player's target is read against and out of Jlorious, as a ladder bot's row is out of the last two ([[R640000]]), until the reset. On a server that is not strict (staging) the rating is set freely and the row stays where it stands. Games still rate a hand-set rating as any other, and still move its season row; a reset restores the earned rating as it stood when it was first hand-set, not what those games would have made of it, and leaves the season row as they left it. Restoring the season row too was rejected: a season may open between the set and the reset, and its soft reset reads the hand-set rating, so no one row is the one to restore.
