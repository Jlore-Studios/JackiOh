§9.5 says a share of a replay lasts until the retention purge takes the log, and every read of a share needs the log to exist (`readable_share!`), so a share whose log is gone can never be opened again. `share_replay` refuses a new share of such a match (#1825). Yet only an unshare, an unfriend or a block deleted a share's row, so the row stayed for good, with who shared what with whom.

**What changes.** The retention loop, in a step of its own right after the purge that takes the logs (`shares.purge`), deletes every replay share whose match holds no action-log row: the test the reads make, negated. A share of a match whose log is held is untouched, and a share of a match that never existed goes with the rest. The step runs under its own failing streak (`FailingStreak`, issue #3770), so one failure holds no other step and is alerted on its own. What a friend reads answers as before: the replay route answers 410 `expired` for a purged log, and the lists leave the share out as they did. A share written as the purge takes the log is deleted by the next pass, and nothing reads it meanwhile.

**Reach.** One row per share. The delete is an anti-join on `match_actions`' primary key, `(match_id, seq)`, once an hour, so it needs no index and no migration.

Hearthstone semantics: a link to a game that is no longer kept goes with it, and is not kept as a dead link.

Rejected: a foreign key from `replay_shares` to `matches`, which needs a migration and follows the match row, which the purge keeps (its result and rating history stay), when it is the log that ends a replay; and deleting the share in the purge's own call, which would widen `purge_expired` for every host when a step beside it holds one failure apart.
