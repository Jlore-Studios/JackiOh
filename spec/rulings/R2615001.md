What an online game records is part of playing it, the owner's call: no setting turns any of it off, and none is to be added. That is the match and its moves while they are kept ([[§9.5]]), the game record and the card statistics it adds to ([[R376]], [[R1044000]]), the record of how each seat played ([[R1442]]), and the player statistics an account's device files ([[R639]], [[R1142000]]), which [[R2000000]] already left with no switch. None of it is new: this ruling changes nothing that is collected, and holds it there. It is data about play: the game records name no account, the play telemetry is kept by match and seat, and none of it holds an email address or a date of birth.

**What the Privacy Policy says.**

- "Why we collect it" ends: playing online means the games are recorded as the policy lists, with no setting to turn that off, since the records are how matches are run, the cards are balanced and the computer opponents play at a person's pace.
- "What we collect" lists the player statistics, which it had left out: games finished, won, lost and drawn, and for each card how often the player played it, saw it played against them, lost a copy and destroyed an opponent's, counted by the browser from the online and practice games it finishes ([[R639]]) and sent to the account while signed in. A practice game's moves are still never sent; what it adds to those statistics is.
- "Who handles it" says the player statistics are public, with the username, on the statistics page's Players tab, with no setting to hide them, though an account that hid them while the setting existed keeps them hidden ([[R2000000]]).
- "Deleting your account" names them among what goes (migration 0021's cascade).

Watching a friend's game is the same kind of choice, and [[R2615000]] makes it: friends may always watch, and the friendship is the way out.

Rejected: an opt-out from the card statistics or the play telemetry, which would leave the balance figures and the bots' pace fitted to the players who stayed in.
