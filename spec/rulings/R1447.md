A ladder bot is an opponent the server plays itself, so a player who queues when no one else is there still gets a ranked game. It is a profile whose `kind` is `bot` (a person's is `human`; migration 0032, and no client role may read the column), a `bots` row holding its strength, speed, persona, schedule and decks ([[R1449]]), and a rating of its own ([[R610]]). A ranked match seats one only through the empty-queue fallback ([[R1448]]), and only while the server's `LADDER_BOTS` is `on`: unset or `off`, nothing seats a bot. [[R610]] rates a bot's games by the same rule as a player's, and the rated game records its side with the `ai` pilot and its bot id ([[R611]], [[R1450]]).

**How it plays.** A bot plays on this spec's resources, whatever its strength: no practice handicap applies to it ([[R180]]–[[R184]]). It decides with the practice AI's search ([[§9.9]]) at its own strength, one of five ([[R1449]]): a search budget, a softmax temperature over the search's finalist lines, and a chance to pass over a lethal line the solver found, after which it plays that decision as if it had not seen the lethal: no line that wins this turn, and none that opens with the solver's lethal first action (unless nothing else is left to play). At a temperature of 0 with no chance to pass, it decides exactly as the practice AI does. It reads only what [[R185]] lets the AI read, so no hidden card changes its moves either. The decision runs beside the match's actor, never holding it up, and is capped in wall-clock time (`BOT_DECISION_DEADLINE_MS`, and never so long that less than `BOT_CLOCK_MARGIN_MS` is left on its acting clock), past which it plays the best line found so far.

**Pace.** A bot moves at a person's pace. Each move waits a think time drawn from [[R1442]]'s fitted think times for its kind of move, first in the turn or later (`THINK_TIME_FITS`), or from hand-set priors until a fit for it lands, times the bot's own speed. The time the decision took counts toward it. The move goes out no sooner than `BOT_THINK_MIN_MS` (600 ms) after the bot came to owe it and no later than `BOT_THINK_MAX_MS` (30 s), and never so late that less than `BOT_CLOCK_MARGIN_MS` (5 s) is left on its acting clock ([[R79]]), so waiting never times it out. If the player acted while the bot was thinking (their auto-end-turn switch, their side of the mulligan), the bot's move still goes as long as it is the same turn and the engine takes it; otherwise the bot decides again. So nothing the player does on the bot's turn can hold its move back. A move the engine refuses is replaced, once, by ending the turn when that is legal (else the first legal action); a move that could not be written to the log is decided again `BOT_WRITE_RETRY_MS` (1 s) later.

**Replay.** A bot's random streams, its search's, its think time's and its persona's, are the server's own, each seeded from the match seed and the seat the bot began in, and none is the match rng (`state.rng`). They reach no state, log or replay hash, so a match with a bot replays from `(seed, log)` like any other ([[§9.5]]). An actor rebuilt after a restart resumes the bot's seat with its streams started again, which changes nothing a replay reads. As for any match, the actor is rebuilt when the person's socket comes back, since nothing starts at boot ([[R744]]); a bot has no socket to bring it back, so a match whose person never returns after a restart stays the reaper's draw at the ceiling ([[R112]]), as a match neither person returns to does, and holds its bot until then.

**The seat.** A bot has no socket and needs none. Its seat never counts as disconnected, so it never gets [[R744]]'s grace and never loses by `disconnectExpired`. It counts as attached from the moment its match's actor arms, as an opponent whose socket has attached: a person who has not opened the match by then is away from that moment, and loses by `disconnectExpired` when [[R744]]'s grace runs out, as they would against a person who had opened it. [[R188]] holds for it as for the practice AI: it never concedes, never offers a draw and declines every draw offer. Its moves carry server nonces (`srv-bot-…`), the prefix no client may send ([[R270]]), so they are the server's own actions, and [[R1442]] keeps no think time for them. It emotes through its persona ([[R645]]), one per bot, choosing only within the hand its seat was dealt ([[R1344]]); the actor relays each emote to the player as it relays a player's, under the same rate limit ([[R643]]), and while a card hears emotes it runs the emote as an `Emote` action exactly as it runs a player's ([[R1127]]), so a hearing card answers a bot's emote too. The bot's own moves never include one, as the practice AI's never do. It never offers or accepts a rematch ([[R672]]): it reads as present on the match while the game goes on, as a person playing it would, and as gone once the game ends, so the player is never shown the offer.

**What a client sees.** Nothing a client reads labels a bot or tells its seat from a person's (CLAUDE.md rule 7), and no response carries its `kind`, its pilot or its nonces. Its username, its portrait and the age of its account are drawn as a player's might be ([[R1449]]). A bot has no season row ([[R610]]), so the match screen's read of both seats' ranks ([[R612]]) shows a bot's seat the rank its rating calls for among the ratings of the season's placed players ([[R606]]'s target), standing on that tier's floor, where a placed player's rank always stands. It never shows Jlorious, and never Mythic Grape unless more players stand in Mythic than Jlorious holds (`JLORIOUS_SIZE`), since until then every one of them is Jlorious ([[R608]]). A rating that calls for Mythic then shows the top of the tier below. Bots stay off the leaderboard ([[R610]]).

**Tells this ruling knowingly leaves.** No field names a bot, but a player who looks closely can still infer one:

- A player who looks for a Grape-ranked opponent's username on the leaderboard will not find it, while a placed person's is there. Listing bots on the leaderboard was rejected, because it would let them take a place that people hold.
- A bot sends no aim arrows ([[R738]]).
- A bot never drops out of its match, so its seat never shows [[R744]]'s reconnect countdown, as a person's does when they lose their connection or open the match late.
- Once the game ends a bot reads as gone, so it never offers a rematch.
- A bot's rank on the match screen is the one its rating calls for when the screen is read, so a player who meets the same bot again may find it a tier lower, while a person's tier never falls inside a season. Keeping each bot's highest tier of the season was rejected: it is a season row in all but name, which [[R610]] keeps bots without.
- A bot keeps no public player statistics, which a person's client files after their games unless they set them private, so a search of the public player list by username ([[R1436]]) never finds a bot's. Filing statistics for bots was rejected: they are what a person's own client counts and files, so the server would have to invent them.
- The public card statistics count a game's human seats and not a bot's ([[R1450]]), so a game between two people adds two decks to them and a game against a bot adds one. A player who reads them (`GET /api/stats/cards`) before and after their own game, with no other game finishing in between, can tell which they played: `totalGames` grows by one either way, but the decks behind each card's `playRate`, and the `games` of the cards only their opponent held, grow only for a person's deck. Any figure that leaves the bot's deck out moves this way, so no change to how the figures are shown removes it. Counting the bot's side in the public figures was rejected, because issue #638 keeps it to the queries that ask for it and a bot's decks and play would move win rates meant to describe people's. Publishing the figures in batches rather than per game was rejected too: it would hide the tell only while many games finish per batch, which is not when bots are seated, and would make [[R378]]'s live figures late for everyone.
- A sign-in attempt with a bot's username is refused as fast as one with a name nobody holds, since a bot has no address to try ([[R1443]]).
