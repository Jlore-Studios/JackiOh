The lethal solver's second walk ([[§9.9]], Search) is best-first by `ready_gap`: the enemy hero's life less what the seat's ready attacks deal it past the Taunts. Among the open positions of one gap it took the deepest, then the oldest, and each position it expanded simulated every one of its moves, so while no step lowered the gap the walk was a dive in move order. The audit of issue #4787 logged three long lethals on boards of ordinary width, which issue #5127 pinned as fixtures. At turn 12 of `audit:ai-vs-greedy:26` the walk needed 510 nodes and at turn 12 of `audit:hard-vs-easy:8` 462, against its 300, and the AI ended both turns without the win. Turn 21 of `audit:hard-vs-easy:12` it proved in 192, and the lethal hint, which gives every state the whole 300, shows that one. At mirror 26 the walk's first child is the seat's one attacker spent killing the Taunt that walls it: the gap stays at 11, and the walk spent 287 nodes below that child before it tried the play whose three Activates kill the Taunt and free the attacker. At hard-vs-easy 8 the first child is the same kind of attack, and below it, four steps down, the walk spent 231 nodes in a play's subtree that holds no lethal before it tried the next play.

**The rule.** The AI decides this. Two changes to the order in which the best-first walk takes its open positions, each with its own switch in `AI_SEARCH`. Each position the walk expands still simulates all its moves.

1. *Attackers kept* (`lethal_keep_attacks`). Among open positions of the same gap above 0 and the same depth, the walk takes first the one whose ready attacks would leave the enemy hero least were no Taunt standing (`taunt_free`: the hero's life less every ready attack that may hit it), then the oldest. An attack on a Taunt that the gap already counted its attacker against leaves the gap where it was and spends the attacker, so it comes after the moves that keep the attacker ready. At a gap at or under 0 the oldest comes first, as before: there the hits and the kills that open the way are the line.
   - *Through a prompt.* `ready_gap` reads the hero's whole life while a prompt is open. It has done so since the first AI (issue #9), because `legal_actions` lists no attack until the prompt is answered. The gap is what the seat can deal from the position it stands at, and what reads it needs exactly that: [[R4788000]]'s reach goes on only from a position whose next steps can be the hits, and the move order puts the hits on the hero first only when they win from there. `taunt_free` answers another question: which of two positions at the same gap has spent an attacker. A play that opens a prompt (a Cry's Discover, an Activate's choice) is one step split in two, the play and its answer, and answering spends no attack. `taunt_free` reads the attackers after a play that opens no prompt, so it reads them through one that does: the attackers it counts are those the line has not spent. Read as `ready_gap` reads it, a position waiting on its own answer would count as one whose every attacker was spent, and it would come after every attack on a Taunt.
2. *A flat dive set aside* (`lethal_dive_moves`, 75). The walk counts the moves it tries at and below each child of a position, from positions no closer to lethal than that position. Once a child's count passes 75, the child is set aside: every open position below it comes after the other open positions of its gap, and a smaller gap still comes first. So while the gap stands flat, no one child takes the whole allowance, and the walk comes back to a child it set aside once nothing else of that gap is open. A sibling that lowers the gap is still found wherever it stands in move order.
   - *A count, not a share of the allowance.* A bound that is a share of the walk's allowance shrinks with a wide decision's scaled-down allowance ([[R2575000]]). Measured at shares of 0.15, 0.2, 0.33 and 0.5, it lost [[R4788000]]'s reach on one of that ruling's four logged wide lethals: hard-vs-easy 4's turn 8 at 0.15 and 0.2, hard-vs-easy 7's turn 18 at 0.33 and 0.5. A count sets a scaled-down walk's children aside no sooner than an ordinary walk's.
   - *Its window.* With attackers kept, the count was measured on both logged sets below at 40, 50, 60, 65, 75, 90, 120 and 150 moves. Every count from 60 to 150 holds every lethal there. At 40 and 50 it loses hard-vs-easy 7's turn-18 lethal (157 nodes, [[R4788000]]'s), because it sets aside the child that lethal lies below. At 200, measured on the three fixtures alone, it loses hard-vs-easy 8's again, so a named position sets the window's upper edge. The bound was first swept as a count of nodes: on the three fixtures, then on both logged sets at 40, 60, 75 and 100, where 40 lost the same lethal and 60 to 100 held. The count was set at 75 after that sweep, then measured as a count of moves beside the other counts.

**Which rule each position rests on.**
- Mirror 26 rests on the attackers kept: alone they prove it in 218 nodes, and the bound alone needs 291 to 301.
- Hard-vs-easy 8 rests on the bound. Alone, the attackers kept take its plays first and dive into the first play's subtree, and its lethal is not proved within 3,000 nodes. With the bound the walk proves it in 195, by a line of five actions:
  - play S #171 Large Asteroid (`c48`) into Units lane 2;
  - play S #60 Cannon V5 (`c47`) into lane 3;
  - two of the Cannon's `cannon` Activates on the Taunt (`c64`);
  - the hit of the attacker the Taunt walled, S #19 Imprisoned Cosmality (`c59`), on the enemy hero.
  The audit logged ten actions for this lethal. Played through the engine on the fixture and on each sampled world, the five-action line wins the game.
- Hard-vs-easy 12 rests on the read through a prompt. Its line Activates a card whose prompt's answer drops the gap to 0. With the attackers kept and the prompt read as `ready_gap` reads it, that Activate's position comes after every attack on a Taunt, and the lethal takes 1,342 nodes. With the read through the prompt, it takes 192, as before.

**What stays.**
- These are unchanged: the first walk ([[R4807000]]), the visited set's lanes ([[R4806000]]), the trim ([[R1965000]]), the lethal hint ([[R2102000]]) and the concession check ([[R2660000]]). Their tests pass unedited, [[R4788000]]'s included.
- `ready_gap` is unchanged.
- `lethal_keep_attacks` off and `lethal_dive_moves` negative restore the walk before this ruling node for node, on the three fixtures and on both logged sets below.

**What it costs.** Measured on the 57 decisions logged at the audit's lethal turns, within 300 nodes and at 3,000, and on the 162 wide decisions of 24 audit games, within 300 nodes and at their scaled allowances with and without [[R4788000]]'s reach.
- *Nothing lost.* The rule loses no lethal the walk proved within 300 nodes, and it changes the nodes of none of them. Hard-vs-easy 12's turn 21 still takes 192. [[R4788000]]'s four wide lethals still take 213, 146, 157 and 122, and their reach still proves them.
- *Gained within 300 nodes.* Among the 57: mirror 26's turn 12 (218), hard-vs-easy 8's turn 12 (195), and a decision at hard-vs-easy 7's turn 18 (174). Among the 162: that same decision, and ten decisions of the mirror game 24 at turns 14 to 18, in 146 to 293 nodes.
- *Gained within the reach.* Four of those wide decisions are now also proved within their scaled allowance with [[R4788000]]'s reach, where they were not.
- *No-lethal positions.* The 22 logged positions with no lethal that the walk searches out (in 1 to 2,265 nodes), and the 5 wide ones it searches out within 300, cost node for node what they did.

**Rejected.**
- *The attackers kept alone.* They lose hard-vs-easy 8's lethal, above.
- *The attackers kept, with `taunt_free` read as `ready_gap` reads a prompt.* That loses hard-vs-easy 12's, above. It is #4790's first pass's order, and issue #5119 measured it.
- *The bound alone.* It holds the three positions only between 60 and 70 nodes at the 300, and mirror 26 then needs 291 to 296.
- *A bound that is a share of the walk's allowance, and a count under 60.* Both are above.
- *A bound counted only below positions above 0.* It is the same walk on every logged position, so it adds a condition for nothing.
- *A count of the moves below a child that the walk refuted, in place of every move it tried.* No count of them brought all three positions within 300.
- *The positions with more moves, fewer moves, or more crystals left tried first, at the same gap and depth.* Each loses hard-vs-easy 8's lethal or puts mirror 26's past 1,500 nodes.
- *Issue #5119's own table.* Chunks of a position's moves, at gap 0 and above and of sizes 1 and 2, and the tie orders by age, by `taunt_free` and by fewer Taunts.
