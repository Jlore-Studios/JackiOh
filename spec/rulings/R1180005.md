"In your hand or deck: After another Spell of yours that targeted an enemy resolves, cast this at it" (Meditative+ #17 Dogpile). A Spell targets what its target declarations pick — declared with the play ([[R81]]), chosen as a cast's or an Echo repeat's choice, or picked in a `target` prompt its own script opens — and an enemy is the opponent's hero or a Unit acting on the opponent's side when it is picked. While the Spell resolves, its enemy picks are kept on it, in the order they were made; nothing is kept unless its player holds, in their hand or deck, a card that answers such Spells, so no other game changes. At [[§10.5]] step 7 the engine names one card to answer it on its `cardResolved`: the first of those enemies still an enemy on the field (the hero always is), and the leftmost such card in the hand, else the topmost in the deck. That card's hand and deck triggers ([[R464]]) cast it there, free and counted as a play ([[R70]]), aimed ([[R946]]); if the aim is no longer a legal pick of its declaration nothing is cast. Every other copy reads that it is not the one named, so one card answers each Spell, and a card's own cast, a Spell that targeted an enemy, can call the next. A Spell that targeted no enemy, an untargeted Spell, the opponent's Spells and a target gone call nothing. The naming is engine bookkeeping that names a hidden card, so no view forwards it ([[§10.8]], [[R97]]). The designer's "a spell" is another Spell of yours, and "them" the enemy it targeted.
