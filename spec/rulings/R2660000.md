The owner asked that ladder bots concede less often: only when a lethal shows on the board and they have no way to stop it, every bot alike, and never where there is no lethal. [[R639000]] had a bot concede once its deficit on [[R1442]]'s scale passed a threshold drawn from people's concessions, from `min_turn` on, while it trailed, which gave up games a person still had outs in. The spec made no rule for when a bot is beaten, so this follows Hearthstone semantics: a player who concedes on board is one who has counted the opponent's damage and every answer they hold, and found none.

**When.** A bot still concedes only at the start of its own turn, and its temper is still drawn once a match on `{seed}:bot-concede:{home}`: whether it is one who concedes at all (`BOT_CONCEDE_FIT.share`, fitted from people). One who is concedes only when the check below holds. The fit's `min_turn` and its deficit's mean and spread are still fitted by `timing-fit`, and no bot reads them. The check runs a search, so it runs only for a conceder, inside the bot's decision deadline (`BOT_DECISION_DEADLINE_MS`, [[R1452]]), and the decision after it shares what is left. It draws on a stream of its own, so the bot's AI stream, its other streams, the state, the log and a replay are as before.

**The check.** `jackioh_ai::unstoppable_lethal` answers whether the opponent's board has a lethal that nothing the bot does this turn stops, playing both turns through the engine. Like the lethal hint ([[R2102000]]) it reads the true state only through `redact` ([[R185]]), and it counts only what shows: the opponent's hand is taken out, and so is every face-down card of its backrow, beneath a backrow pile or waiting to be set. It fills the rest in as `UNSTOPPABLE_LETHAL_DETERMINIZATIONS` worlds (the AI's own number) drawn from `UNSTOPPABLE_LETHAL_SEED`'s stream. On each, it plays every line of the bot's turn: every legal action but those [[R84]] skips and the mulligan, a position once, "end turn" first. For each line that hands the turn over, it asks the lethal solver of [[§9.9]] for the opponent's lethal within `UNSTOPPABLE_LETHAL_REPLY_NODES`, on the board alone (the card the opponent drew is dropped with its hand), and plays the solver's line again. It holds only when every line on every world ends with the opponent's win, within `UNSTOPPABLE_LETHAL_NODES` in all. Both automatic turn ends are off in its worlds ([[R345]]), so the opponent's turn never runs on into the bot's next draw, and the bot's turn passes only by "end turn", which it reaches anyway once nothing else is left ([[R82]]).

Every doubt plays on. The check is false when the walk is cut short, by its nodes or by the deadline; when a step fails; when a step draws on the game's random numbers or moves a card of the bot's library, since the walk sees one outcome of a roll and one guessed order of a library and any other could hold the escape; when the opponent's line rolls, moves a card of its own library or opens a prompt for the bot, whose answer the solver would only have guessed; on a panic; and at every moment but the bot's own main phase with no prompt open. Two more limits err the same way. A lethal that needs the opponent's turn to end, an end-of-turn trigger's, is not counted, since the solver looks inside one turn. And a turn that ends with the Marketplace open ends without it ([[R1853000]]): its offers at the opponent's turn start are rolled on the game's random numbers, which the check refuses, and a card bought from them is no card on the board, so taking it out takes away only the opponent's options. The bot's own Marketplace, while its turn lasts, is one of its ways out. So a bot that can play a card that rolls or draws never concedes on that turn, even where no outcome of it saves the bot: the walk cannot tell which outcomes do.

**Rejected.** Summing the attack on the opponent's board against the bot's health, which misses the Taunt a unit played in Defense Position has ([[§4.1]]), removal in the bot's hand and the bot's own lethal this turn. Counting the opponent's hand, which is no lethal showing. Keeping the face-down cards the determinization guesses in, since a guessed Trap in every world could stop each of the bot's answers and fake a doom. Trusting the worlds' rolls and draws as samples, which concedes some games a lucky roll or a good draw would have saved. Having every bot concede whatever its temper, which the owner's "all bots" does not ask for: it reads as the rule applying to every bot, and the fitted share of people who never concede is kept.
