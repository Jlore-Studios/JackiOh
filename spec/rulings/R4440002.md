Issue #4440, Stinkyscape+ Balance Changes VI: "AI likes to sell spice more often". S #89 Spice sells by being played or discarded: either pays its quote in Credits, as a Resource does ([[R4410000]], [[R2250001]]). Since issue #4410 the AI priced Spice in its own hand as any (1) card and the Credits once gained ([[R4410001]]), so a cast weighed the Discover and the Credits against the card and the crystal spent: one that brought only its Credits, a Vanilla Spice's, read as a loss and was held.

An own hand card that pays Credits as a Resource does (`pays_credits_like_a_resource`, the flag the payment reads, so a Vanilla Spice too) is now held at `AI_EVAL.credits_card_discount` (one half) off its hand price, after a dead Tradable card's discount ([[R4154400]]), as Credits waiting to be cashed: holding it is worth less than a card kept for its play, so every line that plays or discards it gains that much more than it did, and the AI sells it in positions where it held it. With one crystal to spare and a quote of 8, a Vanilla Spice's cast moves the evaluation by the 8 Credits' worth (0.24) less the card (0.65, where it was 1.3), and the crystal it spends is worth 0.8 more than one left unspent: a gain of about 0.39 where it was a loss of about 0.26. It reads the seat's own hand only, which the seat sees; the enemy's hand stays priced as unseen cards, so no hidden card moves a decision. `GREEDY_EVAL`, the frozen baseline, keeps it at 0. This is a judgement about the AI, not a rule of the game, and changes no game.

Rejected: pricing every Credit higher, which would move every Resource's quote and every buy, as [[R4410001]] rejected; counting the quote on top of the hand price, which [[R4410001]] rejected as holding Spice back.
