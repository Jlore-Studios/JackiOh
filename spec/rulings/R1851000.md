[[R703]] lets a declared target keep a play in hand while the board cannot answer it, as Hearthstone keeps a spell that needs a target in hand when nothing can be targeted. A card whose play needs something on the board to act on but declares no target had no way to say so: Meditative #53 Prestige's Radiant face, "De-Radiant every enemy card on the field", acts on every card of a kind and picks none. Hearthstone keeps such a card in hand too (a play requirement, such as "an enemy minion"), so the engine gains a play gate, `Script.play_allowed`: a pure read of the board, `(state, player) -> bool`, which reads only what its player may see ([[§9.1]]).

- **One check.** `play_choices::play_allowed` decides it. `legal_actions` lists no play of a card whose gate does not hold (`listed_play_choices` gives it no combination); the reducer refuses one at [[§10.5]] step 1, ahead of its targets and modes, with "<name> cannot be played without something to act on"; and the viewer's own hand card carries that refusal as `playReason` ([[R979000]], [[§10.8]]). A greyed-out card and a refusal cannot disagree.
- **The face the play resolves.** The gate is read off the face [[§10.5]] step 3 will resolve, as the play's declarations are ([[R214]]): a base card that a rule makes Radiant as it is played (Prestige's own next play, [[R831005]]) answers to its Radiant face's gate.
- It changes no price, zone or Tribute, and a card without one is played as before. Who else asks it, and who does not (a cast, an Echo repeat, a random cast, a set Spell's Reveal), is [[R1851001]]; a fusion and a copier are [[R1851002]].
