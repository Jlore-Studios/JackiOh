Every 1v1 door seats its two profiles by a coin drawn from the match or series seed on a stream of its own, `{seed}:seats` at cursor 0 (`SEAT_COIN_LABEL`), beside the dealt-deck streams (`{seed}:p1-deck`, [[R258]]) and the emote stream (`{seed}:emotes:{seat}`, [[R1341]]). The coin reads only the seed, so a fixed seed set deals both seat orders across seeds and the same seed always seats the same way, and since no game state enters it a replay folds to its hash unchanged. This covers every door a 1v1 game starts through: queue pairs (Best of 1 and All Random), Conquest series starts, room joins, ladder-bot pairings, the kickoff of a match between two bots while the top bots grind ([[R1555000]]), and the bot half of a Conquest series ([[R641000]]).

At a queue pair the coin seats the older ticket; at a room the host; at a bot pairing the person (or the grinder the kickoff names first). Decks, portraits, slots and leans travel with the profile: a Best-of-1 seat plays its own frozen deck, an All Random seat the deck dealt for its new seat leaning on the set its own player picked, if any. A Conquest series' sides index 0 is the coin winner on the seed base, and [[R335]]'s alternation runs from there: the coin winner goes first in the odd games. The ladder bots now start from the first seat as often as the second, so their ratings ([[R610]]) stop being measured from one seat only.

A rematch flips no coin: it swaps the seats of the finished match, the previous P2 taking P1 with that profile's deck, portrait, slots and lean and the previous P1 taking P2 with theirs. A double-or-nothing rides the same swap ([[R672]]), as do the bot's rematch offers ([[R639000]]).

Seeded end-to-end specs pin the seats they were written for the way they pin the seed ([[R143]]): the queue and room endpoints accept an optional `seat`, `"p1"` or `"p2"`, in end-to-end mode and refuse it elsewhere, and a honored pin skips the coin. A conflict pins the precedence-first profile — the older ticket, the host — and the other takes the free seat. [[R1453]]'s old reason against a coin, that a coin would put a lone player second half the time, which no pairing with a person does, falls away with the older-ticket rule it leaned on: every pairing is a coin now.

Where the spec gives the first turn to Player 1 ([[§2.1]] step 5) but does not say who sits there, this follows Hearthstone, which tosses for it.
