Once a board replacement has laid its whole preset ([[R1855000]]), the Cry of each Unit it laid in the unit row fires, in the preset's order: every Cry fires, with its targets and choices random (the designer's answer, `docs/stinkyscape-set.md` ST9 Q7). Each is a triggered Cry ([[R467]]) for the replacement's caster under [[R452]]'s random mode, so the caster is never asked: its declared targets and modes are drawn from the match rng, as is every prompt its own text opens for the caster. The other player's prompts are asked as usual, and a Cry paused by one stays random once it is answered: the rest of it, the answered step and the parked tail alike ([[R113]]), still draws every choice of the caster's, and the Cries that follow it are random too. A delayed effect such a Cry sets up ([[R62]]) is a run of its own, and its choices are its controller's.

Only a card the replacement laid fires, found by a mark it carries from its arrival until its Cry, wherever on the field it acts by then: a laid Unit an earlier Cry has moved fires where it stands. A laid Unit that has left the field ([[R174]]), say exiled by an earlier laid Cry, lost its mark with the rest of its memory ([[R78]]) and fires nothing, and neither does a card that has taken its zone. A replacement first takes off any mark an earlier one left behind. A laid Unit that lies beneath a Stack or is Dormant has no Cry to trigger ([[R467]]), and a card the replacement left standing fires nothing. Firing each Cry as its card lands was rejected: an early Cry would act on a half-laid board, and a later entry's zone could be taken by what it made.
