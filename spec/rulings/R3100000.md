The tutorial's coach used to rank the attacks it names with combat maths of its own: an attack less the target's Armor, and nothing through a Divine Shield. That is a rule in the client (CLAUDE.md rule 7), which could drift from the engine and which a second client would have to port. The engine now answers it (`combat_forecast`, `crates/engine/src/query.rs`): for each attack a seat may declare, in `legalActions`' order,

- `dealt`: the damage the blow does to its target, through the damage pipeline's own steps ([[§4.4]]): a Divine Shield negates it, Armor takes its part unless the attacker pierces ([[R346]]), the target's own cap comes next, an Indestructible unit takes nothing, and a hero's hit is [[R44]]'s projection (Armor, divisors, the Anti-oneshot cap);
- `taken`: what the target's strike back does to the attacker ([[§4.3]]): none from a hero, none to a Ranged attacker, none when a First Strike attacker destroys its target, and none landed by an attacker that a First Strike defender destroys first;
- `kills`: the blow destroys its target (any damage, from a Poisonous attacker), or for a hero ends the game, which a hero that cannot lose never suffers ([[R2201000]]);
- `survives`: the attacker outlasts the exchange, a Poisonous defender's strike back included;
- `pops`: the blow breaks a Divine Shield;
- the attacker's and the target's size, each unit's Attack plus its Health, which the coach ranks its picks by.

The forecast also says whether the attackers that may reach the enemy hero, one blow each, deal it its whole life between them (`lethal`). It reads the stats and keywords the layers give every unit and each hero's Armor and life, and it reads no hand, library or face-down card, so it shows nothing the board does not ([[R292]]). A trap or another answer from the opponent's hidden cards is not in it. The practice worker asks it of a tutorial lesson's game beside the view and the legal actions; the client picks among the numbers and computes none, and the lessons' scripts are unchanged ([[R293]]).

A test holds it to the engine's combat: hundreds of random boards of bodies and keywords, in which every forecast attack is played for real and the damage events, the shield lost and the units left on the field must equal the forecast.

Hearthstone semantics: damage, Armor and Divine Shield read as the pipeline reads them; the forecast is a derived fact, not a rule.

Rejected: simulating each attack with `reduce` on a copy (it would run the opponent's face-down traps and hand, which the forecast must not read); a second copy of the maths in TypeScript, now removed.

Amended by [[R3477000]] (#3477): every seat's view carries the forecast, not a lesson's alone, and aiming an attack outside a lesson shows it.
