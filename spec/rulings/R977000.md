[[R186]]'s sweep flags `timeout` on a decision slower than `AI_SWEEP.decisionMs` (2,000 ms) and on a game still running at 600 actions. It read the raw wall clock, so a busy machine could ban a card: four of the 2026-10-12 sweep's 24 timeouts cleared when they were run again alone ([[R732000]]). Now a decision the clock finds slow is timed again as the timing gate times one ([[§9.9]]): up to `AI_GATE.perfRepeats` (3) runs of the same state, seat, budget and AI stream, each right after the yardstick, the smallest ratio scaled by `calibrationRefMs`. Only a decision still over `decisionMs` that way counts in `timeouts`. `rawTimeouts` counts every decision the clock found, and both count a game past 600 actions; each sweep line and each report row shows both, and a slice written before reads the raw count as 0. Suspects and ban reasons use the counted timeouts. `crates/ai` reads no clock: the sweep hands the position (state, seat, budget and the AI's stream before the decision) to a re-timer its caller passes (`retime`), and without one every slow decision counts. The yardstick measures the development machine, not a phone, as the timing gate does. `SHADOW_BAN` changes only when a person runs the sweep again and copies its rows in by hand.
