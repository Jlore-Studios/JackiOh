**The registry.** The full art of each hero portrait's card ([[R1017001]]: Big D-fender, Gary the Gambler, Mr. Vanilla, Tempo Timmy, Duplicating Felinors and Jlockeed Shredder-10) is a per-card cosmetic of its own kind, `fullArt`, in the registry of [[R1295000]]: one entry per card, naming its card by catalog id (`full-art-mr-vanilla` on `core-008`, and so on). All six are **Epic**. A cosmetic's rarity is a scale of its own, not its card's, so every full art is Epic whatever its card's rarity: a card pack's duplicate of that card that rolls an Epic cosmetic, a cosmetic pack or an Epic cosmetic token pays one, and a Rare token never does. No full art is a default, a pick or a chase: the kind has no default, the settings offer no select for it, and the generated `COSMETIC_KINDS`, the kinds a player picks one of, leave it out. The registry's check refuses a full art that names no card, a card with two full arts, a full art marked as a default, and a cosmetic of any other kind that names a card. `public.cosmetics` keeps no card: an owned full art's card is the `card_id` of its ownership row ([[§14.2]]).

**The gate.** Once ownership decides, a copy of one of the six prints its full art only when it is the viewer's own copy and the viewer owns its full art, the Full-art cards setting is on and the board is not a tutorial lesson's ([[R1108000]]). The viewer's own copies are their own cards, by owner, in hand and in play on a board they play (the cards that wear their card cosmetics, [[R1295000]]), the hover preview and touch sheet of those cards, and every face of the deck builder, its detail view included; they read the viewer's own ownership. Every other copy prints its normal face: the opponent's, a teammate's, a watched game's, those of an online match's replay, a co-op one's or a replay file's, a prompt's options, the showcase of a card played and the piles. The client is told only its own account's cosmetics and never infers another's ([[§14.2]]): showing the opponent's waits for the `Cosmetics` frame and a ruling of its own. While the read is pending, and when it fails, nothing counts as owned.

**The read.** `GET /api/cosmetics`, a route for an active account, answers the caller's own cosmetics, keyed on the verified token's profile and never on the request: `{ cards: [{ cardId, cosmeticId }], account: [id] }`, the per-card ones by card and then cosmetic, the account ones by id. The starter cosmetics have no row and are not listed. A caller with no token is refused (401), and a pending account is turned away (403). The client reads it over every gated screen, once per provider session (a renewed token of the same session keeps the answer, a failed read is read again), and only while ownership decides.

**Where every full art shows.** The landing page's fan ([[R1017002]]) and the Almanac keep showing every full art, so a player sees what there is to chase. Practice, hotseat, a practice game's replay and every development and end-to-end build treat every full art as owned, as they treat every card and cosmetic ([[§14.12]]), but a development build that the one end-to-end spec walking the economy asks to gate ([[R1310004]]).

**The switch.** Ownership decides only while the server's `ECONOMY_LIVE` is on, in a production build (and in that one development build, [[R1310004]]). Until the going-live sub-issue (#1310) turned it on, nobody was gated: every copy of the six printed its full art as [[R1017001]] says, and nothing was read. Its migration took back every full art owned before then, so nobody owned one at the launch ([[R1310002]]). [[§14.2]]'s sentence that a full art took its card's rarity came before this ruling; §14.2 now says every full art is Epic ([[R1333000]]).

Presentation only (CLAUDE.md rule 7): no rule reads a full art, and nothing here enters `PlayerView`, an action, the log or a replay hash.
