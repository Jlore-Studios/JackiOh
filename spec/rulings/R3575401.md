A Flying Unit may be played into a backrow zone, where it stands as a Unit ([[R1081000]]), and the AI's search read each such zone as a position of its own: `search_signature` printed a standing Unit twice, once among the active units and again in the backrow's slots, where its lane is part of the text. Two placements of one play that the evaluation scores alike were therefore two positions, where the same two in the unit row are one position and a lane variant of it (#1797, whose lanes live in `unit_lanes`). On a board with room in several backrow zones the beam filled its width with one card's placements, so the other plays of the turn were never played out. [[R2490000]] had already settled that a standing Unit is priced once, as the Unit it is; this settles that it is placed once too. No Hearthstone rule is involved: the search changes no game.

**What it counts.** `search_signature` leaves a standing Unit out of its backrow text, as [[R2490000]]'s evaluation leaves it out of the backrow's worth: it is already in the signature among the active units, by its id, damage, buffs, exertion and position. In its place the side says how many of its Units stand in its backrow, and never which lanes they take: that count is what the rest of the position turns on, since it says how many zones each row has left for a play, while the lanes belong to `unit_lanes`. So two backrow placements of one play share a signature, and a Unit in the backrow never shares one with the same Unit in the unit row, which offers different plays — the signature alone is what the lethal solver's visited set merges on, and it still holds that two positions it merges offer the same moves. `unit_lanes` says a standing Unit's row as well as its lane, so a backrow lane is never the same placement as the unit row's lane of the same number, and the beam's duplicate check — the signature and the lanes read together — keeps the two apart as two lane variants of one position. A board with no standing Unit gives exactly the signature it gave before.

**Rejected.** Leaving the standing Unit in the backrow's text and taking it out of `unit_lanes`, which would keep each backrow lane a position of its own; reading the backrow slot without its lane, which would merge two placements the reply scores differently (a Cleave into a unit's neighbours); and saying nothing at all in the backrow's place, which merged a Unit standing in the backrow with the same Unit in the unit row though the two offer different plays (`two_one_step_children_with_one_signature_score_the_same_and_offer_the_same_moves`).
