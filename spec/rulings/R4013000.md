A 1v1 action nonce is only its own seat's duplicate check ([[§9.3]]): the seat the server stamps on the row ([[§9.1]]) owns the nonce, and a resend by that seat answers its original ack with no second row. Another seat sending a copy of the nonce is answered `taken` ("that nonce is already taken"): the owner's action still applies, and the copy applies nothing as either seat. A nonce is never proof of who sent it — the seat is the server's stamp, never the client's word — and the server's own nonces stay out of reach behind [[R270]]'s prefix, so none of this lets a seat pre-empt a clock's action. The 1v1 machine answers as the co-op host's does.

Decided here, each with what was rejected:

- Global dedup (any resend of a known nonce answers its ack, whoever sends it): rejected. A copy from the other seat came back as the owner's ack, so a nonce doubled as proof of who sent it: nothing refused the copy and nothing marked it as not the owner's.
- Per-seat-independent nonces (each seat's copy logs its own row under the same string): rejected. The store's append-only nonce key stays global per match ([[R776000]]), so a second row under the same nonce cannot land without a migration. First-writer-wins stays: a nonce string the other seat sends first is taken for the later sender too, which in practice means guessing a per-tab unique string (`nextNonce` mixes time, counter and random).
