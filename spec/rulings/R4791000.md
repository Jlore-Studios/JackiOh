The beam closes every line it leaves open: `close_line` ends the turn of a line still in the seat's main phase, one node, a passed line pays for the crystals it left unspent (`passed_score`), and `decide` scores the best lines again after the opponent's reply ([[§9.9]]). A beam its budget stopped had no node left for those End turns: its slice refuses once spent, and `end_exploring` lifts only a host's wrap-up, so the lines a clock cut short end their turn ([[R3498000]]) and those a budget cut short did not. So each line it was still playing was filed open, mid-turn, scored without the price of its unspent mana, and `reply_score` gave it that same score again, unreplied, beside the lines that had ended their turn and been replied to: a line the cut left unfinished won for a reason that has nothing to do with how good it is. The audit of issue #4787 counted 35 of 1,352 searched decisions stopped on the budget, 24 of which played a line left open, against 32% of all searched decisions. The same stop also closed again the frontier lines the beam had finished expanding, which had filed their End turn already, so such a line held two of its first action's `AI_SEARCH.lines_per_action` places in the shortlist.

**The rule.** The AI decides this. On a stop the beam closes only the frontier line the stop cut short, the frontier lines after it, the children it kept and their lane variants. A line `close_line` could not end the turn of, still open in the seat's main phase, is ranked as a passed line: its hand eval less `AI_EVAL.unspent_mana` for each crystal it holds, with no Float credit ([[R3695000]]), since a turn not ended has had no Float cut to credit. Where the beam's slice refused a node, to its budget or to the host's wrap-up, `decide` ends the turn of each shortlisted line still open in the seat's main phase, one simulated End turn on the decision's own nodes, just before it replies to it, and replies to the line from there; a node refused there ends the replies as a refused reply does, so no line is scored unreplied beside replied ones for want of a node. A line open on the seat's own prompt stays as it was. `SearchStats.replied` counts a line so closed and replied to. `AI_SEARCH.close_cut_lines` off restores the old behaviour.

**What stays.** A beam whose slice refused no node ended every line's turn inside its slice, so its decision takes the nodes, the lines and the scores it did: of 638 decisions on 212 states of six random-policy games at 1,200, 600 and 150 nodes, the 512 that nothing stopped were the same node for node. The nodes the End turns take come from what the decision kept back for the replies and the other worlds, never from the beam's slice ([[R4604014]]), so they come only on a decision that was cut. The root's End turn ([[R5044000]]) is filed as before, and a wrap-up's stop ([[R4801000]]) closes, like a budget's, only the lines it had not finished.

**What it costs.** Of those 638 decisions, the 126 stopped on the budget played 18 other first actions. Against the action the same state's 1,200-node decision played, the 600-node decisions on three worlds that were cut kept 15 of 19 where they had kept 16, and the 150-node ones on one world kept 79 of 100 where they had kept 81. A line cut before its later plays is now priced as if it ended its turn where the cut left it, so it pays for crystals its full turn would have spent; that favors a line that spends its crystals in one play over one the cut stopped short, as the unpriced score favored the unfinished line before, and neither is the score its whole turn gets. Its deal screen, 400 games on improve-dev against the AI without the rule, read 200-200 with 6 deals decided (3 each way) and 25 of 200 played differently: inconclusive, so it says nothing of strength either way and the rule rests on its scene tests. At the practice budget a decision is seldom cut (6 of the sample's 212 at 1,200 nodes), so few of the screen's decisions met the rule.

**Rejected.** Keeping back one node per shortlisted line from the beam's slice: every beam would explore less, the uncut ones too. Ending the turn of every open line on the reserve: up to `beam_width × branching` End turns, past what the reply keeps back. Pricing the unended line with `passed_score`'s Float credit, which reads costs a Float has already cut.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.
