**Why.** A step of the search cost one node however much it ran, so a decision whose candidates run long chains spent its node budget at a far higher cost on the clock. On the sweep's seed `sweep:easy:infiniteassault-029:6` (turn 18), the AI's Hive Overseer stood behind the enemy's Suppressive Aura: each simulated play of a 1/1 Drone summoned a Drone that died at once, Hive Overseer summoned another, and Core Dump shuffled a copy of each, until the engine's cap: 2,002 events and about 200 ms for one node. The decision took 3.5 s on the sweep's yardstick ([[R977000]]), past `AI_SWEEP.decision_ms`.

**The rule.** Every simulated step (a candidate's, an auto-answer's, a reply's) pays, after it runs, one more node for every `AI_SEARCH.events_per_node` (100) events it emitted past `AI_SEARCH.free_events` (1,000), rounded down, as many as the counter still gives, as an announced expensive card's charge is paid. Such a step counts as expensive for the decision's step memo ([[R3575603]]). A step of at most 1,100 events pays its one node as before.

**Scope.** Charging from the first event forked shipped decks' games on the fork screen (R4397000; with R4595002's first reading, 20 forks over 200 games, `forkEdge` -0.031, inconclusive). The heaviest step of 200 fuzz games of the Infinite Assault emitted 375 events, the Drone loop 2,002, so the first thousand are free and only a loop toward the engine's cap pays.

**Rejected.** A charge from the first event: it re-priced ordinary steps of shipped decks.
