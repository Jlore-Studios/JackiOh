The flag ([[R1300]]) is public as `radiant` is. Every view of a card the viewer may read carries it, `chinese: true` on the card's view (a hand card, a unit, a backrow card, a pile's card and a prompt's option), and no view of a card the viewer may not read does: never the hidden sentinel, a card in someone else's hand, a library card, or a face-down card the viewer does not control ([[R97]], [[R177]], [[R351]]). The owner's library list keeps its records as they were shown ([[R311]]), so a deck translated in place shows each card in Chinese as it is drawn.

`translated { instanceId }` reports a translation only where both players read the card (a unit, a face-up backrow card). A change in a hand, a deck or on a face-down trap is silent ([[R440]]), and its owner reads it off their own view; an event whose card has since moved where a player may not read it names the hidden id to that player. The board animates it with a short pulse, it plays no sound, and the log prints no line for it.

In a match, a Chinese card is drawn only from the Chinese tables ([[R1303]]): its name, both faces' text with their params filled as the English is filled, its type line, tags, keywords, glossary entries, the cards its text names ([[R279]], marked by their Chinese names) and its preview labels ([[R280]], filled with the face's numbers), on every surface the match draws it (the hand, the board, the backrow, a prompt's options, the log's names for it and the inspect overlays). It never falls back to its English, because the card's joke is "Get ready to learn Chinese". A fused card's Chinese name and text are its ingredients' Chinese, joined as their English is ([[R468]]). A match-made definition with no catalog entry, such as Craft a Card's, has no Chinese and keeps its English. Outside a match (the Almanac, the deck builder) every card shows its catalog face. The AI reads no text, so nothing changes for it.
