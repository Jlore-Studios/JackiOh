**Why.** The beam ranks an open line by the position it reaches. A play whose worth is the plays it makes cheaper this turn, Infinite Assault #72 Overclock to Infinity's "Your cards cost (0) this turn", adds nothing to the board, so a play that adds a unit outranks it a step in and the beam's width cut it before the cards it pays for were tried. The sweep found the AI never playing it: on its Radiant face at Hard, affordable in hand on 31 turns over 24 pass-2 games, never played.

**The rule.** A line is a discount line once one of its steps plays an `AI_SEARCH.discount_def_ids` card (Overclock to Infinity alone) and leaves some card of the seat's hand, one it held before that step, at a lower `play_cost` than before it. At each depth, after the beam keeps its best `beam_width` children, it keeps as many more discount lines, best first, as `AI_SEARCH.discount_lines` allows beyond those it already kept (1). They are played on and closed like any other line, so the end-of-turn score, a delayed blowback included, still judges whether the discount was worth it.

**Scope.** The fork screen on the improve lane's dev set (R4397000) with the rule reading every cheapening step forked shipped decks' games (20 forks over 200 games, `forkEdge` -0.031, interval -0.127 to 0.064, inconclusive), since many shipped cards discount a hand; so the rule reads the listed cards only, and no shipped card is listed.

**Rejected.** Any step that cheapened the hand: it reached shipped cost reducers, above.
