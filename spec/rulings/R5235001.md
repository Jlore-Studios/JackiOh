The Felinors vs Humans set ([[R5235000]]) reprints 82 Infinite Assault cards as FvH #116 to #197, in the designer's list's order. Each reprint is a card of its own: its own catalog entry and id (`felinorsvshumans-116` and on), named `Plagiarized: <its Infinite Assault card's name>`, and its own script file in the set's folder, a **frozen copy** of its Infinite Assault card's script with its own `ID`, as each Legacy card copies its Core card ([[R2195003]]). A later patch to the Infinite Assault card never moves the reprint, a replay of a game it was played in folds under its own history ([[R767000]]), and its code metrics, its code lines ([[R1846000]]) and its code block ([[R1183004]]) among them, are read from its own file. Where its source reads its own id, the reprint reads its own: a reprint that shuffles copies of itself into a deck shuffles reprints.

Its only tag is Plagiarized ([[R5235002]]), the Infinite Assault card's `AI` and doctrine tags left behind, but for FvH #196 Plagiarized: Singularity, which also carries Wincon. Its faces, cost, stats and rarity are its source's but for the changes the list names (`docs/felinors-vs-humans-set.md` FV4, which are catalog data on the reprint's entry or a frozen copy's own parameters). A token a reprint makes is its source's token, the Infinite Assault's, unchanged.

The reprint and its source are two cards in every rule, as a Legacy card and its Core card are ([[R2511001]]): each is counted by its own id, and a pool that reaches both may hold both. While the Infinite Assault is a single-player set ([[R4577000]]), no deck holds its source at all.

Hearthstone semantics: a reprint in a new set is a new card with its own id, which a later change to the old card does not touch.
