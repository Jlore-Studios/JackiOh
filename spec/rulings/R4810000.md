The AI scores its best lines after the opponent's reply ([[§9.9]], The opponent's reply): a model of the opponent's turn that takes at most `AI_REPLY.max_steps` (12) engine steps, one thing each (a play of a card the AI watched arrive, then a visible Activate, a Deft switch, an attack or a switch of Position), and handed back the state wherever its twelfth step left it. Stinkyscape #66 Miner v5, "Activate 5: Gain a random Stinkyscape Resource" at no cost ([[R384]], [[R1872000]]), took two steps for each use: the Resource it gains is a new card the AI watched arrive, so the reply plays it on the next step, and five uses and five plays are ten of the twelve steps, all of them before the attacks. Issue #4810 measured it on 3a83bd7, seed `audit-reply-miner-7`: the AI at 12 with no Unit against three 4/4s and a Miner v5, a board the 4/4s alone win. The reply made two attacks and stopped with the opponent still on turn 10, the AI at 2 and one 4/4 unswung, and the line was scored as survived. In a fuzz of 12 random-policy games, 5 of 154 replies stopped mid-turn this way, all beside a Miner.

**The rule.** The known plays (`known_play`) and visible Activates (`visible_activation`) a reply makes take at most `AI_REPLY.play_steps` of its `max_steps` steps together; once it has made that many, a step tries neither, and goes on to its Deft switches, its attacks and its switches of Position as before. When its `max_steps` steps are spent with the opponent still on turn, the reply ends that turn: it takes up to `AI_REPLY.close_steps` more steps, the endTurn and the prompts the turn's end opens, each answered as every reply prompt is (`best_auto_answer`), so the line is scored at the AI's next decision, as every other reply's is. Each of those steps costs a node, as every step does: a counter that runs dry there cuts the reply (`None`), and a refused endTurn ends the reply where it stands, as a refused one did before.

**Its values.** `play_steps` is 6, so the 12 steps keep one attack for each of a full row's `UNIT_ZONES` (5) Units and the endTurn; `close_steps` is 3, the endTurn and two prompts its turn's end may open. Only a reply that would make more than six plays and Activates, or that would take more than 12 steps, plays differently, so the boards that move are those with a many-use Activate, a Miner above all. The two together were screened (`training/improve.md`, A screen): 400 games on the improve lane's dev set against a control built from e3fe5705e3 read 199-201, an edge of -0.005 (-0.022 to 0.012), with 3 decided deals of 200 (won 1, lost 2) and 17 diverged, every game clean. Below `SCREEN_MIN_DECIDED`'s 20 decided deals the screen is inconclusive ([[R4319000]]) and neither keeps nor drops the change, so its scene tests settle it, as [[R4433000]]'s did.

Rejected:
- Returning `None` at the cap: it reads as the counter running dry, and the line loses its score.
- Raising `max_steps`: an unlimited Activate still crowds out the attacks, and every reply's worst case costs more nodes.
- Attacking before Activates: [[R4515000]]'s order has an Activate that removes a Taunt go first.
- Counting Deft trials, switches and prompt answers in `play_steps`: each is bounded by the Units on the field, or forced.
- Reserving the last of the 12 steps for the endTurn: the prompts it opens would go unanswered.
- A share of `max_steps` instead of a count for `play_steps`: the two read the same at 12 steps, and a count says what it holds back.

Hearthstone has no counterpart: it is how the AI imagines its opponent's turn and adds no rule of the game; its constants are `AI_REPLY.play_steps` and `AI_REPLY.close_steps`.
