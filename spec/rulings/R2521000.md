The owner's first Stinkyscape balance pass (issue #2521) asks that a Starship Piece that dies fuse "its base version" into its owner's Dormant Starship: a Flak v3 with another card fused into it fuses a Flak v3, not the fusion. [[R1852000]]'s copy was the card as its graveyard holds it, its definition, its Radiant face, its tuning and a token's X/X, so a fusion fused whole and a Buffed piece fused Buffed. The copy is now the piece's base version (`starship::piece_copy`), read off the card as it stood ([[R89]]):

- **The definition.** A card that is no fusion is its own base version. A fusion's is the card the others were fused into, its last ingredient (`CardDef.ingredients`): the card the Fuse kept, an Equip's host or a Fuse's target ([[R77]]), the card [[R1852001]] reads the keyword off; a last ingredient that is itself a fusion is walked the same way. So a piece with a card equipped onto it fuses itself, whatever was equipped onto it, another piece included, and a host that is a piece only by a lend or a grant ([[R1878001]], [[R1878003]]) fuses itself too: an Embiggened S #127 Siege Tank with a Hull Booster equipped onto it fuses the Siege Tank. Taking the first ingredient that prints the keyword was rejected: it would fuse the Hull Booster, a card the owner's example leaves behind.
- **The face.** Radiant when the dead card was, so it fuses on its Radiant face ([[R469]]), and it keeps a token's X/X (`statsOverride`, `armorOverride`, [[R175]]) and its language ([[R1300]]), as [[R57]]'s library copy keeps them.
- **The numbers.** Its tuning is not copied: a Buff, Nerf or Upgrade (`tuning`, [[R386]]) was the dead card's, not the piece's, so the piece fuses with its printed numbers. Buffs, damage and granted keywords were never copied ([[R78]]).
- **Seen by both players.** The Starship's memory ([[R1852005]]) and `starshipFused` name the base definition; its `pieceId` still names the dead card, hidden as before when that card is where the viewer may not read it.

A fresh piece fused by an effect ([[R1882008]]) is a catalog card, already its own base version, so nothing there changes. The spec says nothing of which printed piece in a fusion is its base, nor of what a dead card's enchantments become, so this follows Hearthstone, where a minion summoned again from the dead comes back as its card, without the enchantments and Magnetic upgrades it carried (Kangor's Endless Army has to say its Mechs keep theirs), and where a Magnetic upgrade's card is the one that went into the Mech, not the Mech; [[R1852001]]'s kept card is the Mech.
