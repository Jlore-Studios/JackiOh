[[R4801000]] and [[R4604014]] meant the beam always to close a line, the root's End turn among them: the host's wrap-up and the beam's slice bind only once every move at the root is tried. The decision's own limit still binds there, and a root move whose one simulation draws every node the decision has left was cut by it: `beam_search` stopped at the cut, before End turn, the root's last move (`expand`), and so ended with no line, and `decide` fell back. In #5044's position (the enemy commander on the 2026-11-28 Assault's level 9, turn 16, at the test budget of 150 nodes) the lethal search left 72, and the root's first move, a play of Meditative #21, drew all 72.

**The rule.** At the root the beam simulates End turn before its other moves and files that line in End turn's own place: the loop reuses the stored result when it reaches End turn, and a cut that ends the loop sooner files it after the loop, as a complete line or, when ending the turn leaves the seat a prompt, an open one. With nodes to spare the root's lines are the ones they were, in their order, at the same node count, so every decision is the one it was; only a root cut by the decision's limit or its clock now keeps End turn's line. Nothing changes when End turn's own simulation is cut, or when the root holds no End turn ([[R4604014]]'s two answers to the seat's own prompt). A step refused for a surcharge it cannot pay is skipped, not a cut ([[R4807000]]), as before.

**Rejected.** Moving End turn to the front of the root's list: a tie between complete lines would then go to ending the turn, changing decisions that never ran short. Keeping nodes back for End turn from the lethal search or the reply: every decision would pay for the rare one. Answering an empty beam with End turn as the decision: a fallback by another name.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.
