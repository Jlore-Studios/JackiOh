A prompt for the player who is not active, in the middle of a turn (a trap that fires on its owner's opponent's turn, say), is R79's 30-second prompt clock in a live match. A daily game has no seconds to give: the prompt hands that player the move, with the whole time per move from the moment it opened, and their answer hands it back to the active player with the whole time again. The clock is per move, as on chess.com: each time the move passes to a player, they get the full time per move, and time a move did not use is not carried over.

A hold keeps its deadline while the same seat holds the same move (the mulligan, or one player's turn), so every action of a turn, the active player's own prompts among them, spends from one deadline; a move that passes away and back, or a new turn, is a new hold. `jackioh-shared`'s `daily_sync` decides it from the state alone (`daily_holders`: the open prompt's holder, whoever's turn it is). A Glitch's seat swap ([[R677]]) hands the seat holding the move to the other account mid-turn, so it passes the move too: that account gets the whole time per move from the swap, never what was left of the other's. A move that runs out loses ([[R3727000]]).
