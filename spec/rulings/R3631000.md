A micro's letters count as a spreadsheet counts its columns, the line's own name standing for `a`: after `vA.B.C` a micro is `vA.B.Cb`, after `vA.B.Cy` it is `vA.B.Cz`, after `vA.B.Cz` `vA.B.Caa`, then `ab` … `az`, `ba` … `zz` and `aaa`. A line never runs out of names, and only a patch moves `C`: a patch after `vA.B.Caz` is `vA.B.(C+1)`, and a major `vA.(B+1).0`, as [[R1366000]] has it. A name that already shipped takes its next revision by the same letters, past `z` too ([[R646]]). Every reader of a version takes its letters as a run, not as one letter: `patches ship`, the version scanner a replay's renames go through ([[R1371000]]) and the bot's title checks (`Patch v0.5.13aa: …`, `patches ship: v0.5.13aa`).

The micros [[R3471000]] named after `v0.5.12z`, v0.5.13 to v0.5.13h, take these letters too: the v0.5.13 retcon ([[R3631001]]) names them v0.5.12aa to v0.5.12ah.

Why: the owner asked for it (#3631). [[R3471000]]'s rollover gave a micro a patch's name, which reads as a patch to anyone who sees only the name, and the v0.5.13 line, at v0.5.13g, would roll over again after 19 more micros.

Rejected: refusing to ship past `z`, as `patches ship` did before #3471. Leaving the v0.5.13 line its patch-form names was weighed against a retcon, which bumps the versions scheme and renames every row and record that names them, for names no reader keys off ([[R105]]); the owner asked for the retcon ([[R3631001]]).
