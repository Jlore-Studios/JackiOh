**Why.** [[R3067000]] keeps one snapshot of every patch's totals, and every kept window, history and play versions' table is cut from it. On 2026-11-25 its card totals were about 330,000 rows on staging and production. Each row costs about 1.2 KB of peak memory since #4964, and the rows grow with every patch and every gathered development run. The game server's 1 GiB holds about twice that before the kill #4964 fixed comes back, and that kill drops every live game on the box: the win-rate snapshot each match starts with reads the same snapshot ([[R3049000]]).

**What the snapshot holds** (`api::stats::snapshot_filing`). The records and game totals, one row per key and none per card, are read whole first. The windows every card shares are read off them (`patch_windows`, [[R1973002]]), and the card totals are then read as follows:

- **As filed:** every version from the first of the window before the oldest patch anyone may read (`public_patch_count`, [[R4677000]]) through the newest. A card's window at a patch opens no earlier than the window every card shares at it, and the window before its own opens no earlier than the shared window before that ([[R1367000]]). So every card's figures at every public patch, the game figures and the drill-down's history and drills are cut from it as before, figure for figure. So are the build's own totals, when its version is no shipped one.
- **Folded:** before that run, each deck card's totals are added into one per play version ([[R4675000]]) and key, filed under the play version's first version. The play versions' table ([[R4675001]]), which anyone reads over every patch, sums a play version's versions, so it sums what it summed.
- **Dropped:** every other card total before that run: a token's, and one filed where its card has no play version. No figure read them.

The rows held are the reach's, plus at most one per play version and key before it. The reach is the newest line, two patches of the line before and one window more, or the five newest and one more, so it starts again at every major. Before it, the rows grow only with play versions, a card's changes, and no longer with every patch.

**A dev account's older patch is read on demand.** A patch whose figures read a version outside the run kept as filed is one only a dev account can ask for ([[R4677000]]). Its window and game figures are read then from every patch's records and the card totals of that patch's reach alone: the window before its own through it. They are kept as a public patch's are ([[R978000]]), so a load is made once per key and snapshot. As with an unshipped patch's own read ([[R3067000]]), this goes through the whole table, but it holds only that reach.

**The drill-down's Win rate by patch** ([[R2491000]]) lists the patches whose card totals the snapshot holds as filed, for every reader. Anyone's list is unchanged: their patches are among those held. A dev account's list now stops there too, where it went back to v0.1.0 ([[R4677000]]). Its older patches' figures are still a `?patch=` away.

**The play rates** ([[R1583000]]) sum every patch's live totals alike. Their read now keeps one total per card and key, filed under one patch (`CardRows::Merged`), so it no longer holds every patch's rows while it sums them. Its figures are the same.

The fake store charges a read of the records and game totals alone as `statsTotals.loadRecords`, so [[R3067000]]'s count of loads of every patch's totals is unchanged: one snapshot is still one such load.

Amends [[R3067000]] (what the snapshot holds), [[R2491000]] (which patches the history lists), [[R4677000]] (a dev account's history) and [[R1583000]] (how the play rates read).

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.

Rejected:
- Dropping the pilot and mode split for the older patches: the play versions' table reads them under every `?pilot=` and `?mode=`.
- Keeping each older patch's totals at a smaller cost per row: they would still grow with every patch.
- Reading a dev account's history of older patches on demand: that is one more read of every older patch's totals per drill-down, the very load and peak this note removes.
- A larger instance (`basic` is 1 GiB): a billing decision for the owners, and it only moves the limit.
