"The AI plays the rest of your turn" is the autopilot, a status on its caster for the turn running (`PlayerState.autopilot`), set by the `autopilot` verb only on the caster's own turn: Cognitive Offloading drawn on the opponent's turn, by an effect, gains its mana and draws, and nothing plays anybody's turn. Engaged, it is announced to both players (`autopilotEngaged`, public), and once the action that engaged it has settled — the cast, the draw that cast it, the start of the turn it was drawn in — the engine takes that player's actions for them, one at a time and each through the reducer's own step, so a "your turn ends" rider counts them ([[R456]]) and a turn left with nothing to do ends as [[R82]] ends it. It plays [[R1183003]]'s choices until the turn ends. A question the other player must answer stops it: the turn waits, the seat it plays for takes no action but a concession meanwhile (the prompt is the other player's, [[R113]]), and the answer hands the turn back to it. It ends with the turn's cleanup, as My Pawn's lockout does ([[R152]]); a game in which nothing engages it carries no field for it, so no recorded game moves. A copy played from the hand (past [[R58]]'s chain cap) does the same. Core #96 My Pawn's random AI turn ([[R44]]) is its cousin; this one is the engine's scorer, never `crates/ai`, so the server, the browser and a replay play the same turn from the same state.
