**Why.** On 2026-11-28 Supabase warned that JackiOh-Staging was running through its Disk IO budget, and its egress was climbing. Staging's `pg_stat_statements`, over the 24 days since its reset on 2026-11-04, put about 70% of its IO in reads. The card statistics' snapshot ([[R3067000]]) loaded 960 times and read about 120 MiB of `card_totals` from disk each time, 115 GiB in all, and sent the figures to the server each time. The table does not fit in the cache of the database's small instance, and the gather lane's upserts keep evicting it. Most of the rest was writes: the gather lane's 2,862 batches ([[R1976000]]) each rewrote about 16 MiB of `card_totals`, 46 GiB in all, after reading the same rows out to the runner. Each batch rewrites every card total its games touch, and at the All Random deals that is most of the version's rows however few its games. The lane went from two parts to five on 2026-11-26, and #5176 took its batch from 400 games to 50 on 2026-11-27, about 50 batches an hour into each database.

**Kept six hours.** `CARD_STATS_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS` is 21,600 s, not 300 s. The snapshot and every figure cut from it ([[R978000]], [[R3067000]], [[R5245000]]) are kept six hours from when their load finished. While the server is awake, the snapshot loads at most once in each six hours that something reads it, where it loaded once in each five minutes. Those reads include a match start's `win_rate_snapshot` ([[R3049000]]). Since [[R5245000]], a read past the time answers at once with the figures held while one refresh runs beside it, so the longer time costs only the figures' age: up to about six hours, one load and one cut. A process that starts, after a deploy or a sleep, still loads its first snapshot from nothing ([[R5174000]]).

**Five minutes in a browser.** `Cache-Control` no longer reads that time. The public answers carry `public, max-age=` `CARD_STATS_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` (300 s), as before, so a browser asks again within five minutes and sees the patch a deploy ships. The server answers that ask from what it holds, without the database. A dev account's answer stays `private, no-store` ([[R4677000]]).

**Fetch live.** A dev account ([[R1300000]]) sees a Fetch live button on `/stats`, beside the current patch, when the tab's kept `/api/auth/me` says `isDev`. It posts `POST /api/dev/stats-refresh`, which answers anyone else 403 `forbidden`, as every dev route does. The server lets go of every figure it keeps, cut from the snapshot or computed for a patch it does not ship, and of the snapshot with them, as a boot does. A load under way, folding the older play versions or not ([[R5174000]]), is kept and joined, so no second load starts beside one. The route answers what the load is doing, as `GET /api/stats/loading` does ([[R5186000]]). The page then reloads. With nothing held, its reads wait for figures loaded now, behind the progress bar, as the first read after a boot does. So does every other reader's meanwhile, a match start's among them, once a dev asks. The play rates are kept: they refresh on their own (`PLAY_RATES_REFRESH_SECONDS`).

**250 games a batch.** The gather lane writes `GATHER_FILE_GAMES` (250) games to a file and files each file as one batch, about half an hour of a part's games. That is a fifth of the batches 50 made. A game the deadline cuts short still holds back at most the other games of its file.

Amends [[R978000]] and [[R3067000]] (the figures are kept six hours, and `Cache-Control` reads its own five minutes), and the rejected alternatives of [[R5174000]] and [[R5245000]], which turned down a longer TTL. [[R5174000]] did because a container that sleeps loads from nothing whatever the TTL, which still holds for a boot. [[R5245000]] did because a reload made the reads at its end wait, which [[R5245000]] itself ended.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.

Rejected:
- A larger database instance: a billing decision for the owners, and it only moves the limit.
- Refreshing on a six-hour timer whether anyone reads or not: a sleeping container has no timer to run, and an awake one would load with nobody on `/stats` ([[R5245000]]).
- Keeping the snapshot in the database or a store beside it, so a boot reads it instead of loading: a second copy of the totals to keep true, where the TTL is one number.
- Fetch live serving the held figures while it loads, as a read past its time does: the dev's reload would show the figures it asked to replace, and need a second reload once the cut was made again.
- Compacting `card_totals` in key order with a `fillfactor` that keeps a batch's updates on their own pages: it cuts what each load reads, boots included, but needs the table's size and dead rows read first, and is a migration of its own.
