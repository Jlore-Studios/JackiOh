Every choice of `DISCOVER_OPTION_LIMIT` (4) options or fewer whose options name no card is drawn the way Hearthstone draws a Choose One: each option a **choice card**, with procedural art seeded by the asking card and the option, the option's label as its name and what it does as its text. A choice card is not a card: it has no cost, stats, rarity, set mark or card id, opens no inspect overlay or long-press sheet, and reaches no zone, log line or collection. It is presentation only: the answers, their keys and the engine are unchanged (CLAUDE.md rule 7). Five or more options keep their lists.

An engine prompt ([[§10.6]]) carried no asking card, so a Choose one a card opened as it resolved (Classic #8 Pickle's, by its opponent) could not say which card asked or what each option does. The chooser's `PendingPromptView` now names it, as `sourceDefId` and `sourceRadiant`, read off the prompt's `resume` (its `defId`, `instanceId` and `radiant`), and only while:

- the state still holds that instance and it still has that definition, and
- the chooser may read it where it now is, by the test `sourceId` goes through on `cardPlayed` and `summoned` ([[R1088]], `may_read`): a card in the chooser's own hand, on the field face-up, in a graveyard, in exile or resolving is named; one in the other seat's hand, in a library or face-down in the other seat's backrow is not.

`PendingElsewhereView`, the other seat's, never names it. These are view fields, not `PendingChoice` fields, so no state hash, fuzz replay hash or golden fold moves (the golden fold's view texts leave them out, as they leave out `createdBy`, [[R1956001]]). Hearthstone shows the card that asks on its Choose One and Discover, which is all this follows.

Decisions the issue left open, each with the alternative rejected:

1. A card the state no longer holds, or that changed definition since it asked, is not named. Following a replacement to the card that took its place (as `may_read`'s [[R177]] chain does for events) was rejected: the name would be the old card's, read through a card that is not it.
2. An engine Choose one keeps the engine's label for each option and takes only the detail from the card's words (`MODE_TEXT`), since an engine label can carry a rolled value (KY's Test's rewards, Pickle's counts). Using `MODE_TEXT`'s label too was rejected.
3. When the asking card is not named, its options have no detail rather than a generic sentence, which would say nothing or hint at a card the chooser may not read.
4. A quest's rewards ([[R404]]) and a multiple-choice problem's answers ([[R420]]) are named by their letter ("Reward A", "A"), their words the text. An answer's art is seeded by its letter alone, so nothing tells one answer from another but its letter and words; the sequence's Stop ([[R1184000]]) stays a button under them.
5. A card with five or more abilities lists them as a `mode` list, since a choice card past the limit would break it.
6. The options an engine prompt asks are built as it resolves, so the client's coverage of their words is a hand-kept table beside the export of the scripts' declared modes (`cardModes.json`).
