TG #39's ninth entry adds one random Call to Chaos to its caster's hand at once, then gives its caster a rest-of-game start-of-turn effect ([[R458]]) that adds one more at the start of every second turn of theirs: it remembers the caster's count of turns begun as it is made, and adds on each of their turns whose count since is a positive multiple of `CHAOS_TG_EVERY` (2). So none comes on the caster's next turn and one on the turn after; the opponent's turns never count, and an extra turn is one of the caster's turns ([[R846]]). Each added card is drawn from the pool that names no set, the editions of the sets that ship ([[R380]]), this edition among them once its set ships, as the text names a pool that holds it ([[R387]]), and costs (0). The effect is the engine's own, as M #95's is ([[R1241]]): its hook is `@chaosTgAlternate`, which `work::script_step_for` answers whatever card it names, with no `self` ([[R127]]). Its badge reads the clause on both seats ([[R169]]). The effects stack, and a player holds at most `CHAOS_TG_ALTERNATE_CAP` (3): its terminator (REVIEW A3.6). A roll of the entry for a player who holds 3 resolves into nothing, the add now included, with no re-roll, as [[R87]]'s recursion at the cap does. The designer's "every other turn" is read as every second turn of the caster's, not each of their turns (every other turn of the game), and "a Call to Chaos" as a random edition, not this one (TG6 row 25).
