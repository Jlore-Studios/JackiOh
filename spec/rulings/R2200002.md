TG #39's ninth entry gives its caster a rest-of-game start-of-turn effect ([[R458]]) that adds one random Call to Chaos to their hand at the start of every turn of theirs, at its printed price ([[R3135000]]); through v0.5.11p it read "add a random Call to Chaos to your hand, which costs (0), now and at the start of every second turn of yours for the rest of the game" and added one at once, at (0), then one at the start of every second turn. The effect remembers its caster's count of turns begun as it is made, and adds on each of their turns whose count since is a positive multiple of `CHAOS_TG_EVERY` (1 now, 2 through v0.5.11p): so none comes as the card resolves, and one at the start of each of the caster's next turns. The opponent's turns never count, and an extra turn is one of the caster's turns ([[R846]]). Each added card is drawn from the pool that names no set, the editions of the sets that ship ([[R380]]), this edition among them since its set ships, as the text names a pool that holds it ([[R387]]), and is added on its base face. The effect is the engine's own, as M #95's is ([[R1241]]): its hook is `@chaosTgAlternate`, which `work::script_step_for` answers whatever card names it, with no `self` ([[R127]]). Its badge reads the clause on both seats ([[R169]]). The effects stack, and a player holds at most `CHAOS_TG_ALTERNATE_CAP` (3): its terminator (REVIEW A3.6). A roll of the entry for a player who holds 3 resolves into nothing, with no re-roll, as [[R87]]'s recursion at the cap does (through v0.5.11p the add now was nothing then too). The designer's "every other turn" was read as every second turn of the caster's, not each of their turns (every other turn of the game), and "a Call to Chaos" as a random edition, not this one (TG6 row 25); his "every turn" (issue #3135) is each of the caster's turns, by the same reading of "your hand".
