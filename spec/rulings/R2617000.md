On a touch screen a finger held on a card is the hover that screen lacks: after `LONG_PRESS_MS`,
within `LONG_PRESS_SLOP_PX`, the card's hover preview opens and stays open while the finger is
down ([[§10.10]]). A face-down card shows its back's preview ([[R370]]) and a pile its list's
([[R313]]).

**Where.** Everywhere a card has an inspect trigger: the hand, the field, the backrow, the piles,
the log and a revealed hand, on either player's turn, and off the board as well (a prompt's
options, the marketplace, the deck list, the practice deck preview, the craft editor and the main
menu's fan). Until #2617 it held only on the opponent's turn and on the main menu (#165). On your
own turn a hold opened the modal inspect sheet, so that reading a card could never eat the press
that plays it. But a hold already swallows the click its lift sends, and the preview takes no
pointer, so it neither plays the card nor stops a drag.

**Ending it.** The preview closes when the finger lifts, moves past `LONG_PRESS_SLOP_PX` (the
start of a drag, which goes on) or is cancelled. The click the lift sends goes nowhere, so a hold
never plays, picks, buys or removes the card. A tap that is no hold still does.

**With Hover previews off** a hold opens the inspect sheet, the hold of old. A touch screen can still
read a card at leisure that way, and tap a reference in it ([[R279]]).

**Unchanged.** A hold that has its own use keeps it: the hero portrait's inspect view
([[R1449]]), and the card detail that the deck builder's pool and the Almanac open. So do a
glossary term's own long press ([[R2098000]]), the mouse's hover, and Enter, I and the
context-menu key, which open the sheet.

Hearthstone semantics: on a phone, a held card is shown large until it is let go.

Rejected: keeping the sheet on your own turn (#165), and a hold that turns into the sheet when it is
let go.
