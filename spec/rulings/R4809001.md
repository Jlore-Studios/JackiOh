An Attack that #96 My Pawn answers plays the rest of the attacker's turn with random legal actions inside one `reduce`, so the AI charges it `AI_SEARCH.ai_turn_trap_nodes` (40) extra nodes up front (`surcharge_nodes`, issue #1240; [[§9.9]], Budgets). The charge was an estimate: an attack by a unit whose Attack was at least the defender's `effective_life` ([[R964000]]) while a My Pawn lay in the defender's backrow, whatever the attack's target, and never against a hero that cannot lose ([[R2201000]]). The trap answers far fewer. It overcharged an attack on a Unit with no Trample, which reaches no hero (in issue #4809's board the only legal attack was on a Unit in Defense Position, whose Taunt it gives), and a blow the hero's Armor makes survivable; the other way, it charged nothing for an attack whose projection reaches the hero for more than the attacker's Attack, a sweep's hits beside the blow ([[R3554008]]). With a reply that cuts when it cannot pay a surcharge ([[R4809000]]), every needless charge cut a reply that would have run.

**The rule.** The surcharge is charged for an Attack only when a My Pawn lies in the defender's backrow and the trap's own predicate holds: the hero the hit can reach (`subsystems::defending_hero`: the target itself, or a Unit's controller through Trample) is the trap's controller, and `subsystems::is_lethal` calls the attack lethal there ([[R44]]: projected damage after Armor and the cap, Trample and Cleave excess from an attack on a Unit included, [[R176]]; against `effective_life`, [[R964000]]; never to a hero that cannot lose, [[R2201000]]). It is read up front, before the declaration, as every surcharge is, on the state the step starts from, the same one the trap's window reads but for the declaration itself; the backrow read is first, so the projection runs only while a My Pawn is there. Like the old estimate, it reads the state the step is simulated on: in the beam a determinized world, whose cards the AI cannot see are dealt guesses, so it reads nothing the AI may not know ([[R185]]); in the reply the defender is the AI itself.

Narrowing the code was chosen over rewording `crates/ai/README.md`, which said the surcharge was for "an attack into an armed My Pawn": `AI_SEARCH`'s own doc and [[§9.9]] already described the narrowed intent, an attack that could be the lethal one the trap answers.

Rejected:
- Correcting only `crates/ai/README.md` to the estimate. Every attack on a guarded board behind a My Pawn would go on paying for a trap that cannot fire.

Hearthstone has no counterpart: it adds no rule of the game and no constant.
