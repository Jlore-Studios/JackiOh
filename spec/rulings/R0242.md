A random "becomes Radiant" pick ([[R60]]) whose zones mix cards everyone reads — a unit, a face-up backrow card — with cards only their owner reads (their hand, their face-down trap: [[§9.1]], [[R33]]) or nobody reads (a library, [[§3]]) is made in two draws, so its odds never read a face a player may not. First it draws how many of its picks each of those three groups takes: `count` different slots drawn uniformly among them all, where a public card is a slot only while it is not Radiant (its face is public) and a hidden card is a slot whatever its face (that face is what the other player may not read). Then each group picks its share among its own non-Radiant cards uniformly ([[R60]]), and cues the picks it could not make on its own Radiant cards ([[R177]]). So the chance that a public card is picked, and how many picks land among each player's unread cards, hang on the groups' sizes alone, which both players can count: drawn from the non-Radiant cards alone, #28's two picks always took a public unit when every hidden card was Radiant and sometimes passed it over when one was not, which told the other seat that a hidden card was base-face. The events go out group by group — the public cards', then the owner's hidden ones, then the library's — each in the zones' own order (hand order, lane order, the library top down), since in the zones' order a hidden pick's place beside a public one said whether it was a hand card, before the unit row, or a face-down trap, after it. When every slot is taken, or the zones hold one group only, nothing is drawn for the split and the pick is [[R60]]'s as written ([[R129]]).
