A card patch's record (`title`, `source`, `notes`, [[R388]]) is the history's and the designer's: it names issues, rulings and commits. What players read of a patch on the Patch notes page ([[R724000]]) is its player note, `player` in `patches.json`, beside the record: `{ title, new, balance, changes, text, fixes }`, a short title with no version and one fact per line under its heading. `new` holds the cards, tokens, keywords and tags the patch brings; `balance` a cost, stat, number or rarity moved, written from and to ("Hit Job costs (3), up from (2)."); `changes` how a card or rule plays; `text` wording only; `fixes` what was broken. A note says only what that patch changed, taken from its snapshot diff and its change, never from a pull request's title, and never what the game does not do yet, and it uses today's words ([[R373]], [[R432]], [[R1320]]: `wording.test.ts` holds every title and line of `patches.json`'s notes to them).

A pending fragment ([[R646]]) carries the note as `player`, `{ version, title, sources, notes, player, cards }`, written into the fragment by hand: no flag of `cargo jackioh patches <version> …` sets it, a fragment written again keeps the note it holds, and a new one starts empty. `patches check` fails on a fragment's note, and on any shipped patch's, that has no title, no line, a blank line, a line over 200 characters (`PLAYER_LINE_MAX`), or a ruling (an `R` and digits standing alone), an issue or card number (`#` and a digit) or a `§` in its title or a line, and on a shipped patch with no note. `patches ship` copies the fragment's note onto the patch it appends, after `notes`, and the rebuild of the derived files keeps it.

Issue #724 wrote the notes of the 28 patches shipped before it (v0.1.0 to v0.3.6) by hand, from each patch's snapshot diff and its change: it is the one branch to write `patches.json`, and it only added `player` to each entry, leaving every record, snapshot, `index.json`, `shipped.json` and the order as they were.
