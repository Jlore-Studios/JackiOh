A trap's condition ([[R99]]) is kept apart from its effects so it can decline an event without being spent, and the traps' dispatch reads it before it reveals one. A fused card keeps every ingredient's trigger with its own condition ([[R102]]), and when the fusion is no trap (a Wormhole fused onto a Unit, whose type is the Unit's) its triggers are queued like any card's and resolve in turn ([[R68]]). That path never read the condition, so the Wormhole text on a Unit answered every announce whose targets were its caster's own side, its own controller's among them: it countered its controller's Spell back into their hand and cast it again for them, without end, and the release's handicapped and Duos fuzz waves found games that never ended (seeds 7817, 8291 and Duos 1037).

So a trap ingredient's trigger on a fusion that is no trap runs only when its condition admits the event, read as the trigger resolves, with the context its effects are built from. Declining spends nothing, as a trap that declines is not spent ([[R61]]). [[R2271000]] (issue #2271) makes this the general reading: every queued trigger reads its card's printed condition before it runs, trap text or not, and `subsystems::fuse` carries each ingredient's condition in its place. This ruling stays as the release's finding and the Wormhole case it proves. In a unit zone [[R2226000]] (issue #2226) narrows it: a trap's text fused into a card that stands there and is no trap never fires, so the Wormhole fused onto a Unit counters no Spell at all, and this ruling's reading of the condition holds where such a text still runs, on a backrow card that is no trap.
