A keyword a card's text prints in bold ([[§6]]) shows its glossary line in a tooltip beside it ([[§10.10]]).

**Where.** On the board and in the hand, in the Almanac's and the deck builder's pool, and in the inspect views: the detail view, the touch sheet and a pile's dialog. The hover preview takes no pointer and already prints the glossary beside its own face, so its terms stay plain.

**How.** A mouse or pen resting on a term shows its glossary line ([[R500]]'s short reminder where the glossary prints one) after a short delay; leaving hides it. A touch held on a term for `LONG_PRESS_MS`, within `LONG_PRESS_SLOP_PX`, shows it, and it stays open until the next press, Escape or blur. A move past the slop is a drag and shows nothing. The term's long press replaces the card's, and the click the long press ends with goes nowhere, so a tap that is no long press still plays, aims, selects or opens the card. The tooltip takes no pointer events, so it never blocks play.

**Keyboard.** [[R279]]'s split: where the surface allows a control, a term is a tab stop that shows its rule on focus, `role="tooltip"` and linked by `aria-describedby`. There is no focus inside a button or a card that is a control: the pool's tiles, whose face is decoration to a screen reader, and the board's cards, which the I key or Enter opens, where the term is a tab stop. Escape closes the tooltip, not the dialog.

Hearthstone semantics: a card's keywords explain themselves in a tooltip where they are read, on a held press on mobile.

Rejected: a tab stop per keyword in the hand and the pool (nested controls in a button, an aria-hidden face); a tap that opens the tooltip (it would take the tap from play); closing on lift; hoverable hover-preview terms.
