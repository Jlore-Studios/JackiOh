The opponent's reply ([[§9.9]]) moved a unit out of Defense Position only when it had Deft (`deft_switch`, [[R49]]); issue #1696's switches and [[R4187000]]'s walls only ever moved units into Defense. So a unit of the opponent's standing in Defense where the AI's line ended was still in Defense where the line was scored, at the AI's next turn start: the evaluation counted half its attack and no swing at the AI's face from it. Take the opponent's 7/7 parked in Defense against the AI at 7: the AI read it as a wall that stays a wall, and would give up its own Taunt blocker or aim its removal elsewhere, when a real opponent flips the 7/7 back to Attack on its turn and threatens lethal from the next.

Now, in the reply's step 4, once no attack is worth making, the switches tried are issue #1696's switches to Defense of the opponent's units in Attack Position, up to `AI_REPLY.defense_switches`, and after them the switches back to Attack of its units in Defense Position without Deft, up to `AI_REPLY.attack_switches`, each one whose switch `legal_actions` lists, in that order, one node each. They are weighed together through `best_try`, by the opponent's own evaluation with the AI swinging next, and the best is kept when it beats standing still. A unit that switches back has spent its one exertion of the turn ([[§4.1]]): it attacks nothing that turn and `legal_actions` lists no second switch of it, so no reply flips a unit twice, and its whole attack is read at the AI's next turn start. A Deft unit in Defense stays with step 3: it may switch and still attack, and its Defense already counts its whole attack (`deft_defense_attacks`).

The evaluation prices no kill of the unit the switch opens up: a switch back gains the unit's attack at full worth and as pressure on the AI's face, and loses its Taunt's hold on the AI's attackers and its Armor +1. So the unit stays in Defense while its Taunt holds back an attack of the AI's that would be lethal, or one large beside its own attack, and otherwise switches back even where the AI could then trade into it.

`AI_REPLY.attack_switches` at 0 gives back the reply as it was before. It adds no weight and no rule of the game, and it reads only the public board. Hearthstone has no positions; a player uses every move it has, and one whose big unit stands walled with nothing to fear puts it back to work.

Rejected:
- Deft units among the tries. Their Defense already counts their whole attack, and step 3 tries their switch with the attack after it.
- Sharing `AI_REPLY.defense_switches` as the cap. A cap of its own turns the switches back off alone, as the screen's rule needs.
- Trying the switch back beside an attack worth making, or among [[R4187000]]'s walls. An attack worth making goes first, and the next step tries the switch back once none is left; a switch back lowers no threat, so it is no wall.
- An exposure term in the evaluation, pricing what the AI could kill once the Taunt is gone. It changes what every search reads, and is a change of its own.

The screen (training/improve.md, A screen), 400 games on improve-dev against the control at 7201a2687e, read 198-202, edge -0.01 (-0.044 to 0.024); inconclusive ([[R4319000]]): 12 decided of 200 (won 5, lost 7), 66 diverged, every game clean. Its interval does not lie wholly below 0, so `AI_REPLY.attack_switches` stays at 4 and its scene tests settle it, as issue #1696's switches were settled.
