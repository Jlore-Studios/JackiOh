[[R977000]] counts a re-timed decision in `timeouts` when it is over `AI_SWEEP.decisionMs` (2,000 ms), with no margin, and the re-timer's own spread straddles that limit: one decision of Encrypt re-timed alone three times read 1,940, 2,242 and 1,756 ms, and three of the 2026-11-26 Assault sweep's eight bans cleared when run alone. Now a re-timed decision counts only over `decisionMs` × `AI_SWEEP.retimeMargin` (1.25, so 2,500 ms). That clears 2,242 ms and still catches the real finds, 3.5 s ([[R4595003]]) and 4.2 s ([[R4595004]]), which ran 75% and more over the limit. `time_decision` still stops at the first run under `decisionMs`; the margin is applied to its verdict. `rawTimeouts` is unchanged.

`SweepStats.cappedGames` counts the games still running at 600 actions, which a replay from the seed reproduces and a decision near the limit does not. They count in `timeouts` and `rawTimeouts` too, so `timeouts` keeps both causes. The `timeout` reason prints the two apart: `1 decision(s) re-timed over 2500 ms, 0 game(s) past 600 actions`, the decisions being `timeouts` less `cappedGames`. A line written before has no `cappedGames` and reads 0, so an old line's reason prints all its timeouts as decisions.

`face_flags` takes the tier, so a pass-2 row at a tier that raised nothing reads `cleared` instead of the flag another tier raised.

The perf gate's `AI_GATE.maxDecisionMs` (1,500 ms) is unchanged and still times the shipped AI. The risk is a margin that lets a truly slow card through; a decision between 2,000 and 2,500 ms on the yardstick is no longer banned. No game plays differently, and `SHADOW_BAN` and `SHADOW_WATCH` change only when a person runs the sweep. Hearthstone semantics does not apply: this decides tooling only. Rejected: a margin on the clock's first reading (pass 1 would still false-ban at the raw clock), and a new table column (it would break the committed reports).
