The owner asked in #2715 for art that really animates: [[R2655003]]'s Legendary and Mythic moved
only as a whole, the still picture drifting in its crop under a sweeping band. Every card's art is
procedural SVG, so the animation is the picture's own layers moving.

**The moving art.** A full-art card's picture, given its motion, is a second document: the same
picture, byte for byte, with each layer wrapped by its role and a `<style>` of keyframes after its
`<defs>`. The roles are its background (the backdrop, a figure's hills), its subject (the figure or
the composition, and the emblem), what lies over it (a waves pattern, a family's over layer), its
light (the glow), its props (the motif's glyphs and the accent, staggered along their loop) and its
motes; the set painters' pictures (Stinkyscape, Training Grounds) move their backdrop and their
subject. Over them it draws particles that suit the card, seven, placed by a stream of the card's id:
coins, sparks, stars, embers, bubbles, hearts, crumbs, drops, leaves, notes or confetti. Each card's
recipe is its own (`FULL_ART_MOTION`): which roles move, how (a bob, a breath, a sway, a hop, a drift,
a swell, a pulse, a flare, a spin, a tick, a float, a flutter, a twinkle) and on what beat, and its
particles' kind and move (rising, falling, glinting). Every recipe moves two of its picture's elements
or more on beats of their own, beyond the whole picture panning, no two cards' recipes are alike, and a
test holds every full-art card of the registry to one, so a later full-art card cannot ship without
it. A listed token takes its parent's recipe ([[R2655002]]). Every keyframe moves by `transform` and
`opacity` alone, inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, and a wrapper carries no
`transform` of its own, which the animation would replace. With no motion the painters draw the still
picture exactly, so no still art's golden hash moves.

**Who wears it.** The Animated art ([[R2715000]]), the Legendary and the Mythic, each on a full
layout: the Animated art in its normal face's art window, the Legendary and the Mythic under
[[R2655003]]'s drift and sweep, which stay on top. The still Epic never moves.

**How it is rationed.** A face whose art moves takes one of `COSMETIC_FOIL_ANIMATED_MAX`'s moving
slots, as a moving cosmetic foil, finish or border does ([[R1953001]]); past them the rest draw the
still picture until a slot passes to them, first come first served. A lazy grid face (the Almanac's,
the Collection's, the deck builder's pool, [[R1956000]]) never moves its art. `near.ts`'s visibility
check was the other candidate the issue named, and it is not one: it fires once, when a face first
comes near the screen, and never says it has left, so it cannot hand motion back.

**Reduced motion.** The face decides in script: the moving picture is drawn only while Animated foil
is on, neither the Reduce motion setting nor the system's `prefers-reduced-motion` asks for less,
and the face holds a slot; otherwise it draws the still picture. The gate inside the picture is the
last line only, since the page's own motion gate (the Reduce motion setting on `<html>`) cannot reach
into a CSS background image.

Presentation only (CLAUDE.md rule 7).

Hearthstone semantics: a golden card's portrait moves its own elements (its subject, its background,
its light and its particles), each card's its own, and holds still where the player asks for less
motion.

Rejected: the SVG inline in the face, which would need `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, and whose gradient
ids would collide between two cards on one screen; one shared filter or shimmer over every card,
which the owner ruled out; [[R2655003]]'s drift on the Animated face, which would scale its art past
the bezel of a normal face's art window; and `near.ts` as the rationing, above.
