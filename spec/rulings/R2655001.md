[[R1017001]] made the hero portraits' cards the full-art cards, the list "the portrait roster
itself". Full art now stops meaning the portrait roster: **a card prints full art when the cosmetics
registry has a `fullArt` entry for its catalog id**. The client builds its list from the generated
registry, so the registry is the one list, and a card that becomes a hero's portrait does not become
a full-art card by that alone. This amends [[R1017001]]'s sentence about the list and
[[R1313000]]'s six.

The six portrait cards keep their full art, and fifteen join them, each with an Epic, a Legendary
and a Mythic ([[R2655000]]):

Spells, field spells and traps print full art as Units do. [[R1017001]]'s face is the same on every
type: the cost gem, the rarity gem with the set mark under it, the crest, the name banner and the
scrim, and a Unit's attack and health in the bottom corners, which a card of any other type does not
print. Its art is its own ([[R660]]: a delivered file first, the procedural picture otherwise),
cropped to the face. Everything else of [[R1017001]] and [[R1313000]] holds for the new cards: their
whole text one hover away, the Full-art cards setting, a lesson's board printing none
([[R1108000]]), the ownership gate, and the landing page's fan ([[R1017002]]) and the Almanac
showing them.

Presentation only (CLAUDE.md rule 7): no rule reads a full art.

Hearthstone semantics: Signature cards are a chosen set of iconic cards, of every type, each with its
own art across the face, not the cards a hero portrait shows.

Rejected: keeping the portrait roster beside a second hand-written list, two lists to keep in step
with the registry; and a list of names in the client's code, which would drift from the ids the
server owns and rolls.
