Every card in Chinese has the CN tag, as the designer asked ("all cards in Chinese gain the CN tribe", CN being one of the tribes, [[R1424]]). A card is in Chinese two ways.

- **Printed in Chinese.** A catalog entry with a Han character in its name or on a face (Meditative #32 Spiritually 中国, #33 First Day of 学校, #34 高考, #39 赌石 Addict, #40 Feng Shui, #47 饕餮) prints the CN tag, as every such entry must.
- **Translated in a game.** An instance whose `chinese` flag is set ([[R1300]]: Meditative #32's "get ready to learn Chinese" and #35's translation, the `chinese` rider of #33, #37 and the copiers T-AI-5 Autocomplete, C #17 Counterspell and Core #39 Recycling Initiative, a copy of a Chinese card, a crafted Fuse of one) has the CN tag wherever an instance's tags are read, for as long as it carries the flag, which is for good. `tags_of` adds CN after the definition's tags and the granted ones ([[R923]]), once, so every reader sees it: declarations and targets, board and card scopes, Recruit filters and Discover picks, plays counted by tag, the scripts' tag tests, and the tribal-hate filters that Meditative #25 is immune to ([[R940]]). Meditative #34 高考 spares a translated card and Buffs it, #35 RCTA (CN) can no longer pick one, #45 Knowledge Breaker Nerfs it, #45.1 Knowledge Breaker Prime destroys or exiles it, and #78 Occidentless Mandate neither exiles nor counts it.

What stays: catalog pools read printed tags, so "a random CN card" draws from the pool it drew from, and Meditative #87 Tatches the Totem and #52 Economic Anxiety read printed tags ([[R1120]]). The tag follows the flag. A copy that keeps the flag keeps it, and a Transform's or a Replace's new card has neither. A Fuse that keeps an instance keeps that card's own language ([[R1300]]), so another ingredient's translation does not make it CN, though that ingredient's printed and granted tags join it ([[R923]]); a crafted fused card is Chinese, and so CN, when an ingredient was. The view lists the tag among the card's `tags` (sent where they differ from the definition's) on every view of the card a seat may read, so both seats see it on a card both read and the face shows the CN chip; a prompt's option carries the flag and no tags, as for a granted tag ([[R1301]]). The AI's placeholder for a card its seat cannot read is not in Chinese ([[R185]]). Simplified Chinese as a reader's language ([[R711000]]) is the client's alone and makes no card CN.

A replay folds under its game's catalog version ([[R767000]]), and this change, which changes no card data, ships no version: a game played before it carries the same version as one played after it, so no old rule can be kept for it. A game played before it in which a translated card was played (its play now counts as a CN play) or read by its tags folds to another state, misses its recorded final hash and is refused as `rules_changed`, never shown as another game. A game whose Chinese cards were neither played nor read by a tag folds as it did, its views now listing their CN.
