A unit's Trample applies to its own attack and to nothing else, as Magic: The Gathering's does. When a unit with Trample attacks a unit, the hit it deals to the defender and each hit of its Cleave ([[§4.4]] step 10) count on that unit only up to its health, and the rest goes to the target's controller's hero as a new damage instance ([[§4.4]] steps 5 and 9, [[R63]]). Nothing else a unit with Trample deals does:

- The unit that is attacked and strikes back never tramples. Its strike back deals its whole attack to the attacker, whatever the attacker's health, and nothing goes on to the attacking hero, so a value trade with a Trample unit does not hurt the hero of the one who attacked it. [[R63]] and [[R176]] had a defender's Trample strike back send its excess to the attacking hero; that is revised.
- The damage a unit's text deals (a Cry, a trigger, an end-of-turn effect) never tramples, whatever keywords the unit has. [[R63]] had Trample apply to any damage the unit deals; that is revised as well.

An attack on a unit of the attacker's own side, such as the one Traitorous Blood re-aims an enemy's attack into ([[R1122]]), is still the attacker's, so its Trample sends the excess to "the target's controller's hero", the attacker's own. The attacked ally strikes back without Trample.

A Spell that prints Trample (Classic #16 Book of Flame, Classic #83 Flame Lance), or an effect that states its own damage tramples (`trample`, [[R346]]'s way for Pierce), tramples as before: the rule is about a unit's keyword, and a Spell is not attacking.

The My Pawn projection ([[R44]], [[R176]]) follows: it counts the attacker's Trample as before, and a defender's Lifesteal strike back heals its controller by everything it deals the attacker, in the same simultaneous step ([[§4.3]], [[§4.4]] step 8), with no share of it sent to the attacking hero.
