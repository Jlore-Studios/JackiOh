Issue #3951 asked to make the AI better at playing Meditative #40 Feng Shui. Its search already played each verdict of the turn through `reduce` ([[§9.9]]): a rewarded play entered Radiant ([[R983]]) and a punished one took its Brittle 2 and its hit ([[R985]]), so inside one turn it chose the rewarded card and found two-step chains, its Activate's Auspicious Rock (M #39.1, index 39.1: 水) among them. What it did not see was the element it ends on, which judges its next play ([[R981]], [[R982]]): it played the better body now and left an element that rewards nothing it holds, and a line whose first step only sets up a reward scored no better than its parts, so the beam dropped it before the rewarded play. Both are judgements about the AI, not rules of the game, and change no game.

**The outlook.** While a Feng Shui acts on either side ([[R984]]), `evaluate` adds `AI_EVAL.feng_shui_outlook` (0.5) of the best verdict, among the cards in the seat's own hand a Feng Shui would judge (a Trap's, a Field Trap's and a card without a definition are none), that the seat's last element gives one now (`subsystems::feng_shui::verdicts`, the engine's own judge, which also applies a Radiant Feng Shui's sides):

- rewarded: what the card would gain in hand as Radiant, `hand_card_value` of its Radiant copy less its own (the Radiant text's `radiant_in_hand` and `radiant_face_share` of its Radiant face's stats and keywords over its printed ones);
- punished: the cost of every judge's hit together on `hero_value`'s curve, the life left held at 1 or more;
- neutral, or the first play of the game: nothing.

The best alone: the seat chooses which card it plays first, and only that one is judged by this element, so a hand of rewarded cards is no reason to hold them back, and a hand with one plain card is not punished. The share is at most 1 (`config.rs` asserts it), so a reward held in hand is worth less than the Radiant play that takes it, and a punishment held less than the hit taken. It reads the seat's own hand, the public last element and the face-up Feng Shuis, never the enemy's hand; the evaluation of a sampled world reads the cards that world dealt, as every term does.

**The move order.** While a Feng Shui acts, tier 2 of `candidates.rs` (plays, powers and Activates, round-robin by cost) takes a play its card would be rewarded for first and one it would be punished for last, each group by cost as before (`Auspice`); an Activate, a power and a play none judges stand between. So a level's `branching` cut keeps the play that chains the elements and drops the punished one first. With no Feng Shui acting every source is plain and the order is the one it was. Since issue #4154 the Activate of a Feng Shui of the AI's own comes before every play ([[R4154403]]).

**The position.** While a Feng Shui acts, the seat's last element is part of the beam's position (`search_signature`), so two orders of the same plays that end on different elements are two positions and the outlook can tell them apart. The lethal solver's visited set reads the same signature, so it keeps such orders apart too, as it should, since the next play's verdict can change a lethal. With none acting both are the ones they were.

`GREEDY_EVAL` keeps the share at 0, so the greedy yardstick is unchanged. Not modelled: a later play's verdict after the first (a chain of the hand's cards), the Brittle 2 a punished play would carry, a card the seat would set face-down rather than play, the opponent's own outlook (its hand is hidden), and a Feng Shui leaving the field before the next play. Rejected: summing every hand card's verdict, which counts a hand of rewarded cards many times over, so playing one would read as losing the others' rewards and the AI would hold them all; a bonus on the punished or rewarded play itself, which the search already sees.
