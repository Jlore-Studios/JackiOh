[[§14.3]]'s dupe chain, as an opening runs it:

- **Slot by slot.** Ownership is read before each slot and moved by it, so a card an earlier slot
  of the pack unlocked is a duplicate in a later one, and weighs as owned in the later slots' draws
  (`PACK_UNOWNED_WEIGHTS`).
- **A duplicate's cosmetic rarity is its own roll**, at `COSMETIC_DUPLICATE_RATES` (Common 55%,
  Rare 25%, Epic 15%, Legendary 4.5%, Mythic 0.5%), not its card's rarity: #1301's owner's decision,
  which [[§14.3]] now states ([[R1333000]]).
- **The cosmetic** is one of the registry's per-card cosmetics (a foil, a finish, a border or a
  full art) of that rarity that is neither its kind's default nor a starter and that the account
  does not own on that card (`cosmetics::pack_cosmetics`), picked uniformly and owned on the card.
  A cosmetic that names its card, a full art ([[R1313000]]), is one of that card's alone: another
  card's duplicate never drops it.
- **With none, the duplicate is spent**: the opening adds `CARD_TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_PER_DUPLICATE`
  and `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_PER_DUPLICATE` per spent slot to the two bars ([[§14.5]]), one
  ledger row per bar per opening. No cosmetic is Mythic, so a Mythic roll is always spent.

**The starters.** The registry marks each starter cosmetic ([[§14.2]]), owned by every account on
every card without a ledger row. Every entry [[R1295000]] and [[R1297000]] shipped is one, since
both unlocked their items for every account. A cosmetic added later leaves the mark off and is one
a pack can drop ([[§14.12]]): today those are the six full arts, all Epic ([[R1313000]]), so a
duplicate drops a cosmetic only when its card is one of the six, it rolls Epic and the account does
not own that full art yet, and every other duplicate is spent.

**Not here.** A bar that reaches its size pays as the token bars' sub-issue rules ([[§14.5]],
[[R1305000]]). A chase drops as any other cosmetic does, when the duplicate's roll lands on
Legendary or Mythic ([[R1333000]]); the only Legendary cosmetics are starters, so none drops yet.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone's duplicate protection turns a duplicate into another card; the
spec's chain turns it into a cosmetic or into bar points instead, and this only fixes the order of
its steps and what it reads at each.

Rejected: a cosmetic of the card's own rarity, which was [[§14.3]]'s text until the owner's
decision in #1301; and dropping the shipped non-default items, which would write ledger rows for
cosmetics every account already owns.
