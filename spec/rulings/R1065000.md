[[§9.5]]'s "not in a match" counts a live co-op match, as Hearthstone seats a player in one game at a time. Before, a co-op match set no in-match flag and only co-op's own doors looked for one, so a player who left a co-op game for the menus ([[§13.8]]) could queue, be paired into a ranked game and lose it to the disconnect grace, or be taken into a 1v1 game while the round clock ended their co-op turns. While a live co-op match holds a player:

- the queue, a 1v1 room's open (a friend's challenge included) and a 1v1 room's join refuse them as `409 already_in_match`, with the co-op match in `details.duosMatchId`;
- a join of a 1v1 room whose host is in one is `409 conflict`, as for a host in a 1v1 match;
- the sweep pairs no ticket of theirs, with a person or with a ladder bot;
- a rematch with a seat in one is `409 already_in_match` with no details, since the seat may be the other player's.

Every door into a 1v1 or a co-op game makes the one check: the in-match flag, a series that is not over ([[R264]]), a live co-op match. So a rematch now asks about a series too; a series game is refused earlier, and a bot a series holds is seated nowhere else ([[R641000]]). A co-op match's start cancels both people's open tickets, as a series' start does. A player with a missing profile reads as free.

A player who queued before making a co-op room keeps their ticket until someone joins the room, as a queued player may host a 1v1 room; if the sweep pairs them first, the join is refused as a host in a match is. Rejected: refusing a co-op room to anyone with an open ticket, which is stricter than the 1v1 room.
