Level 1 is the base row alone. Every later level adds one escalation to everything the levels
before it added: a card step, which promotes `ASSAULT_CARD_STEP` of the deck's slots one rarity,
lowest first, never past the level's `ASSAULT_RARITY_CEILING`; or one stack of one upgrade
(IA3), drawn by the upgrades' weights among the rows allowed at that level that hold fewer than
their most stacks. A normal level's kind is the seed's at `ASSAULT_CARD_STEP_PERCENT`; a level
with no card step left takes an upgrade, and a level with no upgrade left takes a card step. A
Commander level takes a card step and an upgrade. Bigger Dataset also grows the slots of the
rarity holding the most, the lower on a tie. The mapping is the engine's own: opening cards
([[R182]]), deck size ([[R184]]), Radiant and Buffed deck marks ([[R2702000]]), mana
([[R181]]), health ([[R290]]), Armor ([[R2922000]]) and draws ([[R183]]), on Easy's resources
([[R180]], [[§9.9]]).

Never weaker compares within a kind. No handicap field other than health and `fewerOpeningCards`
falls from one level to the next; `fewerOpeningCards` never rises and drops off after level 3
([[R3582000]]); health minus its base never falls. But a Commander's base is a peak: the next
level goes back to the normal base and keeps every Redundancy stack, so health is only compared
from each normal level to the next and from each Commander level to the next. No upgrade's stacks
fall, and the count of slots at or above each rarity never falls. Rejected: health that never
falls at all, which IA2's formula forbids (40 at level 5, 26 at level 6); and the dealt deck's
literal counts, since each level's deck is dealt afresh from its own slots. `rarities` holds the
slots, not those counts.
