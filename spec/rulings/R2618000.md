The designer's call on issue #2606 (jgoetzmann, 2026-10-31), which asked for a badge on a stolen card because it went back to the other player's graveyard when it died: the rule changes instead. A card goes to the graveyard of the player whose side of the field it is on, or who played it. A card that leaves the field for a graveyard goes to its controller's, and a card that leaves the resolving zone for a graveyard (a resolved Spell, a countered play, a permanent with no zone to enter, [[R138]]) goes to the graveyard of the player who played or cast it. Either way it lands there as that player's own card: its owner becomes that player before [[R78]]'s reset hands control back, as a Bounce makes a card its controller's for the hand ([[R747]]). So a graveyard, like a hand, holds only its holder's cards, and every rule that reads a graveyard card's owner reads the holder. Examples: a play from "your graveyard" (C #28, C #74, Flashback), C #43 Plague Nuke's summon "from its owner's graveyard", a Starship Piece's fusion into the Starship of the player whose graveyard it entered ([[R1852002]]), and C #14 Shadowstep returning "your Units" to your hand.

It covers every way off the field into a graveyard: a death in the state check, a Destroy, a Sacrifice or Tribute (a wide tribute's enemy permanent goes to the graveyard of the player it stood for, [[R1282]]), and a backrow card that fires, ends or is destroyed. A replacement of that landing (B5 E5, [[R460]]: C #50 Voidwalker's exile, a library's bottom) sends the card to the same player's exile pile or library, because the card is theirs by then, and one that reads the card's owner reads that player: C #50's Radiant face, "Cards your opponent owns that would go to a graveyard are exiled instead", exiles a Unit of yours your opponent stole and killed, and not one of theirs you stole and killed. A stolen Reborn unit returns on the side it died on from the graveyard it landed in, as that player's ([[R172]]); a Reborn unit token, which no pile holds, comes back as theirs too.

Nothing else about ownership changes ([[R12]]). An Exile or a shuffle into a library from the field still sends a card to its owner's pile, a Bounce sends it to its controller's hand ([[R747]]), and a card leaving a hand or a library goes to its owner's piles, where its owner is its holder. A card its owner controls lands where it always did. Hearthstone, which has no ownership apart from control, puts a minion taken with Mind Control in its new controller's graveyard in the same way.
