[[R747000]]'s skill knob handed every decision its roll did not give to the AI to [[§10.7]]'s random policy: a uniform draw over the legal actions, ending the turn one time in ten whatever the board. The weakest ladder bot, the one a new player meets first, played that way at every decision, and the next two on three decisions in four and one in two. It lost, as it was meant to, but it played like nothing a person does: it ended turns with cards it could play, attacked into Units that killed its attacker for nothing, and played cards wherever they fit. That looks broken, and it tells a bot from a person, which [[R1452]]'s seat is built never to do.

**The miss.** A decision the roll does not give to the AI is the skill miss (`skill::skill_miss_action`, `crates/ai`). A draw offer waiting for the seat is declined, as the AI declines one ([[R188]]), and the ladder bot's own roll still turns that into its answer ([[R639000]]); a mulligan keeps what the greedy baseline keeps; neither takes a draw. Otherwise the miss lists the seat's candidates on the true state (`candidate_set`: the legal actions less [[R84]]'s three and the mulligan, as the AI lists them), so every move it can make is legal. It reads them on what the seat sees ([[R185]]): one world dealt from `redact(state, seat)` (`determinize`), and on it each candidate one ply ahead, the greedy baseline's evaluation (`GREEDY_EVAL`) after the move, any prompt it opens for the other seat answered with that prompt's first legal answer, as the baseline simulates (`one_ply_scores`). endTurn, and a move the world cannot simulate, score as standing still, so ending the turn is weighed like any other move and no legal move is barred. One softmax draw at `AI_SKILL_MISS_TEMPERATURE` (4 evaluation points) picks among them: a move 4 points below the best is drawn e⁻¹ times as often. A lone candidate takes no draw. Both draws, the world's and the pick's, are on the seat's own stream, never the match's rng, so a seeded game replays as before. The miss reads no card text and knows no set, which keeps [[R747000]]'s point: the knob is one number that means the same when the AI improves or a set ships. Zero skill is the miss at every decision, with no roll and no search. [[§10.7]]'s policy is unchanged everywhere else: the gates' baseline, the random seat, a timeout's answer, the fuzz.

**Measured.** Over the turn starts of 20 seeded random games, §10.7's random policy ended the turn while a card could be played 0.094 of the time (95 of 1008 picks) and the miss 0.019 (19), and it made an attack the greedy baseline scores below standing still 0.066 of the time (20 of 302) and the miss 0.010 (3) (`r2214000_a_strength_zero_miss_makes_far_fewer_beginners_mistakes_than_random`).

`bot-calibrate` on generation 13 (100 games a pair, `docs/bot-calibration.md`, Results) put the four weaker strengths at −314.8, −255.6, −166.4 and −115.6 Elo from the reference, so their seeds are 1517, 1576, 1666 and 1716, the reference's 1832 as before; the row went over `BOT_SEED_RATINGS` and the same readings over `SKILL_ELO_TABLE` ([[R748000]]). The neighbouring gaps, 59.2, 89.2, 50.8 and 115.6 Elo, are each more than twice the standard error of the difference (24.1, 23.5, 23.3 and 24.8, from the fit's own information matrix), so every strength still stands apart from the next. The spread from the weakest to the reference shrank from 616.6 to 314.8: a one-ply reading plays far better than a uniform move, and the weakest bot now wins 13 games of 100 against the reference, where it won 1. Only a new bot takes a seed: a bot already in the pool keeps its rating and its games move it from there ([[R610]]).

**Rejected.**

- Keeping the uniform miss and moving only the skills: a rarer uniform move is still a move no person makes, and the weakest bot, at zero skill, makes nothing else.
- A temperature for each strength (a field of `BotStrength`): five numbers for what the skills already set, and a second knob that would move each bot's rating apart from its skill.
- Drawing from the greedy baseline's own list, read on the determinized world: the world is dealt from what the seat sees, so nothing holds its list to the true state's, and a move the true state refuses would reach a ladder bot's seat as a refused move.
- Dropping a candidate the world cannot simulate: it would bar a legal move, and a beginner sometimes makes the move nobody would.
- A higher temperature to keep the old spread of ratings: no temperature keeps both. #2214's plan measured the miss at 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12: at 6 it already ends a turn with a card it could play about half as often as the random policy (0.049 against 0.096), and even at 16 the weakest strength won 6 of 100 against the reference. A wider spread needs a miss weaker than one-ply play, which is the uniform move this ruling replaces.
