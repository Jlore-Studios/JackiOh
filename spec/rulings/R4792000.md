While Meditative #86 Mayor Medinamogger acts, the engine draws every attack's target from the attacker's legal ones ([[R1200]]): `legal_actions` lists one attack per attacker, naming `"random"` (`RANDOM_ATTACK_TARGET`), and refuses a named target. The AI priced only an attack that names its target. The opponent model's `trade_value` found no unit called `"random"` and valued the attack at nothing, so the opponent the AI imagines never attacked while a Mayor acted: at 5 life against a ready Mayor with nothing in the way, the AI scored its turn as if it would live. Its own attacks went to the last attacks of its move order, after every play, answer, Trade and buy, where a full hand's `root_branching` cut could drop them; and the lethal solver's `ready_gap` counted each of them as if it chose the hero.

**The rule.** The AI decides this. An attack whose target the engine draws is worth the mean of a hit on each target it may draw (`attack_targets`, each as likely): on the enemy hero by `face_value`, on a unit by `trade_value`. The opponent's reply ranks it by that mean among its other attacks and makes it when the mean is above nothing. The AI's move order puts it with the attacks on the hero when the enemy hero is its only target; otherwise with the even trades, when some draw hits the hero or kills (its target, or with Cleave a neighbour) and the mean is an even trade or better, since each attacker has that one attack; otherwise among the other attacks. `ready_gap` counts such an attack only when the hero is its only target, and only that attack's hit is the win when the attacks left reach the face together (`face_is_lethal`), since a draw that may land elsewhere is no part of that count.

**The mean, not the worst.** A draw is read at its expectation, not at its worst target. The reply and the move order ask whether an attack is worth making, and an attack that most often hits the face or kills is worth making though one of its draws trades badly; read at its worst, a seat would hold back every attack one enemy unit could punish, the opponent the AI imagines among them, though its other draw is the AI's last life. A line the search plays through `reduce` is scored after the draw the engine makes on its sampled world, so the mean only orders the moves and prices the reply's choice. A Radiant Mayor's Lucky 1 ([[R1201]]) rolls its controller's draws again and keeps the better; the mean reads every draw as luck 0 does, a little below what a Lucky seat's attack is worth.

**What stays.** With no Mayor acting no attack names `"random"`, and the move order, the reply and `ready_gap` read every position as before. `AI_REPLY.drawn_attacks` off prices a drawn attack at nothing and counts it on the hero, as before.
