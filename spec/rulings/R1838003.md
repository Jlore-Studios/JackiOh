[[R82]] ends a turn by itself when ending it, conceding and the draw offers are all that is left; a Trade ([[R1838000]]) is none of those, so while one is on offer the turn waits for the player, and once it is made with nothing else left the turn ends by itself. A Duos round seat is kept in its round the same way ([[§13.3]]). The three answerers of a turn each take it as it is:

- **The random policy** ([[§10.7]], [[R44]]) draws a Trade from `legal_actions` like any other action, so My Pawn's turn and the fuzz may trade.
- **The clock** ([[R79]]) answers prompts and ends the turn; it makes no move of its own, so a timeout never trades.
- **The practice AI** ([[§9.9]]) tries a Trade in its search, after its plays and answers and before its other attacks, and keeps it when the line scores best. Every line runs on a determinized copy whose deck order is resampled ([[R185]]), so the card a Trade would draw never steers the decision: two states that differ only in what the seat cannot see give the same decision.
