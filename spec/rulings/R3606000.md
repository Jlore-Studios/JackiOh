Part of #3604 (issue #3606). [[R3351000]]'s shed order was a phone director's only: a desktop director halved its particle cap on slow windows but never shed. A laptop with a weak GPU, or a desktop at a high refresh rate, keeps every layer running however slow it gets, so every viewport's director now publishes shed stages on slow windows.

**Every viewport sheds in the ruled order.** On the same slow test and the same recovery as the phone — a window slower than `FX_ADAPT_SLOW_MS` (`FX_ADAPT_SLOW_FACTOR` × refresh) sheds one stage deeper, and `FX_ADAPT_RECOVER_WINDOWS` healthy windows step one stage back — every viewport's director walks full, then motes, then far auras, then faces: particle emission halves with a one-mote floor, then the auras and tiles past the pointer halve their mote budgets, then the moving faces past the pointer's own hold still, and never a set piece's main beats: a takeover, a fold, a launch, a rift and a title card still mount at the deepest stage, since they are DOM cues with no particle counts for emission scaling to touch ([[R2785000]]). Healthy frames at the display's refresh never leave full.

**What stays phone-only.** The phone keeps its tighter per-layer budgets (`PHONE_MOTION_BUDGET`, the phone particle cap); only the "never sheds on desktop" rule goes. The desktop sheds in [[R3351000]]'s order against its own full particle cap. No desktop-specific threshold was needed: healthy 60 Hz frames clear the existing slow test with margin, so a healthy desktop never sheds.

Presentation only (CLAUDE.md rule 7): nothing here reaches the engine, an action, `PlayerView` or the log. Hearthstone semantics do not reach frame budgets; the numbers stay [[R3351000]]'s.
