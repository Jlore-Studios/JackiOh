The AI charges a passed line for the crystals it leaves unspent, `AI_EVAL.unspent_mana` (0.8) each, less what that Float brings its Float-discount cards ([[R3695000]]), which is why it plays its cards rather than floating them ([[§9.9]], Evaluation). The beam's score of a passed line (`passed_score`) and its score after the reply (`reply_score`) both read the count from the seat's turn log, `unspentAtEnd`, which the engine sets at the turn's cleanup. The engine also ends a turn with nothing but ending it left to do in the same step ([[R82]]), unless its player turned that off ([[R345]]). Against a sampled opponent with no unit and nothing it could play in the hand that world dealt it, the seat's simulated End turn therefore ran on through the opponent's whole turn into the seat's next one, whose start rebuilds the seat's turn log: the count read 0, and a line that floated every crystal scored as one that spent them. That is the AI's first turns, where an opponent often has nothing to do: holding a (1) 0/1 on turn 1 against such an opponent, the AI ended its turn on every seed.

**The rule.** The AI decides this. Every world `decide` samples is played with the opponent's automatic turn end off (`search_world`), as the concession check's worlds already are ([[R2660000]]). So the seat's End turn, or a play that ends its turn, stops where the opponent's turn begins, the seat's turn log still holding what it left unspent, and the line is scored there as every other passed line is, the opponent to move; the opponent's reply then ends that turn itself, one node, and the line is scored at the seat's next decision as every reply is. The world is otherwise the one sampled, and the real opponent's turns still end by themselves. `AI_SEARCH.hold_opponent_turn` off plays the worlds as sampled.

**What stays.** Only an opponent's turn that would have ended by itself ends differently: at its start, as above, or in the reply after its last move, where the reply now plays its End turn, one node more. Every other line and reply is played and scored as before. A Duos duel's sides keep the automatic end off already, and so did an opponent who had turned it off. The seat's own turn still ends by itself inside a step when nothing is left to it.

**What it costs.** On the 212 states of [[R4791000]]'s sample, six random-policy games decided at 1,200, 600 and 150 nodes, 632 of 639 decisions were the same node for node; 5 took the same action at another count of nodes or another score, and 2 another first action. The two states whose scores moved were late games in which the opponent held units and no card in hand or library; the seat's End turn stopped on the opponent's turn in every world sampled, held or not, so what moved them lies inside the opponent's reply, past its attacks. Its deal screen, 400 games on improve-dev against the AI without the rule, read 202-198 with 8 deals decided (5 to 3) and 29 of 200 played differently: inconclusive, so it says nothing of strength either way and the rule rests on its scene tests.

**Rejected.** Carrying with each line the crystals it left unspent, read from the state before the step that ended its turn, and scoring a turn that came round to the seat again with the seat to swing, as the issue proposed: five places end a line's turn, a play that ends it holds no count before the step (the play spends some), and a line that reached the seat's next turn would still be scored beside lines that stop at the opponent's. Reading the count off the step's `turnEnded` event: the auto-answered steps of a simulation do not return their events.

Not done here: the seat's own extra turn ([[R846]]) still begins inside the End turn that earns it, the turn log rebuilt, and is scored with the enemy to swing next.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.
