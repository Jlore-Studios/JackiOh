Each endless loop had its own terminator, found one at a time once the fuzz tripped on it ([[R2197004]] and the caps beside it in `config.rs`), and a loop they missed panicked in the engine, at `STATE_CHECK_PASS_CAP` or `MAX_WORK_STEPS`. #3888 found one no card's terminator reaches: in a Radiant world (S+ #156, [[R3569000]]) a Radiant #3 Right-house defender facing a Radiant S #24 Warp Disruptor II dies, summons another and comes back for ever, since Reborn resets the memory the Disruptor's once-a-turn mark lives in. Hearthstone ends such a loop only with the turn timer, and Magic's answer, a draw for an unbreakable loop, would let three ordinary cards end a game. So one engine rule stops every loop, and the board up to it is a real result ([[§10.3]]).

**What counts.** An *interaction* is what starts a resolution loop: every action a seat takes, each answer a `timeout` gives and the End turn it takes ([[R79]]), each action the autopilot ([[R1183002]]) or an AI turn ([[R44]]) takes, a turn the engine ends for a seat ([[R82]], [[R456]]), and the setup and a Glitch's reset ([[R676]]), which are no seat's. Each starts a new count of the events emitted since, every `GameEvent` counted, the reports nothing answers among them. A prompt that opens before the cap is a pause, not a stop: its answer is the next interaction, and so is the answer to one an engine step opens after it.

**At the cap.** Once the count reaches `RESOLUTION_EVENT_CAP`, the next turn of any loop resolving the interaction stops it: the resolution loop, the state check's repeat ([[§4.5]] step 5), a drain of owed work ([[§9.3]]), a trap dispatch, an announce window. One that goes round that many times with no event stops the same way. Then the trigger queue and every event not yet offered are dropped; a public `loopCapped { player }` names the seat whose interaction it was; and a last state check collects the dead with their text off: no "would die" replacement, Death, Reborn or Starship fusion, the zones held for Reborn returns released, the graveyard step reported, and the hero check, then a held win, still ending a game that has ended, so `gameOver` stays the last event ([[R216]]). Card text is off until the next interaction: no event is offered, no trap fires, no queued trigger runs, no hook is marked, and an effect list stops at its next effect as at a game's end. Owed work still drains with its cards' text off, so a resolving Spell, a combat or a turn's end finishes, and control goes back to the interaction's seat.

**Where it lives.** `loop_cap.rs` keeps the count on the sink's `Frontier`, never in state, so a game that never reaches the cap hashes and replays as before. `STATE_CHECK_PASS_CAP` and `MAX_WORK_STEPS` stay as assertions past it, which play no longer reaches. It is a backstop: every card's own terminator stays (REVIEW A3.6), each deciding what its card does well before the cap, and the AI does not plan around it. The client's turn banner reads "Loop stopped" while the event's entry plays, with the effects on or off ([[§10.10]], [[R3910000]]), and the log says whose chain of effects was stopped.

**Rejected:** a draw at the cap (the owner's call on the issue); counting only the events the trigger loop holds, which gives a big turn more room but lets the reports slip past; dropping the owed work with the queue, which would strand a resolving Spell, a combat or a half-ended turn; 100 as the cap, since a turn with no loop emits hundreds of events; and the AI steering away from lines that reach the cap.
