**Size.** The pool holds `clamp(BOT_POOL_MIN, round(BOT_POOL_PER_WEEKLY_ACTIVE × weekly active people), BOT_POOL_MAX)` active bots: a quarter as many bots as the week's active people, never fewer than 8 and never more than 200. The weekly active people are the distinct people, bots left out, who finished a match in the last `BOT_POOL_ACTIVE_WINDOW_DAYS` (7) days. While `LADDER_BOTS` is `on` ([[R1452]]), the server sizes the pool as it boots and every `BOT_POOL_REFRESH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (an hour) after. An excess retires the active bots with the youngest accounts (the latest `created_at`, the account age drawn for each below), as new accounts are the ones a ladder loses first. A retired bot finishes the match it is in, and the fallback never pairs it again while it is retired ([[R1453]]). A shortfall puts back the most recently retired first, so the ladder keeps meeting the same faces, and only then makes new ones. Each sizing then starts or stops the top bots' grind ([[R1555000]]).

**A new bot.** A new bot is drawn from the pool's seed, as a person might sign up:

- a strength, one of five (`BOT_STRENGTHS`), each seeding the bot's rating at the curve's reading for the strength's skill ([[R747000]]), from 570 for the weakest, which is the random policy, to 1200 for the strongest (`BOT_SEED_RATINGS`, which [[R748000]]'s calibration measures the strengths against), beside a new player's deviation and volatility ([[R610]]), a strength it keeps unless it steps up while it grinds ([[R1555000]]);
- a speed, the multiple of its drawn think times, uniformly from 0.75 to 1.35 (`BOT_SPEED_MIN`, `BOT_SPEED_MAX`);
- a persona, by [[R645]]'s weights, which it keeps;
- a schedule of 8 to 16 consecutive hours a day (UTC) from an hour of its own, around midnight when it must (`BOT_ACTIVE_HOURS_MIN`, `BOT_ACTIVE_HOURS_MAX`), widened to `BOT_GRIND_ACTIVE_HOURS` while it grinds ([[R1555000]]);
- an account age, its profile's `created_at`, from 1 to 180 whole days before it was made (`BOT_ACCOUNT_AGE_MIN_DAYS`, `BOT_ACCOUNT_AGE_MAX_DAYS`), at a time of day drawn too;
- a username: for `BOT_DEFAULT_USERNAME_SHARE` (30%) of bots the `Player#n` every new account is given ([[R1434]]), as a person who skips the prompt keeps it ([[R1435]]). The rest draw one as people pick theirs. First a register by its share (`BOT_NAME_SHARE_*`): plain words, nonsense and gamer filler 35% together, edgy 12%, anime 13%, Chinese in Han characters or pinyin 10%, given names leaning Spanish-speaking 9%, crude and meme bait 9%, game and pop-culture references 6%, politics and public life 6%. Luis is the most common given name (`BOT_NAME_LUIS_SHARE`, 2% of all bots). Then a shape by the register's weights (`BOT_NAME_SHAPES_*`): two words run together or with `_`, a word with digits or a birth year, `xX…Xx`, `The…` or `…TV`, a fan's `…Main`, leetspeak, stretched letters, random syllables, a given name's initial or diminutive. The words are lists beside the pool (`crates/server/src/bots/names/`). The name is claimed as a person's pick is claimed, tag and filter included. One that `normalize_username` ([[R1432]], [[R1433]]) or the bots' own list refuses is drawn again in its register, up to `BOT_NAME_DRAW_ATTEMPTS` (10) times; after that, as when the store refuses the claim, the bot keeps its `Player#n`. Only a new bot draws a name, and a bot keeps its name for good.

**How far a bot's name goes.** The studio writes and wears these names, so [[R1433]]'s light filter is not enough. In: crude, juvenile and innuendo humour, mock violence and trash talk, dark-ish meme jokes, partisan political memes from every side, `69` and `420`. Out, whatever the filter says: slurs and their soundalikes in every language the lists use, hate and extremist references and codes, explicit sexual terms, names that read aloud as a blocked word, self-harm, real tragedies and their victims, and any real person beside a violent, sexual or crude word. A public figure appears only as a meme, nickname or slogan (`Obamna`, `DarkBrandon`, `Trump2028`), never as a bare full name or a `Real…`, `Official…` or `The_Real…` handle. At draw time the bots' own list, `never.txt` (whole tokens and substrings, folded as [[R1433]] folds, Han entries as substrings), refuses what no list may spell, on top of the shared filter. No shape puts a politics word beside an edgy or crude one, or writes a `Real`, `Official` or `The_Real` prefix.

A bot's profile is active and needs no auth user (migration 0032), and it owns no collection: the launch collection that every catalog stamp granted to active profiles ([[R481]]) went to people's alone, and since the economy went live no stamp grants anything to anyone ([[R1310001]]).

**Decks.** A bot holds two or three Best-of-1 decks (`BOT_DECKS_MIN`, `BOT_DECKS_MAX`). They are drawn from the current patch's distinct decklists that people took into live Best-of-1 games, those with enough games in the order the bot's strength takes them and the rest shuffled from the bot's seed, then from random decks dealt as All Random deals a player's ([[R258]]) leaning on the cards that win as far as the bot's strength says ([[R750000]]), trying at most `BOT_DECK_DRAW_ATTEMPTS` in all. Those are not the decks the AI draws for itself: they keep the cards [[R186]]'s shadow ban keeps from the AI's own decks and the sets [[R1390]]'s soft gate holds back, since a bot whose decks never held one would tell its seat from a person's once a gated set ships, as [[R1453]] has it for All Random. Each is kept only if a player could queue it for Best of 1: [[§9.4]]'s L2, L3, L5 and L6, checked against a collection of the deck's own cards, since a bot owns none and needs none. Each deck gets a portrait dealt from the seed ([[R642]]). The decks are drawn for one catalog version: when it changes, every active bot is dealt new ones at the next sizing, as is a bot that holds none. Until then the fallback seats it in no Best-of-1 game ([[R1453]]). Each active bot also holds a Conquest trio, dealt after its decks for the same catalog version and dealt again with them, without which it plays no Conquest ([[R641000]]).

**A start cut off.** The fallback's claim puts the bot in its match before the match starts ([[R1453]]). A start that fails lets both seats go. The queue's sweeper lets go every bot whose claimed match was still unstarted `BOT_STALE_START_SECONDS` (60) after its claim, which is what a crash or a restart mid-start leaves, and does so whatever `LADDER_BOTS` says, since the restart may have turned it off. The reaper's sweep of open rows ([[R1450]]) would let them go too, but only after its five-minute grace. Its player is let go with it, so neither is held out of the queue for good.
