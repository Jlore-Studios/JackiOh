With concessions now rare ([[R2660000]]), the owner allowed that a bot "may offer a draw if they think they are losing". Before, a ladder bot never offered one ([[R639000]], [[R188]]). This follows Hearthstone semantics as [[R639000]] does for the rest of a bot's manner: a person who is losing sometimes offers a draw, and the person facing them answers it.

**When.** At the start of its own turn, when it does not concede, a bot offers a draw when all three hold: [[R36]] lets it offer now (the engine's legal actions hold the offer, so once a turn, in its own main phase, not while a declined offer blocks it, and not on a turn with nothing else left in it, which [[R82]] ends); it trails its opponent by at least `BOT_DRAW_OFFER.min_behind`, 10, on [[R639000]]'s reading of how far it trails (half the gap between the two seats' evaluations, about one point per point of hero health or of a unit's stats, so a 4/4's worth); and its roll comes up, at `BOT_DRAW_OFFER.chance_per_turn`, 0.05, on the turn's own stream `{seed}:bot-offer-draw:{home}:{turn}`, from cursor 0. Both numbers are hand-set: `timing-fit` fits no rate of people's offers. The offer waits out the think time of a first move, the actor plays it as a server action like the bot's other moves, and the bot then plays on in its turn.

**The answer.** The person answers it as they would answer a person's offer, and it stands until they do or the bot's turn ends ([[R269]]). A person who declines blocks the bot's next offers as [[R36]] says.

**What stays.** [[R188]] still holds for `decide` and the practice AI, which never offer. The actor still never plays an emote as a bot's move (`never_played`), and no longer drops a draw offer. Fitting the offer's rate from people's offers was left for later, and offering later in the turn was rejected: a turn's start is the one moment the bot reads where it stands before it acts, as it does for a concession.
