**Why.** On 2026-11-28 `/stats` did not load. Staging's deploy check (run 46096991115) timed out twice at 75 s on `GET /api/stats/cards?mode=all`, which players see as "The card statistics didn't load." Every figure [[R978000]] and [[R3067000]] keep lived `CARD_STATS_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS` (300 s), and once that time was up the next read waited for a whole reload. A reload took over a minute on staging and over five minutes in the owner's account, longer than the client's wait. A match start waited too, since it reads the same figures through `win_rate_snapshot` ([[R3049000]]). A snapshot was also dated from when it was first asked for, not from when its load finished (#5207). So a load longer than 300 s finished already expired, the next read started it again from nothing, and a read made 300 s into a load started a second one beside it, holding both in memory (#5139).

**Kept from when its load finished.** A kept snapshot lasts `CARD_STATS_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS` from when its load finished (`Snapshot::loaded_at`), and every window, history, play versions' table and Hall of Fame cut from it is dated by that time. While a load runs, every read joins it, however long it has run, so only one load runs at a time. A load that fails leaves nothing held, and the next read makes it again, as [[R3067000]] says.

**Stale while it refreshes** (`api::stats::kept_value`). Once a value is held, no read waits on the next one. A read past its time answers at once with the value held and starts one refresh beside it, on a task of its own ([[R5186000]]). Every read meanwhile gets the held value and starts no second refresh. When the refresh lands, later reads get the new value and the held one is let go. A refresh that fails leaves the held value in place, logs `stats.refresh.failed`, and the next read starts another. This holds for the snapshot, for every figure cut from it, for the drill-down's per-patch reports ([[R978000]]) and for the play rates ([[R1583000]], `PLAY_RATES_REFRESH_SECONDS`). A figure cut from the snapshot answers with its last cut while it is cut again from the new snapshot. So right after a refresh lands, a read can still answer the figures of the snapshot before, and the figures shown are at most about one TTL, one load and one cut old. Only a read with nothing held waits: the first after a boot (the bot pool's boot refresh starts that load, [[R3049002]]), or a key past its time by `CARD_STATS_KEPT_IDLE_SECONDS` (an hour) with no read to renew it. Such a key is let go, so a key asked once is not held forever. `CARD_STATS_KEPT_REPORTS` still bounds how many are held.

**The older play versions** ([[R5174000]]). When the held snapshot that the cards read is past its time and a read of the play versions' table or the Hall of Fame comes first, it shares one load with the cards only if a snapshot that folds the older play versions in is held to serve them all while it loads. Otherwise the cards keep the snapshot they read while their own loads, and the folding one loads beside it. A folding load under way is joined, not dropped.

**Memory.** While a refresh runs, the held snapshot and the one loading are both in memory. [[R4965000]] bounds what a snapshot holds, and the one held is let go once the next is held. Before this, a load longer than the TTL could run twice at once, each holding a whole load.

`GET /api/stats/loading` still reports a refresh as a load, and `Cache-Control` is unchanged.

Amends [[R978000]] and [[R3067000]] (a read past the TTL answers what is held while it refreshes), [[R1583000]] (the play rates likewise) and [[R5186000]] (a load is joined whatever its age, and the snapshot is kept from when its load finished).

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone settles nothing here.

Rejected:
- A longer `CARD_STATS_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS`: every reload would still make the reads at its end wait, a match start among them.
- Refreshing on a timer whether anyone reads or not: on staging's quarter of a processor, a load every five minutes is a constant cost even when nobody is on `/stats`.
- Letting the held snapshot go before the next loads, so only one is in memory: every key not cut before would wait on the whole load again, the cards page's own among them on its first read after a refresh.
