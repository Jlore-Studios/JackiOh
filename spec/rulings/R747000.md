One AI ships. A weaker ladder bot ([[R1452]]) used to be that AI made worse by hand: a smaller search, a softer choice among its finalist lines, a chance to pass over a lethal ([[R1454]]'s five strengths), each rating set by hand and none measured. This ruling adds one more knob that knows nothing of the AI's internals and so keeps its meaning when the AI is replaced or a set ships, and puts a measured rating on it.

**The knob.** A seat that plays the AI has a skill, a number from 0 to 1 (`AI_SKILL_MIN`, `AI_SKILL_MAX`, in `crates/ai`): the share of its decisions that follow the AI, the rest being a random legal action, [[§10.7]]'s random policy (`random_action`). At each decision the seat rolls once on its own stream (`skill::plays_best`): the AI when the roll falls under the skill, the policy when it does not. Full skill is the AI exactly as it was, with no roll and so no draw on the stream; zero skill is the random policy exactly, with no roll and no search. In between, a skill advances the stream by one draw a decision, and the same seed plays the same game. A skill outside the range reads as the end it is past, and a NaN as full skill (`skill::clamp_skill`). The AI still reads only what [[R185]] lets it read, and the random policy reads the true state as every random seat does, as the gates' baseline does: neither changes what a client sees. The knob is no practice handicap ([[R180]]): practice's difficulty tiers are untouched, and a skill is for opponents that have to be weaker on this spec's resources.

**Where it applies.** The match harness takes it in a seat's controller (`SeatController::Ai { budget, skill }`, none meaning full), so self-play games, the gates and the development runs ([[R378]]) are unchanged unless a seat sets one. The arena takes one for each of its two agents (`--a-skill`, `--b-skill`, default 1, each in [0, 1] and refused outside it), for `self` and for a `bin:` build alike, and refuses one for `random`, which takes no AI decision; a skill short of 1 is part of the agent's label (`self-s0.5`), so its records, which share seeds with the full-skill run's, are records of their own ([[R378]]). The ladder bot's seat takes its strength's skill: the roll is the first thing on the bot's own AI stream at each decision, after a concession ([[R639000]]), and a decision it hands to the policy has no search, no deadline and is never a lethal for the think time. R188 holds for either: the seat drops a concession, a draw offer, an accepted draw and an emote, and a draw offer waiting for the bot is declined as the search declines it, so [[R639000]]'s own answer is the bot's at every skill.

**The strengths.** Each of `BOT_STRENGTHS` carries a skill beside its search budget, its softmax temperature and its chance to miss a lethal, which are as they were: from the weakest row to the strongest, 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. The weakest bot is therefore the random policy. A bot keeps its index, so each bot of a row takes that row's skill from the deploy on, and its rating goes on from where it stands ([[R610]]); a bot made after it is seeded at the curve's rating for its row's skill (`expected_elo_for_skill`).

**The curve.** `SKILL_ELO_TABLE` in the server's config is the Elo a seat at a skill is expected to hold, one measured row each at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1, and `expected_elo_for_skill` reads it by straight lines between rows, clamped to its ends; `BOT_STRENGTHS`' seed ratings are its readings at the rows' skills. The rows are measured with arena games of the skill against the reference, this build's AI at full skill, and against the random policy, scored as Elo gaps by the protocol in `crates/server/README.md` ("Skill to Elo"), and held to the reference's rating, `SKILL_ELO_ANCHOR`. They are a prior, and the live Glicko ratings ([[R603]], [[R610]]) are the ongoing calibration: a new best AI, or a new set, is measured again by the same protocol, and the table pasted over.

**Rejected.**

- A separate weaker model, or a lower search budget as the only knob, for each bot: a model must be trained and kept as the AI is replaced, and a budget's meaning moves with the AI's search; a share of decisions does not.
- Mixing in the random move inside the search (as an exploration rate on the finalists): [[R1452]]'s temperature already does that, and its effect on strength is not a plain share of decisions that anyone can state.
- Rolling the skill once a turn: a turn is a run of decisions of different weight, and the knob would then say nothing about how often a bot errs.
- Anchoring the reference at a new player's rating, 1000 ([[R603]]): the pairing seats no bot for a player rated above every bot ([[R1453]]), and the strongest bot had been seeded at 1200, so a 1000 anchor would leave every player past it with no opponent until the bots' ratings rose; the anchor is the strongest bot's old seed instead.
- Seeding the rating of an existing bot again: the migration would rewrite ratings that games have since moved, and Glicko-2 already corrects the seed as games come in.
