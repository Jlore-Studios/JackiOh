We ship one AI, so weaker bots come from degrading it, never from a separate model. Every seat
that plays it can set a skill in [0, 1]: the share of its decisions that follow the best AI, the
rest SPEC §10.7's random policy. 1.0 plays exactly what the AI recommends, draw for draw — the
roll draws nothing at the endpoints, so the reference play is bit-for-bit what it was before
skill — and 0.0 is fully random. A middle skill draws one roll per decision, then either decides
at full strength or plays the random policy on the same stream, skipping the search outright when
the roll fails it. The roll draws on the caller's seeded stream, so seeded runs reproduce, and it
knows nothing about cards or sets, so it keeps working when the best AI is updated or a new set
ships. NaN behaves as 0.

A ladder bot plays at its strength's skill ([[R1452]]): the bot seat rolls its strength's skill
before every decision, and a failed roll plays the random policy kept to what [[R188]] lets a bot
play, else the seat's fallback move. Self-play matches seat the same mixture as a `Skill`
controller, and the arena degrades each `self` entrant by `--skill-a`/`--skill-b` (refused with
any other agent), naming the skill in the entrant's record label.

The skill-to-Elo function is `skill_expected_elo(skill, reference_elo)`: the reference's rating
minus a piecewise-linear offset over the measured table `SKILL_ELO_OFFSETS`, which an arena
ladder measures (each skill against the reference at full budget, Elo deltas by the logistic)
instead of guessing, and which is re-measured whenever the best AI is updated. The live
`bot_ratings` ([[R610]]) keep self-correcting between calibrations.
