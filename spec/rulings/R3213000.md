After a result the match screen read `GET /api/matches/:id/rematch` ([[R672]]) or `GET /api/matches/:id/series` every `SERIES_POLL_SECONDS` ([[R3205000]], [[R3207002]]), though the match socket that carried the game was still open and the server knew the moment either answer changed.

**The frames.** The match socket (`/ws/match`) carries two more frames beside `portraits`, never in `PlayerView` (`jackioh_shared::host::post_game::PostGameFrame`):

- `{ type: "rematch", v, youOffered, opponentOffer, opponentHere, matchId, mode }` for a match in no series: what that seat's own `GET /api/matches/:id/rematch` answers, field for field (`mode` `"bo1"` or `"random"`, as [[R1372]] reads it).
- `{ type: "series", v, series }` for a game of a Conquest series: that seat's own `SeriesView`, as its `GET /api/matches/:id/series` answers it.

**When.** Each rides every greeting, after the portraits of every `hello` and attach, so the client knows before the result that this socket tells it of either; a series game's greeting reads the series once, off the actor's queue. A rematch frame goes to each open seat again whenever the rematch book changes for the match (an offer taken, a claim made or given back after a failed start; an offer past the window, which records nothing, sends nothing, [[R2685000]]) and whenever who is here changes: a socket arriving or leaving, or a ladder bot's post-game linger ending ([[R639000]]). A series frame goes to every open seat of every game of the series each time a transition is written or a game's result moves it on.

**One view per seat.** A frame is built for its seat alone, from the book's `seen` and the actor's presence, or from `project_series` for that seat's account, so the leans of [[R924000]] and the other side's sealed pick and decks ([[R331]], [[R336]]) never ride it (CLAUDE.md rule 7). It goes to that seat's own socket only: never to a watcher, and a bot's seat has no socket.

**A bot leaves as a person does.** A person who leaves closes their socket and the seat still there is told. A ladder bot has none, so the machine arms a timer at the end of its linger (`botLeaves`) and, when it fires, outputs `presenceChanged` (unless the bot's reply to an offer is still on its way, which keeps it until the reply lands, when `rematchSent` outputs it instead), and the host tells each seat its status again. The rematch buttons then leave a bot's death screen as they leave a person's ([[R1452]]).

**Order.** Rematch frames are built and sent under the actor's lock, so the last one a seat gets reads the book as it last changed. A series frame is dropped when its row's `version` is older than the newest the actor has sent, so a slow greeting read never takes a seat back.

**The client.** It keeps the newest frame of each kind in its match snapshot while the socket is open and clears both when the socket drops or is replaced. While it holds one it sends no rematch or series-banner read, through the result and after: the rematch buttons and the watcher take the pushed status where they took the poll's ([[R3207002]]'s one poll per match is not joined), and the buttons skip the status read after an offer, since the socket pushes the status that offer made. Once the socket drops, or if it never pushed a frame (an older server), the polls run as before, one read at once and then each `SERIES_POLL_SECONDS`. The series screen (`/series/:id`) opens no match socket and keeps its poll.

**Versions.** `v` is the server's `POST_GAME_FRAME_VERSION`, exported to the client's `serverConfig.ts`. A client reads only a frame of the version it knows, and one with any field it cannot read is no frame, so it polls; a client older than the frames ignores both `type`s, as it ignores every frame it does not know, and polls. A changed shape takes the next number.

**Presentation only.** The server still decides every offer, presence and pick; the frames carry the same answers as the reads, sooner.

Hearthstone semantics: the opponent's answer to a rematch, and their leaving, show the moment they happen.

Rejected: sending the frames only once the game is over (a client that greets during play would not know the socket pushes, and polls once before the first frame); a separate frame announcing the capability (the greeting's own status is that announcement, and is one frame fewer); telling a bot's absence on the reaper's tick (up to 30 s late, while a person's leaving is told at once); deriving ts-rs in the server crate (the frame is the shared crate's, whose types the browser and Roblox both read); nesting the frames inside the server's `ServerMessage` union (an older client already ignores an unknown `type`, and the union is the game's, not the post-game screen's).
