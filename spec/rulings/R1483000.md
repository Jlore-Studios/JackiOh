The Jlore that games pay an account, ladder games and practice games together ([[§14.8]]), is
counted over one UTC day: from 00:00:00.000 UTC to the next, the day the game ended in (for a
practice game, the day its claim was taken). The day's `game` Jlore rows of the account never add
up to more than `JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP`. A game pays its rate, or what is left of the cap if that
is less: the game that crosses the cap is paid exactly the part that fits, and a game after it is
paid no Jlore and writes no Jlore row, since the ledger holds no row of nothing ([[§14.11]]). The
sum is read in the transaction that writes the game's row, after the account's economy lock is
taken ([[R1301003]]), so two games ending at once cannot pay past the cap. The next UTC day pays
in full again.

The cap is on game Jlore alone. A capped game pays its XP in full and moves the account's quests as
any game does ([[R1302000]], [[R1302001]]). The Jlore a quest or a track level pays ([[§14.7]],
[[§14.9]]) neither counts toward the cap nor stops at it, and neither does a pack bought or a dev
tool's grant.

A game's answer says when the cap held its Jlore back, whole or in part. A practice claim answers
`jloreCapped: true`, its `jlore` what was paid. On the ladder the server tells each seat of a
finished match, on that seat's own `view` frames, whether the cap held back that seat's Jlore
(`jloreCapped`, left off a frame while false), and never tells the opponent or a watcher. It reads
the answer off the ledger and the result, not from memory: the account's `game` row for the match
is below the rate for its result and its day has reached the cap, so a repeat of the result, a
reconnect and an actor rebuilt on the finished match all answer alike. The practice result line
and the ladder result panel show "Daily Jlore reached".

An account holds at most `PRACTICE_SEEDS_OPEN_MAX` practice seeds that are unclaimed and issued
within `PRACTICE_SEED_TTL_SECONDS` ([[R1303001]]). A seed request while it holds that many is a
409 and issues nothing; the count is read under the account's economy lock, after the account's
lapsed seeds are pruned, so requests made at once cannot pass it together. A claim, paid or
refused, burns its seed before its check waits for a permit ([[R1303001]]), so it frees its place
at once, and so does a seed's lapsing: the limit is on the seeds an account holds, not on the claims
it has waiting for `PRACTICE_VERIFY_CONCURRENCY`'s checks ([[§14.8]]). A game the page could not
get a seed for is played as before and earns nothing ([[R1303000]]).

`JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP` is 2000, the owner's number on #1483, and `PRACTICE_SEEDS_OPEN_MAX` is 3,
the approved proposal's. The proposal suggested 600, the Jlore of 30 ladder wins, which the owner
raised; a cap set too low would tax a long honest session.

Hearthstone semantics: the gold games pay stops at a daily limit, quests apart.

Rejected: a cap for each mode, which would double what an account can farm and need a second
number; a cap that counted quest and track Jlore, which would stop an honest day's quests paying;
a cap in games played, which would count a game conceded on its first move as much as a win while
Jlore is what is farmed; a rolling 24 hours, which needs a time kept for each account where the UTC
day needs none; a stored flag for what the cap held back, which would need a migration, where the
ledger already says it; and no limit on open seeds beyond `API_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE`, which lets an
account stockpile seeds and claim them in a burst. Counting a claim still waiting for its check as
holding its seed was left out: the burn is the only record of a claim, and a count of them would
need a new column or a per-process counter that a restart forgets.
