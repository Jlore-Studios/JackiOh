A Starship Piece that dies fuses a copy of itself into its owner's Dormant Starship, the Starship summoned first when they have none ([[R1852000]]), and the AI's evaluation counts a Dormant card as the unit it wakes as, at a share ([[R2380000]]). That fusion is the death pass's own last step, not a Death hook, so [[R2424000]]'s probe, which took only units with a live Death, priced it at nothing: the AI read killing an enemy piece as clean removal, though it feeds the enemy's Starship, and its own pieces as their stats. No Hearthstone rule is involved: the evaluation changes no game.

**What it counts.** At a decision's root the probe also takes each Unit that is a Starship Piece as the death pass reads one (`is_starship_piece`: the keyword through [[§10.4]]'s layers, on a face-up card), on either field and in the seat's own hand, on the same probe world ([[R185]]). It prices it as R2424000 prices a Death: its controller's evaluation after the sandbox's destroy, whose death pass fuses the copy, less after its remove, which does not. `unit_worth` adds `AI_EVAL.death` of the price and `kill_worth` takes the whole of it off a kill, while the card is a piece. A piece with a Death of its own was already probed as a Death, its price holding its fusion, and it prices as before.

**Budget order.** Every live Death is probed first, in R2424000's order (the seat's field, the other field, the seat's hand), then each piece with no Death of its own, in the same order, within the same `AI_DEATH_PROBE_NODES` ([[R186]]'s budget). A Death draws the nodes it drew before, so no piece takes them from a Death. The pieces left when the budget runs out are not priced.

**Rejected.** One pass in board order, which let a piece listed ahead of a Death take its nodes. A share of the piece's own worth as its price, which misses that the first piece also summons the Starship and that a Dormant Starship counts at its slumber share. A separate budget for pieces, a new constant for what the order already settles.
