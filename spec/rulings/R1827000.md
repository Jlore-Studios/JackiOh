The global cosmetic roll ([[R1661001]]) drops an account cosmetic the account does not own, Epic or
better, but every playmat, backdrop and card back is a starter ([[R1661000]]), so on the shipped
registry it never fired. Issue #1827 makes the hero portraits of [[R641]] account cosmetics that
accounts earn, so the roll has something to drop:

- **The kind.** The registry gains the kind `portrait`, an account cosmetic, with one entry per
  portrait id, `portrait-<id>`: `portrait-vanilla`, Common, the kind's default and a starter, owned
  by every account with no ledger row; and `portrait-gary`, `portrait-timmy`, `portrait-dfender`,
  `portrait-felinors` and `portrait-shredder`, Epic (`GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY`), no starters.
  A deck whose `portrait` is `null` still reads as `vanilla` ([[R641]]). The registry's check holds
  one entry for each portrait id, none for any other and vanilla alone the default, and
  [[R1661000]]'s floor holds a portrait as it holds any account cosmetic. The kind is not one of the
  kinds the settings pick, since a portrait is a deck's pick, so the client's settings gain no
  group for it.
- **What drops one.** The global roll's account cosmetics are now the five portraits but vanilla,
  in registry order, so on the shipped registry a duplicate takes the global draw while one is left
  unowned ([[R1661001]], whose roll is unchanged). #1833 adds its 18 playmats, backdrops and card
  backs after them, so the pool is 23 and an account that owns all five portraits still takes the
  draw while one of those is unowned; once all 23 are owned it takes none and draws as before
  ([[R1833000]]). Under the rules that already stand, a cosmetic pack's slot may drop one
  ([[R1306000]]) and an Epic or better cosmetic token buys one ([[R1305003]]); none is a chase.
- **Existing accounts keep all six.** Every account that exists when the change ships could pick
  every portrait, and keeps them: migration 0057 gives every person's profile then signed up,
  whatever its status, the five that are not starters, each an account cosmetic on no card with its
  ledger row. A ladder bot is no account of the economy and is given none ([[R1454]]). The row's
  reason is `launch`, the closest of the ledger's closed set, as [[R1310002]]'s move at going live
  was, with the ref `launch:0057`; a portrait an account already owns is passed over and gets no row,
  so every ledger still sums to what is held ([[§14.11]]). The grant runs once per database,
  `app.settings.portraits_granted` marking it done. An account made after it owns vanilla alone and
  earns the rest.
- **The deck save.** On top of D5's known-id check, the server refuses a save whose `portrait`
  names one the account does not own: a 400 `bad_request` carrying one D5 issue, `"portrait" is a
  portrait this account does not own.`, as its message and in its `details`, D5's own shape. `null`
  and vanilla always save. This is the check [[R1310002]] left to the issue that added the first
  cosmetic a player picks that is not a starter. A trio import's decks carry no portrait
  ([[R641]]), so it checks none. Queueing does not ask again: a saved deck's portrait was owned when
  it was saved, and no player's path takes a portrait back.
- **What stays.** All Random still deals each seat a portrait from the whole roster ([[R642]]):
  like its deck, the dealt portrait is the game's, not the account's. Practice, hotseat and the
  end-to-end specs treat every cosmetic as owned ([[R1310004]]), and no end-to-end spec saves a
  portrait.
- **The client.** While ownership decides, as it does for full art ([[R1310004]]: a production
  build), the deck builder's portrait picker offers the starter and the portraits the account owns,
  read from `GET /api/cosmetics`, and always the deck's own pick, so a deck shows what it holds; a
  development or end-to-end build offers every portrait. The Collection's
  owned list ([[R1579000]]) showed the owned portraits until [[R2731000]] removed it. A card pack's
  reveal and a cosmetic pack's that drop a portrait show its name and its portrait's art. The client
  decides nothing: the server refuses an unowned one whatever the picker offered (CLAUDE.md rule 7).

This amends [[R641]] (the six are no longer every account's, and a save's portrait must be owned),
[[R1661000]] and [[R1661001]] (the account cosmetics include the portraits, and the global roll is
live on the shipped registry) and [[R1310002]] (the cosmetic a player picks that is not a starter is
here, with its check).

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone's alternate heroes are account-wide cosmetics a player earns or
buys, its default heroes everyone's, and a hero a player does not own cannot be set on a deck.

Rejected: a reason of its own for the grant in the ledger's closed set, which would change the
check on a table for one migration's rows, where `launch` already says a launch moved them; `portrait`
among the kinds the settings pick, which would give the synced settings a group for a pick a deck
carries; a D6 rule in the shared draft validator, which knows no account and would have the client
judge ownership, where the server already reads it; and granting only active accounts, which would
take from a pending account portraits it could have picked the day it was let in.
