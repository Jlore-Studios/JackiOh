On `/login`, Google's button ([[R666]]'s own, or the one Google Identity Services draws, [[R2983000]]) and Roblox's ([[R3147000]]) read as one matched pair. Both take one size, `OAUTH_BUTTON_WIDTH_PX` by `OAUTH_BUTTON_HEIGHT_PX` (`auth/googleIdentity.ts`): Google Identity Services is drawn at that width through its `width` option (its large button is that tall), and `auth.css` repeats both as `--auth-oauth-width` and `--auth-oauth-height`, held equal by a test. One rule in `auth.css`, `auth-oauth-branded`, sets the height, width, corner radius, padding, mark size and gap and the text's size and weight of Google's fallback and of Roblox's, and each brand's own rule sets its colors only, so neither can drift from the other. The two sit side by side in one row, Google first and Roblox second, centered with an even gap; only when the board is too narrow for both do they wrap, one above the other, each still that size; a build that names one of them shows it alone, centered. Roblox's button has Google's states: hover changes its colors, the focus ring is the tavern's gold one (not set again here), and `busy` dims it and refuses its click, none of them moving anything. The button keeps R3147000's label, "Continue with Roblox", its `data-provider`, its test id and the hint under the row, and the sign-in flows ([[R666]]'s PKCE, [[R2983000]]'s token) are unchanged. Roblox's button carries Roblox's official tilt mark, its path unaltered, drawn inline as an `svg` (`RobloxMark` in `auth/OAuthButtons.tsx`, so nothing is fetched and no file is supplied) at the Google mark's size (`OAUTH_MARK_PX`, one `auth-oauth-mark` rule), hidden from assistive technology and filled with the button's text color, so it is white on the black face; the button's accessible name stays "Continue with Roblox".
