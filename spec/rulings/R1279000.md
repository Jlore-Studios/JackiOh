A prompt held by the non-active player never opens at once (issue #1279; [[§10.6]]). Hearthstone has no non-active-player prompts — its Secrets never ask — so the three cards that held one all defer it the same way, to the start of its holder's next turn.

The effect that would open the prompt instead schedules it with the engine's `delay` verb ([[§2.2]]'s start-of-turn delayed stage): `at` is the start of the holder's turn (`{ phase: "start", player: "enemy" }`, relative to the controller as every other player spec is), carrying the prompt's data, and the deferred step opens the identical prompt when the holder's next turn starts. If the holder is already the active player the prompt opens at once, as before. The options are read when the deferred step opens the prompt, not when the effect fires; the prompt runs on the holder's own turn clock, and a timeout answers it with the AI policy ([[R79]]); if the game ends first the entry dies with it and the prompt never opens.

C #8 Pickle's mode chain ([[§6.3]] Choose one) waits whole: the Cry schedules the first question, and every later question follows its answer on the holder's turn as before. C #9 Income Tax's keep-one prompt waits with the Radiant discount it would have carried. C #90 In Too Deep's reward G waits too: choosing G schedules its discard prompt, and the quest G leads to opens with the answer, once. A discard "of your choice" still asks its discarder while it resolves ([[R682]]); it just resolves on the discarder's turn now. M #1's guard stops only discards off the holder's turn, so the deferred questions open where it sleeps ([[R800]]).

Rejected: answering for the holder (randomly, or by the controller), since the issue prescribes the start-of-turn shape and the choice is the holder's to make; and keeping the prompt on the holder's off-turn clock, since no player answers while they are not active.
