A card with two prices (`CardCost::Embiggen { base, embiggen }`, "A embiggen B" in [[§5]] and [[§8]]) is an **embiggen card**. Played at the cheaper price it is its **normal version**; played at the dearer price it is its **Embiggened version**, and that price is its **Embiggened cost**. The play chooses the version as it chose the price ([[R81]]), and nothing else changes: this is wording, and no rule reads it.

Its text labels what the Embiggened version does "Embiggened (N): …", N its Embiggened cost, on a line of its own and on both faces: "Embiggened (4): −{paidDebuff}/−{paidDebuff} instead.", "Embiggened (3): Equip." The Chinese table writes it "加码(N)：". This amends [[R366]]'s label "Paid (N): …", which no face prints any more; R366 keeps its id and its other words. The client says the same words: the price picker asks "Play the normal or the Embiggened version?" over its Normal and Embiggened cards, the second cost badge beside a card's gem reads "Embiggened (N)" to a screen reader and on hover, the glossary's Embiggened entry ("An embiggen card has two prices. Its normal version costs the cheaper one; its Embiggened version costs more and does more.") shows on every embiggen card, since its text says the word, and a patch diff or a static page writes the price "(2) Cost, Embiggened (4)".

The engine's identifiers keep their names, as [[R373]] kept `library`: `embiggen`, `embiggened`, `CardCost::Embiggen`, the `play` action's `embiggen` and the prompt kind `embiggen`. A card's `params` (`paidDebuff`, `paidArmor`, `paidCount`, `paidChance`) are card data and keep theirs. #84 Going Long's two Armor values are `HERO_ARMOR`'s `normal` and `embiggened`, where they were `paid` (the cheaper price) and `embiggen`. The designer asked for the vocabulary: "standardize the multiple cost cards to have it be that they are called embiggened cards and that the more expensive cost is considered embiggened version and the cheaper cost is considered normal version".
