A player can own every cosmetic of a tier, so a slot's rolled rarity may have nothing unowned to
give; when #1306 shipped no Legendary or Mythic cosmetic could drop at all, and since #1833 some can
([[R1833000]]). The slot then reads the rarities in `fallback_order`'s
order, as a card pack's does for cards ([[R1301001]]): its own, then each lower one from the
nearest down, then each higher one from the nearest up, and takes the first with a cosmetic still
open ([[R1306000]]).

- **A Legendary or Mythic roll** with none left unowned therefore falls to the highest tier
  below with something unowned: Epic, then Rare, then Common. While a chase of its tier is
  unowned it grants that chase ([[R1833000]]).
- **The guaranteed slot** (the first, `PACK_FLOORED_SLOT`) stays Epic or better while anything at
  Epic or above is open: its rarities at or above its floor, `COSMETIC_PACK_FLOOR_RARITY`
  ([[R1339000]]), are read before any below it, so an Epic roll with no Epic open takes a Legendary
  or Mythic before it drops to Rare. Only when none at Epic or above is open does it take what is
  left below.
- **Nothing open in any rarity** spends the slot: it grants nothing and draws nothing, and adds
  the points of the cosmetic rarity it rolled, `TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_BY_COSMETIC_RARITY`'s, to each of
  the two token bars, as a card pack's spent duplicate adds its card rarity's ([[R1953000]]),
  settled as a card pack's are ([[R1305000]]): the same `settle_bars`, one row per bar per opening.
- **The slot keeps its rolled rarity** in the log; the grant names the rarity it fell to.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone gives a duplicate's dust when a pack's card has nothing left to
give; the spec's version is the bars' points, which [[§14.5]] says every spent duplicate pays.

Rejected: falling downward only, under which a Common roll with no Common cosmetic would pay points
while Rares stay unowned, against the issue's "if nothing at all is unowned"; and letting the
guaranteed slot fall below Epic while a Legendary or Mythic is open.
