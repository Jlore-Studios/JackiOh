No Legendary or Mythic cosmetic exists yet, and a player can own every Epic, so a slot's rolled
rarity often has nothing unowned to give. The slot then reads the rarities in `fallback_order`'s
order, as a card pack's does for cards ([[R1301001]]): its own, then each lower one from the
nearest down, then each higher one from the nearest up, and takes the first with a cosmetic still
open ([[R1306000]]).

- **A Legendary or Mythic roll** with none in the registry therefore falls to the highest tier
  below with something unowned: Epic, then Rare, then Common.
- **The guaranteed slot** (the first) stays Epic or better while anything at Epic or above is open:
  its rarities at or above the lowest one it can roll are read before any below it, so an Epic roll
  with no Epic open takes a Legendary or Mythic before it drops to Rare. Only when none at Epic or
  above is open does it take what is left below.
- **Nothing open in any rarity** spends the slot: it grants nothing and draws nothing, and adds
  what a spent duplicate adds, `CARD_TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_PER_DUPLICATE` and
  `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_PER_DUPLICATE`, to the two token bars, settled as a card pack's are
  ([[R1305000]]): the same `settle_bars`, one row per bar per opening.
- **The slot keeps its rolled rarity** in the log; the grant names the rarity it fell to.

Hearthstone semantics: Hearthstone gives a duplicate's dust when a pack's card has nothing left to
give; the spec's version is the bars' points, which [[§14.5]] says every spent duplicate pays.

Rejected: falling downward only, under which a Common roll with no Common cosmetic would pay points
while Rares stay unowned, against the issue's "if nothing at all is unowned"; and letting the
guaranteed slot fall below Epic while a Legendary or Mythic is open.

§14's text is #1333's to change.
