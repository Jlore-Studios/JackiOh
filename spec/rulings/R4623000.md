The fuzz monitor's I21 ([[R4508005]]) blames every event of a Spell's run on the Spell, up to the next card's marker. While issue #4623 was built, the changed cards' fuzz wave dealt a game in which S #29 Drone Core III, a Spell, summoned Drone Interceptors into the last open zones of its caster's unit row, beside S+ #2 Lone Drifter, which held Immune to Spells. At the settled state check Lone Drifter's "Evolves when all your Unit zones are full" held ([[R3536001]]), and it evolved. The `evolved` event lands in whatever run is open, before the Evolve text's own marker, so I21 named the Spell. The same seed fails on the engine from before the issue, so the board, not the issue's change, brought it out.
**This is a rule, not the Spell.** [[§6.1]] keeps a Spell's effects off the Unit ([[R4453000]]). An "Evolves when" ([[§6.2]]) is the card's own standing condition, and the state check ([[§4.5]]) reads it for every card on the field, an immune one included. The Spell changed the board, the condition then held, and the card evolved itself, as a Unit's own trigger answers a Spell's summon. Hearthstone does the same: a minion that cannot be targeted by spells still answers the board a spell leaves.
**What I21 allows.** An `evolved` of an immune Unit that stands on the field after the step with its own "Evolves when" holding there. An `evolved` of a Unit whose condition does not hold after the step is still a reach: then only a Spell's `evolve` verb can have evolved it.
Rejected: a marker of its own for the state check's evolutions. The engine marks where a hook begins, never the rules' work ([[R4508005]]), and the Evolve text that follows already runs under the card's own marker.
