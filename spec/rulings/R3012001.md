A release's artwork (`apps/web/src/patches/releaseArt.ts`, #2681) is a pure function of the patch's record and its snapshots, with no clock or random source, and it never moves: its SVG holds no `<animate>`, no `@keyframes` and no `animation` style, and the Patch notes page does not move it in CSS either (the animated form and `RELEASE_ART_MAJOR_CYCLE_SECONDS` are gone). A major ([[R1369000]]) gets one large poster: the line's name large, its date, its card count, a brass frame, and its `RELEASE_ART_MAJOR_CARD_LIMIT` headline cards, the rarest of the cards its record names first (a stable order, ties in the record's), in a fan whose middle card, on top, is the best of them. A patch gets art in the same style at a patch's scale, at most `RELEASE_ART_CARD_LIMIT` cards sampled evenly from its record. Glitch is never shown ([[R674]]). A micro patch gets no art: the page shows none for it ([[R3012000]]), the static build writes no file for it, and its GitHub release carries no image.

Old links keep their pictures (CLAUDE.md rule 13): every major and patch keeps `/release-art/<version>.svg`, a major's `-still.svg` serves the same bytes as its `.svg` (which is the new still poster), and a micro up to and including `RELEASE_ART_LEGACY_MICRO_LAST` in `patches.json`'s order, shipped while every patch had art, keeps its `.svg`, drawn at a patch's scale. A micro after that version has none. Which version is the last of them is found by equality in the file's order, never by comparing versions ([[R105]]). A major's GitHub release links its `.svg`, the poster, on the host its liveness names; a patch's links its own. Presentation only, over public data (CLAUDE.md rule 7).
