The statistics page ranked cards one by one, so which set's cards win most was a sum a reader had to do by hand over hundreds of rows. A Sets tab of `/stats`, beside the cards, now does it.

- **What it reads.** The patch and match type the page shows, every card of every set: the same `GET /api/stats/cards` the Cards tab reads, without the Cards tab's set, rarity, cost or keyword filters, and the previous patch's for the change. The client sums the rows the server already sends, so the server adds nothing.
- **A set's figures.** Each set with games on the patch, as `SHIPPED_SETS` names it, highest pooled win rate first:
  - its cards with games, and of those, the ones with `CARD_STATS_MIN_SAMPLE` (20) games or more ([[R654]]);
  - its card-games: each card's in-deck games ([[R377]]) added up, a game counting once for each of the set's cards in a deck;
  - its pooled win rate, its cards' wins over their card-games, with one standard error beside it;
  - the mean and the median of its cards' win rates, over the cards with the sample floor's games;
  - its pooled win rate's change since the previous patch, where both have games;
  - its best and its worst card among those with the sample floor's games, each opening the card's details;
  - how many of those cards are `SET_STATS_OUTLIER_Z` (3) or more standard errors over 50% (strong), and how many under (weak).
- **Above and below.** Three tiles name the set with the highest pooled win rate, the highest mean card and the highest median card, and a star marks the first in the table. Below it, the strongest and the weakest cards: those `SET_STATS_OUTLIER_Z` or more standard errors from 50%, furthest first, at most `SET_STATS_CARDS_LISTED` (10) a side, each with its set, win rate, games and standard errors, each opening its details. Rates show to `SET_STATS_DIGITS` (1) decimal place, since sets differ by tenths of a point.
- **How to read it.** Decks mix sets ([[R380]]), so a set's rate is how its cards fare in mixed decks, not how a deck of it would. The standard error counts every card-game as independent, which they are not (one game counts every card of both decks), so it is a floor: the tab says that sets less than two or three of it apart are within noise.
- **Rejected.** The night bot's status issue alone, which already carries the same table: players never see it. A server field computing the sets: it would read nothing the card rows do not already carry.
