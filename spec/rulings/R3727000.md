A daily game (§9.13, #3727) has days per move, not R79's seconds, and nothing waits on a player who never comes back. In a live match R79's `timeout` only answers for the player who ran out and ends their turn; in a daily game that would let an absent player drag a game out to the turn cap (`TURN_CAP_PLAYER_TURNS`) a move a week. So a move whose deadline passes loses the game for the player who held it, as a live player's expired disconnect grace does ([[§9.5]]) and as a daily (correspondence) game on chess.com or Lichess is lost on time.

The host logs the loss as the server's own `disconnectExpired {player}` under the nonce `srv-daily-<seq>` ([[R270]]: no client may send a `srv-` nonce or a server-only action), so `(seed, log)` still folds the game and its replay hashes; its result's reason is `disconnect`, which the daily board shows as lost on time. It never sends `timeout`. Whichever reads the game first past the deadline applies it: the hourly sweep or any request that reads the game, so no screen shows a game past its time between sweeps, and a move sent after it is refused (`time_up`). Of two deadlines past at once the earlier loses, and of two at the same moment the first seat ([[R3727002]]). The clock is `jackioh-shared`'s `host::daily` (`daily_expired`), pure, kept beside the match and never in `GameState`.
