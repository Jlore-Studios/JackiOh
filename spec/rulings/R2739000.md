While one seat answers a card picker, the other seat sees it deciding, as Hearthstone shows an opponent's Discover: one card back per option over the middle of the board, laid out as the chooser's own picker is ([[R2646000]], #2738), with no panel and no words. Its `PendingElsewhereView` carries `optionCount`, the number of cards the picker offers, and never an option, a key or a label; without it the other seat sees [[§10.6]]'s bare notice, as before.

`view_for` gives the other seat a count only when every one of these holds, and otherwise leaves the key out:

- The prompt is one the chooser's client draws as cards: a `discover` of any size, or a `mode`, `reward` or `answer` of `PROMPT_CARD_OPTION_LIMIT` (4) options or fewer. An `answer`'s Stop is a button under the cards, not one of them, so it is not counted ([[R1184000]]); a quest's rewards are cards as a Choose one's are ([[R404]]).
- The card asking (the prompt's `resume`) is still in the state with that definition, and this viewer may read it where it is now, by [[R2646000]]'s test. A prompt's shape narrows a hidden asker ([[R81]]), so a prompt asked from a hand, a library or a face-down card, or by no card, shows no count.
- Neither face of its script sets `secret_option_counts`, the flag of a card whose choices are read off a zone the other seat cannot read: #51 KY's Private Tutor (its types and cost brackets are what its library holds), C+ #41 KY's Constant's Radiant face (the numbers on a hand card) and C+ #29 Portal to the Past's base face (the board the caster's last game ended with, which no view shows).
- Every counted option is a mode or a card this viewer may read ([[R97]]). A Discover out of a library or a hand (#51's reveal, T-AI-1's, Yuma's trade) and every `hand` and `pick` prompt show no count: how many cards matched is that zone's secret.

The chooser's own view never carries it, since it holds the options themselves, and the mulligan, whose hand is hidden, keeps its bare notice ([[R265]]). A count is a view field, not a `PendingChoice` field, so no state hash, fuzz replay hash or golden fold moves; the golden fold's view texts leave it out, as they leave out `sourceDefId`. The I6 hidden-information oracle reads the names a view carries, not a count, so the `r2739000` tests are what hold this rule. The AI's redaction ([[R185]]), which drops the other seat's options, keeps as many blank ones as the seat's view counts, so every world it samples shows the seat the view it has, and a change to a hidden card never moves the count. Every view of the other seat draws the backs (a match, practice, a replay); only a live match outlines the one the opponent's pointer is on ([[R2739001]]).

Decisions the issue left open, each with the alternative rejected:

1. The asking card must be one the viewer may read. Showing a count for any asker was rejected: a card in a hand or face down would be narrowed by its prompt's size.
2. A pick out of a hidden zone shows no count. Capping the count at the printed number was rejected: a deck or a hand that ran short would still say how many cards matched.
3. A card whose choices come from a hidden zone carries a script flag. A `PendingChoice` field set where the prompt opens was rejected, as it moves every state hash and the fuzz and golden replays for a view's sake. KY's Trial (#82) shows its counts: its sets and numbers are drawn from the public catalog ([[R247]]).
4. The view carries no layout field beside the count: every prompt that shows a count is drawn as cards, so its presence says so. A `layout` key was rejected as one more thing to keep in step with the client.
6. A Discover shows a count of any size, and a `mode`, `reward` or `answer` only up to the limit, past which the chooser's client lists the options rather than drawing cards.
7. The mulligan keeps [[R265]]'s notice.
8. Practice and replays draw the backs, since they render the same view. Drawing them on the match route alone was rejected: the client would be deciding what a view means by where it is shown.
