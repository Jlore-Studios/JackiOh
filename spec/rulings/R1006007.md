`cargo jackioh selfplay` ends a game without a result at the first decision during which the board runs to one of the engine's settle caps. Those are §10.3's `SETTLE_PASS_CAP` in the resolution loop and the trap dispatch, the state check's own cap, and R283's. Reaching one raises a "did not settle" panic. It makes no difference whether the panic came from a line the AI's search simulated (the search catches it and drops the line) or from anything else in the decision. The game ends at the cap's first panic, not when the decision returns: each of self-play's AI decisions runs on a thread of its own, and a hook in front of the process's panic hook tells the game as soon as that thread raises one. The arena's own games and the gates' play on as before, and a throw from the true state's `reduce` still ends any game.

The cap stops a loop, but each pass can be slow. Game 31547 of `learned-v0.3.7b-20261018-5`, the learn lane's export in run 39088985993, reached a board whose resolution loop took 12 to 15 minutes to reach its 24,000 passes. The search ran into it again and again: a second panic came 12 minutes after the first within the same decision, and the game raised about ten before it finished. It took 9,144 seconds where every other game of its shard took under 140. Its shard took 5 hours 3 minutes, against 78 to 150 minutes for the other 19, and finished 47 minutes inside GitHub's 6-hour limit. A second such game, or a slower runner, would have failed the shard and the export with it.

Whether a decision raises the panic follows from the seed alone, so the export stays byte-identical whatever the thread count ([[R1006001]]). The game is one without a result, which `positions` drops and counts ([[R1006003]]), as at the turn ceiling ([[R1006005]]). The abandoned decision's thread runs on, unused, until it returns or the process ends: one core lost, in about one game in 100,000. The loop itself is the engine's and the cards' to answer, not the export's.
