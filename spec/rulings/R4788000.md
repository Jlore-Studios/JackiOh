`decide` scales a wide state's budget down ([[§9.9]], Budgets; [[R2575000]]): past `AI_SEARCH.fat_reference` (30) root candidates or cards in play and in hand, the searched section is scaled from `fat_budget_nodes` (600) in inverse proportion to the width, and the lethal solver keeps its quarter of that. So a decision of 30 candidates gives the solver 300 nodes and one of 31 gives it 145, and at 37 it has 121. The solver's best-first walk simulates every move of the position it expands, so on such a board a lethal of four to six actions costs 120 to 220 nodes, and the walk was cut while it was playing out attacks it had already counted. The audit of issue #4787 logged the lethals a 6,000-node run of the same solver proved on a decision's own sampled worlds and the decision did not play: 8 in about 24 games, 4 of them on wide boards needing 122, 146, 157 and 213 nodes against allowances of 121, 132, 140 and 140. After two of the four the AI ended its turn with the win on the board.

**The rule.** The AI decides this. On a decision whose lethal allowance was scaled down, the solver has a reach: the allowance its caller gave (`SearchBudget.lethal_nodes`, 300 at `AI_BUDGET`, and never more than the caller's `nodes`, as [[R4802000]] holds a state that is not fat to). Its depth-first walk and its best-first walk spend the scaled allowance as before. Once that is spent, the best-first walk goes on only from a position whose ready attacks already reach lethal (`ready_gap` at or under 0: the enemy hero's life is within what the seat's ready attackers deal past the Taunts), and it stops the first time the position it would expand next does not, since it expands the smallest gap first. The proof of a line that won on the first sampled world, on the others, is always paid from the reach, so a win found with the last node of the allowance is not dropped. The walk never spends more than the reach in all (`find_lethal_reaching`). What it spent past the scaled allowance is added to the decision's searched section, so the beam, the replies and the finalists' scores on the other worlds keep the nodes the scaled budget gave them; the whole decision stays within its caller's `nodes`: the searched section with room for the reach is held to them, and its additions saturate, as [[R4802000]]'s do.

**What stays.** A state at or under `fat_reference` has no reach, its allowance being the caller's own (at most its `nodes`, [[R4802000]]), and decides node for node as before; so does a wide state whose walk never stands at a position that reads as lethal, the timing gate's pinned improve-dev:200 position among them ([[R2575000]]'s test passes unchanged). A caller that gives the solver nothing (`lethal_nodes` 0) gives it no reach. The lethal hint ([[R2102000]]) and the concession check ([[R2660000]]) call `find_lethal` with their own allowance and no reach. The trim ([[R1965000]]) runs on the decision's searched section as before, now wider by what the walk spent. `AI_SEARCH.lethal_reach` off stops every walk at its scaled allowance.

**What it costs.** On the audit's games played again with the rule, of 49 wide decisions 5 spent nodes past their allowance: 4 found their lethal for 1, 14, 17 and 73 nodes more, and 1 spent 168 and found none (a position that read as lethal behind what `ready_gap` does not count). The turns the AI ended with a lethal a 6,000-node solver proved fell from 4 to 1, the one left being a board of ordinary width whose proof needs 432 nodes (issue #4790). The shadow-ban sweep's time flag ([[R186]]) reads a decision's wall clock, which a reach adds to only on a decision that is closing on a win. Its deal screen, 400 games on improve-dev against the AI without it, read 200-200 with no deal decided and 6 of 200 played differently: inconclusive, so it says nothing of strength either way and the rule rests on its scene tests. What the rule changes is the turn a won position is won in, which that screen's win counts do not see.

**Rejected.** Giving every wide decision the unscaled 300, which adds up to 160 nodes to each of them whether or not a lethal is near, against [[R2575000]]'s "the states whose every step costs the most spend what they did"; scaling the solver's share from 1,200 while the rest scales from 600, for the same reason; and a reach gated on the root's `ready_gap` alone, which misses the lethal a play opens (of the three logged walks cut mid-walk, two stood above 0 at the root, at 3 and 7).
