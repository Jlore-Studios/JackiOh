The empty-queue fallback ([[R1453]]) now covers Conquest, the last mode the queue offers ([[§9.5]]). While `LADDER_BOTS` is `on` ([[R1452]]), a Conquest ticket that has waited past its own fallback time with no person inside its window is paired with a ladder bot, and the two play a ranked series ([[R330]]–[[R336]]) as two people would. Where the spec is silent this follows Hearthstone semantics: the bot brings decks of its own, chooses as a person would, and is rated as the bot it is.

**The seat.** The ticket's bot is chosen as [[R1453]] chooses any ticket's, with one condition more: it holds a trio dealt for the catalog version the server plays. A bot that holds none, or one dealt for another catalog, plays no Conquest until the pool deals it one.

**The trio.** The pool ([[R1454]]) deals every active bot a trio when it holds none and again whenever the catalog version changes, after its Best-of-1 decks. The trio is three decks a player could queue as one: they pass [[§9.4]]'s L1–L6 and share no card ([[R253]]), checked against a collection of their own cards, since a bot owns none and needs none. The bot's own Best-of-1 decks come first, in their order, each taken when it shares no card with the decks taken before it and a player could queue it. While the trio is short, decks are dealt as All Random deals a player's ([[R258]]) with every card already taken left out, kept on the same terms, at most `BOT_DECK_DRAW_ATTEMPTS` (20) in all, each with a portrait dealt from the seed ([[R642]]). A bot whose deal finds no three holds no trio until the next sizing. The trio is frozen into the series as a person's is ([[R259]]), so a new deal never reaches a series already made.

**The claim.** The ticket and the bot are claimed, and the series written, in one transaction, so neither lands without the other. Between games a series' players are in no match ([[R264]]), and the bot is no different: what holds it then is the series. So every claim of a bot, the fallback's in any mode and a rematch's ([[R639000]]), takes the bot only when it is a side of no series that is not over, and checks that after the claim has locked the bot, so it sees a series that an earlier claim committed. The person is series Player 1, who goes first in odd games ([[R335]]), as the older ticket of a pair is and as [[R1453]] seats a person against a bot in a match. The series is ranked: the queue paired it ([[R604]]).

**The pick.** Before each game the bot makes its sealed pick through the same transition as a person's ([[R331]]), so it is final once in and the person learns only that it is in ([[R336]]). Its rule reads only what its own side of the series sees: its trio, which of its decks have won ([[R330]]), and who goes first in the next game ([[R335]]). It never reads the person's trio or pick, as [[R185]] holds for its play. A deck's pace is the mean cost of its cards (the cost outside play, [[R65]]). On the play the rule takes the fastest deck that has not won, and on the draw the slowest, ties going to the lower slot: the aggressive deck where the first turn helps it, the controlling one where it has to answer. The bot follows the rule `BOT_PICK_RULE_SHARE` (75%) of the time and otherwise picks uniformly among its decks that have not won, so the matchup alone does not give its pick away. With one deck left that has not won, [[R332]] picks for it as for a person.

**When.** The pick comes `BOT_PICK_DELAY_MIN_MS` (4 s) to `BOT_PICK_DELAY_MAX_MS` (20 s) after the pick phase opens, drawn uniformly, as a person reads the matchup before choosing. The series sweeper makes it (every `SERIES_SWEEP_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, 5 s), at the first sweep once that time has come, so always well before the pick clock (`SERIES_PICK_SECONDS`, 60 s) runs out; a pick the clock had beaten would be [[R333]]'s, as anyone's is. The delay is hand-set, since [[R1442]] records no pick. A bot already seated in a series makes its picks whatever `LADDER_BOTS` says now, as a bot already in a match plays it out.

**Rating.** The series' games are recorded and not rated ([[R262]]). The series is rated once, as one game, when it ends ([[R604]]). Its bot side is rated as the bot it is, on its own rating ([[R610]]) and its season row ([[R640000]]), and the rated game records that side with the `ai` pilot and its bot id ([[R611]]). An abandoned series rates nothing, as any does.

**No rematch.** A game of a series offers no rematch ([[R672]]): the series' next pick phase is what follows it. So the bot draws no rematch plan when a series game ends, and reads as gone from the finished game at once, not after [[R639000]]'s linger, as a person who has gone on to the pick has.

**Replay.** The pick's two streams are the server's own. Each is drawn from the series seed, which no client holds, with the bot's seat and the game picked for (`{seed_base}:bot-pick-delay:{seat}:{game}` and `{seed_base}:bot-pick:{seat}:{game}`), from cursor 0, apart from every game's seed (`{seed_base}:{game}`, [[R335]]). Nothing they draw reaches a game's state, its log or a replay hash, and a write retried on a fresh read of the series ([[R263]]) draws the same pick.

**Tells this ruling knowingly leaves.** No field names a bot ([[R1452]]), but a player who looks closely can still infer one:

- A bot never forfeits a series ([[R334]]) and never lets its pick clock run out.
- Its pick lands on the series sweeper's beat, every five seconds, where a person's lands whenever they click.

**Rejected.**

- Picking in trio order, or always by the rule: either makes the bot's next deck one the person can work out.
- Reading the person's trio to pick against it: it is hidden from the bot's side ([[R336]]), as cards are from its play ([[R185]]).
- "Run it back", picking again the deck that just won: the deck that won is locked ([[R330]]), so a series holds no such pick.
- A trio drawn for each series, or seeded from the bot's profile id: the first changes a bot's decks from one series to the next, which a person's do not, and the second seeds them from an id that reaches clients, where the pool's seed is the server's.

**Ghost results.** Issue #641 also proposes results made up between two bots, to keep the leaderboard moving. They are not built: they wait on a person's decision once issue #640's changes ([[R640000]], [[R640001]]) are live. No game is played between two bots on the engine.
