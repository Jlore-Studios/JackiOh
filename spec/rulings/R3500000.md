Opening a replay the server does not hold in memory folds the whole game ([[R768]]), on a server that is a
quarter of a processor, and a fold of a 256-action game takes about 200 ms of a full core. The routes'
per-account allowance ([[R109]]'s and `REPLAY_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE`) let one account with more finished matches
than `REPLAY_CACHE_MATCHES` start sixty of them a minute, and two requests for one match folded it twice, so
one account could slow every live match, and the ladder bots that think against a clock, for minutes.

**What changes.** The folds of the 1v1 replays (`GET /api/replays/:matchId`) and of the co-op ones
(`GET /api/duos/matches/:matchId/replay`, [[§13.9]]) take a permit from one pool for the whole server,
`REPLAY_FOLDS_RUNNING_MAX` (1) of them, and hold it until the fold's job ends. An opening first takes its
match's turn, so concurrent openings of one match are one fold: the one holding the turn folds, the others wait
for it and find the replay kept. Both waits together are bounded by `REPLAY_FOLD_WAIT_MS` (5000); past it the
request is refused with 429 `rate_limited` and `retryAfterMs` (`REPLAY_FOLD_RETRY_AFTER_MS`, 2000), as every
refusal for going too fast states its wait ([[R192]]), and the client shows it as it shows any replay error. The
fold itself is not timed. No database connection is held while an opening waits: the rows are read and the
transaction closed before the turn is taken, as in [[R2378000]]'s waits.

**Reach.** A replay held in memory, a listing and a share wait for nothing and answer as before. The seat check,
the refusals and what a page carries are unchanged; the two caches stay apart, only the permits are shared. A
request dropped mid-fold (a closed tab) leaves its fold to finish, the permit held until it does, and its
replay unkept, so the next opening of the match folds it again, behind the permit. The ladder bots' and the
co-op AI's searches have the same unbounded shape and may share the permits later; this ruling leaves them as
they are.

Hearthstone semantics: a slow request waits its turn, and is told to come back when the line is too long.

Rejected: two folds at a time, which on a quarter of a processor only makes each slower; a shared future for the
fold, which would hold the first request's cancellation against the others, where a turn lets the next request
fold afresh; a cap on how many requests may wait, which the wait's own bound already gives and a count would
duplicate; and a change to the client, since `onlineFailure` already shows a refusal's words for any code.
