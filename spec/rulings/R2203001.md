The Training Grounds set's choices are plays `legal_actions` already lists: a declared target (TG #2 Personal Trainer's other Human of yours; #11 Thwomp's, #26 Path of Exile's and #34 Dino-size's Unit, #12 Fortify's, #27 Hopeful Jelly Bean's and #37 Seance's permanent), a lane (a Cry that reads where its Unit stands: TG #5 Midlane Menace's midlane, #16 Celestial Caller's neighbours, #21 Flame Tongue Totem's neighbours, #22 Right-Hand Felinor's Unit to its left, #28 Occidental's and #29 Oriental's swept lane), a face-down set (TG #24 Goliath Situation), what a Call to Chaos rolls (TG #39), and which Unit a removal takes (an enemy TG #30 Floodgates, whose Death destroys every Unit of its side). The AI's search tries each through `reduce` and keeps the one whose result it scores best, the random policy picks among them, and a timeout answers as R79 says; no Training Grounds card needs a rule of its own in the AI.

One thing the search did not see: it tries a play once per distinct placement (#1014, #1635), a placement being the zones beside the lane and across it, and kept the lowest lane of each. On a board empty around them lanes 2, 3 and 4 are one placement, so the midlane play of a Cry that reads the midlane ([[R685]]: Classic #22 Mid Runner, TG #5 Midlane Menace) was folded into lane 2's and never tried. Now a placement that holds a lane of the seat's midlane is tried there, and the AI plays Midlane Menace in the midlane. The placements and their number are unchanged, so the search branches no wider; a card that reads nothing of its lane lands in the midlane where it landed in lane 2. The midlane is public, so the choice still reads only what the seat can see ([[R185]]).
