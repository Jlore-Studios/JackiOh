A ladder bot is an opponent the server plays itself, so a player who queues when no one else is there still gets a ranked game. It is a profile whose `kind` is `bot` (a person's is `human`; migration 0032, and no client role may read the column), a `bots` row holding its strength, speed, persona, schedule and decks ([[R1454]]), and a rating of its own ([[R610]]). A ranked match seats one only through the empty-queue fallback ([[R1453]]) or a rematch of a match it played ([[R639000]]), and a ranked Conquest series only through the same fallback ([[R641000]]), and only while the server's `LADDER_BOTS` is `on`: unset or `off`, nothing seats a bot. [[R610]] rates a bot's games by the same rule as a player's, and the rated game records its side with the `ai` pilot and its bot id ([[R611]], [[R1451]]).

**How it plays.** A bot plays on this spec's resources, whatever its strength: no practice handicap applies to it ([[R180]]–[[R184]]). It decides with the practice AI's search ([[§9.9]]) at its own strength, one of five ([[R1454]]): a search budget, a softmax temperature over the search's finalist lines, a chance to pass over a lethal line the solver found, and a skill, the share of its decisions that follow the search at all, the rest being a random legal action ([[R747000]]), after which it plays that decision as if it had not seen the lethal: no line that wins this turn, and none that opens with the solver's lethal first action (unless nothing else is left to play). At a temperature of 0 with no chance to pass, it decides exactly as the practice AI does. It reads only what [[R185]] lets the AI read, so no hidden card changes its moves either. The decision runs beside the match's actor, never holding it up, and is capped in wall-clock time (`BOT_DECISION_DEADLINE_MS`, and never so long that less than `BOT_CLOCK_MARGIN_MS` is left on its acting clock), past which it plays the best line found so far.

**Pace.** A bot moves at a person's pace. Each move waits a think time drawn from [[R1442]]'s fitted think times for its kind of move, first in the turn or later (`THINK_TIME_FITS`), or from hand-set priors until a fit for it lands, times the bot's own speed. [[R639000]] draws it longer on a board with more to do and on a turn that wins, gives a mulligan, a draw answer and a concession priors of their own, and takes a targeted move's aim out of it. The time the decision took counts toward it. The move goes out no sooner than `BOT_THINK_MIN_MS` (600 ms) after the bot came to owe it and no later than `BOT_THINK_MAX_MS` (30 s), and never so late that less than `BOT_CLOCK_MARGIN_MS` (5 s) is left on its acting clock ([[R79]]), so waiting never times it out; a turn it lets run out on purpose is [[R639000]]'s. If the player acted while the bot was thinking (their auto-end-turn switch, their side of the mulligan), the bot's move still goes as long as it is the same turn and the engine takes it; otherwise the bot decides again. So nothing the player does on the bot's turn can hold its move back. A move the engine refuses is replaced, once, by ending the turn when that is legal (else the first legal action); a move that could not be written to the log is decided again `BOT_WRITE_RETRY_MS` (1 s) later.

**Replay.** A bot's random streams, its search's, its think time's, its persona's and [[R639000]]'s, are the server's own, each seeded from the match seed and the seat the bot began in, and none is the match rng (`state.rng`). They reach no state, log or replay hash, so a match with a bot replays from `(seed, log)` like any other ([[§9.5]]). An actor rebuilt after a restart resumes the bot's seat with its streams started again, which changes nothing a replay reads. As for any match, the actor is rebuilt when the person's socket comes back, since nothing starts at boot ([[R744]]); a bot has no socket to bring it back, so a match whose person never returns after a restart stays the reaper's draw at the ceiling ([[R112]]), as a match neither person returns to does, and holds its bot until then.

**The seat.** A bot has no socket and needs none. Its seat never counts as disconnected, so it never gets [[R744]]'s grace and never loses by `disconnectExpired`. It counts as attached from the moment its match's actor arms, as an opponent whose socket has attached: a person who has not opened the match by then is away from that moment, and loses by `disconnectExpired` when [[R744]]'s grace runs out, as they would against a person who had opened it. [[R188]] holds for its search as for the practice AI, but not for its seat: it never offers a draw, and it concedes, answers a draw offer and lets a turn run out as a person does ([[R639000]]). Its moves carry server nonces (`srv-bot-…`), the prefix no client may send ([[R270]]), so they are the server's own actions, and [[R1442]] keeps no think time for them. It emotes through its persona ([[R645]]), one per bot, choosing only within the hand its seat was dealt ([[R1344]]); the actor relays each emote to the player as it relays a player's, under the same rate limit ([[R643]]), and while a card hears emotes it runs the emote as an `Emote` action exactly as it runs a player's ([[R1127]]), so a hearing card answers a bot's emote too. The bot's own moves never include one, as the practice AI's never do. It shows its aim before a targeted move as a person's client does ([[R738]], [[R639000]]). It reads as present on the match while the game goes on, as a person playing it would, and once a ranked game ends it stays a while and may offer or meet a rematch ([[R672]]), as [[R639000]] has it, except after a game of a Conquest series, which offers none ([[R641000]]).

**What a client sees.** Nothing a client reads labels a bot or tells its seat from a person's (CLAUDE.md rule 7), and no response carries its `kind`, its pilot or its nonces. Its username, its portrait and the age of its account are drawn as a player's might be ([[R1454]]). A bot holds a season row as a person does ([[R640000]]), so the match screen's read of both seats' ranks ([[R612]]) shows a bot's seat that row, read as a person's is, and the leaderboard lists it at the same rank. It never shows Jlorious, which is people's alone ([[R608]], [[R640000]]).

**Tells this ruling knowingly leaves.** No field names a bot, but a player who looks closely can still infer one ([[R640000]] leaves one more, a Mythic Grape opponent while Jlorious has room):

- A bot never drops out of its match, so its seat never shows [[R744]]'s reconnect countdown, as a person's does when they lose their connection or open the match late.
- A bot keeps no public player statistics, which a person's client files after their games unless they set them private, so a search of the public player list by username ([[R1436]]) never finds a bot's. Filing statistics for bots was rejected: they are what a person's own client counts and files, so the server would have to invent them.
- The public card statistics count every counted seat merged ([[R807000]]), so a game against a bot adds two decks like any game, and the per-game deck tell this bullet once recorded is gone. (`?pilot=human` still reads the people's sides only, so a player who compares the splits can still infer a bot game that way; that comparison is the documented split, not a leak.)
- A sign-in attempt with a bot's username is refused as fast as one with a name nobody holds, since a bot has no address to try ([[R1443]]).
