A bot's rating ([[R610]]) measures the AI that plays it, and a promotion ([[R732001]]) swaps in a stronger one, so a rating earned on the older AI is no longer as certain as its deviation says. A bot whose rating has settled, with a small deviation, moves little per game and would take many games to reach the new AI's strength; re-seeding it from a new calibration was rejected ([[R748000]]), since that throws away what its games earned.

So each bot's rating row names the AI generation (`crates/ai/generation.json`) it was last rated on. When a rated game seats a bot whose row names an older generation than the server's, the bot is rated from its rating with the deviation widened first, as [[R609]]'s season reset widens a player's: `min(√(RD² + n × BOT_PROMOTION_DEVIATION_BOOST²), RATING_DEVIATION_START)`, for n the generations between the row's and the server's. Its rating and volatility are kept. A row that names no generation, rated before this ruling, counts as one promotion behind; a row at the server's generation, or a newer one, is not widened. The game's record holds the widened deviation as the bot's rating before it ([[R611]]), and the game stamps the row with the server's generation, so a bot widens once per promotion. A new bot already starts at a new player's deviation, which the cap keeps.

The rating then moves to the new AI's strength through the bot's games against people, by [[R603]]'s update unchanged, and a person's update already weighs a bot's wider deviation (Glicko-2's g(φ)), so a game against a recently promoted bot moves the person's rating less, not more. Nothing a client reads changes ([[R612]]). Seeds and calibration are [[R748000]]'s, unchanged.

Rejected: widening every bot at boot, which would widen a bot that never plays again and write the pool on every deploy; resetting the deviation to a new player's whatever the gap, which forgets that one promotion changes a strength less than four; and shifting the ratings by the promotions' `vsParent` Elo, which overstates the gain against people ([[R748000]]) and would move ratings that no game had tested.
