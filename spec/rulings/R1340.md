The emotes a game may deal are a pool of twenty-four ids, the wire's whole vocabulary ([[R643]]): the five voice lines `greetings`, `wellPlayed`, `oops`, `thanks` and `threaten`, which every portrait speaks in its own voice ([[R644]]), then nineteen shared emoji — patch v0.2.X's five, `sob`, `yawn`, `laugh`, `angry` and `wahWah`, and MN03's fourteen, `wave`, `clap`, `thumbsUp`, `facepalm`, `shrug`, `thinking`, `heart`, `fire`, `skull`, `sweat`, `cool`, `gasp`, `salute` and `party` ([[R1345]]). The new ones are emoji, not voice lines: a voice line is rendered on macOS alone and the voice-asset check expects a file for every line, so the pool grows by stickers and their synthesized sounds, and every portrait keeps its five lines. The ten ids of patch v0.2.X keep their spelling and their place at the head of the pool, so a frame an older client sends still parses. The engine's `EmoteId` (`crates/engine/src/wire/emotes.rs`) and the client's hand-kept copy (`apps/web/src/wire/emotes.ts`) list the same ids in the same order; the client's tests fail when the union ts-rs writes from Rust and the hand-kept one differ. An id outside the pool is `malformed`, as [[R643]] has it.
