`/terms` was about 520 words and `/privacy` about 1,800: they listed every stored field, every retention period and how the system works inside, and read as machine-written. The owner replaced both with short text that names data by category and leaves the implementation out. The words are the owner's, kept as written, and nothing is to be added back. Everything collected stays the same; only what the pages say about it changes.

**What the pages say now.**

- The Terms: agreement to them and to the Privacy Policy by creating an account, and the minimum age ([[R757000]]); "Play fair", "Your content" (the player keeps their rights, lets the game store what they create or post and show it to the players it is meant for, and the game removes infringing content and bans repeat infringers, with a link to Copyright), "AI opponents", "No guarantees", "Changes" (every account is asked to agree again) and "Contact".
- The Privacy Policy: "Who we are", "What we collect", "Why", "Who sees it", "Your browser", "How long", "Your choices", "Children" and "Changes". "Who we are" holds the contact address.
- The minimum age is `MIN_ACCOUNT_AGE_YEARS` and the address is `CONTACT_EMAIL`; both pages read them and hard-code neither.

**What this amends.**

- *AI opponents* ([[R2614000]]; [[R640001]]'s disclosure paragraph). The Terms say that when no one near a player's rank is available, ranked may match them against an AI opponent, and that nothing in the game marks which opponents are AI. They no longer describe an opponent's looks or behavior, that it sees what its seat could see, that its games count toward rank, or that a friend's challenge is always that friend. The bots do not change, and nothing marks one ([[R1452]]); the section still names no seat, so it labels no bot.
- *Watching and public stats* ([[R2615000]], [[R2615001]]). The policy keeps that a player's friends, and their opponent's friends, can watch their online games, that removing or blocking someone stops it, and that the player's stats are public. It drops the delay, the co-op teammate's view, the replay details and the "no opt-out" sentence. The rulings still hold: friends may always watch with no setting to stop it but the friendship, and online play's data has no opt-out.
- *The age check* ([[R757000]]'s policy wording). The policy says it collects "confirmation that you're 13 or older" in place of describing what the check stores. Only the pass is still kept.
- *Dropped from the policy*: provider names and their locations, individual retention periods, the full list of what goes on the device, sign-in session details, invite-code records, the play telemetry's fields ([[R1442]]), custom-card fields and the presence heartbeat ([[R970002]], whose gate stays). The "Do Not Track" sentence stays, in one line.
- *Dropped from the Terms*: the chat word filter and the report details. The license to store and show what a player submits stays, in one sentence.

`TERMS_VERSION` moves to the two new dates, so every account is asked to agree again, and `AGREED_TEXT` gets the new row ([[R757001]], [[R970001]]). `/ai` stops saying that the Terms describe how an AI opponent looks and plays, and says only that nothing in the game marks it, as the Terms say.

**Hearthstone semantics.** Hearthstone's own terms and privacy notice are short and category-level: they name what is collected and why, and say nothing of fields, retention periods or storage.

Open for the lawyer's review, and not blocking: "We keep your data while you have an account" reads as a maximum. Chat (30 days), move-by-move records (90) and play timing (365) are deleted sooner, which fits within it.

Rejected: keeping the detail, since the owner chose category-level text and asked that none be added back; and a heading on the Terms' opening paragraph, which would add words the owner did not write.
