"For the rest of the game, the first time you play each …" (Stinkyscape #44 Great Replacement Theory) is a `never` player modifier on its caster, `ModifierKind::PlayWatch`, made by the `watch_first_plays` effect and badged in the card's own words in both players' views ([[R169]]), as [[R946]]'s lane watcher is for a turn and [[R458]]'s rest-of-game effects are for good. It answers its owner's plays only, each once it has resolved (`cardResolved`), a cast included ([[R70]]), so what it adds arrives after the played card's own effect, as Hearthstone's "after you play" does: Drone Core I's random discard never takes the tier it earns. A countered play is never counted and never resolves ([[R448]], as [[R451]]'s play records leave it out), so it answers nothing and its card stays watched.

Each watched card's first resolved play takes it off the watch and queues one answer (`@playWatch`), which re-enters the maker's step as its owner with the card's definition in its data, with no `self` whatever became of the maker ([[R127]]), under the face it was made with. Great Replacement Theory's step adds the next tier to its owner's hand for Drone Core I to IX (Stinkyscape #27 to #35), Radiant on its Radiant face; Drone Core X (#36) adds nothing, so the watch never names it; a full hand burns the added card ([[§2.4]]), and the tier is spent anyway.

A play before the watch was made counts for nothing. Each watch keeps its own record, so two casts answer one play with two cards. The watch and its badge stay for the rest of the game, even once every card it named has answered. The reason: Hearthstone's rest-of-game effects belong to the player, not to the card that made them, so the watcher is a player modifier rather than a trigger on the card, which an exile would end. It edits [[§8.10]] and [[§10.1]].
