[[R169]] put each side's modifiers in the view, and the chip beside the board's controls lists them in full: on a phone the badges beside the heroes are cut short, and a touch screen never shows a badge's `title`. A fight is a practice game ([[R2706000]]), but its rail passed the board only the enemy's name, the run's timer and the thinking indicator. So no chip appeared there, though a fight plays catalog cards that install modifiers (extra turns owed, a refresh that gives no mana, a delayed effect).

**The rail.** A fight's rail carries the chip while any modifier is live on either side, and nothing while none is. Tapping it opens every label in full, headed You and the enemy's name, as practice's heads them You and AI. The chip sits beside the rail's text, not inside it: it is 32px tall and the text wraps, so inside the text a phone's one-row control bar would grow a line taller. It comes first, so that on a very narrow screen a long enemy name is clipped, not the chip.

Presentation only: it reads `SideView.modifiers` of the viewer's own `view`, nothing else and nothing hidden ([[§10.8]], CLAUDE.md rule 7), and enters no log, state or hash. [[R3855000]] did the same for the daily board.

Hearthstone semantics: the board's information is the same at every table.

Rejected: the chip inside the wrapping text span, whose height would grow a phone's control bar; the heading "Opponent" or "AI" over the enemy's list, since the rail already names the enemy.
