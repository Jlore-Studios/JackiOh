A step whose `reduce` does far more work than most is charged extra nodes up front (`surcharge_nodes`, issue #1240; [[§9.9]], Budgets): a Play of #97 Zephrys or C+ #27 Zephrys Zealotism, a play the other side's Pareto Optimality judges, and an Attack #96 My Pawn would answer ([[R4809001]]). In the beam, a step whose surcharge the budget cannot pay is refused like any refused action, and the beam tries its siblings, which is right there: one dear step must not stop every cheaper one beside it. The opponent's reply (`simulate_reply`) took the same refusal as "skip this step", and in the reply a skipped step is a decision of the opponent's. An Attack it could not pay for ended the opponent's turn, so the reply came back whole with the opponent having attacked with nothing; a known play it could not pay for was held back, and the reply played on as if the opponent preferred not to play it. At `AI_BUDGET` the replies run on a reserve of at most 114 nodes, and such an Attack costs 41, so after two such replies every later line on the first sampled world, and every finalist on the others, was scored against an opponent that passed, and lines scored late in a decision looked better than lines scored early in the same one.

**The rule.** A reply step whose surcharge the counter cannot pay cuts the reply: `simulate_reply` answers `None`, as it does when the counter is spent, which is what its callers read as "the budget ran out here". That holds for the walk's steps (the best-ranked attack, or the End turn: `reduce_for`) and for each try of the steps that weigh several (`best_try`, which checks a try's surcharge, past the node `simulate` takes, before it calls `simulate`): a known play, and the attack a wall against lethal is weighed against; an Activate and a position switch pay no surcharge, so their tries run as before. An Attack the engine itself refuses still ends the turn instead, as before, and a try the engine refuses is still passed over. `decide` treats the cut like any refused reply ([[R3498000]]): on the first sampled world no later shortlisted line is replied to, so the finalists are drawn from the lines replied to before it; on another world the finalist's replay answers `None`, and that world and every later one are left out for every finalist alike, so each finalist's row of scores keeps the same length ([[R4608000]]), and `SearchStats.replied` and `worldsScored` report it.

**The counter is not drained.** The cut takes no node, so the counter's `stoppedBy` reads as it did, and a later reply that needs no surcharge (on a sampled world where the same attack is not lethal, say) still runs on what is left. So `Decision::fell_short` need not show a decision whose only cut was a surcharge on the first sampled world.

Rejected:
- Draining the counter on a cut, so that it reads as a spent budget. Every later world would go unscored even where its replies fit.
- Skipping the step, as the beam does. In the reply that is the defect itself: a skipped attack becomes a passed turn, a skipped play a play held back.
- A larger reply reserve. It would shrink the beam's share on every decision to pay for a few.

Hearthstone has no counterpart: it adds no rule of the game and no constant.
