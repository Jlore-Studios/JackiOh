**Why.** Open all on the Shop sent one `POST /api/packs/open` per pack, one after another. Each was its
own request, spend turn and transaction, about twenty statements to the database each, so ten packs
cost ten round trips and some two hundred statements. Tracker #3203 asks for less compute and faster
traffic, and Claim all's batch route ([[R3191000]]) is the pattern.

**The route.** `POST /api/packs/open-all` takes `{ pack, count }`, `count` a whole number from 1 to
`PACKS_OPEN_ALL_MAX`; anything else is 400 and opens nothing. It is a spend of the economy
([[§14.11]]): it claims its account's turn ([[R2378000]]) and takes an optional `Idempotency-Key`
([[R2124000]]) kept under its own route, so the same key sent to one pack's open, or with another
count, is another request, 422. Under the account's economy lock ([[R1301003]]), before anything is
read, a key the account committed answers what it answered then. While `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off it is
refused with 409 and writes nothing. The count only bounds the batch: the server draws a fresh seed for
each pack as one open does, so nothing the client sends chooses a roll.

**Each pack as its own open.** The records are read once. Each pack then rolls from its own seed on what
the pack before it left, its cards, cosmetics, pity counters and bars, so the openings are the ones that
many opens one after another would roll from the same seeds and the same records: the same slots, pity,
bars and ledger rows, each economy row carrying its own opening's log as its `ref` ([[R1301003]]). The
only difference is the stamp: every row of the batch shares one `at`. Fewer packs held than asked is
409 `no unopened pack of that kind`, decided before anything is written: Open all opens all it was
asked for or nothing.

**One transaction.** Every grant is written at once: the inventory in one statement, the cards'
quantities and their ledger rows in two, the cosmetics in one and the counters in one, each moving a
holding by the sum of its grants and writing one ledger row per grant in the order one open at a time
would. A holding that one grant at a time would refuse, a count below 0 where its grant falls or a
cosmetic already owned, refuses the whole batch, which writes nothing. However many packs, the
transaction costs the same statements.

**The answer and the client.** It answers `{ openings }`, each what one open answers, in order; each
opening's `unopened` is what it left, so the last one's counters are the account's. The Shop's Open all
sends one request for `min(held, PACKS_OPEN_ALL_MAX)` packs, labels its button with that count, shows
the openings a pack at a time with skip-all between them and sends nothing more, and folds only the
last opening into its panels ([[R3211000]]). A refusal opens nothing and shows the server's words with
the pack still sealed. `POST /api/packs/open` stays for a single pack and for tabs of an older client.

Hearthstone semantics: opening several packs at once rolls each as if it were opened alone.

Rejected: a batch of single opens sent side by side, which the account's one turn at a time
([[R2378000]]) would queue or refuse and which would still cost a transaction each; opening "up to"
`count`, which would leave the answer's count to the store's state rather than the request; a cap of
50, since every ledger row of the batch carries its opening's log of tens of KB and is bound into one
statement; a refusal of Open all keyed on the single open's economy switch too, which changes nothing
while the economy is live; and a new decision in `jackioh-shared` for the batch, since each pack is the
existing decision on the records the one before left.
