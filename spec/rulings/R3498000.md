A decision's budget counts nodes, and a host with a clock adds a hard stop beside it: the browser's `PRACTICE_AI_CLOCK_MS` (3 s), the ladder bots' and the Duos AI team's `BOT_DECISION_DEADLINE_MS` (4 s). The search keeps a reserve of nodes back from its beam to score its finalists after the opponent's reply and on every other sampled world, but a reserve of nodes is no reserve of time: on a device slow enough that the clock fired while the beam was still exploring, every later node was refused, so no finalist was replied to and each was judged on one sampled world alone. No Hearthstone rule is involved: the AI's use of time changes no game.

**The wrap-up.** `AiOptions.wrap_up` is a second callback beside `should_stop`, and every host with a clock fires it at `AI_WRAP_UP_SHARE` (0.6) of its limit, counted from the host's start (`wrap_up_after_ms`, the one place the share is applied): the wasm module's `ai_decide` and `bot_decide` (the practice AI, a dungeon fight's enemy through the practice driver, and a bot the browser plays) from the time left at the call, and the server's ladder bots (`spawn_bot`) and Duos AI team (`AiJob::ask`) from their deadline. Only the beam's slice of the node counter polls it, before each of its nodes. Once it answers true that slice refuses, the beam explores no further, and its stop reads `stoppedBy: clock`; the beam's open lines then end their turn (`end_exploring`), so the lines it cut short can be replied to, and the replies and the other worlds draw on the decision's counter until `should_stop`, which still bounds the whole decision. The wrap-up is first polled once the beam has drawn its root's worth of nodes (`root_branching` and endTurn), so a wrap-up that fired before the beam began still leaves it lines to play rather than a fallback. A ladder bot's concession check is held to the hard stop alone.

**The report.** `SearchStats.replied` counts the shortlisted lines scored after a simulated reply on determinization 0, and `worldsScored` the determinizations the finalists were scored on, determinization 0 included; both are 0 for a decision that never reached the reply. `Decision::fell_short` is a searched decision that left its finalists unscored on a sampled world, and `cargo jackioh think` and the timing gate print all three; the gate fails nothing on them, since its search has no clock.

**No clock, no change.** With no clock, or a wrap-up that never fires, every take of the counter is the one it was, node for node, and every decision is the one it was: the quality gates, the shadow-ban sweep and the server's fold of a practice claim ([[R2560000]]) decide bit for bit as before, and the C module, which hands its bodies no deadline, reads no clock. The share is a judgement: no clocked strength screen has tested it, and CI cannot run one; on a slow device the beam explores 40% less of its time, for the reply and the other worlds that the measurement in `docs/polish/3-ai.md` values more (174 wins of 300 against greedy without the reply, 193 with it).

**Rejected.** The share passed in from TypeScript: the client restates no number (CLAUDE.md rule 9), and every host must wrap up alike. A `StoppedBy` of its own for the wrap-up: the wrap-up is the host's clock ahead of its deadline, and the wire's three stop reasons stay as they were. No floor: a wrap-up that fired before the beam's first node would leave no line and the decision would fall back. One `should_stop` told which phase is asking: every host's callback would change for what one second callback says. Failing the timing gate on a decision that fell short: the gate's search has no clock, so none falls short there. Ending the open lines' turns under a budget cap as well: that would change decisions made with no clock.
