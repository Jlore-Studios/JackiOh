§9.5 has an enqueue freeze the chosen deck into a ticket (`frozen_deck`, `frozen_trio` for Conquest, and the deck's cosmetics). A ticket stops waiting in three ways: `DELETE /api/queue`, R897000's sweep of a ticket unheard from for `QUEUE_TICKET_STALE_MS`, and the stray cancel at a match end. Each only marks it cancelled. Nothing reads a cancelled ticket again: the queue readout counts claimed tickets, the pairing scan reads queued ones, and the pairing's re-read of a ticket it lost already takes a missing row as gone. So the row stayed for good with its copy of the deck, and a join-and-cancel loop left up to about 150 of them a minute per account (the API's `API_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE`, 300, being the only limit).

**What changes.** The retention loop, in a step of its own after the share step (`tickets.purge`, its own `FailingStreak`, issue #3770), deletes every ticket whose status is cancelled and whose `enqueued_at` is before now − `QUEUE_TICKET_CANCELLED_KEEP_SECONDS` (1 hour), and logs `tickets.purged` with the count. A ticket enqueued exactly at the cutoff stays. A ticket that waited over an hour before it was cancelled goes at the next pass, since nothing reads it. A queued ticket and a claimed ticket are never touched. The enqueue's read-back and the pairing's re-read happen inside one request, long inside the hour, and already treat a missing row as gone, and the same account can queue, leave and queue again as before.

**Reach.** One row per cancelled ticket. No index and no migration: `tickets_pairing_scan_idx (status, rating, enqueued_at)` leads with `status`, nothing references `tickets` by foreign key, and once the first pass has deleted the whole backlog in one statement the table holds about an hour of cancelled rows.

Hearthstone semantics: a left queue entry is not kept as history.

Rejected: deleting a ticket as it is cancelled, which needs all three cancel paths to delete and leaves the backlog, as R4390000 rejected for the daily invites; a limit on how often an account may join the queue, which would refuse an honest player switching decks, while the purge bounds the rows without refusing anyone; deleting claimed tickets, since `matches_mode_of` and `replays_list_with_modes` read a queue match's mode from its ticket, which would first need `matches.mode` stamped and backfilled by a migration; a `cancelled_at` column to count the hour from the cancel, which is a migration for a bound the enqueue time already gives; and deleting in batches, since the table is small and one statement holds no lock a queue path waits on.
