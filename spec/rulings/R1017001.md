With a face's whole text one hover or click away ([[R1017000]]), the most iconic cards give their picture the whole face and print none of their words, as Hearthstone's Signature cards do: the cards [[R641]]'s hero portraits are drawn from (Mr. Vanilla, Gary the Gambler, Tempo Timmy, Big D-fender, Duplicating Felinors and Jlockeed Shredder-10) print their art across the whole full face, base and Radiant, in hand too, over the frame's panel, which shows only as the rarity rim around it. The face keeps the cost gem, the rarity gem in the corner opposite it with the set mark under it, the crest, the name banner at the foot, level with a Unit's attack and health in the bottom corners, and in play its state badges; it draws no rules box, tags or type line. A scrim darkens the top and the foot behind them. Such a face prints less than its whole text, so its whole text, Radiant marks and references included, stands on its left in the hover preview, under it in the touch sheet and at reading size in the detail view, and the Almanac's and the deck builder's grids open its hover preview. The list is the portrait roster itself, by card name, so a card that becomes a hero's portrait becomes a full-art card. The Full-art cards setting (Visuals, on by default) turns them off, and they print their text like any card. A compact face and a board minion keep their windows. The art is the card's own ([[R660]]: a delivered file first, the procedural picture otherwise), cropped to the face. Presentation only: no rule reads it.
