The designer asked (#1563) that KY's Competition have a 60-second timeout, against the AI and in a match. Time is no part of `reduce` ([[§9.3]]), so the window is its host's: a sequence has `KY_COMPETITION_SECONDS` (60) from its first question (the question whose streak is 0, [[R1184000]]), and when they run out with a question of it still open, the host answers that question Stop for its player, the answer [[R1184001]] gives a timeout: the sequence ends with the right answers so far, their Kenny is summoned, and the turn goes on. The window covers the whole sequence, not each question; a second sequence in the same turn has its own. It runs beside the turn clock ([[R79]]), never instead of it: whichever runs out first acts, and the turn clock's timeout still answers Stop and ends the turn.

The engine names the open question for a host (`prompts::sequence_question_of`: its player, streak and prompt id). The server's match clock arms the window at a sequence's first question and keeps it over the questions after it (`ClockExpiry::Sequence`), and its actor logs the Stop as a server action, so `(seed, log)` still folds the match. The practice host is the client (§9.9): its controller keeps the same window and sends the Stop as the human's answer. The prompt shows the seconds left either way, counted from when the client first saw the first question. Duos keeps its round and prompt clocks ([[§13]]), which already bound a sequence there. It edits [[§8.9]].
