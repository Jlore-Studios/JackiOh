Only the best `finalists × AI_SEARCH.linesPerAction` lines of the beam (six, at most two per first action) are scored after the opponent's reply, and they are picked by the static score, which prices almost none of the seat's own delayed effects: a countdown ([[R1181000]], Supply Drop's "in 2 turns") is weighed only inside the reply's score (#1995), and a next-turn reward (Mind Games', [[R861]]) not before it comes due. A line whose value is such a payoff ranked on its cost alone, so one unit play tried in five lanes filled the six places first and the line was cut before the AI ever weighed it: the sweep of 2026-10-28 saw Supply Drop cast on 25 of 619 affordable turns at Hard and Mind Games on 12 of 470. No Hearthstone rule is involved: the search changes no game.

**What changes.** A line has a payoff still to come when it is one the reply answers, passed or yielded ([[R3498000]]), and an entry of the seat's own waits in its end's `delayed` that the root did not hold, matched by id. When none of the lines `shortlist_lines` took has one, the best line that does is kept beside them, past the count and the per-action cap, so a decision replies to at most one more line. It is replied to last, once every line `shortlist_lines` took has been, on a slice of the counter of its own (`payoff_score`) that holds only the nodes left past those the reserve keeps for scoring the finalists on every other determinization, `finalists × (determinizations − 1) × (maxDepth + 1 + AI_REPLY.reserveSteps)`, so its reply, both of them for a countdown ([[R1181000]]), never takes one of theirs. It is not replied to at all once a host's clock has passed its wrap-up ([[R3498000]]), which the search polls once before it. So a counter or a clock that runs short drops it first, and the decision's `stoppedBy` says which (`budget`, `clock`). When one made the cut, or none has one, the shortlist is the one it was. The entries read are the seat's own and public ([[R185]]): a countdown's badge, a reward the seat filed.

**Rejected.** Replying to it on the decision's own counter before the finalists are scored on the other determinizations, as #3696 first built it: on a short counter its reply came out of the nodes those worlds were kept, and the decision left its finalists unscored on one (issue #3751). Replying to it after the other worlds are scored: the finalists are picked from determinization 0's replies, so it could then be weighed only on fewer worlds than they are, or played again on each world already scored. Checking the nodes left against an estimate and then replying on the whole counter: a reply longer than the estimate would still take theirs, which a slice cannot. Reading the beam's stop for the clock: a clock that passes its wrap-up while the shortlist is replied to would go unseen. Holding the extra line to the per-action cap: it could never add a line whose first action was already taken twice. Putting it first: it would take a place from a line the static score ranked better whenever the counter runs short. A larger reserve of nodes: it would shrink every decision's beam for the few that hold such a line. Pricing delayed entries in the static evaluation: the reply already weighs a countdown where it comes due (#1995), and a static price would count it twice there.
