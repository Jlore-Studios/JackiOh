Only the best `finalists × AI_SEARCH.linesPerAction` lines of the beam (six, at most two per first action) are scored after the opponent's reply, and they are picked by the static score, which prices almost none of the seat's own delayed effects: a countdown ([[R1181000]], Supply Drop's "in 2 turns") is weighed only inside the reply's score (#1995), and a next-turn reward (Mind Games', [[R861]]) not before it comes due. A line whose value is such a payoff ranked on its cost alone, so one unit play tried in five lanes filled the six places first and the line was cut before the AI ever weighed it: the sweep of 2026-10-28 saw Supply Drop cast on 25 of 619 affordable turns at Hard and Mind Games on 12 of 470. No Hearthstone rule is involved: the search changes no game.

**What changes.** A line has a payoff still to come when it is one the reply answers, passed or yielded ([[R3498000]]), and an entry of the seat's own waits in its end's `delayed` that the root did not hold, matched by id. When none of the lines `shortlist_lines` took has one, the best line that does is added after them, past the count and the per-action cap, so a decision replies to at most one more line, and last, so a counter that runs short drops it first. When one made the cut, or none has one, the shortlist is the one it was. The entries read are the seat's own and public ([[R185]]): a countdown's badge, a reward the seat filed.

**Rejected.** Holding the extra line to the per-action cap: it could never add a line whose first action was already taken twice. Putting it first: it would take a place from a line the static score ranked better whenever the counter runs short. A larger reserve of nodes: it would shrink every decision's beam for the few that hold such a line. Pricing delayed entries in the static evaluation: the reply already weighs a countdown where it comes due (#1995), and a static price would count it twice there.
