## 8. Complete card catalog

All 268 cards of the three sets — Core's 100 ([[§8.1]]–[[§8.5]]), Classic's 90 ([[§8.6]]) and Classic+'s 78 ([[§8.7]]) — plus the tokens a card defines (Core's 5, index N.1, and Classic+'s 28, index N.k, each after its card) and the ten AI generated cards ([[§8.7]]'s end), in index order (Core's 4 shared tokens, The Coin and the Ghoul Token are in section 7), described by what they do to the game state rather than by their printed text; stats read base → radiant, and the Engine column names the primitive from section 6 or the ruling from section 11 that the implementation depends on. Every Radiant face meets [[R275]]'s standard, and `docs/radiant-audit.md` records each card against it. The catalog prints each Radiant face in full: its `radiant.text` is the Radiant cell read by the Conventions below and written out ([[R277]]). A card's `refs` list the cards and tokens its text names ([[R279]]).

Conventions: "target" means the player picks at play time from all legal units and heroes on either side unless narrowed. "Your" means the controller. A Cry or spell whose target set is empty fizzles: the unit still enters, the spell still counts as played. Damage always goes through the pipeline in section 4.4; "lose health" does not. Reading a Radiant cell: a cell that lists keywords without "Plus" gives the radiant form's complete keyword list; "Plus X" adds keyword X to the base keywords; a cell that names no keywords keeps the base keywords. A clause the cell restates replaces the base version and every base clause it does not restate is kept; a cell that changes only a number ("+10 attack", "5 damage") changes only that number. "same" says so explicitly, and "Also" and "Then" add effects.

Core's rarity is assigned by mechanical complexity and game impact, and is the value the catalog's `rarity` field carries. Common: keywords only, one primitive with at most one target, or one fixed start-of-turn, end-of-turn or cast-on-draw effect. Rare: a trigger that reads state or asks for a choice, a modal choice or prompt, a cost or mana modifier, or a simple trap. Epic: chained prompts, delayed or cross-turn effects, traps that act on the opponent's turn, or effects that read several zones. Legendary: control changes across the board, zone-wide rewrites, recursion, or a stat layer that exists for one card. Mythic: a subsystem of its own. Distribution: 32 Common, 40 Rare, 16 Epic, 7 Legendary, 5 Mythic (patch v0.2.9, issue #44, made #27, #28 and #40 Rare). This supersedes the rarities printed in the source list (#1–20 Common, #21–50 Rare, #51–80 Epic, #81–95 Legendary, #96–100 Mythic); a review comparing catalog to source should treat that difference as intended. Classic and Classic+ keep the designer's rarities: their source list gives rarity card by card, so each is a choice rather than an index block, and rarity feeds pools (a random Legendary, a random Book). Classic: 35 Common, 26 Rare, 18 Epic, 10 Legendary, 1 Mythic (90). Classic+: 13 Common, 24 Rare, 25 Epic, 13 Legendary, 3 Mythic (78). A Classic+ token carries `rarity: "Token"` like every token, so no pool ever finds one by rarity, and the rarity the designer printed on it as `printedRarity` ([[§5]]), which the Rarity column shows in parentheses: Legendary on the eight Pancakes, the five Losers, KY's Gift, Felinor Flagbearer Prime, Classic Golem and the J-lease Pack; Epic on Otherworldly Removal's three and Solarius Prime; Rare on Bone Storm and Brother Ping; Common, Common, Rare, Legendary and Mythic on the five Grapes; none on the AI generated cards.

Rarity measures complexity and how much a card can swing a game, not raw stats; power is tuned with numbers and Radiant. Common is one idea read in one pass: vanilla or keyword Units, or a single effect (damage, draw, heal, destroy, bounce, counter); it can touch one card in another zone or reset the board in one line, with no build-arounds and no rule changes. Rare is one idea with a twist: conditions, Choose one, cost changes, making cards Radiant, stealing a single card; small engines that repeat a simple effect; a theme rewarded that still works in any deck. Epic is engines and payoffs a deck is built around; repeated generation of a random card or token every turn; mass effects on the opponent's hand or deck; multi-part effects and set showcases. Legendary takes over, swaps or replaces a whole resource (deck, hand, board or Hero health); breaks a core rule for one player; named characters and effects a game is remembered by. Mythic breaks the frame of the game: sets rules for the whole match, persists across games, hands a turn to an AI, or could not exist on a normal card. Distribution targets: Common 20-40%, Rare 25-30%, Epic 20-30%, Legendary 10-15%, Mythic 5% or less.

Families share one rarity: 2+ cards with the same naming template, card type and effect shape, so one card can be guessed from another. The family's rarity wins over the criteria; a member too strong or weak for it changes numbers, not rarity (Book of Vital Kill stays Epic although setting Hero health is Legendary territory).

Tags are not families: Human, Felinor, KY, CN, Fruit and Plague cards take any rarity, and Call to Arms is no Call to Chaos, the templates differing. Tokens take the rarity of the card that makes them; generic tokens many cards make (Rush, Sheep, Felinor, Ghoul) have no rarity. Real cards made by other cards keep their own rarity (Book of Flame from Devil's Pact, Reminisce from Field of Dreams). A bigger version of the same effect is never a lower rarity than the smaller one (Call to Arms Recruits at Common, so Pile On's Recruit of the deck is Epic). Radiant never changes rarity. One name names one card ([[R739]]).
