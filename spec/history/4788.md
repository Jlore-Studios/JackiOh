**[[R4788000]] A wide decision's lethal walk runs past its scaled allowance while it is closing on the lethal (issue #4788, 2026-11-24)**: `decide` scales a wide state's budget down, the lethal solver's quarter with it ([[R2575000]]): 300 nodes at 30 root candidates, 145 at 31, 121 at 37, where a lethal of four to six actions costs the best-first walk 120 to 220. The audit of issue #4787 logged 8 lethals in about 24 AI-against-AI games that a 6,000-node run of the same solver proved on the decision's own worlds and the decision did not play; 4 were wide boards needing 122 to 213 nodes against allowances of 121 to 140, and after 2 of them the AI ended its turn. Once a scaled allowance is spent, the best-first walk now goes on only from a position whose ready attacks already reach lethal (`ready_gap` at or under 0), and the proof of a line that won on the first sampled world is always finished, up to the allowance the caller gave (`find_lethal_reaching`); what it spends past the scaled allowance is added to the searched section, so the beam and the replies keep their nodes. A state of ordinary width, and a wide one whose walk never reads as lethal, decide node for node as before; the lethal hint and the concession check have no reach; `AI_SEARCH.lethal_reach` off restores the old stop. On the same games with the rule, 5 of 49 wide decisions spent past their allowance, 4 of them finding the lethal for 1 to 73 nodes more, and the turns ended with a provable lethal fell from 4 to 1.
