## 14. Economy

How an account comes to own cards and cosmetics: Jlore, the cosmetics there are to own, card packs and their slot roll, pity, the token bars and the cosmetic token cycle, cosmetic packs and the Chase Shard, set tracks and XP, what play earns, daily quests, dev accounts, the ledger every grant is written to, and going live. Every gameplay card is earned by playing, and generously; the endgame is a collection of cosmetics. The model is the designer's, tracked in #1294; each of its sub-issues builds a part and writes the rulings it needs ([[§11]]), #1294's numbers go into `crates/server/src/config.rs` (exported to `apps/web/src/wire/serverConfig.ts`) together, before the parts that use them; a constant whose value #1294 does not give goes in with the sub-issue that first needs it, and #1333 brought every part built before the owner's numbers in line with them ([[R1333000]], [[R1333001]]); `DEV_PROFILE_IDS`, an environment variable, is read with the rest of the server's environment.

Every value here is a constant named in backticks, never a number (CLAUDE.md rule 9). The server owns every balance, roll, counter, bar and grant ([[§9.1]]): the client sends intent, such as buying or opening a pack, redeeming a token or picking a chase, and renders the server's projection of its own account, deciding nothing (CLAUDE.md rule 7). An account reads its own wallet, inventory, pity counters, bars, tracks, quests, cosmetics and ledger rows, and nobody else's: what a pack held, a counter or a balance reaches no other player. Only non-token cards of the sets that ship are collected: no pack, reward or grant holds a token or a card of a set that has not shipped ([[§9.4]] L3, [[R1420]]). Real money is out of scope: nothing here is sold, and nothing is bought but with Jlore. Art cosmetics are out of scope too: this section says which cosmetics an account owns, and [[§14.2]] where what it equips is shown, to the opponent, a watcher and a replay's viewer among them ([[R1834002]]). A ladder bot owns no collection ([[R1454]]) and earns nothing; it wears cosmetics drawn as a person's, from a wardrobe kept off the ledger ([[R1834005]]). The economy went live with #1310 ([[§14.12]]): a new account starts with the starter cards and earns the rest ([[R1310000]]), every account active before then kept every card it owned ([[R1310002]]), and a practice game records nothing but what [[R1303000]] pays ([[R187]], [[§14.8]]).

### 14.1 Jlore

Jlore is the soft currency: a whole-number balance in each account's wallet, never below zero. It is earned only by playing: games ([[§14.8]]), daily quests ([[§14.9]]) and set track levels ([[§14.7]]), besides what a dev account grants itself ([[§14.10]]). It is never sold, and no money becomes Jlore. It buys card packs ([[§14.3]]). A spend the wallet cannot cover is refused and changes nothing.

### 14.2 Cosmetics

A cosmetic is an entry of the cosmetics registry, one data file the server and the client both read: its id, its kind, its name, its rarity, on the cosmetics' own scale from Common to Mythic, whether it is its kind's default and whether it is a chase. A per-card cosmetic (a foil, a finish, a border, a full-art face) is owned for one card; an account cosmetic (a board, a field, a card back, a hero portrait) is owned for the whole account. Apart from the starter cosmetics, to own a cosmetic is to hold it in the ledger ([[§14.11]]). A player's equipped, owned cosmetics show on every board that shows their seat: the server checks every pick as it is written ([[R1834001]]) and sends a loadout per seat, frozen with the match, to the other seat, a watcher and a replay's viewer, a card's own picks only once a view has shown that card ([[R1834002]], [[R1834003]]); the client wears it and never infers what another player owns, and a viewer may turn the other seats' off for themselves ([[R1834004]]). Duos still shows each player's cosmetics on their own side only. A per-card cosmetic is equipped on the card it is owned for, its picks kept on the account ([[R1655000]], [[R1834000]]).

- **Two scales.** A card's rarity is the catalog's ([[§5]]). A cosmetic's rarity is a scale of its own, Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary or Mythic, never tied to the card it goes on: a duplicate's cosmetic ([[§14.3]]), a token ([[§14.5]]) and a cosmetic pack's slot ([[§14.6]]) each read the cosmetic's own rarity. The two are kept as separate types, so no code can take one for the other ([[R1333000]]).
- **Starter cosmetics.** Each kind's default and the starter items of the card and deck slots are owned by every person's account, on every card, before and after going live.
- **Account cosmetics are Epic or better.** Every account cosmetic but its kind's default is `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY` or better, the floor of a duplicate's global roll ([[§14.3]]), and the registry's check refuses one below it. The first boards, fields and card backs, three of each kind past its default, are starters, which every account owns ([[R1661000]]); every hero portrait but `vanilla` is not ([[R1827000]]), and nor are the six boards, six fields and six card backs added since, Epic, Legendary and Mythic, so those and the portraits are what the global roll drops ([[R1833000]]). While ownership decides, the pickers of a board, a field and a card back offer the defaults, the starters and the ones the account owns; a stored pick the account does not own, one synced from another device say, draws as its kind's default; and a deck's save refuses one it does not own ([[R1833000]]).
- **Hero portraits.** A deck's hero portrait ([[R641]]) is an account cosmetic, one per portrait id: `vanilla`, Common, its kind's default and a starter, and the other five Epic, owned only by the ledger. They drop from a duplicate's global roll ([[§14.3]]) and from cosmetic packs ([[§14.6]]), and an Epic cosmetic token buys one ([[§14.5]]). A deck's save refuses a portrait its account does not own, and the deck builder offers the owned ones ([[R1827000]]).
- **Chase cosmetics** are the Legendary and Mythic ones, the chase tier, and the registry's chase mark agrees with the rarity: it is set exactly on them, but the Jlorious card back, which nothing drops ([[R2755000]]). No token pays for one ([[§14.5]]). One drops when a duplicate's cosmetic roll ([[§14.3]]) or a cosmetic pack's slot lands on its tier, and a full Chase Shard bar buys one ([[§14.6]]). The first two Legendary cosmetics, a foil and a finish, are starters ([[R1333000]]); every chase added since is earned, the first Mythics among them: a Legendary and a Mythic foil, finish and border, and two Legendary and a Mythic board, field and card back ([[R1833000]]).
- **The Jlorious card back.** `card-back-jlorious`, a Mythic card back, is owned only by reaching Jlorious ([[§9]], [[R608]]): a rated game grants it to each person it leaves Jlorious, with the ledger reason `jlorious` ([[§14.11]]), and keeps it theirs once they fall out. It is no chase, and no duplicate's roll, cosmetic pack, token, Chase Shard bar, dev fill or ladder bot's wardrobe offers it; a ladder bot, never Jlorious, never owns it ([[R640000]], [[R2755000]]).
- **Full-art faces.** A card prints full art when the registry has a full art for it ([[R2655001]]): the hero-portrait cards ([[R1017001]]) and fifteen more, twenty-one today. Each has three per-card cosmetics on the cosmetics' own scale, whatever its card's rarity ([[R1313000]], [[R2655000]]): an **Epic** full art, the still face, which is no chase, drops from packs like any other Epic cosmetic and is bought by an Epic cosmetic token and not a Rare one ([[§14.5]]); a **Legendary**, animated; and a **Mythic**, animated and signed, both chases ([[R2655003]]). A card's own pick chooses which owned one it wears, or none, and a card with no pick wears its Epic where the Full-art cards setting is on ([[R2655000]]); the tokens of five of those cards wear their parent's ([[R2655002]]). Since the economy went live, a player's own copy prints its full art when they own it and their pick or their Full-art cards setting says so; an opponent, a watcher or a replay's viewer sees it printed, at the tier its owner's frozen loadout names, where that loadout says so (owned, and its owner's pick or switch on) and their own Full-art cards setting is on, and its normal face otherwise ([[R1834002]], [[R1834004]], [[R2655000]]). Nobody owned one when the economy went live ([[§14.12]], [[R1310002]]). The landing page's fan keeps showing full art, and so does the Almanac on every card but a signed-in viewer's own, which prints it as their own copy does ([[R1956000]]); practice, hotseat and every end-to-end test but the one that walks the economy treat every cosmetic as owned ([[R1310004]]).

### 14.3 Card packs and the slot roll

There is a card pack for each set that ships ([[R1420]]) and a global card pack, whose pool is every set that ships. Each costs `PACK_PRICE_JLORE` and holds `PACK_SLOTS` slots. Buying one debits the wallet and adds the pack, unopened, to the account's inventory, in one transaction. Packs also come from set track rewards ([[§14.7]]). Opening a pack rolls every slot and grants what each holds, its pity counters and bars included, in one transaction or not at all.

Every drop of the economy, a card pack slot's, a duplicate's cosmetic and a cosmetic pack slot's ([[§14.6]]), is a **tiered roll** of two draws ([[R1339000]]). The first is the tier: one integer draw below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` against a table that sets a weight for each tier above the lowest. The lowest tier is the fallback and takes whatever weight is left, `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` less the rest, computed and never stored, so raising one tier takes from the fallback alone. A tier's chance is its weight over `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`: the table is the published odds. A floor is a fallback too: a slot that must be at least some tier rolls the same table, and any result below the floor becomes the floor tier, the tiers above it keeping their own odds. The second draw picks the item within the tier, as the roll's rules say. Every roll goes through one pure function, from the set tiers' weights, the fallback, an optional floor and a draw to a tier, which refuses set weights summing past `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`; each roll's draws are seeded and logged with its opening ([[§14.11]], [[R1339002]]).

Each slot of a card pack rolls a rarity, one of [[§5]]'s from Common to Mythic, on `CARD_TIER_WEIGHTS`, Common its fallback. One slot, `PACK_FLOORED_SLOT`, the Rare-or-better slot, is floored at `PACK_FLOOR_RARITY`, so every pack holds a Rare or better. Pity then forces a rarity on top of the rolled slots when a counter runs out ([[§14.4]]). The slot then draws a card of its rarity from the pack's pool, the non-token cards of its set or, for the global pack, of every set that ships, weighted toward the cards the account does not own by `PACK_UNOWNED_WEIGHTS`. A rarity the pool lacks falls to the highest rarity below it that the pool holds, down to the slot's fallback (the floor, for the floored slot), then to the nearest one above, and below the floor last; the slot keeps the rarity it rolled ([[R1301001]]). What the slot grants follows the dupe chain:

- **A new card**, one the account owns fewer than `MAX_COPIES` of, unlocks: the account is granted a copy.
- **A duplicate**, a card the account already owns `MAX_COPIES` of, the most a deck may hold and never more than the account owns ([[§9.4]] L3, L5), unless its global roll below drops an account cosmetic in its place, makes its own tiered roll on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS` for a cosmetic rarity, on the cosmetics' own scale whatever the card's rarity ([[§14.2]], [[R1301002]]), and becomes a per-card cosmetic of that rarity for that card, one the account does not own on it; it falls to no other rarity. A Legendary or Mythic one is a chase ([[R1333000]]).
- **The global roll.** A duplicate first, while the account has an account cosmetic it does not own at or above `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY`, draws one integer below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`. Below `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_ROLL_WEIGHT` ([[R1953000]]) it is a hit: the duplicate becomes one of those account cosmetics, owned for the account, instead of a per-card one, its rarity a tiered roll on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS` floored at `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY` that falls as a cosmetic pack's floored slot does ([[§14.6]]); at or past it the duplicate rolls its per-card cosmetic. With nothing left unowned the draw is not taken, so a duplicate whose account owns every account cosmetic draws as it did before the roll ([[§14.2]], [[R1827000]], [[R1833000]]). The draw comes after the slot's card's, and a hit's tier and pick after it ([[R1661001]]).
- **A spent duplicate**, a duplicate whose card has no cosmetic of the rarity it rolled left for the account to own, grants nothing more.
- **Bar points.** Every slot, whatever it grants, adds the points of the rarity its slot rolled, pity's force included, to both token bars ([[§14.5]], [[R1953000]], [[R1999000]]).

No cosmetic roll, a duplicate's, its global roll's included, or a cosmetic pack's ([[§14.6]]), touches the pity counters: they read the slots' card rarities alone ([[§14.4]]).

### 14.4 Pity

Pity keeps a counter for each of Epic, Legendary and Mythic; whether an account keeps one set of counters for all its card packs or one per kind of pack is the pity math's sub-issue's to rule ([[R1298000]]). A counter counts card packs, not slots: a pack with a slot that rolls the counter's rarity resets it, whatever that slot then grants, and a pack with none adds to it. A counter resets only when its own rarity drops: a Mythic leaves the Epic and Legendary counters running, and a Legendary the Epic one. The counters are the account's to see, with each rarity's odds, on the Purchases page (`/purchases`), linked from the site footer and from the Shop and shown nowhere else: not on the pack screen, the Shop or the cosmetic packs panel ([[R1665000]]). The odds there and in every odds `GET` are the tables of [[§14.3]] and [[§14.6]] as percentages the server computes, the fallback included, never a rate the client computes: a card pack slot's and the guaranteed slot's, a duplicate's cosmetic, the global roll's line (its chance, then its tiers from Epic, [[R1661001]]), and a cosmetic pack slot's and its guaranteed slot's. The page adds the counters, `n / threshold packs` for each of the three, once for every card pack ([[R1298000]]), and a line that pity guarantees each within its threshold ([[R1339000]]). It opens with the disclaimer that Jlore is earned by playing and never sold ([[§14.1]]) and that a pack holds the server's seeded, logged roll ([[§14.11]]); a visitor with no account sees that and a link to sign in, since the odds and the counters are read from the server with an account.

A rarity's threshold (`PITY_EPIC_PACKS`, `PITY_LEGENDARY_PACKS`, `PITY_MYTHIC_PACKS`) is the longest an account waits for it: of any `PITY_EPIC_PACKS` card packs in a row that one Epic counter counts, at least one holds an Epic, the last of them when none before it did, and likewise for Legendary and Mythic. No counter ever passes its threshold, and `PITY_MYTHIC_PACKS` is the longest wait of all.

Pity sits on top of the tiered roll and changes no table ([[R1339001]]): the table is exactly what one roll does, and a counter only forces a rarity when it runs out. A pack in which a counter would reach its threshold, and none of whose slots rolled that rarity, forces one slot to it, Mythic first, then Legendary, then Epic, each into the first slot not yet forced, the floored slot first, that is below it and whose loss leaves the pack every Epic, Legendary and Mythic it rolled, or else into one whose rarity another slot also holds. Before pity, a card pack holds a rarity of weight w in 1 − (1 − w/`TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`)^`PACK_SLOTS` of packs, a chance per pack the floored slot leaves alone, since it lifts only Commons; with pity, a little more. A seeded simulation over many packs reports both rates, checks that each rate with pity is at least the one before it and that no counter passes its threshold, and nothing is tuned to it. Pity is pure, from the counters and the slots' rolled rarities to the slots after it and the next counters, and takes no draw, so the pack roll calls it on its own seeded, logged draws ([[§14.11]]).

### 14.5 The token bars and the cosmetic token cycle

Every opened slot fills two bars ([[R1999000]]): a card pack's ([[§14.3]]) adds the points `TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_BY_CARD_RARITY` gives the card rarity it rolled, pity's force included, whatever it grants, the same points to the card token bar and to the cosmetic token bar. A cosmetic pack's slot adds those `TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_BY_COSMETIC_RARITY` gives the cosmetic rarity it rolled, not the one it fell to, likewise to both ([[§14.6]], [[R1953000]]). A bar that reaches its size (`CARD_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE`, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE`) pays and keeps the overflow, in the opening's own transaction. The sizes are set so that, on any collection, each bar fills once every `CARD_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS` or `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS` card packs on average: before pity, a pack's expected points are its slots' rarity odds ([[§14.3]]) weighted by those points, and each bar's size over them, rounded, is its target ([[R1953000]], [[R1999000]]). An opening answers each slot's points with the bars, and the client draws them flying into the bars, pinned at the top of the screen, and counts none. A held token is spent from the Collection's fullscreen preview of a locked item it covers, which holding the item or its inspect key opens: a Rare or Epic cosmetic token on a card's own cosmetic or a playmat, backdrop or card back, a card token on a card the account does not own. No list or grid of cosmetics or cards carries a spend button ([[R1953001]], [[R2730000]]).

- **The card token bar** pays a card token. A card token unlocks any new card ([[§14.3]]) of a set that ships that the account chooses, from that card's preview in the Collection ([[R2730000]]).
- **The cosmetic token bar** pays the next step of `COSMETIC_TOKEN_CYCLE`: Rare, Epic, Rare, Epic, Rare, Cosmetic Pack, and then the same again. The account keeps its place in the cycle. A Rare or Epic step is a cosmetic token of that tier, which unlocks any cosmetic at its tier or below on the cosmetics' own scale ([[§14.2]]) that the account chooses and does not own: a Rare token a Common or Rare one, an Epic token a Common, Rare or Epic one, an Epic full art included. It buys a per-card cosmetic for a card the account owns, or an account cosmetic; since every account cosmetic but the defaults is Epic or better ([[§14.2]]), a Rare token buys none ([[R1661000]]). No token reaches Legendary or Mythic, so none pays for a chase ([[R1305003]]). A Cosmetic Pack step adds an unopened cosmetic pack to the inventory ([[§14.6]]).

Redeeming a token is one transaction. A choice the token cannot pay for, a card or cosmetic already owned, a cosmetic above the token's tier or a chase, is refused and spends nothing.

### 14.6 Cosmetic packs and the Chase Shard

A cosmetic pack is earned only, for now: it is the last step of the cosmetic token cycle ([[§14.5]]), and nothing sells one. Opening one rolls `COSMETIC_PACK_SLOTS` slots of cosmetics, not cards, through the same seeded, logged roll as a card pack ([[§14.11]]). Each slot's cosmetic rarity is a tiered roll ([[§14.3]]) on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS`, a duplicate's table, Common its fallback, and one slot, `PACK_FLOORED_SLOT`, is floored at `COSMETIC_PACK_FLOOR_RARITY`, so every pack holds an Epic or better ([[R1339000]]). There is no pity on cosmetic rarity, and no cosmetic roll moves a card pack's counters ([[R1306000]]).

A slot grants one cosmetic of its rarity that the account does not own, for a card or for the account. A slot whose cosmetic is for a card the account does not own, a new card ([[§14.3]]), also grants a copy of the card. A rolled tier with nothing unowned falls to the highest tier below it that has something, down to the roll's fallback tier, which for the floored slot is its floor, then to the nearest tier above, and the floored slot below its floor last, so it stays Epic or better while anything there is unowned. A Legendary or Mythic roll grants a chase of its tier while one is unowned ([[R1833000]]). If nothing at all is unowned, the slot grants nothing ([[R1306001]]). Every slot, whatever it grants, adds the bar points of the cosmetic rarity it rolled ([[§14.5]], [[R1999000]]).

The Chase Shard bar counts the cosmetic packs the account opens. At `CHASE_SHARD_PACKS` it is full: it buys one chase cosmetic, Legendary or Mythic, that the account chooses and does not own, and empties. A full bar holds, counting no further, until it buys, so with no chase left to buy it waits ([[R1306002]]). Chases also drop by luck: a duplicate's cosmetic roll ([[§14.3]]) or a cosmetic pack slot that lands on Legendary or Mythic gives one ([[R1333000]]).

The client asks before it spends the bar, since nothing takes a purchase back ([[R2683000]]): the first press on a chase's Buy sends nothing and asks in the row's place, an alert dialog, "Buy Galaxy? This spends your full Chase Shard bar.", naming the cosmetic in the reader's language, with Confirm and Cancel. Cancel holds the focus as the question opens; Confirm sends the one purchase; Cancel and Escape spend nothing and put the focus back on that Buy. The question is the client's alone: whether the bar is full, what it buys and the spend itself stay the server's ([[R2124000]]).

### 14.7 Set tracks and XP

Each set that ships has a track ([[R1420]]) of `SET_TRACK_LEVELS` levels. A track starts at its first level with no XP, and going from level n to the next costs `SET_TRACK_XP_PER_LEVEL` × n XP: the cost rises with every level and is never capped. The XP of games ([[§14.8]]) and quests ([[§14.9]]) goes to every active track, each gaining all of it; a dev account's own grant goes to the track it picks ([[§14.10]]). The newest set's track is active from the start, and an older set's once it unlocks: the older tracks unlock one at a time once the newest set's track reaches `SET_TRACK_UNLOCK_LEVEL`, nearest-older first, one per payout ([[R1302003]]). A track's last level is the last it reaches; XP past it still lands ([[R1302005]]).

Each level pays rewards, cards, Jlore and card packs, from a reward table per set kept as data. A level pays once, as ledger rows, when its track reaches it ([[§14.11]]): a track's first level when its first XP lands, or, for a new account's newest track, when the account becomes active ([[R1310000]]). A track does not pay out every card of its set; pack luck covers the rest ([[§14.3]]).

### 14.8 Earning

Games earn Jlore and XP for the people who play them, never for a ladder bot. A ladder game, a ranked match ([[§9.12]]) that is not a game of a Conquest series, pays its winner `JLORE_LADDER_WIN` and its loser `JLORE_LADDER_LOSS`. A practice game ([[§9.9]]) pays `JLORE_PRACTICE_WIN` for a win and `JLORE_PRACTICE_LOSS` for a loss. An unranked game, a match the queue did not pair that is no game of a Conquest series (a room challenge, [[R604]]), pays `JLORE_UNRANKED_WIN` and `JLORE_UNRANKED_LOSS`; each game of a Conquest series ([[§9.5]]), ranked or not, pays as its own result `JLORE_CONQUEST_WIN` and `JLORE_CONQUEST_LOSS`; and a co-op game ([[§13]]) pays each of its people `JLORE_COOP_WIN` when their team wins and `JLORE_COOP_LOSS` otherwise, and nothing when a void ended it ([[R1502000]]). The Jlore an account's games pay it, every mode's together, stops once the UTC day's `game` Jlore reaches `JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP`, as Hearthstone's games pay gold up to a daily limit: the game that crosses the cap is paid the part that fits, the ones after it no Jlore and no ledger row, and the next UTC day pays again. A capped game still pays its XP and moves quests, and the Jlore a quest or a track level pays neither counts toward the cap nor stops at it; a game's answer says when the cap held its Jlore back ([[R1483000]]).

XP stands in for time played: `XP_PER_ACTION` for each action the player took, a move of the game and never a row the server writes itself ([[R1302004]]), and `XP_WIN_BONUS` more for a win, the game's total never above `XP_GAME_CAP`, so a loop of empty actions cannot farm it. A practice game earns the XP the same game would earn on the ladder, times `XP_PRACTICE_SCALE`, rounded down ([[R1302007]]). An unranked game and a game of a Conquest series earn what the same game earns on the ladder, and a co-op game that times `XP_COOP_SCALE`, rounded down, counting only the moves its person sent in their own seat, never one the AI made for it ([[R1502000]]). The amounts are tuned so a ladder game averages `XP_LADDER_TARGET_MEAN` over [[R1333001]]'s measured games, and a practice game `XP_PRACTICE_SCALE` of it. What a draw, a concede or a game the reaper ends pays, [[R1302000]], [[R1302001]] and [[R1302002]] rule.

Every payout goes through one reward hook on the server, which takes a finished game's summary (the account, the mode, the result and the actions taken), pays it, and moves the account's quests ([[§14.9]]).

- **On the ladder, in an unranked game, a Conquest series and co-op** the server ran the match, so it counts the actions from its own log and pays in the transaction that records the result; the client reports nothing.
- **In practice** the game runs in the browser ([[§9.9]]), so the server takes nothing on the client's word. It issues the game's seed and remembers it with the account, the AI's difficulty and the deck. At the end the client sends the action log; the server folds it ([[§9.3]]) from the seed's setup to the claimed result, each action under its player's nonce and seat. It does not re-check the AI's side, since practice pay is held to the day's Jlore cap ([[R1483000]]): a move of the AI's seat folds like any other ([[R2560000]]). Only a game that passes pays; a seed pays once and expires unclaimed after `PRACTICE_SEED_TTL_SECONDS`. Only an active account's free game on a server seed pays, never a tutorial lesson ([[§9.10]]), a sandbox game ([[§9.9]]) or a game with custom cards ([[§12]]) ([[R1303000]]); a claim burns its seed, and a game resumed after a reload claims on its own ([[R1303001]]); and a paying game brings the last board the server kept, not the device's ([[R1303003]]). The server checks at most `PRACTICE_VERIFY_CONCURRENCY` claims at once, and a claim past that waits its turn, never dropped. A claim that passes is paid in one transaction, which the server tries again when the store fails it, `PRACTICE_PAY_ATTEMPTS` tries in all; one that never lands pays nothing and leaves its seed burned ([[R1688000]]). A claim is kept with its seed until it settles, paid or refused, once: a server that stops waits for the claims it is settling, and the retention loop settles any claim still unsettled `PRACTICE_SETTLE_GRACE_SECONDS` after it was made ([[R2422000]]). An account holds at most `PRACTICE_SEEDS_OPEN_MAX` unclaimed, unexpired seeds, a request for another is refused and issues nothing, so one account cannot stockpile seeds to claim in a burst ([[R1483000]]). A game left without saving gives its seed back: the server deletes the account's own unclaimed seed under the account's economy lock, so its place is free for the next request and a claim of it is an unknown seed, and it leaves another account's seed, or one already claimed, as it is. A game the server would not seed says from its start that it earns nothing, in the server's words ([[R2379000]]).

### 14.9 Daily quests

A quest is a goal read from a finished game's public events, such as winning games, playing spells, dealing damage with Units, playing cards of a set, switching Units' positions or making cards Radiant, and a payout of Jlore from `QUEST_JLORE_MIN` to `QUEST_JLORE_MAX`, more for a harder goal, with `QUEST_XP_PER_JLORE` XP for each Jlore of it. Quests are drawn from a pool kept as data, which holds a quest for playing the cards of each set that ships, and a position switch or a Radiant upgrade counts as the game's record counts it ([[R2253000]]). An account holds at most `QUEST_MAX_HELD`; while it holds fewer, a new quest arrives `QUEST_INTERVAL_HOURS` after the last one did, by the server's clock, so a player away for a long while comes back to `QUEST_MAX_HELD` waiting. Every finished game the reward hook sees ([[§14.8]]) moves the held quests' progress, and a quest that completes pays its Jlore and XP once, as ledger rows; the XP goes to every active track ([[§14.7]]). Its rhythm and top-up are [[R1304000]]'s, a game ending as a quest arrives is [[R1304001]]'s, and none arrives while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off ([[R1304002]]). A held quest may be replaced `QUEST_REPLACES_PER_DAY` times a UTC day, as Hearthstone's daily reroll allows: it leaves with its progress, and a quest drawn as an arrival is, neither held nor the one replaced, takes its slot at once, the arrival rhythm unmoved; the panel offers it behind a confirm while one is left, and none is replaced while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off ([[R2210000]]). `/collection` shows the held quests, and the tavern landing shows them behind a bookmark on its doors' box to an active account while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is on ([[R2035000]]).

### 14.10 Dev accounts

A dev account is one whose profile id the server's `DEV_PROFILE_IDS` environment variable lists, read at boot with the rest of the environment: a malformed id fails the boot, naming it, and none set means no dev accounts. The server decides: it tells the client whether the caller is a dev account, and every dev route checks the list itself and refuses anyone else as forbidden. The client only chooses whether to show the Dev tools panel. Any signed-in account can reveal its own profile id on the account page, hidden until asked for, so a new developer can be listed. In end-to-end mode the fixture account `e2e-econ` is a dev account too, for the test that walks the economy ([[R1310004]]).

A dev account can, for itself alone:

- give XP to a set track it picks, through the level curve ([[§14.7]]), and give Jlore;
- fill its collection with every card and every cosmetic but the Jlorious card back, which only reaching Jlorious grants ([[R2755000]]);
- clear its cards, its cosmetics, its Jlore, its bars and pity counters, or its track progress, each apart, or all of them;
- be given a quest at once, or complete one ([[§14.9]]);
- fill its card token bar or its cosmetic token bar, so it pays at once ([[§14.5]], [[R1310003]]).

Every dev grant and clear is written as ledger rows marked `dev` ([[R1300000]]), so a dev grant can always be told apart ([[§14.11]]). No new catalog version grants a dev account its cards, nor anyone ([[R1310001]]), so a clear stays cleared; before the economy went live, [[R481]]'s grant of each new catalog version passed over a dev account for that reason ([[R1300001]]). Otherwise a dev account counts as any other does: its games go into the card statistics ([[§9.11]]) and the ladder ([[§9.12]]).

### 14.11 The ledger

The ledger today is `collection` plus the append-only `collection_grants` (`crates/server/migrations/0002_collection.sql`): every change writes both in one transaction, and no client path writes either ([[§9.4]]). Every holding of the economy follows that pattern, a current-state table beside an append-only one, readable by its own account alone. Every grant of anything (cards, Jlore, packs, tokens, cosmetics, XP) is one append-only row, written server-side in the transaction that changes the state; so is every spend, such as a pack bought or a token redeemed. A row is never edited, and is deleted only with its account ("Delete my account", [[§9.4]]): a mistaken grant is corrected by an offsetting row.

A game's Jlore row has the reason `game`, and an account's `game` rows over a UTC day are what `JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP` counts, read in the transaction that writes the next one, under the account's economy lock ([[R1301003]], [[R1483000]]); no row is written for a game the cap leaves nothing for.

A replaced quest writes its `removed` row and its replacement's `arrived` row, both with the reason `quest`, in one transaction, and an account's `removed` rows with the reason `quest` over a UTC day are what `QUEST_REPLACES_PER_DAY` counts, read under the account's economy lock ([[R2210000]]); going live's `launch` removals count for nothing.

Every row says why. The collection's reasons are `pack`, `craft`, `reward`, `refund`, `admin`, `launch` and `dev`, a dev tool's own ([[R1300000]]); every economy ledger takes `launch` too, for the reset of going live ([[R1310002]]), and the cosmetic ledger `jlorious`, the Jlorious card back's grant ([[R2755000]]); a new kind of grant gets its reason from the sub-issue that adds it. A row a dev tool wrote is marked `dev` ([[§14.10]]).

Every roll is seeded on the server, and its seed and result are logged with the grant it makes, so any opening replays exactly and can be audited. Each opening's seed is drawn afresh, so no seed an account can read foretells a later roll.

A spend runs once, however often it is sent ([[R2124000]]). Every spend, a pack bought or opened, a token redeemed, a chase bought or an invite code redeemed ([[§9.4]]), may carry an idempotency key the client mints for a press and sends again on that press's retries. The server reads it under the account's economy lock: a key the account committed a spend with answers what that spend answered and spends nothing, the same key with another request is refused, and a new key's answer is written in the transaction of its grant. Only a committed spend keeps its key, for `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_TTL_HOURS`, and no client reads one. A spend sent without a key runs as it always did.

An account runs one spend at a time ([[R2378000]]). Each economy spend, a pack bought or opened, a token redeemed, a cosmetic pack opened or a chase bought, claims its account's turn before it reads the store and holds it until it answers: `ECONOMY_SPENDS_RUNNING_MAX` run at once, and one sent while they run waits its turn in the server, in the order sent and holding no database connection, then runs. One sent while `ECONOMY_SPENDS_WAITING_MAX` already wait is refused at once with 429, stating `SPEND_BUSY_RETRY_AFTER_MS` and spending nothing, so a burst of presses buys or opens one at a time and never holds the database's connections on the lock. Every other request that writes under the account's economy lock for a press, a card's picks saved or uploaded ([[§14.2]]), a practice seed asked for or given back ([[§14.8]]) or a quest replaced ([[§14.9]]), takes the same turn, waiting or refused alike, and writes nothing when refused. That refusal ran nothing and ends no press, and the client holds its Buy, Open, Open all, sealed pack, Reveal all and Next pack, and the cosmetic pack panel's Open and Chase Shard Buy ([[R2683000]]), while one of its spends is out, its Buy and Open buttons turning a loading wheel. An invite code's redemption takes no turn ([[§9.4]]).

### 14.12 Going live

Until the going-live sub-issue (#1310) shipped, these stood: the launch grant, a copy of every non-token card on becoming active ([[R111]]), and [[§9.4]]'s grant of each patch's new cards to every person's active account ([[R481]]). Every person's active account owned every card, a dev account that had cleared its own aside ([[§14.10]]), the server's `ECONOMY_LIVE` switch was off, and the parts of this section were built behind them without changing what a player owned: no caller of the reward hook paid while the switch was off ([[R1302006]]), and no quest arrived ([[R1304002]]). The going-live sub-issue wrote the rulings that replace them ([[R1310000]] to [[R1310004]]), edited [[§9.1]] and [[§9.4]], and turned `ECONOMY_LIVE` on, in the server and in the client, which reads it from `serverConfig.ts`. Since then:

- A new account starts with the starter cards and its tracks' first rewards ([[§14.7]]) and earns the rest as this section says. The starter cards are `STARTER_CARDS`, the Core cards of the three practice decks ([[§9.9]]), whose new ones an account already active when the decks grew to 25 cards was granted as its activation would have granted them ([[R1705002]]), and the first rewards are the newest set's track's first level, paid as the account becomes active ([[R1310000]]). Queueing checks ownership where it already did ([[§9.4]] L5), and a deck save still checks structure alone ([[R250]]): a draft may hold a card the player does not own, and is judged when queued ([[R1310000]]).
- An existing account kept every card granted to it, so nobody lost a deck. Its economy started at zero, and the ledger recorded the move with the reason `launch` ([[R1310002]]).
- A new catalog version grants no account its cards: a new set's cards come from its track and its packs ([[R1310001]]).
- The starter cosmetics stay owned by every person's account. The full-art faces started unowned for everyone, existing accounts included ([[R1310002]]), and every cosmetic added later must be owned ([[§14.2]]). When the hero portraits became cosmetics, every account then signed up kept all six, and a new one starts with `vanilla` alone ([[R1827000]]). The 36 cosmetics added after them are granted to no account, existing ones included: every account earns them ([[R1833000]]). So are the 57 full arts added after those, the six portrait cards' Legendary and Mythic and fifteen more cards' three ([[R2655000]]).
- Practice and hotseat let a player use every card and every cosmetic whatever the ledger holds, and so do the end-to-end tests, but the one that walks the economy itself, on a fixture account of its own ([[R1310004]]), whose token bars a dev account's tool fills ([[R1310003]]).
