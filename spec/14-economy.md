## 14. Economy

How an account comes to own cards and cosmetics: Jlore, the cosmetics there are to own, card packs and their slot roll, pity, the token bars and the cosmetic token cycle, cosmetic packs and the Chase Shard, set tracks and XP, what play earns, daily quests, dev accounts, the ledger every grant is written to, going live, the news, each entry with a reward to claim, card proficiency, how often an account has played each card and the stamps its plays unlock, and the Fleet track, a cosmetic track of its own earned by playing Stinkyscape. Every gameplay card is earned by playing, and generously; the endgame is a collection of cosmetics. The model is the designer's, tracked in #1294; each of its sub-issues builds a part and writes the rulings it needs ([[§11]]), #1294's numbers go into `crates/server/src/config.rs` (exported to `apps/web/src/wire/serverConfig.ts`) together, before the parts that use them; a constant whose value #1294 does not give goes in with the sub-issue that first needs it, and #1333 brought every part built before the owner's numbers in line with them ([[R1333000]], [[R1333001]]); `DEV_PROFILE_IDS`, an environment variable, is read with the rest of the server's environment.

Every value here is a constant named in backticks, never a number (CLAUDE.md rule 9). The server owns every balance, roll, counter, bar and grant ([[§9.1]]): the client sends intent, such as buying or opening a pack, redeeming a token or picking a chase, and renders the server's projection of its own account, deciding nothing (CLAUDE.md rule 7). An account reads its own wallet, inventory, pity counters, bars, tracks, quests, cosmetics and ledger rows, and nobody else's: what a pack held, a counter or a balance reaches no other player. Only non-token cards of the sets that ship are collected: no pack, reward or grant holds a token or a card of a set that has not shipped ([[§9.4]] L3, [[R1420]]). Real money is out of scope: nothing here is sold, and nothing is bought but with Jlore. Art cosmetics are out of scope too: this section says which cosmetics an account owns, and [[§14.2]] where what it equips is shown, to the opponent, a watcher and a replay's viewer among them ([[R1834002]]). A ladder bot owns no collection ([[R1454]]) and earns nothing; it wears cosmetics drawn as a person's, from a wardrobe kept off the ledger ([[R1834005]]). The economy went live with #1310 ([[§14.12]]): a new account starts with the starter cards and earns the rest ([[R1310000]]), every account active before then kept every card it owned ([[R1310002]]), and a practice game records nothing but what [[R1303000]] pays ([[R187]], [[§14.8]]).

### 14.1 Jlore

Jlore is the soft currency: a whole-number balance in each account's wallet, never below zero. It is earned only by playing: games ([[§14.8]]), daily quests ([[§14.9]]) and set track levels ([[§14.7]]), besides what a dev account grants itself ([[§14.10]]) and what a news entry's reward pays ([[§14.13]]). It is never sold, and no money becomes Jlore. It buys card packs ([[§14.3]]). A spend the wallet cannot cover is refused and changes nothing.

### 14.2 Cosmetics

A cosmetic is an entry of the cosmetics registry, one data file the server and the client both read: its id, its kind, its name, its rarity, on the cosmetics' own scale from Common to Mythic, whether it is its kind's default and whether it is a chase. A per-card cosmetic (a foil, a finish, a border, a full-art face, a version, which is a variant's level, an augment) is owned for one card; an account cosmetic (a board, a field, a card back, a hero portrait, a Starship's hull design, livery, trail or piece skin) is owned for the whole account. Apart from the starter cosmetics, to own a cosmetic is to hold it in the ledger ([[§14.11]]). A player's equipped, owned cosmetics show on every board that shows their seat: the server checks every pick as it is written ([[R1834001]]) and sends a loadout per seat, frozen with the match, to the other seat, a watcher and a replay's viewer, a card's own picks only once a view has shown that card ([[R1834002]], [[R1834003]]); the client wears it and never infers what another player owns, and a viewer may turn the other seats' off for themselves ([[R1834004]]). Duos still shows each player's cosmetics on their own side only. A per-card cosmetic is equipped on the card it is owned for, its picks kept on the account ([[R1655000]], [[R1834000]]).

- **Two scales.** A card's rarity is the catalog's ([[§5]]). A cosmetic's rarity is a scale of its own, Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary or Mythic, never tied to the card it goes on: a duplicate's cosmetic ([[§14.3]]), a token ([[§14.5]]) and a cosmetic pack's slot ([[§14.6]]) each read the cosmetic's own rarity. The two are kept as separate types, so no code can take one for the other ([[R1333000]]).
- **Starter cosmetics.** Each kind's default and the starter items of the card and deck slots are owned by every person's account, on every card, before and after going live.
- **Account cosmetics are Epic or better.** Every account cosmetic but its kind's default is `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY` or better, the floor of a duplicate's global roll ([[§14.3]]), and the registry's check refuses one below it. The first boards, fields and card backs, three of each kind past its default, are starters, which every account owns ([[R1661000]]); every hero portrait but `vanilla` is not ([[R1827000]]), and nor are the six boards, six fields and six card backs added since, Epic, Legendary and Mythic, so those and the portraits are what the global roll drops ([[R1833000]]). While ownership decides, the pickers of a board, a field and a card back offer the defaults, the starters and the ones the account owns; a stored pick the account does not own, one synced from another device say, draws as its kind's default; and a deck's save refuses one it does not own ([[R1833000]]).
- **Hero portraits.** A deck's hero portrait ([[R641]]) is an account cosmetic, one per portrait id: `vanilla`, Common, its kind's default and a starter, and the other five Epic, owned only by the ledger. They drop from a duplicate's global roll ([[§14.3]]) and from cosmetic packs ([[§14.6]]), and an Epic cosmetic token buys one ([[§14.5]]). A deck's save refuses a portrait its account does not own, and the deck builder offers the owned ones ([[R1827000]]).
- **Chase cosmetics** are the Legendary and Mythic ones, the chase tier, and the registry's chase mark agrees with the rarity: it is set exactly on them, but the Jlorious card back, which nothing drops ([[R2755000]]). A Legendary cosmetic token buys a Legendary one, and no token a Mythic ([[§14.5]], [[R2728000]]). One drops when a duplicate's cosmetic roll ([[§14.3]]) or a cosmetic pack's slot lands on its tier, and a full Chase Shard bar buys one ([[§14.6]]). The first two Legendary cosmetics, a foil and a finish, are starters ([[R1333000]]); every chase added since is earned, the first Mythics among them: a Legendary and a Mythic foil, finish and border, and two Legendary and a Mythic board, field and card back ([[R1833000]]).
- **The Jlorious card back.** `card-back-jlorious`, a Mythic card back, is owned only by reaching Jlorious ([[§9]], [[R608]]): a rated game grants it to each person it leaves Jlorious, with the ledger reason `jlorious` ([[§14.11]]), and keeps it theirs once they fall out. It is no chase, and no duplicate's roll, cosmetic pack, token, Chase Shard bar, dev fill or ladder bot's wardrobe offers it; a ladder bot, never Jlorious, never owns it ([[R640000]], [[R2755000]]). The **Jlorious Starship**, `starship-hull-jlorious`, a Mythic hull design in its own violet and ivory livery, is owned the same way and only that way: the game that grants the card back grants it beside it, with the same reason, and migration 0086 granted it once to every person who had held Jlorious before, as 0074 granted the back ([[R3335003]]).
- **Starship cosmetics.** Four account kinds dress every Starship an account launches ([[§6]]): its **hull design** (`starshipHull`), its **livery**, colours and emblem (`starshipLivery`), its **trail**, engine glow and launch trail (`starshipTrail`), and its **piece skin**, a recolour of every module it mounts (`starshipPieceSkin`). Each kind's default, Common and a starter, changes nothing: the standard hull is the design the Starship's name gives it ([[R3333000]]). They are picked on the account alone, in the Collection's Starship tab, never on a deck; a seat's frozen loadout carries its four picks, so the opponent, a watcher and a replay's viewer see them as they see the seat's board and card back, and a loadout frozen before them reads each kind's default ([[R1834002]]). Only a launched Starship wears them; a Dormant one keeps its shipyard. Every other Starship cosmetic is earned only: the Fleet track's ([[§14.15]]) and the Jlorious Starship ([[R3335000]]).
- **Earned only.** A cosmetic the Fleet track pays and a Jlorious one are **earned only**: no duplicate's roll ([[§14.3]]), cosmetic pack ([[§14.6]]), token ([[§14.5]]), Chase Shard bar or ladder bot's wardrobe ([[R1834005]]) offers one, a token or the Chase Shard aimed at one is a 400 that spends nothing, and the registry's check refuses one marked a chase, a default, a starter or a per-card cosmetic, or marked both ([[R3335002]]).
- **Variants and levels.** A card's look is a **variant**, one picture, and each variant a **ladder of levels**, each level the one below it plus one more thing: the variant's art in its normal frame, then across the whole face where it has a full art ([[R1017001]]), then **animated**, its picture's own layers moving ([[R2715001]]), then **signed**, the card's name signed across it ([[R2715002]]), only above an animated full art. Each level is a `version-…` entry of the registry, a per-card cosmetic owned card by card as a foil is and picked by the card's own pick alone; the **Default** variant, which every card has, starts at the normal face, which no entry names and every account owns ([[R3015000]]), drawn on a Mythic card as Full art and on a Legendary as Extended art, a token by its printed rarity ([[R3343000]], [[R3343001]]). Owning a level means owning every level below it ([[R3015003]]). The ladders, each level's rarity beside it ([[R3015001]]):

  | Alt art | Alt art, Rare | Alt full art, Legendary | animated, Mythic | animated and signed, Mythic |

  Every ladder follows one rule, which the registry's check holds: its ranks run gapless; it adds its art, then its full art (at least one of the two, and neither in the Default), then animated, then signed only above a full art; each level is rarer than the one below it until Mythic, and a signed one is Mythic; and every level is offered where its variant's first level is. So a variant's first level keeps the rarity its version had, each new level above it is a tier rarer, and every Legendary and Mythic level is a chase ([[R3015000]], [[R3015001]]). The thirteen one-art variants get no second art level, so no signed one: Extended art and Out of frame already break the frame, Immersive and Prismatic already fill and move it, and Pixel, Comic and Set showcase restyle the picture in a frame of their own ([[R3015000]]); Prismatic's one art level is drawn as a full art ([[R3343001]]); and part 6's six stay in the normal frame, so no Mythic level sits on their ladders ([[R3017000]]). Every level of a variant draws its first level's picture, a level adding only the full-art frame, its picture's own motion or the signature, so a signed level is its variant's full art, moving, signed, and no level draws a picture of its own ([[R3015002]]).

  The variants' first levels came in six parts. **Full art** is the card's art across its whole face with no words ([[R2725000]]). Part 2 adds three looks that draw the card anew ([[R2940000]]): **Alt art**, a new picture of the card's subject in the normal frame, and **Alt full art**, its second level, that picture across the whole face; and the **Sketch**, the normal picture in pencil and ink on paper with its frame a pencilled outline, whose full art is the sketch across the whole face ([[R2940001]], [[R3015002]]). Part 3 adds three that restyle the card's own art ([[R2942000]]): **Extended art**, the card's own picture past its window across the face behind a translucent frame and rules box; **Pixel**, the picture as 8-bit pixel art in a retro frame with the name in a pixel face; and **Comic**, the picture as pop art, flat colour, halftone dots and ink outlines, with the name in a burst ([[R2942001]]). Part 4 adds two that restyle the whole card ([[R2943000]]): **Set showcase**, the card in its set's own look, its picture in its set's treatment (Core's glass orb, Classic's temple stone, Meditative's ink wash, Stinkyscape's deep space and Training Grounds' carved wood, Classic+ and Meditative+ in their parents') and on a base face its set's edge, bezel and name banner; and **Out of frame**, the card's subject cut out of a smaller window and stepping over its frame, under its cost gem, crest, name, stats and rules text and inside its rim ([[R2943001]]). Part 5, the last, adds the top chases ([[R2944000]]): **Immersive**, a layered scene, the card's subject on a near layer over its backdrop hazed as far off, the near layer shifting with the pointer or the device's tilt and breathing slowly; and **Prismatic**, the picture in mirror chrome under an iridescent sheen and on a base face a rainbow edge, a chrome bezel and a chrome name banner, chrome and never gold ([[R2944001]]), drawn across the whole face as a full art at both levels ([[R3343001]]). Part 6 gives every card several more ([[R3017000]]), each in the normal frame: four new pictures of the card's subject, each under a treatment of its own, **Night** under a night sky with a moon and stars, **Watercolor** in washes on paper, **Stained-glass window** in leaded panes of flat colour lit from behind and **Tarot card** in a fortune card's arch with a sun, a crescent and stars and no words; and two restyles of the card's own picture, **Woodcut**, a three-tone print gouged in its middle tone, and **Neon sign**, glowing tubes along its shapes on a dark wall ([[R3017002]]).

  Each variant's **offer** is data in the registry, the cards that may own and wear its levels by type, rarity, set or id, a clause taking all its cards or a seeded share of them, each variant past the Default on cards of its own so cards differ ([[R2725001]], [[R2949001]]): the Default on every non-token card; Full art on a seeded share of the Rare and Epic Units and the Legendary and Mythic cards, the twenty-one full-art cards below always among them; Alt art on half of every card and on the Epic Units and Legendary and Mythic cards Alt full art took before, but a Legacy one ([[R3015000]]); the Sketch on Units and Legendary and Mythic cards; Extended art on Spells and Field Spells and Legendary and Mythic cards; Pixel on Rare and Epic Units, Traps and Field Traps and Legendary and Mythic cards; Comic on Rare and Epic Units, Spells and Traps and Legendary and Mythic cards; Set showcase on Legendary and Mythic cards; Out of frame on Rare to Mythic Units but a Legacy card and the Starship, whose pictures have no subject of their own; Immersive on Legendary and Mythic cards but the Starship; Prismatic on every Mythic card and some Legendary ones ([[R2940000]], [[R2942000]], [[R2943000]], [[R2944000]]); and part 6's six in three pairs, each pair splitting every non-token card between its two by one seed, a sample taking the cards from its `from` to below its `percent`: Night 60 in 100 of the Units, Field Spells and Field Traps and 40 in 100 of the Spells and Traps, Neon sign the rest; Watercolor 60 of the Units and Field Spells and 40 of the Spells, Traps and Field Traps, Stained-glass window the rest; Woodcut 60 of the Units, Traps and Field Traps and 40 of the Spells and Field Spells, Tarot card the rest ([[R3017001]]). So every card, Legacy ones included, is offered at least `VARIANT_MIN_PER_CARD` variants besides the Default, one of each pair, and no two cards are offered the same mix by design, as the owner asked (2026-11-03). A level that moves is never offered on a Legacy card, whose picture never moves, so its ladders stop before animated ([[R2724000]], [[R3015000]]). Each variant's levels reach at least `VERSION_OFFER_MIN_CARDS` cards and `VERSION_OFFER_MIN_PER_SET` of every set that ships. A card is never offered, sold, dropped or shown a level its variant's offer does not take, and the server refuses one with a 400 ([[R2725001]]); nor the first level of Full art or Extended art where it is the Default's look below ([[R3343000]]).

  **Default looks.** A variant's first level may be a card rarity's default look, data in the registry (`defaultOn`): on a card whose face prints that rarity, a token by its printed one, the Default variant draws it, its normal face and its Animated level alike; Full art's first level is the Mythics', Extended art's the Legendaries', and no rarity has two ([[R3343000]]). Every account owns it there with no ledger row, as every Default, so it is offered there nowhere: no pick, token, Chase Shard bar, duplicate's roll, cosmetic pack or dev fill names it, a pick, token or bar naming it is a 400, and its variant's ladder starts at the level above it, which is next with nothing owned. An account that owned it there before keeps its row, which still counts as that level and buys nothing more, and its pick wears the Default, which draws the same look ([[R3343000]]). A Mythic's Full art shows while the Full-art cards setting is on, its normal face otherwise; a Legendary's Extended art shows whatever the setting, its words kept, and on the board it is drawn art first, as every Legendary in play is ([[R3339000]], [[R3343001]]); a Mythic's Full art has depth planes, and a face drawing a Legendary's Extended art, which keeps to one picture, has none ([[R3343001]], [[R3344000]]). ### 14.3 Card packs and the slot roll

There is a card pack for each set that ships ([[R1420]]) and a global card pack, whose pool is every set that ships. Each costs `PACK_PRICE_JLORE` and holds `PACK_SLOTS` slots. Buying one debits the wallet and adds the pack, unopened, to the account's inventory, in one transaction. Packs also come from set track rewards ([[§14.7]]) and news rewards ([[§14.13]]). Opening a pack rolls every slot and grants what each holds, its pity counters and bars included, in one transaction or not at all. Open all opens up to `PACKS_OPEN_ALL_MAX` packs of one kind in one spend: each rolls from a seed of its own on what the pack before it left, exactly as that many openings one after another would, and every grant is written in one transaction whose statements do not grow with the count, or none is ([[R3212000]]).

Every drop of the economy, a card pack slot's, a duplicate's cosmetic and a cosmetic pack slot's ([[§14.6]]), is a **tiered roll** of two draws ([[R1339000]]). The first is the tier: one integer draw below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` against a table that sets a weight for each tier above the lowest. The lowest tier is the fallback and takes whatever weight is left, `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` less the rest, computed and never stored, so raising one tier takes from the fallback alone. A tier's chance is its weight over `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`: the table is the published odds. A floor is a fallback too: a slot that must be at least some tier rolls the same table, and any result below the floor becomes the floor tier, the tiers above it keeping their own odds. The second draw picks the item within the tier, as the roll's rules say. Every roll goes through one pure function, from the set tiers' weights, the fallback, an optional floor and a draw to a tier, which refuses set weights summing past `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`; each roll's draws are seeded and logged with its opening ([[§14.11]], [[R1339002]]).

Each slot of a card pack rolls a rarity, one of [[§5]]'s from Common to Mythic, on `CARD_TIER_WEIGHTS`, Common its fallback. One slot, `PACK_FLOORED_SLOT`, the Rare-or-better slot, is floored at `PACK_FLOOR_RARITY`, so every pack holds a Rare or better. Pity then forces a rarity on top of the rolled slots when a counter runs out ([[§14.4]]). The slot then draws a card of its rarity from the pack's pool, the non-token cards of its set or, for the global pack, of every set that ships, weighted toward the cards the account does not own by `PACK_UNOWNED_WEIGHTS`. A rarity the pool lacks falls to the highest rarity below it that the pool holds, down to the slot's fallback (the floor, for the floored slot), then to the nearest one above, and below the floor last; the slot keeps the rarity it rolled ([[R1301001]]). What the slot grants follows the dupe chain:

- **A new card**, one the account owns fewer than `MAX_COPIES` of, unlocks: the account is granted a copy.
- **A duplicate**, a card the account already owns `MAX_COPIES` of, the most a deck may hold and never more than the account owns ([[§9.4]] L3, L5), unless its global roll below drops an account cosmetic in its place, makes its own tiered roll on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS` for a cosmetic rarity, on the cosmetics' own scale whatever the card's rarity ([[§14.2]], [[R1301002]]), and becomes a per-card cosmetic of that rarity for that card, one the account does not own on it and the card is offered, so a level only on a card its variant's offer takes, and a retired full art or augment never ([[R2725001]], [[R3015000]]); it falls to no other rarity. A level drops **in order**, its variant's next on that card, every level below it owned there. Where a level past the next is open at the rolled tier, one draw below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` comes first: below `LEVEL_SKIP_WEIGHT` the duplicate grants such a level **out of order**, picked uniformly, with every level below it the account lacks there; at or past it, it picks among the in-order ones as before. With nothing open out of order the draw is not taken, and a seeded simulation holds out-of-order pulls to at most `LEVEL_SKIP_MAX_PERCENT_OF_IN_ORDER` percent of in-order ones ([[R3015003]]). A Legendary or Mythic one is a chase ([[R1333000]]).
- **The global roll.** A duplicate first, while the account has an account cosmetic it does not own at or above `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY`, draws one integer below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`. Below `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_ROLL_WEIGHT` ([[R1953000]]) it is a hit: the duplicate becomes one of those account cosmetics, owned for the account, instead of a per-card one, its rarity a tiered roll on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS` floored at `GLOBAL_COSMETIC_FLOOR_RARITY` that falls as a cosmetic pack's floored slot does ([[§14.6]]); at or past it the duplicate rolls its per-card cosmetic. With nothing left unowned the draw is not taken, so a duplicate whose account owns every account cosmetic draws as it did before the roll ([[§14.2]], [[R1827000]], [[R1833000]]). The draw comes after the slot's card's, and a hit's tier and pick after it ([[R1661001]]).
- **A spent duplicate**, a duplicate whose card has no cosmetic of the rarity it rolled left for the account to own, or only levels past the next with its extra draw at or past `LEVEL_SKIP_WEIGHT` ([[R3015003]]), grants nothing more.
- **Bar points.** Every slot, whatever it grants, adds the points of the rarity its slot rolled, pity's force included, to both token bars ([[§14.5]], [[R1953000]], [[R1999000]]).

No cosmetic roll, a duplicate's, its global roll's included, or a cosmetic pack's ([[§14.6]]), touches the pity counters: they read the slots' card rarities alone ([[§14.4]]).

### 14.4 Pity

Pity keeps a counter for each of Epic, Legendary and Mythic; whether an account keeps one set of counters for all its card packs or one per kind of pack is the pity math's sub-issue's to rule ([[R1298000]]). A counter counts card packs, not slots: a pack with a slot that rolls the counter's rarity resets it, whatever that slot then grants, and a pack with none adds to it. A counter resets only when its own rarity drops: a Mythic leaves the Epic and Legendary counters running, and a Legendary the Epic one. The counters are the account's to see, with each rarity's odds, on the Purchases page (`/purchases`), linked from the site footer and from the Shop and shown nowhere else: not on the pack screen, the Shop or the cosmetic packs panel ([[R1665000]]). The odds there and in every odds `GET` are the tables of [[§14.3]] and [[§14.6]] as percentages the server computes, the fallback included, never a rate the client computes: a card pack slot's and the guaranteed slot's, a duplicate's cosmetic beside the global roll's (its chance, then its tiers from Epic, [[R1661001]]), and a cosmetic pack slot's and its guaranteed slot's. The page sets every one of them in a table, a rarity to a row, and a rarity with no chance shows a dash. Beside each of the two cosmetic rolls' tables, a line gives the chance of an out-of-order pull where one is open (`levelSkip`, [[R3015003]]). Its pity table gives each of the three rarities' threshold and the account's counter, `n / threshold`, once for every card pack ([[R1298000]], [[R1339000]]). It opens with the disclaimer that Jlore is earned by playing and never sold ([[§14.1]]); a visitor with no account sees that and a link to sign in, since the odds and the counters are read from the server with an account.

A rarity's threshold (`PITY_EPIC_PACKS`, `PITY_LEGENDARY_PACKS`, `PITY_MYTHIC_PACKS`) is the longest an account waits for it: of any `PITY_EPIC_PACKS` card packs in a row that one Epic counter counts, at least one holds an Epic, the last of them when none before it did, and likewise for Legendary and Mythic. No counter ever passes its threshold, and `PITY_MYTHIC_PACKS` is the longest wait of all.

Pity sits on top of the tiered roll and changes no table ([[R1339001]]): the table is exactly what one roll does, and a counter only forces a rarity when it runs out. A pack in which a counter would reach its threshold, and none of whose slots rolled that rarity, forces one slot to it, Mythic first, then Legendary, then Epic, each into the first slot not yet forced, the floored slot first, that is below it and whose loss leaves the pack every Epic, Legendary and Mythic it rolled, or else into one whose rarity another slot also holds. Before pity, a card pack holds a rarity of weight w in 1 − (1 − w/`TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL`)^`PACK_SLOTS` of packs, a chance per pack the floored slot leaves alone, since it lifts only Commons; with pity, a little more. A seeded simulation over many packs reports both rates, checks that each rate with pity is at least the one before it and that no counter passes its threshold, and nothing is tuned to it. Pity is pure, from the counters and the slots' rolled rarities to the slots after it and the next counters, and takes no draw, so the pack roll calls it on its own seeded, logged draws ([[§14.11]]).

### 14.5 The token bars and the cosmetic token cycle

Every opened slot fills two bars ([[R1999000]]): a card pack's ([[§14.3]]) adds the points `TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_BY_CARD_RARITY` gives the card rarity it rolled, pity's force included, whatever it grants, the same points to the card token bar and to the cosmetic token bar. A cosmetic pack's slot adds those `TOKEN_BAR_POINTS_BY_COSMETIC_RARITY` gives the cosmetic rarity it rolled, not the one it fell to, likewise to both ([[§14.6]], [[R1953000]]). A bar that reaches its size pays and keeps the overflow, in the opening's own transaction. The card token bar's size is `CARD_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE`; the cosmetic token bar takes a size of its own at each step of its cycle, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE_RARE`, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE_EPIC`, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE_LEGENDARY` or `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_SIZE_PACK`, and the points it keeps count toward the next step against that step's size, so one gain can pay several steps, each in order ([[R2728000]]). The sizes are set so that, on any collection, the card token bar fills once every `CARD_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS` card packs on average, and the cosmetic token bar pays each step once every `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS_RARE`, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS_EPIC`, `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS_LEGENDARY` or `COSMETIC_TOKEN_BAR_TARGET_PACKS_PACK`: before pity, a pack's expected points are its slots' rarity odds ([[§14.3]]) weighted by those points, and each size over them, rounded, is its target ([[R1953000]], [[R1999000]], [[R2728000]]). An opening answers each slot's points with the bars, and the client draws them flying into the bars, pinned at the top of the screen, and counts none. A held token is spent from the Collection's fullscreen preview of a locked item it covers, which holding the item or its inspect key opens: a Rare, Epic or Legendary cosmetic token on a card's own cosmetic or a playmat, backdrop or card back, a card token on a card the account does not own. No list or grid of cosmetics or cards carries a spend button ([[R1953001]], [[R2728000]], [[R2730000]]).

- **The card token bar** pays a card token. A card token unlocks any new card ([[§14.3]]) of a set that ships that the account chooses, from that card's preview in the Collection ([[R2730000]]).
- **The cosmetic token bar** pays the next step of `COSMETIC_TOKEN_CYCLE`: Rare, Epic, Legendary, Rare, Epic, Cosmetic Pack, and then the same again, each step at its own size ([[R2728000]]). The account keeps its place in the cycle. A Rare, Epic or Legendary step is a cosmetic token of that tier, which unlocks any cosmetic at its tier or below on the cosmetics' own scale ([[§14.2]]) that the account chooses and does not own: a Rare token a Common or Rare one, an Epic token a Common, Rare or Epic one, the Default's Animated level included, and a Legendary token a Common, Rare, Epic or Legendary one, a Legendary chase such as Full art's first level included. It buys a per-card cosmetic for a card the account owns and the cosmetic is offered on, or an account cosmetic, and a level only as its card's next, every level below it owned there; a retired full art or augment, a level on a card it is not offered, or a level past the next is a 400 that spends nothing ([[R2725000]], [[R2725001]], [[R3015004]]); since every account cosmetic but the defaults is Epic or better ([[§14.2]]), a Rare token buys none ([[R1661000]]). No token reaches Mythic ([[R1305003]], [[R2728000]]). A Cosmetic Pack step adds an unopened cosmetic pack to the inventory ([[§14.6]]).

A token also comes as a news entry's reward ([[§14.13]]). Redeeming a token is one transaction. A choice the token cannot pay for, a card or cosmetic already owned or a cosmetic above the token's tier, a Mythic always, is refused and spends nothing.

### 14.6 Cosmetic packs and the Chase Shard

A cosmetic pack is earned only, for now: it is the last step of the cosmetic token cycle ([[§14.5]]) or a news entry's reward ([[§14.13]]), and nothing sells one. Opening one rolls `COSMETIC_PACK_SLOTS` slots of cosmetics, not cards, through the same seeded, logged roll as a card pack ([[§14.11]]). Each slot's cosmetic rarity is a tiered roll ([[§14.3]]) on `COSMETIC_TIER_WEIGHTS`, a duplicate's table, Common its fallback, and one slot, `PACK_FLOORED_SLOT`, is floored at `COSMETIC_PACK_FLOOR_RARITY`, so every pack holds an Epic or better ([[R1339000]]). There is no pity on cosmetic rarity, and no cosmetic roll moves a card pack's counters ([[R1306000]]).

A slot grants one cosmetic of its rarity that the account does not own, for a card it is offered (a retired full art or augment never, [[R2725001]], [[R3015000]]) or for the account, a level only on a card where it is its variant's next. At each tier it tries where a level past the next is open on some card, one draw below `TIER_WEIGHT_TOTAL` comes first: below `LEVEL_SKIP_WEIGHT` the slot grants such a level **out of order**, picked uniformly, on a card it is open on so, with every level below it the card lacks; at or past it, it tries the tier in order as before ([[R3015003]]). A slot whose cosmetic is for a card the account does not own, a new card ([[§14.3]]), also grants a copy of the card. A rolled tier with nothing unowned falls to the highest tier below it that has something, down to the roll's fallback tier, which for the floored slot is its floor, then to the nearest tier above, and the floored slot below its floor last, so it stays Epic or better while anything there is unowned. A Legendary or Mythic roll grants a chase of its tier while one is unowned ([[R1833000]]). If nothing at all is unowned, the slot grants nothing ([[R1306001]]). Every slot, whatever it grants, adds the bar points of the cosmetic rarity it rolled ([[§14.5]], [[R1999000]]).

The Chase Shard bar counts the cosmetic packs the account opens. At `CHASE_SHARD_PACKS` it is full: it buys one chase cosmetic, Legendary or Mythic, that the account chooses and does not own, on a card it is offered for a per-card one, a level only as its card's next, and empties; a retired full art or augment, a level on a card it is not offered, or a level past the next is a 400 and the bar stays full ([[R2725001]], [[R3015004]]). A full bar holds, counting no further, until it buys, so with no chase left to buy it waits ([[R1306002]]). Chases also drop by luck: a duplicate's cosmetic roll ([[§14.3]]) or a cosmetic pack slot that lands on Legendary or Mythic gives one ([[R1333000]]).

The client asks before it spends the bar, since nothing takes a purchase back ([[R2683000]]): the first press on a chase's Buy sends nothing and asks in the row's place, an alert dialog, "Buy Galaxy? This spends your full Chase Shard bar.", naming the cosmetic in the reader's language, with Confirm and Cancel. Cancel holds the focus as the question opens; Confirm sends the one purchase; Cancel and Escape spend nothing and put the focus back on that Buy. The question is the client's alone: whether the bar is full, what it buys and the spend itself stay the server's ([[R2124000]]).

### 14.7 Set tracks and XP

Each set that ships has a track ([[R1420]]) of `SET_TRACK_LEVELS` levels. A track starts at its first level with no XP, and going from level n to the next costs `SET_TRACK_XP_PER_LEVEL` × n XP: the cost rises with every level and is never capped. The XP of games ([[§14.8]]) and quests ([[§14.9]]) goes to every active track, each gaining all of it; a dev account's own grant goes to the track it picks ([[§14.10]]). The newest set's track is active from the start, and an older set's once it unlocks: the older tracks unlock one at a time once the newest set's track reaches `SET_TRACK_UNLOCK_LEVEL`, nearest-older first, one per payout ([[R1302003]]). A track's last level is the last it reaches; XP past it still lands ([[R1302005]]).

Each level pays rewards, cards, Jlore and card packs, from a reward table per set kept as data. A level pays once, as ledger rows, when its track reaches it ([[§14.11]]): a track's first level when its first XP lands, or, for a new account's newest track, when the account becomes active ([[R1310000]]). A track does not pay out every card of its set; pack luck covers the rest ([[§14.3]]).

### 14.8 Earning

Games earn Jlore and XP for the people who play them, never for a ladder bot. A ladder game, a ranked match ([[§9.12]]) that is not a game of a Conquest series, pays its winner `JLORE_LADDER_WIN` and its loser `JLORE_LADDER_LOSS`. A practice game ([[§9.9]]) pays `JLORE_PRACTICE_WIN` for a win and `JLORE_PRACTICE_LOSS` for a loss. An unranked game, a match the queue did not pair that is no game of a Conquest series (a room challenge, [[R604]]), pays `JLORE_UNRANKED_WIN` and `JLORE_UNRANKED_LOSS`; each game of a Conquest series ([[§9.5]]), ranked or not, pays as its own result `JLORE_CONQUEST_WIN` and `JLORE_CONQUEST_LOSS`; and a co-op game ([[§13]]) pays each of its people `JLORE_COOP_WIN` when their team wins and `JLORE_COOP_LOSS` otherwise, and nothing when a void ended it ([[R1502000]]). The Jlore an account's games pay it, every mode's together, stops once the UTC day's `game` Jlore reaches `JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP`, as Hearthstone's games pay gold up to a daily limit: the game that crosses the cap is paid the part that fits, the ones after it no Jlore and no ledger row, and the next UTC day pays again. A capped game still pays its XP and moves quests, and the Jlore a quest or a track level pays neither counts toward the cap nor stops at it; a game's answer says when the cap held its Jlore back ([[R1483000]]).

XP stands in for time played: `XP_PER_ACTION` for each action the player took, a move of the game and never a row the server writes itself ([[R1302004]]), and `XP_WIN_BONUS` more for a win, the game's total never above `XP_GAME_CAP`, so a loop of empty actions cannot farm it. A practice game earns the XP the same game would earn on the ladder, times `XP_PRACTICE_SCALE`, rounded down ([[R1302007]]). An unranked game and a game of a Conquest series earn what the same game earns on the ladder, and a co-op game that times `XP_COOP_SCALE`, rounded down, counting only the moves its person sent in their own seat, never one the AI made for it ([[R1502000]]). The amounts are tuned so a ladder game averages `XP_LADDER_TARGET_MEAN` over [[R1333001]]'s measured games, and a practice game `XP_PRACTICE_SCALE` of it. What a draw, a concede or a game the reaper ends pays, [[R1302000]], [[R1302001]] and [[R1302002]] rule.

Every payout goes through one reward hook on the server, which takes a finished game's summary (the account, the mode, the result and the actions taken), pays it, and moves the account's quests ([[§14.9]]).

- **On the ladder, in an unranked game, a Conquest series and co-op** the server ran the match, so it counts the actions from its own log and pays in the transaction that records the result; the client reports nothing.
- **In practice** the game runs in the browser ([[§9.9]]), so the server takes nothing on the client's word. It issues the game's seed and remembers it with the account, the AI's difficulty and the deck. At the end the client sends the action log; the server folds it ([[§9.3]]) from the seed's setup to the claimed result, each action under its player's nonce and seat. It does not re-check the AI's side, since practice pay is held to the day's Jlore cap ([[R1483000]]): a move of the AI's seat folds like any other ([[R2560000]]). Only a game that passes pays; a seed pays once and expires unclaimed after `PRACTICE_SEED_TTL_SECONDS`. Only an active account's free game on a server seed pays, never a tutorial lesson ([[§9.10]]) or a sandbox game ([[§9.9]]) ([[R1303000]]); a claim burns its seed, and a game resumed after a reload claims on its own ([[R1303001]]); and a paying game brings the last board the server kept, not the device's ([[R1303003]]). The server checks at most `PRACTICE_VERIFY_CONCURRENCY` claims at once, and a claim past that waits its turn, never dropped. A claim that passes is paid in one transaction, which the server tries again when the store fails it, `PRACTICE_PAY_ATTEMPTS` tries in all; one that never lands pays nothing and leaves its seed burned ([[R1688000]]). A claim is kept with its seed until it settles, paid or refused, once: a server that stops waits for the claims it is settling, and the retention loop settles any claim still unsettled `PRACTICE_SETTLE_GRACE_SECONDS` after it was made, and skips one the server is still settling ([[R2422000]], [[R3500001]]). An account holds at most `PRACTICE_SEEDS_OPEN_MAX` unclaimed or claimed-and-unsettled, unexpired seeds, so a claim waiting for its check keeps its place; a request for another is refused and issues nothing, so one account cannot stockpile seeds to claim in a burst, nor queue claims faster than they are checked ([[R1483000]], [[R3500001]]). A game left without saving gives its seed back: the server deletes the account's own unclaimed seed under the account's economy lock, so its place is free for the next request and a claim of it is an unknown seed, and it leaves another account's seed, or one already claimed, as it is. A game the server would not seed says from its start that it earns nothing, in the server's words ([[R2379000]]).

### 14.9 Daily quests

A quest is a goal read from a finished game's public events, such as winning games, playing spells, dealing damage with Units, playing cards of a set, switching Units' positions or making cards Radiant, and a payout of Jlore from `QUEST_JLORE_MIN` to `QUEST_JLORE_MAX`, more for a harder goal, with `QUEST_XP_PER_JLORE` XP for each Jlore of it. Quests are drawn from a pool kept as data, which holds a quest for playing the cards of each set that ships, and a position switch or a Radiant upgrade counts as the game's record counts it ([[R2253000]]). An account holds at most `QUEST_MAX_HELD`; while it holds fewer, a new quest arrives `QUEST_INTERVAL_HOURS` after the last one did, by the server's clock, so a player away for a long while comes back to `QUEST_MAX_HELD` waiting. Every finished game the reward hook sees ([[§14.8]]) moves the held quests' progress, and a quest that completes pays its Jlore and XP once, as ledger rows; the XP goes to every active track ([[§14.7]]). Its rhythm and top-up are [[R1304000]]'s, a game ending as a quest arrives is [[R1304001]]'s, and none arrives while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off ([[R1304002]]). A held quest may be replaced `QUEST_REPLACES_PER_DAY` times a UTC day, as Hearthstone's daily reroll allows: it leaves with its progress, and a quest drawn as an arrival is, neither held nor the one replaced, takes its slot at once, the arrival rhythm unmoved; the panel offers it behind a confirm while one is left, and none is replaced while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off ([[R2210000]]). `/collection` shows the held quests, and the tavern landing shows them behind a bookmark on its doors' box to an active account while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is on ([[R2035000]]).

### 14.10 Dev accounts

A dev account is one whose profile id the server's `DEV_PROFILE_IDS` environment variable lists, read at boot with the rest of the environment: a malformed id fails the boot, naming it, and none set means no dev accounts. The server decides: it tells the client whether the caller is a dev account, and every dev route checks the list itself and refuses anyone else as forbidden. The client only chooses whether to show the Dev tools: a Dev button in the front screen's top bar, on a computer alone (a wide window whose main pointer is not a touch screen's), opens them in a dialog, and the account page has none ([[R3156000]]). Any signed-in account can reveal its own profile id on the account page, hidden until asked for, so a new developer can be listed. In end-to-end mode the fixture account `e2e-econ` is a dev account too, for the test that walks the economy ([[R1310004]]).

A dev account can, for itself alone:

- give XP to a set track it picks, through the level curve ([[§14.7]]), and give Jlore;
- fill its collection with every card and every cosmetic but the Jlorious card back and Starship, which only reaching Jlorious grants ([[R2755000]], [[R3335003]]), the Fleet track's rewards among them ([[R3335002]]), every level of a variant on each card it is offered ([[R3015000]]), so no card's default look, which every account owns there ([[R3343000]]), and set every card's proficiency to its top level ([[R3013001]]);
- unlock every cosmetic the fill grants, and nothing else: no card and no proficiency ([[R3156001]]);
- clear its cards, its cosmetics, its Jlore, its bars and pity counters, or its track progress, each apart, or all of them;
- be given a quest at once, or complete one ([[§14.9]]);
- fill its card token bar or its cosmetic token bar, so it pays at once, the cosmetic token bar to its current step's size ([[§14.5]], [[R1310003]], [[R2728000]]).

Every dev grant and clear is written as ledger rows marked `dev` ([[R1300000]]), so a dev grant can always be told apart ([[§14.11]]); proficiency is progress, no holding, and has no ledger, so the fill writes none of it there ([[§14.14]]). One request gives at most `DEV_JLORE_PER_REQUEST_MAX` Jlore or `DEV_XP_PER_REQUEST_MAX` XP, and every dev tool writes an audit row ([[R3074000]]). No new catalog version grants a dev account its cards, nor anyone ([[R1310001]]), so a clear stays cleared; before the economy went live, [[R481]]'s grant of each new catalog version passed over a dev account for that reason ([[R1300001]]). Otherwise a dev account counts as any other does: its games go into the card statistics ([[§9.11]]) and the ladder ([[§9.12]]).

**The dev dashboard.** A dev account also has the dev dashboard, an app of its own (`apps/admin`) at its own hostname, behind Cloudflare Access, its own sign-in and the server's check of the list on every route ([[R3074000]]). It offers everything the Dev tools panel does, and three things that reach other players, each with its rule:

- its own rating on either ladder, set by hand within `DEV_RATING_MIN` to `DEV_RATING_MAX` and reset to the rating its games earned; on a strict server a hand-set rating keeps the account off the public leaderboard and out of the percentiles and Jlorious until the reset ([[R3074001]]);
- the whole ladder, every row with its rating, marked person or bot, a bot's strength and persona beside it: the one read that tells a bot apart, and the dashboard's alone ([[R3074002]]);
- news posts with no reward, plain text checked as the news file's entries are, and their take-down ([[§14.13]], [[R3074003]]).

The server's `DEV_STRICT` is on for production and off for staging: on a strict server every dashboard route needs the account's two-step code. Every dev tool and every dashboard write leaves an audit row, who, which route, the request, the values before and after, and when, which the dashboard reads and no player does ([[R3074000]]).

### 14.11 The ledger

The ledger today is `collection` plus the append-only `collection_grants` (`crates/server/migrations/0002_collection.sql`): every change writes both in one transaction, and no client path writes either ([[§9.4]]). Every holding of the economy follows that pattern, a current-state table beside an append-only one, readable by its own account alone. Every grant of anything (cards, Jlore, packs, tokens, cosmetics, XP) is one append-only row, written server-side in the transaction that changes the state; so is every spend, such as a pack bought or a token redeemed. A row is never edited, and is deleted only with its account ("Delete my account", [[§9.4]]): a mistaken grant is corrected by an offsetting row.

A game's Jlore row has the reason `game`, and an account's `game` rows over a UTC day are what `JLORE_GAMES_DAILY_CAP` counts, read in the transaction that writes the next one, under the account's economy lock ([[R1301003]], [[R1483000]]); no row is written for a game the cap leaves nothing for.

A replaced quest writes its `removed` row and its replacement's `arrived` row, both with the reason `quest`, in one transaction, and an account's `removed` rows with the reason `quest` over a UTC day are what `QUEST_REPLACES_PER_DAY` counts, read under the account's economy lock ([[R2210000]]); going live's `launch` removals count for nothing.

Every row says why. The collection's reasons are `pack`, `craft`, `reward`, `refund`, `admin`, `launch` and `dev`, a dev tool's own ([[R1300000]]); every economy ledger takes `launch` too, for the reset of going live ([[R1310002]]), the cosmetic ledger `jlorious`, the Jlorious card back's and Starship's grant ([[R2755000]], [[R3335003]]), and `track`, a Fleet track level's reward ([[§14.15]]), and the Jlore and inventory ledgers `news`, a news entry's reward claimed, its reference the entry ([[R2875000]]); a new kind of grant gets its reason from the sub-issue that adds it. A row a dev tool wrote is marked `dev` ([[§14.10]]). A hand-set rating and its reset write a row of the rating ledger, its reason `dev` and marked `dev`, with the rating before and after; the hidden rating stays server-only, so no client reads it ([[R3074001]]). Every dev tool and dashboard write also leaves an audit row, append-only and server-only, which outlives its account and forgets who ([[R3074000]]).

Every roll is seeded on the server, and its seed and result are logged with the grant it makes, so any opening replays exactly and can be audited. Each opening's seed is drawn afresh, so no seed an account can read foretells a later roll.

A spend runs once, however often it is sent ([[R2124000]]). Every spend, a pack bought or opened, a token redeemed, a chase bought, a news reward claimed ([[§14.13]]) or an invite code redeemed ([[§9.4]]), may carry an idempotency key the client mints for a press and sends again on that press's retries. The server reads it under the account's economy lock: a key the account committed a spend with answers what that spend answered and spends nothing, the same key with another request is refused, and a new key's answer is written in the transaction of its grant. Only a committed spend keeps its key, for `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_TTL_HOURS`, and no client reads one. A spend sent without a key runs as it always did.

An account runs one spend at a time ([[R2378000]]). Each economy spend, a pack bought or opened, a token redeemed, a cosmetic pack opened, a chase bought or a news reward claimed, claims its account's turn before it reads the store and holds it until it answers: `ECONOMY_SPENDS_RUNNING_MAX` run at once, and one sent while they run waits its turn in the server, in the order sent and holding no database connection, then runs. One sent while `ECONOMY_SPENDS_WAITING_MAX` already wait is refused at once with 429, stating `SPEND_BUSY_RETRY_AFTER_MS` and spending nothing, so a burst of presses buys or opens one at a time and never holds the database's connections on the lock. Every other request that writes under the account's economy lock for a press, a card's picks saved or uploaded ([[§14.2]]), a card's stamp picked ([[§14.14]]), a practice seed asked for or given back ([[§14.8]]) or a quest replaced ([[§14.9]]), takes the same turn, waiting or refused alike, and writes nothing when refused. That refusal ran nothing and ends no press, and the client holds its Buy, Open, Open all, sealed pack, Reveal all and Next pack, and the cosmetic pack panel's Open and Chase Shard Buy ([[R2683000]]), while one of its spends is out, its Buy and Open buttons turning a loading wheel. An invite code's redemption takes no turn ([[§9.4]]).

### 14.12 Going live

Until the going-live sub-issue (#1310) shipped, these stood: the launch grant, a copy of every non-token card on becoming active ([[R111]]), and [[§9.4]]'s grant of each patch's new cards to every person's active account ([[R481]]). Every person's active account owned every card, a dev account that had cleared its own aside ([[§14.10]]), the server's `ECONOMY_LIVE` switch was off, and the parts of this section were built behind them without changing what a player owned: no caller of the reward hook paid while the switch was off ([[R1302006]]), and no quest arrived ([[R1304002]]). The going-live sub-issue wrote the rulings that replace them ([[R1310000]] to [[R1310004]]), edited [[§9.1]] and [[§9.4]], and turned `ECONOMY_LIVE` on, in the server and in the client, which reads it from `serverConfig.ts`. Since then:

- A new account starts with the starter cards and its tracks' first rewards ([[§14.7]]) and earns the rest as this section says. The starter cards are `STARTER_CARDS`, the Core cards of the three practice decks ([[§9.9]]), whose new ones an account already active when the decks grew to 25 cards was granted as its activation would have granted them ([[R1705002]]), and the first rewards are the newest set's track's first level, paid as the account becomes active ([[R1310000]]). Queueing checks ownership where it already did ([[§9.4]] L5), and a deck save still checks structure alone ([[R250]]): a draft may hold a card the player does not own, and is judged when queued ([[R1310000]]).
- An existing account kept every card granted to it, so nobody lost a deck. Its economy started at zero, and the ledger recorded the move with the reason `launch` ([[R1310002]]).
- A new catalog version grants no account its cards: a new set's cards come from its track and its packs ([[R1310001]]).
- The starter cosmetics stay owned by every person's account. The full-art faces started unowned for everyone, existing accounts included ([[R1310002]]), and every cosmetic added later must be owned ([[§14.2]]). When the hero portraits became cosmetics, every account then signed up kept all six, and a new one starts with `vanilla` alone ([[R1827000]]). The 36 cosmetics added after them are granted to no account, existing ones included: every account earns them ([[R1833000]]). So are the 57 full arts added after those, the six portrait cards' Legendary and Mythic and fifteen more cards' three ([[R2655000]]), and the 21 Animated arts after them, one to each full-art card ([[R2715000]]). So are the Full art version and the Animated and Signature augments after them ([[R2725000]]): no account is granted one, and an account that owns a retired full art owns what it combines on its card by reading, never by a row ([[R2725002]]). So are the Alt art, Sketch and Alt full art versions after them ([[R2940000]]), and the Extended art, Pixel and Comic versions after those ([[R2942000]]), and the Set showcase and Out of frame versions after those ([[R2943000]]), and the Immersive and Prismatic versions after those ([[R2944000]]), and part 6's twelve levels after #3015's ([[R3017000]]). - Practice and hotseat let a player use every card and every cosmetic whatever the ledger holds, and so do the end-to-end tests, but the one that walks the economy itself, on a fixture account of its own ([[R1310004]]), whose token bars a dev account's tool fills ([[R1310003]]).

### 14.13 News

The news is every shipped patch and every alert sent to the players, each entry with one reward an account claims once ([[R2875000]]). The entries are data, `crates/server/news.json`, compiled into the server, which checks them before it serves anything, oldest first: each an `id`, a slug never renamed once shipped; a `kind`, `patch` or `alert`; a `title`; a `summary` of at most `NEWS_SUMMARY_MAX_SENTENCES` sentences; a `postedAt`; and a `reward`. A patch names the version it summarises, one of `patches.json`'s ([[R388]]); an alert names none. A title and a summary are English, shown as written.

**The reward** is one of what the economy already grants: `NEWS_REWARD_JLORE` Jlore ([[§14.1]]); `NEWS_REWARD_CARD_PACKS` unopened card packs, the global one or a set's that ships ([[§14.3]]); `NEWS_REWARD_CARD_TOKENS` card tokens, or `NEWS_REWARD_COSMETIC_TOKENS` cosmetic tokens of a tier the cosmetic token cycle pays, Rare, Epic or Legendary ([[§14.5]]); or `NEWS_REWARD_COSMETIC_PACKS` unopened cosmetic packs ([[§14.6]]). So no reward holds a token or a card of a set that has not shipped ([[R1420]]). The entry's author picks it; an entry that names none takes the default: a global card pack for a patch that adds or reprints cards, and Jlore for every other patch and every alert ([[R2875003]]).

**The window.** An entry is live from its `postedAt` until `NEWS_TTL_DAYS` after it, on the server's clock ([[R2875001]]). Before it, it is not posted; after it, it has expired, claimed or not: it leaves the list and can no longer be claimed.

**The claim.** `GET /api/news` answers an active account the live entries, newest first, each marked claimed or not for that account alone. `POST /api/news/:newsId/claim` claims one entry's reward: a spend, under an idempotency key whose hash covers the entry ([[R2124000]]), in the account's turn ([[R2378000]]) and under its economy lock. It pays the reward as ledger rows with the reason `news` ([[§14.11]]) and keeps the account's claim of the entry in the same transaction, once per account and entry, by a table's key ([[R2875000]]). A second claim, a claim of an expired, unposted or unknown entry, and any claim while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is off are refused and pay nothing. `POST /api/news/claim-all` is Claim all, one spend in the same turn and under the same lock: it claims, in one transaction, every entry live now that the account has not claimed, each decided and paid as its own claim would be, its own ledger rows included, in statements whose number does not grow with the entries ([[R3191000]]).

**Posts.** A dev may post news from the dev dashboard ([[§14.10]]): plain text, its title and summary checked as an entry's are, with no reward, since a reward mints currency for every account. A post lists beside the file's entries for the same `NEWS_TTL_DAYS`, an alert whose reward is null; its claim marks it read and pays nothing, and the client shows its text as text, never as markup. A dev may take a post down, which leaves the news and refuses its claim. A post with a reward still goes in `news.json` by pull request ([[R3074003]]).

**The bookmark.** The tavern landing shows the news behind a bookmark beside the quests' ([[R2035000]]), unrolling the same parchment, to an active account while `ECONOMY_LIVE` is on and some entry is live. An entry is read once its reward is claimed: unread is unclaimed. The bookmark carries a red count of the live entries the server marks unclaimed, and none at zero; a claim takes it down once the server has answered, never before ([[R2875002]]). The client counts nothing it was not sent and decides no expiry (CLAUDE.md rule 7).

### 14.14 Proficiency

An account's **proficiency** of a card is how often it has played that card, and the level those plays reached ([[R3013000]]). It is progress and a presentation, never a rule: no rule, AI decision, action, log entry, game record or replay hash reads it, and the engine, the cards and the AI know nothing of it (CLAUDE.md rules 4 and 7).

**What counts.** A play is a card of the seat's own deck, as dealt, played from its hand: a Unit summoned, a Spell cast or a Trap set, its base and Radiant faces counting toward the same card. A Created card ([[R2089000]]) and a token count for nothing, and so does a card cast without being played ([[§2.4]]). Only a game the server ran and pays for counts: a ladder game, an unranked game and each game of a Conquest series ([[§14.8]]). The server counts each seat's plays from its own fold of the log, as it counts the actions XP pays for ([[R1502000]]), and writes them through the reward hook in the transaction that records the result, so a failure rolls both back; the client reports nothing. A practice game, played against the AI in the browser, counts nothing, so proficiency cannot be farmed on Easy; nor does a co-op game, whose log the server pays from without folding it; nor a ladder bot's side ([[R1454]]). Nothing played before #3013 shipped was counted: every account started at zero.

**Levels.** A card's level is how many of `PROFICIENCY_LEVEL_PLAYS` its plays have reached, from 0 to `PROFICIENCY_LEVELS`; the first comes within a game or two, the last after a long commitment to the card. A level never falls. A dev account's fill sets every collectable card to its top level ([[§14.10]], [[R3013001]]).

**Stamps.** Each level unlocks one **stamp**, a small marker drawn by the client, the same design of each level on every card, a higher level's more ornate; level n unlocks stamp n ([[R3013001]]). A card wears at most one of the stamps its level has unlocked, or none. A stamp is not a cosmetic of the registry ([[§14.2]]) and is never a ledger grant: what a card has unlocked is read off its level where it is worn, never written ([[§14.11]]). `GET /api/proficiency` answers an active account its own cards' plays, levels and stamps; `PUT /api/proficiency/stamp` picks a card's stamp, or none, in the account's turn ([[R2378000]]), and refuses with a 400, writing nothing, a stamp the card's level has not reached. The Collection shows each owned card's level, its plays toward the next level and a picker of its stamps, a locked one with the level it needs.

**Where it shows.** A card wears its stamp in a fixed corner of its face, in hand, on the board, in the deck builder and in the Collection, clear of its cost, name, stats, rules text, crest and every Radiant mark, in no colour of Radiant's gold ([[R3013002]]). The opponent, a watcher and a replay's viewer see a seat's stamps through the loadout the match froze ([[R1834002]]), each card's only once a view has shown that card ([[R1834003]]), and a viewer's switch that turns the other seats' cosmetics off hides them too ([[R1834004]]). A ladder bot wears none. Duos shows none.

The account's rows are kept apart from the cosmetics registry and the card picks ([[R1834000]]), in a table of their own that an account reads alone ([[§9.1]]).

### 14.15 The Fleet track

The **Fleet track** is a cosmetic track of its own, apart from the Stinkyscape set track ([[§14.7]], which it leaves as it is): `FLEET_TRACK_LEVELS` levels, each `FLEET_TRACK_XP_PER_LEVEL` XP, earned by playing Stinkyscape, each level paying one Stinkyscape cosmetic ([[R3335001]]). An account starts it at level 0 with no XP. It is progress and a presentation, never a rule: no rule, AI decision, action, log entry or replay hash reads it (CLAUDE.md rules 4 and 7).

**What counts.** Only a game the server ran and records pays Fleet XP: a ladder game, an unranked game and each game of a Conquest series ([[§14.8]]). A practice game, played in the browser, pays none, and nor does a hotseat game, a tutorial lesson, a sandbox game or a dungeon run, which the server never records; a co-op game, whose log the server pays from without folding it ([[§14.14]]), pays none yet; and a ladder bot earns nothing ([[R1454]]). The server counts each seat's sources from its own fold of the game's public log, in the transaction that records the result, the client reporting nothing:

| Source | XP |
| --- | --- |
