## Findings

### 1. Two builds at once run the box short, and Muse is half of every such pair

Each of the 11 runs that stalled 5 s or more had a Muse build or revision and an agy build or
revision on the box at the same time. The Muse builds and revisions that ran alone stalled
1.2 s at most, the longest of them a 128-minute build (#1256):

| Muse run, alone | Took | `full` stall | Pages swapped | Least available |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| build #1192 (10-18 07:41Z) | 108 min | 0.2 s | 27,017 | 6.2 GB |
| revise #1248 (10-18 21:24Z) | 28 min | 1.2 s | 20,921 | 6.1 GB |
| build #1242 (10-18 22:15Z) | 33 min | 0.8 s | 141,837 | 6.1 GB |
| build #1256 (10-19 04:00Z) | 128 min | 0.8 s | 108,574 | 5.9 GB |

| Muse run, beside an agy build | Took | `full` stall | Pages swapped | Least available |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| build #1151 (10-18 02:15Z), beside agy's build #1147 and revision #1167 | 156 min | 12.8 s | 3,062,589 | 3.0 GB |
| build #1216 (10-18 16:29Z), beside agy's builds #1218 and #1220 | 243 min | 17.9 s | 4,767,941 | 5.3 GB |
| build #1179 (10-18 19:28Z), beside agy's build #1220 | 122 min | 17.1 s | 2,184,939 | 5.6 GB |

#383's two-Muse-builds reading (4.6 GB free, no swap) was taken before the checks were Rust. Since
the cutover a build compiles the workspace inside its model call, and two of them at once no
longer fit in 7.6 GB. The "least available" column does not show it, because a job reads memory
only at its start, after each model call and check run, and at its end
(`bot/harness/memory.py`). A peak inside a two-hour call never appears there; the stall and swap
counters, which accumulate, catch it.

The sharing also seems to slow Muse. Muse builds that shared the box with another build for at
least half their run took a median 57 minutes in the build call, against 34 minutes alone
(n = 14 and 44). The items differ, so this suggests a cost without proving one.

### 2. Muse's plan is not what limits it; its one lane and the box are

#682 records that on the $15 plan Muse ran out on 10-09 after 71.8 model hours and stood idle for
2.5 of 7 days. On the $50 plan it did 75.3 model hours in 7 days and its meters never passed 9%.
At that rate the plan has room for several times Muse's present use, if the meter is right. The
rule that holds Muse to one lane (#695 change 11) was written for claude-2, whose two lanes raced
one 90% window to its cap. With caps at 100% and readings under 10%, Muse has no such race. What
holds it back is the box: a second Muse lane there makes another pair of builds like those in
finding 1.

The meter needs a caveat. It reset in the middle of the week (the table above), and Muse's CLI
reports no tokens, so nothing else checks it. `runner.py` notes that Muse's words for a refusal
are not known (`_with_usage`): only a full window in the reading turns a failed call into a
refusal. If the meter undercounts, the first sign will be a failed call the bot does not
recognise as a refusal.

### 3. #383's sizing for Muse is out of date

- Machine 1 is sized "so the pool fits 4 jobs" with "Muse ×4, agy, gpt, and Devin's overflow".
  The readings now say an `m7i-flex.large` fits one building job, with lighter jobs beside it.
  Two building jobs already swap.
- Phase 2 moves "Muse (4 runners)" to a scale set, and the design gives a scale set "maximum runners
  … that subscription's lanes". For Muse that maximum is 1.
- A per-pod memory request has nothing to start from: no job's own peak has been measured, since
  every reading is the whole box's. That two builds at once overflow the roughly 6.7 GB the idle box
  has free suggests a building pod needs a request somewhere around 3.5 GB. Phase 1's cgroup
  readings (`memory.peak`, hard part 5) should settle it before the pools are sized.

### 4. For Muse, hard part 1 needs no volume

The harness can already hand a Muse job its login from a secret on any runner: `prepare` writes
`auth.json` under `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/muse/`, and `seal` puts a refreshed copy in the vault for the
next job. A Muse pod could therefore start from `MUSE_AUTH` with no persistent volume, as the Claude
accounts do on GitHub's runners. Only agy and Devin keep logins that exist only on the machine
(`providers.SECRET_CLIS`). One limit: a refreshed login goes back through the vault, so two jobs
running at once on one secret would race to write it. With Muse at one lane that cannot happen; a
second lane on one secret would need a test first.

### 5. Hard part 5 is already a problem on the box

Readings are the box's, filed under the job that took them. Today's "short of memory" Muse runs
are short because agy built beside them, and nothing in `machine_memory` says which job used what.
If the PC runner takes a `night-vm-*` job, its container's `/proc` figures will be recorded as the
night box's. No PC job appears in the last 60 readings. Each pool, and the PC, needs its own
readings before `machine_parallel` or a pod request is set from them.

### 6. The docs still say four lanes

None of these says that Muse runs one lane:

- `.harness/providers.json:200`, Muse's note: "Four lanes, `night-vm-muse` and `night-vm-muse-2` to
  `night-vm-muse-4` … registers one runner per lane", beside `"lanes": 1`.
- `bot/machine/README.md:42` ("Muse has four"), and `:279` ("twelve runners' installs").
- `bot/README.md:220` ("Muse's four lanes"), `:437` ("on four lanes"), `:498` ("Muse has four, on one
  login") and `:528` ("Muse's is 4, and claude-1, claude-2, claude-3 and claude-7 have 2 each"; of
  those only claude-3 has 2).
- `bot/machine/register-runners.sh` registers one runner per lane but never removes one, so
  `night-vm-muse-2` to `-4` are still registered. They take Muse's single job in turn (the last 60
  readings name all four), and each keeps a runner install of about 0.7 GB, twice that after a
  self-update. The planner, not the runner count, holds Muse to one job.

## Recommendation for Muse

**Before any of plans 2 to 5 is built for Muse's sake, run Muse from a secret on GitHub's runners
for a week, on its one lane.** It is the one option that costs nothing on AWS, gives Muse a whole
four-vCPU, 16 GB machine per job, and removes the night box's main load. On the journals' figures
the box was on about 16.2 hours a day since #700, counting a job or the 30 minutes after one.
Without Muse's runs it would have been on about 5.7. The change:

1. A person logs Muse in once more, on a machine of their own (`muse login`, a second login of the
   same account, as the PC runner's is), and pastes that `~/.config/muse/auth.json` into the secret
   `MUSE_AUTH`. A second login leaves the box's working copy alone.
2. In its own pull request (as #682 asks for any change to `providers.json`): Muse's `login` becomes
   `secret`, its `secret` becomes `MUSE_AUTH` and its `runs_on` becomes `ubuntu-latest`. Its note,
   and the four docs above, then say one lane. `bot-night.yml` already installs the CLI on GitHub's
   runners ("Install the muse CLI").
3. A week on, compare against this audit's week: Muse's runs, merges and model hours, its run
   lengths, whether the login and its refresh held (a `vault/muse.enc` on `bot-state` once Muse
   first refreshes it, and no run backing off for its login), and the night box's memory line on
   the status issue.

What it costs:

- On GitHub's runners every gate runs, clippy, the cargo tests and the web tests included
  (`"machine": false`), which #1263 measures at 12 to 34 minutes for a first check round there
  against 4 to 6 for Muse on the box. #1264, open, caches that build. Muse's runs get longer until
  it lands. They are also checked as the Claude accounts' runs are.
- Muse's job takes one of the organization's 20 hosted jobs. A `learn` export can hold 12
  (#1263, Not in this issue), and Muse counts against `max_parallel` (11), where the Claude
  accounts hold 8 lanes.
- The CLI is installed fresh each job from `dev.meta.ai/install.sh`, so GitHub's runners get the
  newest Muse while the box keeps the one `setup.sh` last installed. The `/usage` parser
  (`parse_muse_usage`) depends on the panel's wording, and a new CLI could change it.
- Unknowns that only the week answers: whether a Muse login works from GitHub's addresses at all,
  and how often it rotates.

**If that works**, the night box carries agy, Codex, and Devin until 11-02. Plan 1 with
`machine_parallel` lowered (the first step of #383's phase 0) is then enough for the box, and the
case for Machine 2 and for k3s rests on agy and training alone. **If it does not**, Muse stays on
the box, and whichever plan is built must allow for findings 1 and 3. Lower `machine_parallel` so
that two building jobs cannot share the 8 GB box. Make Muse's scale set (plan 5) or cap (plan 2)
one runner, not four. Give Muse's builds the 16 GB machine if both Muse and agy are to build at
the same time.
