**Should Muse go from $15 to $50, with Devin dropped? Yes, both.**

## Recommendations

1. **Upgrade Muse to the $50 plan.** Keep it first among the medium builders.
2. **Turn Devin off in the night bot** before 2026-10-15, with `enabled: false` or by letting
   `off_from` do it.
3. **Stop the `unban` training lane.** Under its own rule it cannot promote. Keep `improve` on
   Devin until 2026-10-16. After that, pause it or give it a capped schedule. A loop of four-hour
   sessions would use up a week of any capped subscription in days.
4. **Make Sonnet the default builder on the Claude accounts**, and keep Opus for plans, hard items,
   reviews and the reconcile. Opus's caps are the binding limit: 86 of 96 Opus-built runs were
   cut short by one.
5. **Stop counting a Muse or Gemini approval as enough on its own.** Two medium approvals from
   the same model are allowed today (`bot/harness/review_rule.py`), and that is how #142, #166,
   #304, #324, #362, #615 and #626 merged, or tried to. Wherever Opus looked at that kind of work,
   it found something (section 7). On #304 and #324, later medium approvals merged the change past
   Opus's open blocking findings. Let only a strong review clear a strong reviewer's block.
6. **Check fullsend parts before they land.** A Muse part that nothing checks broke compilation and
   18 tests (section 3). Have it run `cargo build` and the crate's tests before it lands on the
   tree's branch.
7. **Drop or pause the Codex (ChatGPT) seat** until its allowance can support real work.

Opus's lower rate does not mean weaker work. Opus gets the hard items, since a run starts on the

Once Opus approved in the run, a pull request merged clean. The 16 Sonnet-built and 8 Opus-built
pull requests met no CI failure after they opened, and only 3 of each needed a revision. After a

### What this says

- **Opus is the strongest model, and it is starved.** 40% to 50% of every kind of Opus step ends
  at a usage cap: 48 of 120 plans, 48 of 96 builds, 53 of 108 reviews. With no cap in the way, it
  delivers 80% to 94% of the time. The caps are the owner's settings in
  `.harness/providers.json` (most accounts stop at 40% to 90% of their 5-hour session), so what
  holds Opus back is capacity, not quality.
- **Sonnet is the better use of a Claude account for medium work.** It builds in about three
  quarters of Opus's time (21 minutes median, against 29). A cap cuts its builds short a third
  as often (9 of 53, against 48 of 96). Its pull requests merged as cleanly as Opus's did, on the
  work each was given.
- **Sonnet's two weak spots:** a long sweep under a strict rule over many files (#602), and a
  build that ends with no change (12 of 53). After Sonnet is sent back twice, the harness moves
  the run to Opus (`bot/README.md`, Switching between Opus and Sonnet).

## 3. Muse

- **The most consistent builder at delivering something:** 91% of its outcomes all time. The
  2026-10-05 audit counted 75 of 77, the best of any model (#317: "Muse is the most reliable
  builder"). Its 18 merged pull requests carry +45,404 lines, more than any other single model.
- **It built most of the Meditative set:** 18 of the 33 fullsend parts (#575). Fifteen of those
  18 ran to the 150-minute limit (`timed out`) and landed whatever was on disk. Sonnet's parts
  took 16 to 34 minutes, and Opus's 89 and 109.
- **Its logic is right, its finishing is not.** Squishy reviewed three of its parts on their own
  (#518, #519 and #520, in a comment on #575 on 2026-10-09). The behaviour was faithful: every
  card did what the designer wrote, and the rules suite and the golden replay passed. But it also
  found:
  - one part that didn't compile (a borrow error);
  - one that used a `HashSet`, which CLAUDE.md rule 4 bans;
  - generated wire TypeScript that was never committed;
  - 18 card tests and 5 cross tests failing, all of them test bugs rather than card bugs;
  - placeholder seeds such as `"TBD-success"`.

  The reconcile (#652) fixed all of it. On its own pull requests the pattern is the same: 24 CI
  failures over 20 pull requests, and 2.5 revisions each. One person put it on #142: "If youre
  failing the CI the code youre writing is shit". Muse runs on the night box, where a run skips
  clippy and the tests, so CI was often the first to compile its work.
- **It reviews too kindly.** It approved its own builds 21 times in 22, and CI failed after 10 of
  those approvals. Opus reviewed Muse's work 7 times and blocked 5 of them:
  - #296: "Match found!" never shows;
  - #304, three times: SPEC §10.11 still named the removed Account tab;
  - #324: web tests still expected the old text, and tutorial lesson 1 broke R291.

  Muse had approved each of these.
- **Its capacity is the $15 plan's.** Its 7-day reading hit 100% on 2026-10-09 at 11:09 UTC,
  after 71.8 model hours since its window opened on 2026-10-05, about 39 of them on 2026-10-09
  alone. It has logged nothing since. The window resets on 2026-10-12.

## 8. Method and limits

- **The journals start on 2026-10-06**, so the split between Opus and Sonnet covers five days.
  Before then, the Claude accounts ran Opus only. Sonnet came with #317 part 5.
- **The eras differ.** Most of Muse's and Devin's pull requests came from 2026-10-03 to 10-05,
  before the plans-first rule, the easy rule and the work-keeping fixes of #317. Sonnet's all
  came after them. Some of the gap between them is the harness, not the model.
- **Difficulty is missing.** Few items record their difficulty in the state file, so the Opus and
  Sonnet comparison can't hold it fixed. The harness gives Opus the hard items, which favours
  Sonnet's numbers.
- **Model hours are wall-clock time** in the model's CLI, not tokens. The subscriptions'
  allowances don't compare in the same unit.
- **Light checks on the night box.** Muse, agy and Devin run there, where a run skips clippy and
  the tests. Claude runs run every check before review, so some of the CI failures after a Muse
  or Devin approval are failures a Claude run would have caught before its review.
- **The $50 Muse plan's allowance isn't in the repository.** The recommendation assumes it grows
  about in line with the price.
