# Shadow-ban sweep of record, 2026-10-07

The first sweep of the Rust AI, and the first over all 268 non-token cards (Core, Classic and Classic+): the
TypeScript sweep of record (2026-09-27) covered the 100 Core cards and #65 left the other 168 unswept. R186
and R390's two passes, run by `jackioh sweep` exactly as its help describes, on generation 0 (the port of
`packages/ai` at `91cc43c`, #306 part 40). It changes no deck: `SHADOW_BAN` stays generation 0's eleven (#306),
`SHADOW_WATCH` takes the 41 cards it names, and the cards it would ban or clear are the unban
lane's starting notes.

## How it ran

- **Tree**: `staging` at `32713ca` (the AI, engine and cards last changed in `367cbc3`), built once with
  `cargo build --release -p jackioh-tools` and run as `target/release/jackioh`; `SHADOW_WATCH` was empty.
- **Machine**: 4 cores (Intel Xeon, 2.1 GHz), nothing else running. Each pass ran as four slices, one per
  core, as the CLI's help describes: pass 1 as `jackioh sweep --json <67 ids>` four times (16:38 to 17:22
  UTC), pass 2 as `jackioh sweep --pass2 <the four pass-1 files> <a quarter of the at-risk ids>` (17:22 to
  18:22 UTC), the report as `jackioh sweep --report <all eight files>`. Slicing changes no result.
- **Seeds**: pass 1 `sweep:<tier>:<id>:<n>`, n = 1 to 8, for every non-token id at `easy` and `hard`
  (4,288 games); pass 2 `sweep2:<tier>:<id>:<n>`, n = 1 to 24, for the 91 at-risk ids listed below at both
  tiers (4,368 games). A game's decks come from `<seed>:deck:<seat>` and its controllers from
  `<seed>:ctl:<seat>`, so every game replays; only the `timeout` flag reads the clock.
- **Budget**: `AI_GATE_BUDGET` {"nodes":600,"lethalNodes":150,"determinizations":3,"beamWidth":4,
  "rootBranching":20,"branching":6,"maxDepth":8,"finalists":3}; `AI_SWEEP.decision_ms` 2000, `max_actions`
  600.
- **Generation 0's gates** on the same build (`jackioh gate --full`, before the sweep): 94 of 100 against
  random, 35 of 50 against greedy, 47 of 50 as Hard against Easy, no turn-cap draws (needed: 91, 28, 40);
  the perf gate's slowest decision 402.5 ms as the development machine would time it (284.1 ms raw; game
  6, turn 4, 344 nodes), the wide boards 396.6 ms (hard) and 244.0 ms (easy), against a limit of 1,500.

### What the timeouts measure

63 of the 64 bans carry `timeout`, a decision over 2,000 ms of wall clock or a game past 600 actions, where the
TypeScript sweep of 2026-09-27 flagged none. They are slow decisions, not a busy machine: replayed alone on the
idle machine, `sweep:easy:core-019:2` (Midrange Menace, all 8 of its pass-1 timeouts) again took 8 or 9
decisions over 2 s, up to 4.1 s. In one of those reruns all eight came between turns 26 and 38 of a 39-turn
game, on boards of one to five units a side with 5 to 20 legal actions, and were mostly attacks; the same
game's early decisions take tens of milliseconds, and its state grows from 16 KB of JSON at the deal to 104 KB
by turn 36. The perf gate times only the first six ai-vs-greedy games and two wide boards, so it never reaches
such turns. So a `timeout` here says the forced card was dealt into a game that reached them; it does not show
that the card itself is slow to think about. Pass 1 counted 56 timeouts over its 4,288 games (and one error),
pass 2 240 over its 4,368, whose decks deal the at-risk cards as boosted filler (no error); 74 of pass 2's games
with a timeout also dealt other at-risk cards, the suspects. A decision near 2 s can land on either side of the
limit from one run to the next, so a card's count can differ by one or two between runs.

The one `error` is `sweep:easy:core-076:2`: inside the AI's reply simulation the engine looks up a fused card's
id (`t-4:classicplus-039+classic-060`, Book Worm fused with Pile On) in the static catalog and panics
(`unknown defId ...: register the catalog first`, from `copied_text::copied_chooses_x` under
`play_choices::play_prices`, `candidate_actions` and `reply::simulate_reply`); the decision fell back.
