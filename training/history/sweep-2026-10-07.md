# Shadow-ban sweep of record, 2026-10-07

The first sweep of the Rust AI, and the first over all 268 non-token cards (Core, Classic and Classic+): the
TypeScript sweep of record (2026-09-27) covered the 100 Core cards and #65 left the other 168 unswept. R186
and R390's two passes, run by `jackioh sweep` exactly as its help describes, on generation 0 (the port of
`packages/ai` at `91cc43c`, #306 part 40). It changes no deck: `SHADOW_BAN` stays generation 0's eleven (#306),
`SHADOW_WATCH` takes the 67 cards it names, and the cards it would ban or clear are the unban lane's starting
notes.

**The first run.** This replaces a first run of the same day (in git history at `fa03797`), which would have
banned 64 cards. 63 of its bans were `timeout`: a fused card's scripts were composed again on every lookup, so
a card fused onto again and again (Classic+ #74) cost the AI seconds per decision late in a game (fixed in
`7e91089`). Its one `error` (`sweep:easy:core-076:2`) was the AI's redaction dropping a fused definition that a
play record still named (fixed in `b8ad9f9`). This run is the same sweep on the build with both fixes.

## How it ran

- **Tree**: `staging` at `7e91089` (both fixes above), built once with `cargo build --release -p jackioh-tools`
  and run as `target/release/jackioh`. `SHADOW_WATCH` held the first run's 41 cards, which R390 counts at risk
  from the start. It added none to pass 2: all 41 were at risk by their own pass-1 numbers again. The 91
  at-risk cards are pass 1's 88 and three of `SHADOW_BAN` (core-051, core-082, core-091), the same 91 as the
  first run's.
- **Machine**: 4 cores (Intel Xeon, 2.1 GHz). Each pass ran as four slices, one per core, as the CLI's help
  describes: pass 1 as `jackioh sweep --json <67 ids>` four times, ids dealt to the slices in turn (20:43:31 to
  21:19:13 UTC); pass 2 as `jackioh sweep --pass2 <the four pass-1 files> <a quarter of the at-risk ids>`
  (21:57:38 to 22:35:55 UTC, nothing else running); the report as `jackioh sweep --report <all eight files>`.
  Slicing changes no result. The last seven cards of pass 1's fourth slice ran while a replay harness was
  built and one of the timeouts replayed beside them; the one flag among them (Chaos Machine, hard) repeats
  alone on an idle core.
- **Seeds**: pass 1 `sweep:<tier>:<id>:<n>`, n = 1 to 8, for every non-token id at `easy` and `hard`
  (4,288 games); pass 2 `sweep2:<tier>:<id>:<n>`, n = 1 to 24, for the 91 at-risk ids listed below at both
  tiers (4,368 games). A game's decks come from `<seed>:deck:<seat>` and its controllers from
  `<seed>:ctl:<seat>`, so every game replays; only the `timeout` flag reads the clock.
- **Budget**: `AI_GATE_BUDGET` {"nodes":600,"lethalNodes":150,"determinizations":3,"beamWidth":4,
  "rootBranching":20,"branching":6,"maxDepth":8,"finalists":3}; `AI_SWEEP.decision_ms` 2000, `max_actions`
  600.
- **Generation 0's gates** on the same tree (`jackioh gate --full`): 94 of 100 against random, 35 of 50 against
  greedy, 47 of 50 as Hard against Easy, no turn-cap draws (needed: 91, 28, 40); the perf gate's slowest
  decision 363.6 ms as the development machine would time it (271.4 ms raw; game 6, turn 4, 344 nodes), the
  wide boards 459.3 ms (hard) and 276.2 ms (easy), against a limit of 1,500.

### What the timeouts measure

22 of the 25 bans are `timeout`, from 23 games: 8 in pass 1 and 15 in pass 2, each the forced card's own game.
Every one of those games was replayed alone on an idle core and profiled, and two causes cover them all. Alone,
each again has a decision over 2 s, except Second Wind's and Final Gambit's, whose slowest come to 1.7 to 2.0 s
(a decision near 2 s can land on either side of the limit from one run to the next, so a card's count can
differ by one or two between runs). As with the first run's timeouts, a ban here says the forced card was dealt
into a game that reached one of the causes, not that the card itself is slow to think about.

- **The R29 scorers, 22 games.** The slow decisions take 2 to 10 s for the usual 130 to 540 nodes, because
  a play they simulate resolves a scorer that dry-runs a whole pool of cards: Classic+ #27 Zephrys Zealotism's
  perfect hand plays every Classic and Classic+ card on a copy of the state (R416), and #97 Zephyrs ranks the
  catalog for its Discover. The play can be the AI's own or the opponent's in the reply, from a card dealt or
  one a determinization sampled into the opponent's hand (R185), so neither card need be in the game. The
  scorers' dry runs are 18 to 93% of each whole game's profile, and more of its slow decisions'. This is the
  rule's own cost, not a regression of the port: the TypeScript AI makes the same decisions with the same node
  counts and is slower here. Decision 59 of `sweep:easy:classic-029:6` (540 nodes, the same action) takes
  7.9 s in TypeScript at `05f5cfd` and 2.9 s in Rust.
- **A turn that never ends, 1 game.** `sweep:easy:classicplus-054:2` stops at the 600-action bound on turn 22.
  The AI took In Too Deep's (Classic #90) quest reward L, "you may play cards from your graveyard", and a Book
  of Stats (Classic+ #57) that Book of Books priced at (0) keeps that price in the graveyard. So the AI plays
  it from there again and again, about 490 times, each play giving the same unit +5/+5. The TypeScript engine
  allows the same free replays on the same state.
